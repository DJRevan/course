The **Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor** is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") (AMPAS). It has been awarded since the [9th Academy Awards](/wiki/9th_Academy_Awards "9th Academy Awards") to an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a [supporting role](/wiki/Supporting_character "Supporting character") in a film released that year. The award is traditionally presented by the previous year's [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress") winner. However, in recent years, it has shifted towards being presented by previous years' Best Supporting Actor winners instead. In lieu of the traditional [Oscar statuette](/wiki/Academy_Awards#Oscar_statuette "Academy Awards"), supporting acting recipients were given plaques up until the [16th Academy Awards](/wiki/16th_Academy_Awards "16th Academy Awards"),[1] when statuettes were awarded to each category instead.[2]

The Best Supporting Actor award has been presented a total of 90 times, to 81 actors. The first winner was [Walter Brennan](/wiki/Walter_Brennan "Walter Brennan") for his role in _[Come and Get It](/wiki/Come_and_Get_It_\(1936_film\) "Come and Get It \(1936 film\)")_ (1936). The most recent winner is [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn "Sean Penn") for _[One Battle After Another](/wiki/One_Battle_After_Another "One Battle After Another")_ (2025).[3] The record for [most wins](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_two_or_more_Academy_Awards_in_acting_categories "List of actors with two or more Academy Awards in acting categories") is three, held by Brennan–who won every other year within a succession of the first five years. Seven other actors have won twice. Brennan is also tied for receiving the [most nominations](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_more_than_one_Academy_Award_nomination_in_the_acting_categories "List of actors with more than one Academy Award nomination in the acting categories") in the category (with four altogether) along with [Jeff Bridges](/wiki/Jeff_Bridges "Jeff Bridges"), [Robert Duvall](/wiki/Robert_Duvall "Robert Duvall"), [Arthur Kennedy](/wiki/Arthur_Kennedy "Arthur Kennedy"), [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson "Jack Nicholson"), [Al Pacino](/wiki/Al_Pacino "Al Pacino"), [Claude Rains](/wiki/Claude_Rains "Claude Rains"), and [Mark Ruffalo](/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo "Mark Ruffalo"). For his performance in _[The Dark Knight](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight "The Dark Knight")_ (2008), [Heath Ledger](/wiki/Heath_Ledger "Heath Ledger") became the first actor to [win posthumously in this category](/wiki/List_of_posthumous_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees "List of posthumous Academy Award winners and nominees")—and second overall. [Christopher Plummer](/wiki/Christopher_Plummer "Christopher Plummer") is the oldest actor to [receive a nomination in any category](/wiki/List_of_oldest_and_youngest_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Oldest_nominees_4 "List of oldest and youngest Academy Award winners and nominees") at age 88, for _[All the Money in the World](/wiki/All_the_Money_in_the_World "All the Money in the World")_ (2017). 

## Nominations process

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Nominations process")]

Nominees are currently determined by [single transferable vote](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") within the actors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a [plurality vote](/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\) "Plurality \(voting\)") from the entire [eligible voting members](/wiki/Film_industry "Film industry") of the Academy.[4]

## Winners and nominees

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Winners and nominees")]

In the following table, the years are listed as per Academy convention, and generally correspond to the year of film release in [Los Angeles County](/wiki/Los_Angeles_County,_California "Los Angeles County, California"); the ceremonies are always held the following year.[5] For the first five ceremonies, the eligibility period spanned twelve months, from August 1 to July 31.[6] For the 6th ceremony held in 1934, the eligibility period lasted from August 1, 1932, to December 31, 1933.[6] Since the 7th ceremony held in 1935, the period of eligibility became the full previous calendar year from January 1 to December 31.[6]

## Films with multiple Supporting Actor nominations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Films with multiple Supporting Actor nominations")]

There have been 23 instances in which films have produced more than one nominee within this category. All resulted in two nominations, with the exceptions of _On the Waterfront_ (1954), _The Godfather_ (1972), and _The Godfather Part II_ (1974), which each obtained three. 

Winners are in **bold**. 

  * _[Mr. Smith Goes to Washington](/wiki/Mr._Smith_Goes_to_Washington "Mr. Smith Goes to Washington")_ (1939) – [Harry Carey Sr.](/wiki/Harry_Carey_\(actor\) "Harry Carey \(actor\)") and [Claude Rains](/wiki/Claude_Rains "Claude Rains")
  * _[Quo Vadis](/wiki/Quo_Vadis_\(1951_film\) "Quo Vadis \(1951 film\)")_ (1951) – [Leo Genn](/wiki/Leo_Genn "Leo Genn") and [Peter Ustinov](/wiki/Peter_Ustinov "Peter Ustinov")
  * _[Shane](/wiki/Shane_\(film\) "Shane \(film\)")_ (1953) – [Brandon deWilde](/wiki/Brandon_deWilde "Brandon deWilde") and [Jack Palance](/wiki/Jack_Palance "Jack Palance")
  * _[On the Waterfront](/wiki/On_the_Waterfront "On the Waterfront")_ (1954) – [Lee J. Cobb](/wiki/Lee_J._Cobb "Lee J. Cobb"), [Karl Malden](/wiki/Karl_Malden "Karl Malden"), and [Rod Steiger](/wiki/Rod_Steiger "Rod Steiger")
  * _[Peyton Place](/wiki/Peyton_Place_\(film\) "Peyton Place \(film\)")_ (1957) – [Arthur Kennedy](/wiki/Arthur_Kennedy "Arthur Kennedy") and [Russ Tamblyn](/wiki/Russ_Tamblyn "Russ Tamblyn")
  * _[Anatomy of a Murder](/wiki/Anatomy_of_a_Murder "Anatomy of a Murder")_ (1959) – [Arthur O'Connell](/wiki/Arthur_O%27Connell "Arthur O'Connell") and [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott "George C. Scott")
  * _[The Hustler](/wiki/The_Hustler "The Hustler")_ (1961) – [Jackie Gleason](/wiki/Jackie_Gleason "Jackie Gleason") and George C. Scott
  * _[Bonnie and Clyde](/wiki/Bonnie_and_Clyde_\(film\) "Bonnie and Clyde \(film\)")_ (1967) – [Gene Hackman](/wiki/Gene_Hackman "Gene Hackman") and [Michael J. Pollard](/wiki/Michael_J._Pollard "Michael J. Pollard")
  * _[The Last Picture Show](/wiki/The_Last_Picture_Show "The Last Picture Show")_ (1971) – [Jeff Bridges](/wiki/Jeff_Bridges "Jeff Bridges") and **[Ben Johnson](/wiki/Ben_Johnson_\(actor\) "Ben Johnson \(actor\)")**

## Notes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Notes")]

  1. **^** Due to category confusion, [Barry Fitzgerald](/wiki/Barry_Fitzgerald "Barry Fitzgerald") received nominations in both [lead actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor#1940s "Academy Award for Best Actor") and supporting for this same role, winning the latter award. To prevent this from occurring in the future, the Academy revised its rules so that if ever a single actor/actress was submitted in both categories for the same performance, they would only get nominated for whichever one had the higher overall percentage within that respective category.[15]
  2. **^** [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott "George C. Scott") refused his nomination.[34]
  3. ^ _**a**_ _**b**_ An anomaly occurred when both [LaKeith Stanfield](/wiki/LaKeith_Stanfield "LaKeith Stanfield") and [Daniel Kaluuya](/wiki/Daniel_Kaluuya "Daniel Kaluuya") received nominations in the supporting category, despite the former being campaigned for the lead race. This occurrence is likely a direct effect of the [AMPAS](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") rule enacted after Fitzgerald's nominations at the 17th ceremony. Stanfield and Kaluuya received votes in both categories, but each of them had more nominations in supporting, thus each making the top five nominees in this category.[94]
  4. **^** _[Judas and the Black Messiah](/wiki/Judas_and_the_Black_Messiah "Judas and the Black Messiah")_ was not released in theaters until 2021, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") disrupting theatrical releases. However, Academy guidelines were adjusted for 2020, thus allowing this and several other films to be submitted for the 2020 calendar year of nominees/winners.[102]
