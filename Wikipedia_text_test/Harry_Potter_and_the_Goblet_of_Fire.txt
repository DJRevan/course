_**Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire**_ is a [fantasy novel](/wiki/Fantasy_novel "Fantasy novel") by British author [J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling"). It is the fourth novel in the _[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ series. It follows [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)"), a wizard in his fourth year at [Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry](/wiki/Hogwarts_School_of_Witchcraft_and_Wizardry "Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry"), and the mystery surrounding the entry of Harry's name into the Triwizard Tournament, in which he is forced to compete. 

The book was published in the United Kingdom by [Bloomsbury](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing") and in the United States by [Scholastic](/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation "Scholastic Corporation"). In both countries, the release date was 8 July 2000. This was the first time a book in the series was published in both countries at the same time. The novel won a [Hugo Award](/wiki/Hugo_Award "Hugo Award"), the only _Harry Potter_ novel to do so, in 2001. The book was adapted into a [film](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire \(film\)"), released worldwide on 18 November 2005, and a [video game](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire_\(video_game\) "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire \(video game\)") by [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts"). 

## Plot

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")]

During the summer, there is an attack at the [Quidditch](/wiki/Quidditch "Quidditch") World Cup. The cloaked assailants resemble [Death Eaters](/wiki/Death_Eaters "Death Eaters"), the followers of [Voldemort](/wiki/Voldemort "Voldemort"). When the school term begins at Hogwarts, [Alastor "Mad-Eye" Moody](/wiki/Mad-Eye_Moody "Mad-Eye Moody") is introduced as the new [Defence Against the Dark Arts](/wiki/Defence_Against_the_Dark_Arts "Defence Against the Dark Arts") professor. Dumbledore announces that students from the foreign wizarding schools [Beauxbatons](/wiki/Beauxbatons "Beauxbatons") and [Durmstrang](/wiki/Durmstrang "Durmstrang") will be arriving at Hogwarts to participate in the Triwizard Tournament. Students are invited to nominate themselves for the tournament by putting their names into an artefact called the [Goblet of Fire](/wiki/Goblet_of_Fire "Goblet of Fire"). Although Harry does not nominate himself, he is mysteriously selected to compete against the older students [Cedric Diggory](/wiki/Cedric_Diggory "Cedric Diggory"), [Fleur Delacour](/wiki/Fleur_Delacour "Fleur Delacour") and [Viktor Krum](/wiki/Viktor_Krum "Viktor Krum"). 

Harry is interviewed by the _[Daily Prophet](/wiki/Daily_Prophet "Daily Prophet")_ reporter [Rita Skeeter](/wiki/Rita_Skeeter "Rita Skeeter"), who writes a scathing article portraying him as a disturbed attention-seeker. Using the Hogwarts [Floo Network](/wiki/Floo_Network "Floo Network"), Harry speaks with his godfather [Sirius Black](/wiki/Sirius_Black "Sirius Black"), who warns him about Durmstrang's headmaster [Igor Karkaroff](/wiki/Igor_Karkaroff "Igor Karkaroff"), who is a former Death Eater. Sirius believes that Harry's selection for the dangerous tournament is somehow connected to the attack at the World Cup. Hagrid alerts Harry that the First Task involves a dragon. After Moody reminds Harry that he is an expert flyer, Harry uses his broomstick to fly past the dragon and accomplish the task. 

As Christmas approaches, Harry asks a girl named [Cho Chang](/wiki/List_of_Harry_Potter_characters#Cho_Chang "List of Harry Potter characters") to the [Yule Ball](/wiki/Yule_Ball "Yule Ball"), but she is already going with Cedric. Harry and Ron end up going to the ball with the twin sisters [Parvati](/wiki/Parvati_Patil "Parvati Patil") and [Padma Patil](/wiki/Padma_Patil "Padma Patil"). Ron is sullen as he observes Hermione dancing with Krum. As the Second Task nears, Harry learns that he will need to rescue someone from the lake. The house-elf [Dobby](/wiki/Dobby_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dobby \(Harry Potter\)") gives him [Gillyweed](/wiki/Gillyweed "Gillyweed"), which allows him to breathe underwater long enough to rescue both Ron and Fleur's sister Gabrielle. He is awarded extra points for his bravery and is tied for first place. 

In the Third Task, Harry and his competitors must navigate a maze of obstacles to reach the Triwizard Cup. Harry and Cedric reach the Cup at the same time and decide to grab it together. They are immediately transported to a graveyard, where [Peter Pettigrew](/wiki/Peter_Pettigrew_\(character\) "Peter Pettigrew \(character\)") kills Cedric and subdues Harry. Using Harry's blood, he performs a rite that returns Voldemort to bodily form. As Voldemort and Harry duel, their wands magically connect. This distraction allows Harry the chance to escape back to Hogwarts with the Cup and Cedric's body. 

Dumbledore, McGonagall and Snape discover that Moody is actually the Death Eater [Barty Crouch Jr](/wiki/Barty_Crouch_Jr "Barty Crouch Jr"), who has been constantly drinking [Polyjuice Potion](/wiki/Polyjuice_Potion "Polyjuice Potion"). When they administer him a truth potion, he reveals that he placed Harry's name in the Goblet, supported him through the Tasks so that he would reach the Triwizard Cup first, and ensured he was transported to the graveyard for the rite by turning the cup into a Portkey. Crouch Jr. is handed over to the [Dementors](/wiki/Dementors "Dementors"), who render him soulless with the Dementor's Kiss. Recovering in the hospital wing, Harry narrates the events to Cedric's parents and offers them his tournament winnings ; they will not accept the gold, so he gives it to [Fred and George Weasley](/wiki/Fred_and_George_Weasley "Fred and George Weasley") so they can open their joke shop. During a memorial service for Cedric, Dumbledore tells the students of Hogwarts that Voldemort has returned. 

## Development

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Development")]

_Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ is the fourth book in the _[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ series. The first, _[Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Philosopher%27s_Stone "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone")_ , was published by Bloomsbury on 26 June 1997. The second, _[Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets")_ , was published on 2 July 1998. The third, _[Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Prisoner_of_Azkaban "Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban")_ , followed on 8 July 1999.[1] _Goblet of Fire_ is almost twice the size of the first three books (the paperback edition was 636 pages). [J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling") stated that she "knew from the beginning it would be the biggest of the first four." She said there needed to be a "proper run-up" for the conclusion and rushing the "complex plot" could confuse readers. She also stated that "everything is on a bigger scale," which was symbolic, as [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)")'s horizons widened both literally and metaphorically as he grew up. She also wanted to explore more of the magical world.[2]

Until the official title's announcement on 27 June 2000, the book was called by its working title, "_Harry Potter IV_ ". Previously, in April, the publisher had listed it as _Harry Potter and the Doomspell Tournament_. However,[3] J. K. Rowling expressed her indecision about the title in an _[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ interview. "I changed my mind twice on what [the title] was. The working title had got out — _Harry Potter and the Doomspell Tournament_. Then I changed _Doomspell_ to _Triwizard Tournament_. Then I was teetering between _Goblet of Fire_ and _Triwizard Tournament_. In the end, I preferred _Goblet of Fire_ because it's got that kind of _cup of destiny_ feel about it, which is the theme of the book."[2]

Rowling mentioned that she originally wrote a Weasley relative named Mafalda, who, according to Rowling, "was the daughter of the 'second cousin who's a stockbroker' mentioned in _Philosopher's Stone_. This stockbroker had been very rude to Mr. and Mrs. Weasley in the past, but now he and his (Muggle) wife had inconveniently produced a witch, they came back to the Weasleys asking for their help in introducing her to wizarding society before she starts at Hogwarts".[4] Mafalda was supposed to be a talented nosy [Slytherin](/wiki/Slytherin "Slytherin") match for Hermione Granger and was to fill in the [Rita Skeeter](/wiki/Rita_Skeeter "Rita Skeeter") subplot, but she was eventually removed because "there were obvious limitations to what an eleven-year-old closeted at school could discover." Rowling considered Rita Skeeter to be "much more flexible".[4] Rowling also admitted that the fourth book was the most difficult to write at the time because the Mafalda character also served as a giant [plot hole](/wiki/Plot_hole "Plot hole"), which she discovered halfway through writing.[2]

## Themes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Themes")]

Jeff Jensen, who interviewed Rowling for _[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ in 2000, pointed out that [bigotry](/wiki/Bigotry "Bigotry") is a big theme in the _Harry Potter_ novels and _Goblet of Fire_ in particular. He mentioned how Voldemort and his followers are prejudiced against Muggles and how, in _Goblet of Fire,_ Hermione forms a group to liberate Hogwarts' house-elves who have "been indentured servants so long they lack desire for anything else."[2] When asked why she explored this theme, Rowling replied, 

> Because bigotry is probably the thing I detest most. All forms of intolerance, the whole idea of _that which is different from me is necessarily evil._ I really like to explore the idea that difference is equal and good. But there's another idea that I like to explore, too. Oppressed groups are not, generally speaking, people who stand firmly together – no, sadly, they kind of subdivide among themselves and fight like hell. That's human nature, so that's what you see here. This world of wizards and witches, they're already ostracized, and then within themselves, they've formed a loathsome pecking order.[2]

She also commented that she did not feel this was too "heavy" for children, as it was one of those things that a "huge number of children at that age start to think about."[2]

## Publication and reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Publication and reception")]

### UK/US release

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: UK/US release")]

_Goblet of Fire_ was the first book in the Harry Potter series to be released in the United States on the same date as the United Kingdom, on 8 July 2000, strategically on a Saturday so children did not have to worry about school conflicting with buying the book.[1] It had a combined first-printing of over five million copies.[1] It was given a record-breaking print run of 3.9 million. Three million copies of the book were sold over the first weekend in the US alone.[5] [FedEx](/wiki/FedEx "FedEx") dispatched more than 9,000 trucks and 100 planes to fulfil book deliveries.[6] The pressure in editing caused a mistake which shows Harry's father emerging first from Voldemort's wand; however, as confirmed in _Prisoner of Azkaban_ , James died first, so then Harry's mother ought to have come out first.[7] This was corrected in later editions.[8]

#### Launch publicity

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Launch publicity")]

To publicise the book, a special train named _[Hogwarts Express](/wiki/Hogwarts_Express "Hogwarts Express")_ was organised by [Bloomsbury](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing"), and run from [King's Cross](/wiki/London_King%27s_Cross_railway_station "London King's Cross railway station") to [Perth](/wiki/Perth_railway_station,_Scotland "Perth railway station, Scotland"), carrying J.K. Rowling, a consignment of books for her to sign and sell, also representatives of Bloomsbury and the press. The book was launched on 8 July 2000, on platform 1 at King's Cross – which had been given "Platform 9+3⁄4" signs for the occasion – following which the train departed. En route it called at [Didcot Railway Centre](/wiki/Didcot_Railway_Centre "Didcot Railway Centre"), [Kidderminster](/wiki/Kidderminster_railway_station "Kidderminster railway station"), the [Severn Valley Railway](/wiki/Severn_Valley_Railway "Severn Valley Railway"), [Crewe](/wiki/Crewe_railway_station "Crewe railway station") (overnight stop), [Manchester](/wiki/Manchester_Piccadilly_station "Manchester Piccadilly station"), [Bradford](/wiki/Bradford_Interchange "Bradford Interchange"), [York](/wiki/York_railway_station "York railway station"), the [National Railway Museum](/wiki/National_Railway_Museum "National Railway Museum") (overnight stop), [Newcastle](/wiki/Newcastle_railway_station "Newcastle railway station"), [Edinburgh](/wiki/Edinburgh_Waverley_railway_station "Edinburgh Waverley railway station"), arriving at Perth on 11 July.[9]

The locomotive was [_West Country_ class steam locomotive](/wiki/SR_West_Country_and_Battle_of_Britain_classes "SR West Country and Battle of Britain classes") no. 34027 _Taw Valley_ , which was specially repainted red for the tour; it later returned to its normal green livery (the repaints were requested and paid for by Bloomsbury). The coaches of the train included a sleeping car. A Diesel locomotive was coupled at the other end, for use when reversals were necessary, such as the first stage of the journey as far as Ferme Park, just south of [Hornsey](/wiki/Hornsey_railway_station "Hornsey railway station"). The tour generated considerably more press interest than the launch of the film _[Thomas and the Magic Railroad](/wiki/Thomas_and_the_Magic_Railroad "Thomas and the Magic Railroad")_ , which premiered in London the same weekend.[10][11][12]

### Critical reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Critical reception")]

In [_The New York Times_ Book Review](/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Book_Review "The New York Times Book Review"), author [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King") stated the _Goblet of Fire_ was "every bit as good as Potters 1 through 3" and praised the humour and subplots, although he commented that "there's also a moderately tiresome amount of adolescent squabbling...it's a teenage thing".[13] _[Kirkus Reviews](/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews "Kirkus Reviews")_ called it "another grand tale of magic and mystery...and clicking along so smoothly that it seems shorter than it is". However, they commented that it did tend to lag, especially at the end where two "bad guys" stopped the action to give extended explanations, and that the issues to be resolved in sequels would leave "many readers, particularly American ones, uncomfortable".[14] For _[The Horn Book Magazine](/wiki/The_Horn_Book_Magazine "The Horn Book Magazine")_ , Martha V. Parravano gave a mixed review, saying "some will find [it] wide-ranging, compellingly written, and absorbing; others, long, rambling, and tortuously fraught with adverbs".[15] A _[Publishers Weekly](/wiki/Publishers_Weekly "Publishers Weekly")_ review praised the book's "[red herrings](/wiki/Red_herring "Red herring"), the artful clues and tricky surprises that disarm the most attentive audience" and saying it "might be her most thrilling yet."[16] Writing for _[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ , [Joan Acocella](/wiki/Joan_Acocella "Joan Acocella") noted that "where the prior volumes moved like lightning, here the pace is slower, the energy more dispersed. At the same time, the tone becomes more grim."[17]

Kristin Lemmerman of [CNN](/wiki/CNN "CNN") said that it is not great literature: 'Her prose has more in common with your typical beach-blanket fare and the beginning contained too much recap to introduce characters to new readers, although Rowling quickly gets back on track, introducing readers to a host of well-drawn new characters.'[18] Writing for [Salon.com](/wiki/Salon.com "Salon.com"), Charles Taylor was generally positive about the change of mood and development of characters.[19] _Entertainment Weekly_ 's reviewer Kristen Baldwin gave _Goblet of Fire_ the grade of A−, praising the development of the characters as well as the many themes presented. However, she did worry that a shocking climax may be a "nightmare factory" for young readers.[20]

In 2012 it was ranked number 98 on a list of the top 100 children's novels published by _[School Library Journal](/wiki/School_Library_Journal "School Library Journal")_.[21]

### Awards and honours

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Awards and honours")]

_Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ won several awards, including the 2001 [Hugo Award for Best Novel](/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novel "Hugo Award for Best Novel").[22] It won the 2002 [Indian Paintbrush Book Award](/wiki/Indian_Paintbrush_Book_Award "Indian Paintbrush Book Award"), the third after _Philosopher's Stone_ and _Prisoner of Azkaban_.[23] The novel also won an [Oppenheim Toy Portfolio Platinum Award](/wiki/Oppenheim_Toy_Portfolio_Award "Oppenheim Toy Portfolio Award") for one of the best books, who claimed it was "more intense than the first three books".[24] In addition, _Entertainment Weekly_ listed _Goblet of Fire_ in second place on their list of _The New Classics: Books – The 100 best reads from 1983 to 2008_.[25] _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ ranked _Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ #97 in its list of 100 Best Books of the 21st Century.[26]

## Adaptations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Adaptations")]

### Film

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Film")]

Main article: [_Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ (film)](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire \(film\)")

_Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ was adapted into a film, released worldwide on 18 November 2005, which was directed by [Mike Newell](/wiki/Mike_Newell_\(director\) "Mike Newell \(director\)") and written by [Steve Kloves](/wiki/Steve_Kloves "Steve Kloves"). The film grossed $102.7 million for the opening weekend,[27] and eventually grossed $896 million worldwide.[28] The film was also nominated for [Best Art Direction](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Art_Direction "Academy Award for Best Art Direction") at the [78th Academy Awards](/wiki/78th_Academy_Awards "78th Academy Awards").[29]

### Video game

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Video game")]

Main article: [_Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire_ (video game)](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire_\(video_game\) "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire \(video game\)")

It was also made into a video game for [Windows](/wiki/Windows "Windows"), [PlayStation 2](/wiki/PlayStation_2 "PlayStation 2"), [Nintendo DS](/wiki/Nintendo_DS "Nintendo DS"), [GameCube](/wiki/GameCube "GameCube"), [Xbox](/wiki/Xbox_\(console\) "Xbox \(console\)"), [Game Boy Advance](/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance"), and [PlayStation Portable](/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable") by [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts"). It was released just before the film.
