The **Academy Award for Best Animated Feature** is an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards "Academy Awards") presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") (AMPAS) for the best [animated](/wiki/Animation "Animation") [feature film](/wiki/Feature_film "Feature film"). An animated feature is defined by the academy as a film with a running time of more than 40 minutes in which characters' performances are created using a frame-by-frame technique, a significant number of the major characters are animated, and animation figures in no less than 75 percent of the running time.[1] The Academy Award for Best Animated Feature was first awarded in 2002 for films released in 2001.[2][3][4]

For much of the Academy Awards' history, AMPAS was resistant to the idea of a regular award for animated features, considering there were simply too few produced to justify such consideration.[5] Instead, the Academy occasionally bestowed special Oscars for exceptional productions, usually for [Walt Disney Pictures](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures "Walt Disney Pictures"), such as [Academy Honorary Award](/wiki/Academy_Honorary_Award "Academy Honorary Award") for _[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs](/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_\(1937_film\) "Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs \(1937 film\)")_ in 1938,[6] and the [Special Achievement Academy Award](/wiki/Special_Achievement_Academy_Award "Special Achievement Academy Award") for the live action/animated hybrid _[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit "Who Framed Roger Rabbit")_ in 1989[7] and _[Toy Story](/wiki/Toy_Story "Toy Story")_ in 1996.[8] Prior to the award's creation, only one animated film was nominated for [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"): 1991's _[Beauty and the Beast](/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_\(1991_film\) "Beauty and the Beast \(1991 film\)")_ , also by Disney.[9][10]

By 2001, the rise of sustained competitors to Disney in the feature animated film market, such as [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation") (founded by former Disney executive [Jeffrey Katzenberg](/wiki/Jeffrey_Katzenberg "Jeffrey Katzenberg")), created an increase of film releases of significant annual number enough for AMPAS to reconsider.[5] The Academy Award for Best Animated Feature was first given out at the [74th Academy Awards](/wiki/74th_Academy_Awards "74th Academy Awards"),[11] held on March 24, 2002.[12]

When the category was first instated, the nomination went to the person(s) most involved in creating the film. This could be the producer, the director, or both. For the [76th Academy Awards](/wiki/76th_Academy_Awards "76th Academy Awards") in 2003, only the director(s) of the film received the nomination. For the [86th Academy Awards](/wiki/86th_Academy_Awards "86th Academy Awards") ten years later, this was amended to include one producer and up to two directors. For the [91st Academy Awards](/wiki/91st_Academy_Awards "91st Academy Awards"), this was amended once again to include up to four individuals, one of whom must be a director and one of whom must be a producer; an exception to this is that "[i]n the case of a TWO-PERSON TEAM with shared and equal director or producer credit, an additional statuette may be awarded."[13]

The Academy included a rule that stated that the award would not be presented in a year in which fewer than eight eligible films opened in theaters.[14] In regards of the Academy, it allows for all members to make voting for animated films more acceptable.[15]

At the same year, the Academy enacted a new rule regarding the [motion capture](/wiki/Motion_capture "Motion capture") technique employed in films such as _[A Christmas Carol](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol_\(2009_film\) "A Christmas Carol \(2009 film\)")_ (2009) and _[The Adventures of Tintin](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tintin_\(film\) "The Adventures of Tintin \(film\)")_ (2011), directed by [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director "Academy Award for Best Director") winners [Robert Zemeckis](/wiki/Robert_Zemeckis "Robert Zemeckis") and [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg") respectively, and how they might not be eligible in this category in the future.[16] The new rule now reads "An animated feature film is defined as a motion picture with a running time of greater than 40 minutes, in which movement and characters’ performances are created using a frame-by-frame technique. Motion capture by itself is not an animation technique. In addition, a significant number of the major characters must be animated, and animation must figure in no less than 75 percent of the picture’s running time."[16] This rule was possibly made to prevent nominations of live-action films that rely heavily on motion capture, such as _[Avatar](/wiki/Avatar_\(2009_film\) "Avatar \(2009 film\)")_ (2009).[16]

Only three films (most are [live-action/animation hybrid](/wiki/Live-action_animation "Live-action animation")) have been disqualified for not meeting the 75 percent of animation threshold under [submission](/wiki/List_of_submissions_for_the_Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature "List of submissions for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature"). With exceptions, it was unclear whether _[Marcel the Shell with Shoes On](/wiki/Marcel_the_Shell_with_Shoes_On_\(2021_film\) "Marcel the Shell with Shoes On \(2021 film\)")_ would be eligible for the award at the [95th Academy Awards](/wiki/95th_Academy_Awards "95th Academy Awards") due to being a stop-motion animated film with the use of live-action elements. Director [Dean Fleischer Camp](/wiki/Dean_Fleischer_Camp "Dean Fleischer Camp") said that he and [A24](/wiki/A24 "A24") had to submit documentation in order to prove the film had enough animation to meet the award's minimum requirements.[17][1] The AMPAS officially deemed the film eligible for consideration in the Animated Feature category and was eventually nominated for said category.[18]

## Milestones and records

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Milestones and records")]

### Films and production companies

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Films and production companies")]

  * [Pixar](/wiki/Pixar "Pixar") holds the most wins for a studio with 11, the most nominations with 20, and the most consecutive wins (4, between 2007 and 2010). 
    * Pixar, with 11 wins, and [Walt Disney Animation Studios](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios "Walt Disney Animation Studios"), with 4, are both owned by [the Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company"), which has 15 wins for the category in total.
  * [Laika](/wiki/Laika,_LLC "Laika, LLC") has the most nominations without a win, with 6 films. 
    * [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation") has the most nominations after last win, with 10 films.
  * _[Toy Story](/wiki/Toy_Story_\(franchise\) "Toy Story \(franchise\)")_ is the only franchise to win this award twice, for its [third](/wiki/Toy_Story_3 "Toy Story 3") and [fourth films](/wiki/Toy_Story_4 "Toy Story 4"). Additionally, the third and fourth films are so far the only two sequels to win this award.
  * _[Shrek](/wiki/Shrek_\(franchise\) "Shrek \(franchise\)")_ and _[Wallace& Gromit](/wiki/Wallace_%26_Gromit "Wallace & Gromit")_ are the most-nominated franchise, with 4 (and having won once) each. Other franchises with three nominations include _[How to Train Your Dragon](/wiki/How_to_Train_Your_Dragon "How to Train Your Dragon")_ and [Cartoon Saloon](/wiki/Cartoon_Saloon "Cartoon Saloon")'s "Irish Folklore Trilogy" (consisting of _[The Secret of Kells](/wiki/The_Secret_of_Kells "The Secret of Kells")_ , _[Song of the Sea](/wiki/Song_of_the_Sea_\(2014_film\) "Song of the Sea \(2014 film\)")_ , and _[Wolfwalkers](/wiki/Wolfwalkers "Wolfwalkers")_); both hold the record as the most-nominated franchises without a win.
  * Of the several [adult animated](/wiki/Adult_animation "Adult animation") films (judging from their MPAA ratings), _[The Triplets of Belleville](/wiki/The_Triplets_of_Belleville "The Triplets of Belleville")_ was the first PG-13-rated nominee, _[Anomalisa](/wiki/Anomalisa "Anomalisa")_ and _[Memoir of a Snail](/wiki/Memoir_of_a_Snail "Memoir of a Snail")_[43] are so far the only R-rated animated films to be nominated, and _The Boy and the Heron_ became the first PG-13-rated winner.[44]
  * [Studio Ghibli](/wiki/Studio_Ghibli "Studio Ghibli") (Japan) has the most wins (two) and nominations (seven) for a non-US studio; _Spirited Away_ and _The Boy and the Heron_ are the only non-English language films to win.[45][46][47][48]
  * _[Flee](/wiki/Flee_\(film\) "Flee \(film\)")_ is the first [animated documentary](/wiki/Animated_documentary "Animated documentary") film to be nominated.[49]
  * Since 2019, each year has had at least one nominee that was mainly released via streaming, with three winners (denoted in bold): _[Klaus](/wiki/Klaus_\(film\) "Klaus \(film\)")_ ([Netflix](/wiki/Netflix "Netflix")) in 2019; _**[Soul](/wiki/Soul_\(2020_film\) "Soul \(2020 film\)")**_ ([Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+")) in 2020; _[Luca](/wiki/Luca_\(2021_film\) "Luca \(2021 film\)")_ (Disney+) and _[The Mitchells vs. the Machines](/wiki/The_Mitchells_vs._the_Machines "The Mitchells vs. the Machines")_ (Netflix) in 2021; _**[Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio](/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro%27s_Pinocchio "Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio")**_ (Netflix), _[The Sea Beast](/wiki/The_Sea_Beast_\(2022_film\) "The Sea Beast \(2022 film\)")_ (Netflix), and _[Turning Red](/wiki/Turning_Red "Turning Red")_ (Disney+) in 2022; _[Nimona](/wiki/Nimona_\(film\) "Nimona \(film\)")_ (Netflix) in 2023; _[Wallace& Gromit: Vengeance Most Fowl](/wiki/Wallace_%26_Gromit:_Vengeance_Most_Fowl "Wallace & Gromit: Vengeance Most Fowl")_ (Netflix) in 2024; and _**[KPop Demon Hunters](/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters "KPop Demon Hunters")**_ (Netflix) in 2025.
  * 2005 and 2011 are the only years that did not have a Disney or Pixar film nominated in the category.
  * _[Flow](/wiki/Flow_\(2024_film\) "Flow \(2024 film\)")_ is the first [independent film](/wiki/Independent_animation "Independent animation") to win the award.[50]
    * Due to being an independent film with a minimal budget, _Flow_ is the only film with a seven-figure budget to win.[50]
  * There were only six non-Disney/Pixar films to win the category until 2022. The consecutive wins of _[Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio](/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro%27s_Pinocchio "Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio")_ , _[The Boy and the Heron](/wiki/The_Boy_and_the_Heron "The Boy and the Heron")_ , _[Flow](/wiki/Flow_\(2024_film\) "Flow \(2024 film\)")_ , and _[KPop Demon Hunters](/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters "KPop Demon Hunters")_ between 2022 and 2025 marked the first time that a non-Disney/Pixar film won the award four years in a row.
  * _[KPop Demon Hunters](/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters "KPop Demon Hunters")_ is the first non-Disney/Pixar film to win 2 Academy Awards.[51][52]



### People

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: People")]

  * [Pete Docter](/wiki/Pete_Docter "Pete Docter") has the most wins of any individual (3), and is tied with [Hayao Miyazaki](/wiki/Hayao_Miyazaki "Hayao Miyazaki") and [Chris Sanders](/wiki/Chris_Sanders "Chris Sanders") for the most nominations (4). Additionally, Miyazaki has the most wins and nominations for a non-US individual.
  * Chris Sanders has the most nominations without winning (4).
  * Hayao Miyazaki became the oldest winner in 2024 at the age of 83; he previously held the record between 2003 and 2023 (briefly being succeeded by [Mark Gustafson](/wiki/Mark_Gustafson "Mark Gustafson") for _[Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio](/wiki/Guillermo_del_Toro%27s_Pinocchio "Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio")_).
  * [Matīss Kaža](/wiki/Mat%C4%ABss_Ka%C5%BEa "Matīss Kaža") became the youngest winner in 2025 at the age of 29, with [Gints Zilbalodis](/wiki/Gints_Zilbalodis "Gints Zilbalodis") also the second youngest winner at the age of 30, beating [Andrew Stanton](/wiki/Andrew_Stanton "Andrew Stanton") in a 21-year streak between 2004 (at the age of 38) and 2025.
  * In diversity, [Brenda Chapman](/wiki/Brenda_Chapman "Brenda Chapman") was the first woman to win for _[Brave](/wiki/Brave_\(2012_film\) "Brave \(2012 film\)")_ , [Peter Ramsey](/wiki/Peter_Ramsey "Peter Ramsey") was the first black director to win for _[Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse](/wiki/Spider-Man:_Into_the_Spider-Verse "Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse")_ and [Maggie Kang](/wiki/Maggie_Kang "Maggie Kang") became the first Korean director to win for _[KPop Demon Hunters](/wiki/KPop_Demon_Hunters "KPop Demon Hunters")._



## Legacy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Legacy")]

The winners of non-Disney/Pixar or Dreamworks Animation films led its significant influence among animation studios for its aesthetics as well as displaying statuettes at museums, solidifying animation's recognition in mainstream cinema. 

  * On March 20, 2024, [Studio Ghibli](/wiki/Studio_Ghibli "Studio Ghibli") displayed Hayao Miyazaki's Oscar statuette for a limited time at [Ghibli Park](/wiki/Ghibli_Park "Ghibli Park")'s "Ghibli's Grand Warehouse" in the Broadcast Room.[53]
  * In 2025, after the success of _Flow_ and its eventual win in the Best Animated Feature category, the statuette, along with the [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards "Golden Globe Awards") and the [European Film Award](/wiki/European_Film_Awards "European Film Awards"), was later put on display at the [Latvian National Museum of Art](/wiki/Latvian_National_Museum_of_Art "Latvian National Museum of Art") for a week.[54]
