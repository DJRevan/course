**Tasmania** ([/tæzˈmeɪniə/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); [palawa kani](/wiki/Palawa_kani "Palawa kani"): _Lutruwita_) is an island [state](/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia "States and territories of Australia") of [Australia](/wiki/Australia "Australia").[13] It is located 240 kilometres (150 miles) to the south of the [Australian mainland](/wiki/Mainland_Australia "Mainland Australia"), and is separated from it by the [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait"). The state encompasses the main island of Tasmania, the [26th-largest island in the world](/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area#Islands "List of islands by area"), and the [surrounding 1000 islands](/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Tasmania "List of islands of Tasmania").[14] It is Australia's smallest and least populous state, with 573,479 residents as of June 2023[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tasmania&action=edit). The [state capital](/wiki/List_of_Australian_capital_cities "List of Australian capital cities") and largest city is [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart"), with around 40% of the population living in the Greater Hobart area.[15] Tasmania is the most decentralised state in Australia, with the lowest proportion of its residents living within its capital city.[16]

Tasmania's main island was first inhabited by [Aboriginal peoples](/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians "Aboriginal Australians"), who today generally identify as Palawa or Pakana.[17][18][19] It is believed that [Aboriginal Tasmanians](/wiki/Aboriginal_Tasmanians "Aboriginal Tasmanians") became isolated from mainland Aboriginal groups around 11,700 years ago, when rising sea levels formed [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait").[20]

In 1803, Tasmania was permanently settled by Europeans as a [penal settlement](/wiki/Penal_colony "Penal colony") of the [British Empire](/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"), partly to pre-empt French territorial claims during the [Napoleonic Wars](/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars "Napoleonic Wars").[21] At the time of British arrival, the Aboriginal population is estimated to have been between 3,000 and 7,000. Within three decades, however, this number declined drastically due to violent conflict, known as the [Black War](/wiki/Black_War "Black War"), and the spread of [infectious diseases](/wiki/Infectious_disease "Infectious disease"). The Black War peaked between 1825 and 1831, resulting in the declaration of martial law for more than three years and causing the deaths of nearly 1,100 Aboriginal people and settlers. 

Under British rule, the island was initially part of the [Colony of New South Wales](/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales "Colony of New South Wales"); however, it became a separate colony under the name [Van Diemen's Land](/wiki/Van_Diemen%27s_Land "Van Diemen's Land") (named after [Anthony van Diemen](/wiki/Anthony_van_Diemen "Anthony van Diemen")) in 1825.[22] Approximately 80,000 [convicts](/wiki/Convicts_in_Australia "Convicts in Australia") were sent to Van Diemen's Land before this practice, known as [transportation](/wiki/Penal_transportation "Penal transportation"), ceased in 1853.[23] In 1855, the present Constitution of Tasmania was enacted, and the following year the colony formally changed its name to Tasmania. In 1901, it became a founding constituent state of Australia (one of six) through the process of the [federation of Australia](/wiki/Federation_of_Australia "Federation of Australia"). 

Today, Tasmania has the [second smallest economy of the Australian states and territories](/wiki/List_of_Australian_states_and_territories_by_gross_state_product "List of Australian states and territories by gross state product"); economic activity involves principally tourism, agriculture, aquaculture, education, and healthcare.[24] Tasmania is a significant agricultural exporter, as well as a significant destination for eco-tourism. About 42% of its land area, including [national parks](/wiki/National_parks "National parks") and [World Heritage Sites](/wiki/Tasmanian_Wilderness "Tasmanian Wilderness") (21%), is protected in some form of reserve.[25] The [first environmental political party](/wiki/United_Tasmania_Group "United Tasmania Group") in the world was founded in Tasmania.[26]

## Toponymy

Tasmania is named after [Dutch](/wiki/Dutch_people "Dutch people") [explorer](/wiki/Explorer "Explorer") [Abel Tasman](/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman"), who made the first reported European sighting of the island on 24 November 1642. Tasman named the island **Anthony van Diemen's Land** after his sponsor [Anthony van Diemen](/wiki/Anthony_van_Diemen "Anthony van Diemen"), the Governor of the [Dutch East Indies](/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies "Dutch East Indies"). The name was later shortened to **Van Diemen's Land** by the British. It was officially renamed "Tasmania" in honour of its first European discoverer on 1 January 1856.[27]

Tasmania was sometimes referred to as "Dervon", as mentioned in the [Jerilderie Letter](/wiki/Jerilderie_Letter "Jerilderie Letter") written by the notorious Australian [bushranger](/wiki/Bushranger "Bushranger") [Ned Kelly](/wiki/Ned_Kelly "Ned Kelly") in 1879. The colloquial expression or nickname for the state is "Tassie". Tasmania is also sometimes referenced by its short-form name "Tas", mainly when written (eg, in business names, websites, addresses). TAS is also the [Australia Post abbreviation](/wiki/Australian_postal_abbreviations "Australian postal abbreviations") for the state. 

### Names from Indigenous languages

In the constructed [palawa kani](/wiki/Palawa_kani "Palawa kani") language, the main island of Tasmania is called "Lutruwita",[28] a name originally derived from the [Bruny Island Tasmanian language](/wiki/Bruny_Island_Tasmanian_language "Bruny Island Tasmanian language"). [George Augustus Robinson](/wiki/George_Augustus_Robinson "George Augustus Robinson") recorded it as _Loe.trou.witter_ , but also as _Trow.wer.nar_ , probably from one or more of the [eastern](/wiki/Eastern_Tasmanian_languages "Eastern Tasmanian languages") or [Northeastern Tasmanian languages](/wiki/Northeastern_Tasmanian_languages "Northeastern Tasmanian languages"). However, he also recorded this name for [Cape Barren Island](/wiki/Cape_Barren_Island "Cape Barren Island"). In the 20th century, some writers used it as an Aboriginal name for Tasmania—spelled "Trowenna" or "Trowunna". It is now believed that that name is more properly applied to Cape Barren Island,[28] which has had an official [dual name](/wiki/Dual_naming "Dual naming") of "Truwana" since 2014.[29]

A number of [palawa kani](/wiki/Palawa_kani "Palawa kani") names, based on historical records of aboriginal names, have been accepted by the Tasmanian government. A dozen of these (below) are 'dual-use' (bilingual) names, and another two are unbounded areas with only palawa names.[29]

Bilingual names

  * Kanamaluka / [Tamar River](/wiki/Tamar_River "Tamar River")
  * Kunanyi / [Mount Wellington](/wiki/Mount_Wellington_\(Tasmania\) "Mount Wellington \(Tasmania\)")
  * Laraturunawn / Sundown Point
  * Nungu / West Point
  * Pinmatik / [Rocky Cape](/wiki/Rocky_Cape,_Tasmania "Rocky Cape, Tasmania")
  * Takayna / [The Tarkine](/wiki/The_Tarkine "The Tarkine")
  * Taypalaka / [Green Point](/wiki/Green_Point,_Tasmania "Green Point, Tasmania")
  * Titima / [Trefoil Island](/wiki/Trefoil_Island "Trefoil Island")
  * Truwana / [Cape Barren Island](/wiki/Cape_Barren_Island "Cape Barren Island")
  * Wukalina / [Mount William](/wiki/Mount_William_National_Park "Mount William National Park")
  * Yingina / [Great Lake](/wiki/Great_Lake_\(Tasmania\) "Great Lake \(Tasmania\)")



Palawa names

  * Larapuna: an unbounded area centred on the [Bay of Fires](/wiki/Bay_of_Fires "Bay of Fires")
  * [Narawntapu National Park](/wiki/Narawntapu_National_Park "Narawntapu National Park") (formerly Asbestos Range National Park)
  * Putalina: an unbounded area centred on Oyster Cove (including the community of [Oyster Cove](/wiki/Oyster_Cove,_Tasmania "Oyster Cove, Tasmania"))



There are also a number of archaeological sites with Palawa names. Some of these names have been contentious, with names being proposed without consultation with the aboriginal community, or without having a connection to the place in question.[30]

As well as a diverse First Nations geography, where remnants are preserved in rough form by European documentation, Tasmania is known as a place for [unorthodox place-names](/wiki/Place_names_considered_unusual "Place names considered unusual").[31] These names often come about from lost definitions, where descriptive names have lost their old meanings and have taken on new modern interpretations (e.g. 'Bobs Knobs'). Other names have retained their original meaning, and are often quaint or endearing descriptions (e.g. ['Paradise'](/wiki/Paradise,_Tasmania "Paradise, Tasmania")). 

## History

Main article: [History of Tasmania](/wiki/History_of_Tasmania "History of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Tasmania_simple_geology_map.png)Map showing the general geological surface features of Tasmania. Note the extent of [dolerite](/wiki/Diabase "Diabase"), and the mosaics in the west.

### Physical history

Main article: [Geology of Tasmania](/wiki/Geology_of_Tasmania "Geology of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Tessellated_Pavement_Sunrise_Landscape.jpg)[Tessellated pavement](/wiki/Tessellated_pavement "Tessellated pavement"), a rare rock formation on the [Tasman Peninsula](/wiki/Tasman_Peninsula "Tasman Peninsula")

The island was adjoined to the mainland of Australia until the end of the [last glacial period](/wiki/Last_glacial_period "Last glacial period") about 11,700 years ago.[20] Much of the island is composed of [Jurassic](/wiki/Jurassic "Jurassic") [dolerite](/wiki/Diabase "Diabase") intrusions (the upwelling of [magma](/wiki/Magma "Magma")) through other rock types, sometimes forming large columnar joints. Tasmania has the world's largest areas of dolerite, with many distinctive mountains and cliffs formed from this rock type. The [central plateau](/wiki/Central_Plateau_\(Tasmania\) "Central Plateau \(Tasmania\)") and the southeast portions of the island are mostly dolerites. [Mount Wellington](/wiki/Mount_Wellington_\(Tasmania\) "Mount Wellington \(Tasmania\)") above [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart") is a good example, showing distinct columns known as the Organ Pipes. 

In the southern midlands as far south as Hobart, the dolerite is underlaid by [sandstone](/wiki/Sandstone "Sandstone") and similar sedimentary stones. In the southwest, [Precambrian](/wiki/Precambrian "Precambrian") [quartzites](/wiki/Quartzite "Quartzite") were formed from very ancient sea sediments and form strikingly sharp ridges and ranges, such as Federation Peak or [Frenchmans Cap](/wiki/Frenchmans_Cap "Frenchmans Cap"). 

In the northeast and east, continental [granites](/wiki/Granite "Granite") can be seen, such as at Freycinet, similar to coastal granites on mainland Australia. In the northwest and west, mineral-rich volcanic rock can be seen at [Mount Read](/wiki/Mount_Read_\(Tasmania\) "Mount Read \(Tasmania\)") near [Rosebery](/wiki/Rosebery,_Tasmania "Rosebery, Tasmania"), or at [Mount Lyell](/wiki/Mount_Lyell,_Tasmania "Mount Lyell, Tasmania") near [Queenstown](/wiki/Queenstown,_Tasmania "Queenstown, Tasmania"). Also present in the south and northwest is [limestone](/wiki/Limestone "Limestone") with caves. 

The quartzite and dolerite areas in the higher mountains show evidence of [glaciation](/wiki/Glaciation "Glaciation"), and much of Australia's glaciated landscape is found on the Central Plateau and the Southwest. [Cradle Mountain](/wiki/Cradle_Mountain "Cradle Mountain"), another dolerite peak, for example, was a [nunatak](/wiki/Nunatak "Nunatak"). The combination of these different rock types contributes to scenery which is distinct from any other region of the world.[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] In the far southwest corner of the state, the geology is almost wholly quartzite, which gives the mountains the false impression of having snow-capped peaks year round. 

### Aboriginal people

Main article: [Aboriginal Tasmanians](/wiki/Aboriginal_Tasmanians "Aboriginal Tasmanians")

[](/wiki/File:Terre_de_Diemen,_navigation,_vue_de_la_cote_orientale_de_l%27Ile_Schouten.jpg)1807 engraving by French explorer [Charles Alexandre Lesueur](/wiki/Charles_Alexandre_Lesueur "Charles Alexandre Lesueur") shows seafaring Aboriginal people and a large canoe on the eastern shore of [Schouten Island](/wiki/Schouten_Island "Schouten Island").

Evidence indicates the presence of [Aboriginal people](/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians "Aboriginal Australians") in Tasmania about 42,000 years ago. [Rising sea levels](/wiki/Sea_level_rise "Sea level rise") cut Tasmania off from mainland Australia about 10,000 years ago and by the time of European contact, the Aboriginal people in Tasmania had nine major nations or ethnic groups.[32] At the time of the British occupation and colonisation in 1803, the indigenous population was estimated at between 3,000 and 10,000. 

Historian [Lyndall Ryan's](/wiki/Lyndall_Ryan "Lyndall Ryan") analysis of population studies led her to conclude that there were about 7,000 spread throughout the island's nine nations;[33] Nicholas Clements, citing research by [N.J.B. Plomley](/wiki/Brian_Plomley "Brian Plomley") and [Rhys Jones](/wiki/Rhys_Jones_\(archaeologist\) "Rhys Jones \(archaeologist\)"), settled on a figure of 3,000 to 4,000.[34] They engaged in [fire-stick farming](/wiki/Fire-stick_farming "Fire-stick farming"), hunted game including [kangaroo](/wiki/Kangaroo "Kangaroo") and [wallabies](/wiki/Wallaby "Wallaby"), caught seals, mutton-birds, shellfish and fish and lived as nine separate "nations" on the island, which they knew as "Trouwunna". 

### European arrival and governance

[](/wiki/File:Abel_Tasman_Navigateur_en_Australie_\(cropped\).jpg)Tasmania is named after Dutch explorer [Abel Tasman](/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman"), the first European to sight the island, in 1642.

The first reported sighting of Tasmania by a [European](/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe "Ethnic groups in Europe") was on 24 November 1642 by Dutch explorer [Abel Tasman](/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman"), who landed at today's [Blackman Bay](/wiki/Blackman_Bay,_Tasmania "Blackman Bay, Tasmania"). More than a century later, in 1772, a French expedition led by [Marc-Joseph Marion du Fresne](/wiki/Marc-Joseph_Marion_du_Fresne "Marc-Joseph Marion du Fresne") landed at (nearby but different) [Blackmans Bay](/wiki/Blackmans_Bay,_Tasmania "Blackmans Bay, Tasmania"), and the following year [Tobias Furneaux](/wiki/Tobias_Furneaux "Tobias Furneaux") became the first Englishman to land in Tasmania when he arrived at [Adventure Bay](/wiki/Adventure_Bay,_Tasmania "Adventure Bay, Tasmania"), which he named after his ship [HMS _Adventure_](/wiki/HMS_Adventure_\(1771\) "HMS Adventure \(1771\)"). Captain [James Cook](/wiki/James_Cook "James Cook") also landed at [Adventure Bay](/wiki/Adventure_Bay,_Tasmania "Adventure Bay, Tasmania") in 1777. [Matthew Flinders](/wiki/Matthew_Flinders "Matthew Flinders") and [George Bass](/wiki/George_Bass "George Bass") sailed through [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait") in 1798–1799, determining for the first time that Tasmania was an island.[35]

Sealers and whalers based themselves on Tasmania's islands from 1798,[36] and in August 1803 [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales") Governor [Philip King](/wiki/Philip_Gidley_King "Philip Gidley King") sent Lieutenant [John Bowen](/wiki/John_Bowen_\(Royal_Navy_officer\) "John Bowen \(Royal Navy officer\)") to establish a small military outpost on the eastern shore of the [Derwent River](/wiki/River_Derwent_\(Tasmania\) "River Derwent \(Tasmania\)") in order to forestall any claims to the island by French explorers who had been exploring the southern Australian coastline. Bowen, who led a party of 49, including 21 male and three female convicts, named the camp Risdon.[35][37]

[](/wiki/File:John_Glover_-_Mount_Wellington_and_Hobart_Town_from_Kangaroo_Point_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)Painting by [John Glover](/wiki/John_Glover_\(artist\) "John Glover \(artist\)") of [Mount Wellington](/wiki/Mount_Wellington_\(Tasmania\) "Mount Wellington \(Tasmania\)") and [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart"), 1834

Several months later, a second settlement was established by Captain [David Collins](/wiki/David_Collins_\(lieutenant_governor\) "David Collins \(lieutenant governor\)"), with 308 convicts, 5 kilometres (3.1 miles) to the south in [Sullivans Cove](/wiki/Sullivans_Cove "Sullivans Cove") on the western side of the Derwent, where fresh water was more plentiful. The latter settlement became known as Hobart Town or Hobarton, later shortened to [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart"), after the [British Colonial Secretary](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies "Secretary of State for the Colonies") of the time, [Lord Hobart](/wiki/Lord_Hobart "Lord Hobart"). The settlement at Risdon was later abandoned. Left on their own without further supplies, the Sullivans Cove settlement suffered severe food shortages and by 1806 its inhabitants were starving, with many resorting to scraping seaweed off rocks and scavenging washed-up whale blubber from the shore to survive.[35]

A smaller colony was established at Port Dalrymple on the Tamar River in the island's north in October 1804 and several other convict-based settlements were established, including the particularly harsh [penal colonies](/wiki/Penal_colony "Penal colony") at [Port Arthur](/wiki/Port_Arthur,_Tasmania "Port Arthur, Tasmania") in the southeast and [Macquarie Harbour](/wiki/Macquarie_Harbour "Macquarie Harbour") on the West Coast. Tasmania was eventually sent 75,000 convicts—four out of every ten people transported to Australia.[35] By 1819, the Aboriginal and British population reached parity with about 5000 of each, although among the colonists men outnumbered women four-to-one.[38] Free settlers began arriving in large numbers from 1820, lured by the promise of land grants and free convict labour. Settlement in the island's northwest corner was monopolised by the [Van Diemen's Land Company](/wiki/Van_Diemen%27s_Land_Company "Van Diemen's Land Company"), which sent its first surveyors to the district in 1826. By 1830, one-third of Australia's non-Indigenous population lived in Van Diemen's Land and the island accounted for about half of all land under cultivation and exports.[39]

#### Black War

Main article: [Black War](/wiki/Black_War "Black War")

[](/wiki/File:Benjamin_Duterrau_-_Timmy,_a_Tasmanian_Aboriginal,_throwing_a_spear_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)Painting of a Tasmanian Aboriginal throwing a spear, 1838

Tensions between Tasmania's Aboriginal and white inhabitants rose, partly driven by increasing competition for kangaroo and other game.[40][41][42] Explorer and naval officer [John Oxley](/wiki/John_Oxley "John Oxley") in 1810 noted the "many atrocious cruelties" inflicted on Aboriginal people by convict [bushrangers](/wiki/Bushranger "Bushranger") in the north, which in turn led to black attacks on solitary white hunters.[43] Hostilities increased further with the arrival of 600 colonists from [Norfolk Island](/wiki/Norfolk_Island "Norfolk Island") between 1807 and 1813. They established farms along the River Derwent and east and west of [Launceston](/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania "Launceston, Tasmania"), occupying ten percent of Van Diemen's Land. By 1824 the colonial population had swelled to 12,600, while the island's sheep population had reached 200,000. The rapid colonisation transformed traditional kangaroo hunting grounds into farms with grazing livestock as well as fences, hedges and stone walls, while police and military patrols were increased to control the convict farm labourers.[44]

Violence began to spiral rapidly from the mid-1820s in what became described as the "[Black War](/wiki/Black_War "Black War")".[45] Aboriginal inhabitants were driven to desperation by hunger – that included a desire for agricultural produce, as well as feeling anger at the prevalence of abductions of women and girls. New settlers motivated by fear carried out self-defence operations as well as attacks as a means of suppressing the native threat – or even in some cases, exacting revenge.[46] Van Diemen's Land had an enormous gender imbalance, with male colonists outnumbering females six to one in 1822—and 16 to one among the convict population. Historian Nicholas Clements has suggested the "voracious appetite" for native women was the most important trigger for the explosion of violence from the late 1820s.[47]

From 1825 to 1828, the number of native attacks more than doubled each year, raising panic among settlers. Over the summer of 1826–1827 clans from the Big River, Oyster Bay and North Midlands nations speared stock-keepers on farms and made it clear that they wanted the settlers and their sheep and cattle to move from their kangaroo hunting grounds. Settlers responded vigorously, resulting in many mass-killings. In November 1826, Governor [Sir George Arthur](/wiki/Sir_George_Arthur,_1st_Baronet "Sir George Arthur, 1st Baronet") issued a government notice declaring that colonists were free to kill Aboriginal people when they attacked settlers or their property, and in the following eight months more than 200 Aboriginal people were killed in the Settled Districts in reprisal for the deaths of 15 colonists. After another eight months, the death toll had risen to 43 colonists and probably 350 Aboriginal people.[48] In April 1828, Arthur issued a [Proclamation of Demarcation](/wiki/1828_Proclamation_of_Demarcation "1828 Proclamation of Demarcation") forbidding Aboriginal people to enter the settled districts without a passport issued by the government.[49][50] Arthur declared [martial law](/wiki/Martial_law "Martial law") in the colony in November that year, and this remained in force for over three years, the longest period of martial law in Australian history.[51][52]

In November 1830, Arthur organised the so-called "[Black Line](/wiki/Black_Line "Black Line")", ordering every able-bodied male colonist to assemble at one of seven designated places in the Settled Districts to join a massive drive to sweep Aboriginal people out of the region and on to the [Tasman Peninsula](/wiki/Tasman_Peninsula "Tasman Peninsula"). The campaign failed and was abandoned seven weeks later, but by then Tasmania's Aboriginal population had fallen to about 300.[53]

#### Removal of Aboriginal people

[](/wiki/File:Truganini_and_last_4_tasmanian_aborigines.jpg)Four elderly full-blood Tasmanian Aboriginal people, c. 1860s. [Truganini](/wiki/Truganini "Truganini"), for many years claimed to be the last full-blood Aboriginal person to survive, is seated far right.

After hostilities between settlers and Aboriginal peoples ceased in 1832, almost all of the remnants of the Indigenous population were persuaded by government agent [George Augustus Robinson](/wiki/George_Augustus_Robinson "George Augustus Robinson") to move to [Flinders Island](/wiki/Flinders_Island,_Tasmania "Flinders Island, Tasmania"). Many quickly succumbed to infectious diseases to which they had no immunity, reducing the population further.[54][55] Of those removed from Tasmania, the last to die was [Truganini](/wiki/Truganini "Truganini"), in 1876. 

The near-destruction of Tasmania's Aboriginal population has been described as an act of genocide by historians including [Robert Hughes](/wiki/Robert_Hughes_\(critic\) "Robert Hughes \(critic\)"), [James Boyce](/wiki/James_Boyce_\(author\) "James Boyce \(author\)"), [Lyndall Ryan](/wiki/Lyndall_Ryan "Lyndall Ryan") and Tom Lawson.[35][56][57] However, other historians including [Henry Reynolds](/wiki/Henry_Reynolds_\(historian\) "Henry Reynolds \(historian\)"), [Richard Broome](/wiki/Richard_Broome "Richard Broome") and Nicholas Clements do not agree with the genocide thesis, arguing that the colonial authorities did not intend to destroy the Aboriginal population in whole or in part.[58][59] Boyce has claimed that the April 1828 "Proclamation Separating the Aborigines from the White Inhabitants" sanctioned force against Aboriginal people "for no other reason than that they were Aboriginal".[60] However, as Reynolds, Broome and Clements point out, there was open warfare at the time.[58][59] Boyce described the decision to remove all Tasmanian Aboriginal people after 1832—by which time they had given up their fight against white colonists—as an extreme policy position. He concluded: "The colonial government from 1832 to 1838 [ethnically cleansed](/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing "Ethnic cleansing") the western half of Van Diemen's Land."[60] Nevertheless, Clements and Flood note that there was another wave of violence in north-west Tasmania in 1841, involving attacks on settlers' huts by a band of Aboriginal Tasmanians who had not been removed from the island.[61][62]

#### Proclamation as a colony

Further information: [Colony of Tasmania](/wiki/Colony_of_Tasmania "Colony of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Convict_labourers_in_Australia_in_the_early_20th_century.jpg)A [convict](/wiki/Convicts_in_Australia "Convicts in Australia") ploughing team breaking up new ground at the farm at Port Arthur

Van Diemen's Land—which thus far had existed as a territory within the colony of [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales")—was proclaimed a separate colony, with its own judicial establishment and [Legislative Council](/wiki/Tasmanian_Legislative_Council "Tasmanian Legislative Council"), on 3 December 1825. Transportation to the island ceased in 1853 and the colony was renamed Tasmania in 1856, partly to differentiate the burgeoning society of free settlers from the island's convict past.[63]

The [Legislative Council of Van Diemen's Land](/wiki/Tasmanian_Legislative_Council "Tasmanian Legislative Council") drafted a new constitution which gained Royal Assent in 1855. The [Privy Council](/wiki/Privy_Council "Privy Council") also approved the colony changing its name from "Van Diemen's Land" to "Tasmania", and in 1856 the newly elected [bicameral parliament](/wiki/Bicameral_parliament "Bicameral parliament") sat for the first time, establishing Tasmania as a [self-governing](/wiki/Responsible_government "Responsible government") colony of the British Empire.[64]

The colony suffered from economic fluctuations, but for the most part was prosperous, experiencing steady growth. With few external threats and strong trade links with the Empire, Tasmania enjoyed many fruitful periods in the late 19th century, becoming a world-centre of shipbuilding. It raised a local defence force that eventually played a [significant role](/wiki/Military_history_of_Australia_during_the_Second_Boer_War "Military history of Australia during the Second Boer War") in the [Second Boer War](/wiki/Second_Boer_War "Second Boer War") in South Africa, and Tasmanian soldiers in that conflict won the first two [Victoria Crosses](/wiki/Victoria_Cross "Victoria Cross") awarded to Australians. 

#### Federation

In 1901, the Colony of Tasmania [united](/wiki/Federation_of_Australia "Federation of Australia") with the five other Australian colonies to form the Commonwealth of Australia. Tasmanians voted in favour of federation with the largest majority of all the Australian colonies. 

### 20th and 21st century

Tasmania was an early adopter of [electric](/wiki/Electric "Electric") [street lighting](/wiki/Street_lighting "Street lighting"). Australia's first electric street lights were switched on in [Waratah](/wiki/Waratah,_Tasmania "Waratah, Tasmania") in 1886.[65] [Launceston](/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania "Launceston, Tasmania") became the first completely electrified city on the island in 1885, followed closely by the township of [Zeehan](/wiki/Zeehan "Zeehan") in 1900. 

The state economy was riding mining prosperity until World War I. In 1901, the state population was 172,475.[66] The 1910 foundation of what would become [Hydro Tasmania](/wiki/Hydro_Tasmania "Hydro Tasmania") began to shape urban patterns, as well as future major damming programs.[67] Hydro's influence culminated in the 1970s when the state government announced plans to flood environmentally significant [Lake Pedder](/wiki/Lake_Pedder "Lake Pedder"). As a result of the eventual flooding of Lake Pedder, the world's first green party was established; the [United Tasmania Group](/wiki/United_Tasmania_Group "United Tasmania Group").[68] National and international attention surrounded the campaign against the [Franklin Dam](/wiki/Franklin_Dam "Franklin Dam") in the early 1980s.[69]

Tasmanian [Enid Lyons](/wiki/Enid_Lyons "Enid Lyons") became the first female member of the [House of Representatives](/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives "Australian House of Representatives") at the [1943 federal election](/wiki/1943_Australian_federal_election "1943 Australian federal election") and first female to serve in the [federal cabinet](/wiki/Cabinet_of_Australia "Cabinet of Australia"). In May 1948, [Margaret McIntyre](/wiki/Margaret_McIntyre "Margaret McIntyre") achieved another milestone as the first female elected to the Parliament of Tasmania. Less than six months after her election, McIntyre died in the [crash of the _Lutana_](/wiki/1948_Australian_National_Airways_DC-3_crash "1948 Australian National Airways DC-3 crash") near [Quirindi](/wiki/Quirindi,_New_South_Wales "Quirindi, New South Wales") on 2 September 1948. 

After the end of World War II, the state saw major urbanisation, and the growth of towns like [Ulverstone](/wiki/Ulverstone,_Tasmania "Ulverstone, Tasmania").[67] It gained a reputation as "Sanitorium of the South" and a health-focused tourist boom began to grow. The _[Princess of Tasmania](/wiki/Princess_of_Tasmania "Princess of Tasmania")_ began her maiden voyage in 1959, the first car ferry to Tasmania.[67] As part of the boom, Tasmania allowed the opening of the first casino in Australia in 1968.[67] Queen [Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") visited the state in 1954, and the 50s and 60s were charactered by the opening of major public services, including the Tasmanian Housing Department and [Metro Tasmania](/wiki/Metro_Tasmania "Metro Tasmania") public bus services. A jail was opened at Risdon in 1960, and the [State Library of Tasmania](/wiki/State_Library_of_Tasmania "State Library of Tasmania") the same year. The University of Tasmania also moved to its present location in 1963. 

The state was badly affected by the [1967 Tasmanian fires](/wiki/1967_Tasmanian_fires "1967 Tasmanian fires"), killing 64 people and destroying over 652,000 acres in five hours.[70] In 1975 the [Tasman Bridge collapsed](/wiki/Tasman_Bridge_disaster "Tasman Bridge disaster") when the bridge was struck by the bulk ore carrier _[Lake Illawarra](/wiki/SS_Lake_Illawarra "SS Lake Illawarra")_. It was the only bridge in Hobart, and made crossing the [Derwent River](/wiki/Derwent_River_\(Tasmania\) "Derwent River \(Tasmania\)") by road at the city impossible. The nearest bridge was approximately 20 kilometres (12 mi) to the north, at Bridgewater. 

Throughout the 1980s, strong environmental concerns saw the building of the Australian Antarctic Division headquarters, and the proclamation of the [Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area](/wiki/Tasmanian_Wilderness_World_Heritage_Area "Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area"). The Franklin Dam was blocked by the federal government in 1983, and [CSIRO](/wiki/CSIRO "CSIRO") opened its marine studies centre in Hobart. [Pope John Paul II](/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II "Pope John Paul II") would hold mass at [Elwick Racecourse](/wiki/Elwick_Racecourse "Elwick Racecourse") in 1986. 

The 1990s were characterised by the fight for [LGBT rights in Tasmania](/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Tasmania "LGBT rights in Tasmania"), culminating in the intervention of the [United Nations Human Rights Committee](/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Rights_Committee "United Nations Human Rights Committee") in 1997 and the decriminalization of homosexuality that year.[71] [Christine Milne](/wiki/Christine_Milne "Christine Milne") became the first female leader of a Tasmanian political party in 1993, and major council amalgamations reduce the number of councils from 46 to 29. 

Following the [Port Arthur massacre](/wiki/Port_Arthur_massacre "Port Arthur massacre") on 28 April 1996, which resulted in the loss of 35 lives and injured 23 others, the Australian Government conducted a review of its [firearms](/wiki/Firearm "Firearm") policies and enacted new [nationwide gun ownership laws](/wiki/Gun_laws_of_Australia "Gun laws of Australia") under the [National Firearms Agreement](/wiki/National_Firearms_Agreement "National Firearms Agreement"). 

In 2000, Queen Elizabeth II once again visited the state. [Gunns](/wiki/Gunns "Gunns") rose to prominence as a major forestry company during this decade, only to collapse in 2013. In 2004, Premier [Jim Bacon](/wiki/Jim_Bacon_\(politician\) "Jim Bacon \(politician\)") died in office from lung cancer. In January 2011 philanthropist [David Walsh](/wiki/David_Walsh_\(art_collector\) "David Walsh \(art collector\)") opened the [Museum of Old and New Art](/wiki/Museum_of_Old_and_New_Art "Museum of Old and New Art") (MONA) in Hobart to international acclaim. Within 12 months, MONA became Tasmania's top tourism attraction.[72]

[](/wiki/File:Port_Arthur_Panorama.jpg "Port Arthur, declared a World Heritage Site in 2010")

[ ](/wiki/File:Port_Arthur_Panorama.jpg "File:Port Arthur Panorama.jpg")

[Port Arthur](/wiki/Port_Arthur,_Tasmania "Port Arthur, Tasmania"), declared a [World Heritage Site](/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site") in 2010

The [COVID-19 pandemic in Tasmania](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Tasmania "COVID-19 pandemic in Tasmania") resulted in at least 230 cases and 13 deaths as of September 2021[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tasmania&action=edit).[73] In 2020, after the outbreak of the [coronavirus pandemic](/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019 "Coronavirus disease 2019") ([SARS-CoV-2](/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2 "Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2")) and its spread to Australia, the [Tasmanian government](/wiki/Tasmanian_Government "Tasmanian Government") issued a public health emergency on 17 March,[74] the following month receiving the state's most significant outbreak from the [North-West](/wiki/North_West_Tasmania "North West Tasmania") which required assistance from the [Federal government](/wiki/Australian_Government "Australian Government"). In late 2021, Tasmania was leading the nationwide vaccination response.[75]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Tasmania](/wiki/Geography_of_Tasmania "Geography of Tasmania")

| This article or section **may need to be cleaned up or summarized.** It has been split from/to _[Geography of Tasmania](/wiki/Geography_of_Tasmania "Geography of Tasmania")_.  
---|---  
[](/wiki/File:Topography_of_Tasmania.jpg)Topography of Tasmania

Tasmania, the largest island of Australia, has a landmass of 68,401 km2 (26,410 sq mi) and is located directly in the pathway of the notorious "[Roaring Forties](/wiki/Roaring_Forties "Roaring Forties")" wind that encircles the globe. To its north, it is separated from mainland Australia by [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait"). Tasmania is the only Australian state that is not located on the Australian mainland. About 2,500 kilometres (1,300 nautical miles) south of Tasmania island lies the [George V Coast](/wiki/George_V_Coast "George V Coast") of [Antarctica](/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"). Depending on which [borders of the oceans](/wiki/Borders_of_the_oceans "Borders of the oceans") are used, the island can be said to be either surrounded by the Southern Ocean, or to have the Pacific on its east and the Indian to its west. Still other definitions of the ocean boundaries would have Tasmania with the [Great Australian Bight](/wiki/Great_Australian_Bight "Great Australian Bight") to the west, and the [Tasman Sea](/wiki/Tasman_Sea "Tasman Sea") to the east. The southernmost point on mainland Tasmania is approximately [43°38′37″S 146°49′38″E﻿ / ﻿43.64361°S 146.82722°E﻿ / -43.64361; 146.82722](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Tasmania&params=43_38_37_S_146_49_38_E_) at [South East Cape](/wiki/South_East_Cape "South East Cape"), and the northernmost point on mainland Tasmania is approximately [40°38′26″S 144°43′33″E﻿ / ﻿40.64056°S 144.72583°E﻿ / -40.64056; 144.72583](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Tasmania&params=40_38_26_S_144_43_33_E_) in [Woolnorth / Temdudheker](/wiki/Woolnorth_/_Temdudheker,_Tasmania "Woolnorth / Temdudheker, Tasmania") near [Cape Grim / Kennaook](/wiki/Cape_Grim/Kennaook "Cape Grim/Kennaook"). Tasmania lies at similar latitudes to [Te Waipounamu / South Island](/wiki/South_Island "South Island") of New Zealand and parts of [Patagonia](/wiki/Patagonia "Patagonia") in South America. Areas at equivalent latitudes in the Northern Hemisphere include [Hokkaido](/wiki/Hokkaido "Hokkaido") in Japan, [Northeast China](/wiki/Northeast_China "Northeast China") ([Manchuria](/wiki/Manchuria "Manchuria")), [Central Italy](/wiki/Central_Italy "Central Italy"), and United States cities such as [New York](/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). 

[](/wiki/File:Mortimer_Bay-3.jpg)Due to Tasmania's proximity to the [south magnetic pole](/wiki/South_magnetic_pole "South magnetic pole") towards [Antarctica](/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), the [_Aurora australis_](/wiki/Aurora "Aurora") can sometimes be seen.

The most mountainous region is the [Central Highlands](/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(Tasmania\) "Central Highlands \(Tasmania\)") area, which covers most of the central western parts of the state. The [Midlands](/wiki/Midlands_\(Tasmania\) "Midlands \(Tasmania\)") located in the central east, is fairly flat, and is predominantly used for agriculture, although farming activity is scattered throughout the state. Tasmania's tallest mountain is [Mount Ossa](/wiki/Mount_Ossa_\(Tasmania\) "Mount Ossa \(Tasmania\)") at 1,617 m (5,305 ft).[76] Much of Tasmania is still densely forested, with the [Southwest National Park](/wiki/Southwest_National_Park "Southwest National Park") and neighbouring areas holding some of the last temperate rain forests in the [Southern Hemisphere](/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere"). The [Tarkine](/wiki/Tarkine "Tarkine"), containing [Savage River National Park](/wiki/Savage_River_National_Park "Savage River National Park") located in the island's far north west, is the largest [temperate rainforest](/wiki/Temperate_rainforest "Temperate rainforest") area in Australia covering about 3,800 square kilometres (1,500 sq mi).[77] With its rugged topography, Tasmania has a great number of rivers. Several of Tasmania's largest rivers have been dammed at some point to provide [hydroelectricity](/wiki/Hydroelectricity "Hydroelectricity"). Many rivers begin in the Central Highlands and flow out to the coast. Tasmania's major population centres are mainly situated around [estuaries](/wiki/Estuaries "Estuaries") (some of which are named rivers). 

Tasmania is in the shape of a downward-facing triangle, likened to a shield, heart, or face. It consists of the main island as well as at least a thousand neighbouring islands within the state's jurisdiction. The largest of these are [Flinders Island](/wiki/Flinders_Island "Flinders Island") in the [Furneaux Group](/wiki/Furneaux_Group "Furneaux Group") of [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait"), [King Island](/wiki/King_Island_\(Tasmania\) "King Island \(Tasmania\)") in the west of Bass Strait, [Cape Barren Island](/wiki/Cape_Barren_Island "Cape Barren Island") south of Flinders Island, [Bruny Island](/wiki/Bruny_Island "Bruny Island") separated from Tasmania by the [D'Entrecasteaux Channel](/wiki/D%27Entrecasteaux_Channel "D'Entrecasteaux Channel"), [Macquarie Island](/wiki/Macquarie_Island "Macquarie Island") 1,500 km from Tasmania, and [Maria Island](/wiki/Maria_Island "Maria Island") off the east coast. Tasmania features a number of separated and continuous mountain ranges. The majority of the state is defined by a significant [dolerite](/wiki/Diabase "Diabase") exposure, though the [western half](/wiki/Western_Tasmania "Western Tasmania") of the state is older and more rugged, featuring [buttongrass](/wiki/Buttongrass "Buttongrass") plains, temperate rainforests, and [quartzite](/wiki/Quartzite "Quartzite") ranges, notably [Federation Peak](/wiki/Federation_Peak "Federation Peak") and [Frenchmans Cap](/wiki/Frenchmans_Cap "Frenchmans Cap"). The presence of these mountain ranges is a primary factor in the [rain shadow effect](/wiki/Rain_shadow "Rain shadow"), where the western half receives the majority of rainfall, which also influences the types of vegetation that can grow. The Central Highlands feature a large plateau which forms a number of ranges and escarpments on its north side, tapering off along the south, and radiating into the highest mountain ranges in the west. At the north-west of this, another plateau radiates into a system of hills where [takayna / Tarkine](/wiki/Tarkine "Tarkine") is located. 

The [ Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia (IBRA)](/wiki/Interim_Biogeographic_Regionalisation_for_Australia "Interim Biogeographic Regionalisation for Australia") divides Tasmania into 9 bioregions:[78] [Ben Lomond](/wiki/Ben_Lomond_bioregion "Ben Lomond bioregion"), [Furneaux](/wiki/Furneaux_bioregion "Furneaux bioregion"), [King](/wiki/King_bioregion "King bioregion"), [Central Highlands](/wiki/Tasmanian_Central_Highlands "Tasmanian Central Highlands"), [Northern Midlands](/wiki/Tasmanian_Northern_Midlands "Tasmanian Northern Midlands"), [Northern Slopes](/wiki/Tasmanian_Northern_Slopes "Tasmanian Northern Slopes"), [Southern Ranges](/wiki/Tasmanian_Southern_Ranges "Tasmanian Southern Ranges"), [South East](/wiki/Tasmanian_South_East "Tasmanian South East"), and [West](/wiki/Tasmanian_West "Tasmanian West").

[ ](/wiki/File:Wineglass_Bay_seen_from_Mount_Amos_at_Freycinet_National_Park_\(Tasmania\).jpg "File:Wineglass Bay seen from Mount Amos at Freycinet National Park \(Tasmania\).jpg")

[Wineglass Bay](/wiki/Wineglass_Bay "Wineglass Bay") seen from Mount Amos at [Freycinet National Park](/wiki/Freycinet_National_Park "Freycinet National Park")

[](/wiki/File:TAS_04-08-20.jpg)Satellite image showing snow covering Tasmania's highlands, August 2020

Tasmania's environment consists of many different biomes or communities across its different regions. It is the most forested state in Australia, and preserves the country's largest areas of [temperate rainforest](/wiki/Temperate_rainforest "Temperate rainforest"). A distinctive type of [moorland](/wiki/Moorland "Moorland") found across the west, and particularly south-west of Tasmania, are [buttongrass](/wiki/Buttongrass "Buttongrass") plains, which are speculated to have been expanded by [Tasmanian Aboriginal](/wiki/Tasmanian_Aboriginal "Tasmanian Aboriginal") [burning practices](/wiki/Fire-stick_farming "Fire-stick farming").[79] Tasmania also features a diverse [alpine garden](/wiki/Alpine_plant "Alpine plant") [environment](/wiki/Alpine_vegetation_of_Tasmania "Alpine vegetation of Tasmania"), such as [cushion plant](/wiki/Tasmanian_cushion_plants "Tasmanian cushion plants"). Highland areas receive consistent [snowfall](/wiki/Snow "Snow") above ~1,000 metres every year, and due to cold air from [Antarctica](/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica"), this level often reaches 800 m, and more occasionally 600 or 400 metres. Every five or so years, snow can form at sea level.[80] This environment gives rise to the [cypress](/wiki/Cypress "Cypress") forests of the [Central Plateau](/wiki/Central_Highlands_\(Tasmania\) "Central Highlands \(Tasmania\)") and mountainous highlands. In particular, the [Walls of Jerusalem](/wiki/Walls_of_Jerusalem_National_Park "Walls of Jerusalem National Park") with large areas of rare [pencil pine](/wiki/Athrotaxis_cupressoides "Athrotaxis cupressoides"), and its closest relative [King Billy pine](/wiki/Athrotaxis_selaginoides "Athrotaxis selaginoides"). On the [West Coast Range](/wiki/West_Coast_Range "West Coast Range") and partially on [Mount Field](/wiki/Mount_Field_National_Park "Mount Field National Park"), Australia's only winter-[deciduous](/wiki/Deciduous "Deciduous") plant, [deciduous beech](/wiki/Nothofagus_gunnii "Nothofagus gunnii") is found, which forms a carpet or [krummholz](/wiki/Krummholz "Krummholz"), or very rarely a 4-metre tree.[81]

Tasmania features a high concentration of [waterfalls](/wiki/Waterfalls "Waterfalls"). These can be found in small creeks, alpine [streams](/wiki/Streams "Streams"), [rapid](/wiki/Rapids "Rapids") [rivers](/wiki/Rivers "Rivers"), or off precipitous plunges. Some of the tallest waterfalls are found on mountain [massifs](/wiki/Massifs "Massifs"), sometimes at a 200-metre cascade. The most famous and most visited waterfall in Tasmania is [Russell Falls](/wiki/Russell_Falls "Russell Falls") in [Mount Field](/wiki/Mount_Field_National_Park "Mount Field National Park") due to its proximity to [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart") and stepped falls at a total height of 58 metres.[82] Tasmania also has a large number of [beaches](/wiki/Beaches "Beaches"), the longest of which is [Ocean Beach](/wiki/Ocean_Beach_\(Tasmania\) "Ocean Beach \(Tasmania\)") on the [West Coast](/wiki/West_Coast,_Tasmania "West Coast, Tasmania") at about 40 kilometres.[83] Wineglass Bay in [Freycinet](/wiki/Freycinet_National_Park "Freycinet National Park") on the east coast is a well-known [landmark](/wiki/Landmark "Landmark") of the state. 

The [Tasmanian temperate rainforests](/wiki/Tasmanian_temperate_rainforests "Tasmanian temperate rainforests") cover a few different types. These are also considered distinct from the more common wet [sclerophyll](/wiki/Sclerophyll "Sclerophyll") forests, though these [eucalypt](/wiki/Eucalyptus "Eucalyptus") forests often form with [rainforest](/wiki/Rainforest "Rainforest") [understorey](/wiki/Understorey "Understorey") and [ferns](/wiki/Ferns "Ferns") (such as [tree-ferns](/wiki/Man_fern "Man fern")) are not usually absent. Rainforest found in deep [gullies](/wiki/Gully "Gully") are usually difficult to traverse due to dense understorey growth, such as from [horizontal (_Anodopetalum biglandulosum_)](/wiki/Anodopetalum_biglandulosum "Anodopetalum biglandulosum"). Higher-elevation forests (~500 to 800 m) have smaller ground vegetation and are thus easier to walk in. The most common rainforests usually have a 50-metre[84] [canopy](/wiki/Canopy_\(biology\) "Canopy \(biology\)") and are varied by environmental factors. Emergent growth usually comes from [eucalyptus](/wiki/Eucalyptus_regnans "Eucalyptus regnans"), which can tower another 50 metres higher (usually less), providing the most common choice of nesting for giant [wedge-tailed eagles](/wiki/Tasmanian_wedge-tailed_eagle "Tasmanian wedge-tailed eagle"). 

The human environment ranges from urban or industrial development to farming or grazing land. The most cultivated area is the [Midlands](/wiki/Midlands_\(Tasmania\) "Midlands \(Tasmania\)"), where it has suitable soil but is also the driest part of the state. 

Tasmania's [insularity](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/insular#Adjective "wiktionary:insular") was possibly detected by Captain [Abel Tasman](/wiki/Abel_Tasman "Abel Tasman") when he charted Tasmania's coast in 1642. On 5 December, Tasman was following the [east coast](/wiki/East_Coast_of_Australia "East Coast of Australia") northward to see how far it went. When the land veered to the north-west at [Eddystone Point](/wiki/Eddystone_Point "Eddystone Point"),[85] he tried to keep in with it but his ships were suddenly hit by the [Roaring Forties](/wiki/Roaring_Forties "Roaring Forties") howling through [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait").[86] Tasman was on a mission to find the [Southern Continent](/wiki/Terra_Australis "Terra Australis"), not more islands, so he abruptly turned away to the east and continued his continent-hunting.[87]

The next European to enter the strait was Captain [James Cook](/wiki/James_Cook "James Cook") on [HMS _Endeavour_](/wiki/HMS_Endeavour "HMS Endeavour") in April 1770. However, after sailing for two hours westward into the strait against the wind, he turned back east and noted in his journal that he was "doubtful whether they [i.e. Van Diemen's Land and New Holland] are one land or no".[88]

The strait was named after George Bass, after he and Matthew Flinders passed through it while circumnavigating Van Diemen's Land in the _Norfolk_ in 1798–99. At Flinders' recommendation, the Governor of New South Wales, John Hunter, in 1800 named the stretch of water between the mainland and Van Diemen's Land "Bass's Straits".[89] Later it became known as Bass Strait. 

The existence of the strait had been suggested in 1797 by the master of Sydney Cove when he reached Sydney after deliberately grounding his foundering ship and being stranded on Preservation Island (at the eastern end of the strait). He reported that the strong south westerly swell and the tides and currents suggested that the island was in a channel linking the Pacific and southern Indian Ocean. Governor Hunter thus wrote to Joseph Banks in August 1797 that it seemed certain a strait existed.[90]

### Climate

See also: [Climate of Tasmania](/wiki/Climate_of_Tasmania "Climate of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:CradleMountainTas_b.jpg)Snow on [Cradle Mountain](/wiki/Cradle_Mountain "Cradle Mountain") [](/wiki/File:Tasmania_K%C3%B6ppen.svg)The Köppen climate classifications of Tasmania

Tasmania has a relatively cool temperate climate compared to the rest of Australia, spared from the hot summers of the mainland and experiencing four distinct seasons.[91] Summer is from December to February when the average maximum sea temperature is 21 °C (70 °F) and inland areas around Launceston reach 24 °C (75 °F). Other inland areas are much cooler, with [Liawenee](/wiki/Liawenee "Liawenee"), located on the Central Plateau, one of the coldest places in Australia, ranging between 4 and 17 °C (39 and 63 °F) in February. Autumn is from March to May, with mostly settled weather, as summer patterns gradually take on the shape of winter patterns.[92] The winter months are from June to August and are generally the wettest and coldest months in the state, with most high lying areas receiving considerable snowfall. Winter maximums are 12 °C (54 °F) on average along coastal areas and 3 °C (37 °F) on the central plateau, as a result of a series of cold fronts from the [Southern Ocean](/wiki/Southern_Ocean "Southern Ocean"). Inland areas receive regular freezes throughout the winter months. Spring is from September to November, and is an unsettled season of transition, where winter weather patterns begin to take the shape of summer patterns, although snowfall is still common up until October. Spring is generally the windiest time of the year with afternoon sea breezes starting to take effect on the coast.

### Biodiversity

Main article: [Ecology of Tasmania](/wiki/Ecology_of_Tasmania "Ecology of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Hellyer_Gorge,_Tasmania.jpg)[Ferns](/wiki/Fern "Fern") in [Hellyer Gorge](/wiki/Hellyer_Gorge "Hellyer Gorge"), to the northeast of [Savage River National Park](/wiki/Savage_River_National_Park "Savage River National Park") [](/wiki/File:Sarcophilus_harrisii_taranna.jpg)The [Tasmanian Devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil "Tasmanian Devil"), Tasmania's state animal emblem

Geographically and biologically isolated, Tasmania is known for its unique [endemic](/wiki/Endemic "Endemic") flora and fauna. 

#### Flora

Tasmania has [extremely diverse](/wiki/Biodiversity "Biodiversity") vegetation, from the heavily grazed grassland of the dry Midlands to the tall evergreen [eucalypt](/wiki/Eucalypt "Eucalypt") forest, [alpine](/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate") heathlands and large areas of cool [temperate rainforests](/wiki/Temperate_rain_forest "Temperate rain forest") and moorlands in the rest of the state. Many species are unique to Tasmania, and some are related to species in South America and New Zealand through ancestors which grew on the supercontinent of [Gondwana](/wiki/Gondwana "Gondwana"), 50 million years ago. _[Nothofagus gunnii](/wiki/Nothofagus_gunnii "Nothofagus gunnii")_ , commonly known as Australian beech, is Australia's only temperate native deciduous tree and is found exclusively in Tasmania.[105]

Distinctive species of plant in Tasmania include: 

  * [_Eucalyptus regnans_ (mountain ash)](/wiki/Eucalyptus_regnans "Eucalyptus regnans") – the [tallest](/wiki/List_of_superlative_trees "List of superlative trees") flowering plant and hardwood in the world, [reaching 100 m](/wiki/Centurion_\(tree\) "Centurion \(tree\)") (328 ft).[106]
  * [_Nothofagus cunninghamii_ (myrtle beech)](/wiki/Nothofagus_cunninghamii "Nothofagus cunninghamii") – the most abundant temperate rainforest canopy species found in Tasmania.
  * [_Nothofagus gunnii_ (deciduous beech)](/wiki/Nothofagus_gunnii "Nothofagus gunnii") – Australia's only winter-deciduous tree.
  * [_Atherosperma moschatum_ (blackheart sassafras)](/wiki/Atherosperma_moschatum "Atherosperma moschatum") – a co-dominant rainforest tree with a nutmeg aroma.
  * [_Lagarostrobos franklinii_ (Huon pine)](/wiki/Lagarostrobos_franklinii "Lagarostrobos franklinii") – one of the oldest-lived tree species, and a self-preserving timber.
  * [_Phyllocladus aspleniifolius_ (celery-top pine)](/wiki/Phyllocladus_aspleniifolius "Phyllocladus aspleniifolius") – a celery-leaved conifer found in rainforests.
  * [Athrotaxis (Tasmanian cedar/redwood)](/wiki/Athrotaxis "Athrotaxis") – a genus comprising three extant species related to [sequoia](/wiki/Sequoiadendron "Sequoiadendron") found in Tasmania.[107]
  * [_Eucryphia lucida_ (leatherwood)](/wiki/Eucryphia_lucida "Eucryphia lucida") – a prominent floral symbol of Tasmania and a unique [monofloral honey](/wiki/Monofloral_honey "Monofloral honey") species.[108]



##### Bush tucker

See also: [Bush tucker § Tasmania](/wiki/Bush_tucker#Tasmania "Bush tucker")

Tasmania also has a number of [native](/wiki/Native_species "Native species") [edibles](/wiki/Edible "Edible"), known as [bush tucker](/wiki/Bush_tucker "Bush tucker") in Australia. These plants were [foraged](/wiki/Foraging "Foraging") by the [Tasmanian Aboriginals](/wiki/Tasmanian_Aboriginals "Tasmanian Aboriginals") and also used for other purposes, such as [construction](/wiki/Construction "Construction"). Unusual trees such as [cider gum (_Eucalyptus gunnii_)](/wiki/Eucalyptus_gunnii "Eucalyptus gunnii") had their [manna](/wiki/Sap "Sap") used to make a [syrup](/wiki/Syrup "Syrup") or an [alcohol](/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\) "Alcohol \(drug\)") ([cider](/wiki/Cider "Cider")). Other trees such as [wattles (acacias)](/wiki/Acacia "Acacia") like [blackwood (_Acacia melanoxylon_)](/wiki/Acacia_melanoxylon "Acacia melanoxylon") and [mimosa (_Acacia dealbata_)](/wiki/Acacia_dealbata "Acacia dealbata") could have their [seeds](/wiki/Wattleseed "Wattleseed") eaten or crushed into a [powder](/wiki/Powder "Powder"). There are also many [berries](/wiki/Berries "Berries") such as [snowberry (_Gaultheria hispida_)](/wiki/Gaultheria_hispida "Gaultheria hispida"), [fruits](/wiki/Fruits "Fruits") such as [heartberry (_Aristotelia peduncularis_)](/wiki/Aristotelia_peduncularis "Aristotelia peduncularis"), and [vegetables](/wiki/Vegetables "Vegetables") such as [river mint (_Mentha australis_)](/wiki/Mentha_australis "Mentha australis"), though no [crops](/wiki/Crops "Crops") like [maize](/wiki/Maize "Maize") that are used for large production.[109]

#### Fauna

Tasmania has a large percentage of [endemism](/wiki/Endemism "Endemism") whilst featuring many types of animals found on mainland Australia. Many of these species, such as the [platypus](/wiki/Platypus "Platypus"), are larger than their mainland relatives.[110] The island of Tasmania was home to the [thylacine](/wiki/Thylacine "Thylacine"), a [marsupial](/wiki/Marsupial "Marsupial") which resembled a [fossa](/wiki/Fossa_\(animal\) "Fossa \(animal\)") or some say a wild dog. Known colloquially as the Tasmanian tiger for the distinctive striping across its back, it became extinct in mainland Australia much earlier because of competition by the [dingo](/wiki/Dingo "Dingo"), introduced in prehistoric times. Owing to persecution by farmers, government-funded bounty hunters and, in the final years, collectors for overseas museums, it appears to have been exterminated in Tasmania. The [Tasmanian devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_devil "Tasmanian devil") became the [largest carnivorous marsupial](/wiki/Largest_mammals#Marsupials_\(Marsupialia\) "Largest mammals") in the world following the [extinction](/wiki/Extinction "Extinction") of the [thylacine](/wiki/Thylacine "Thylacine") in 1936 and is now found in the wild only in Tasmania. Tasmania was one of the last regions of Australia to be introduced to domesticated dogs. Dogs were brought from Britain in 1803 for hunting kangaroos and [emus](/wiki/Tasmanian_emu "Tasmanian emu"). This introduction completely transformed Aboriginal society, as it helped them to successfully compete with European hunters and was more important than the introduction of guns for the Aboriginal people.[111] Emus became extinct on Tasmania around 1865.[112]

Tasmania is a hotspot for [giant habitat trees and the large animal species](/wiki/Island_gigantism "Island gigantism") that occupy them, notably the endangered [Tasmanian wedge-tailed eagle (_Aquila audax fleayi_)](/wiki/Tasmanian_wedge-tailed_eagle "Tasmanian wedge-tailed eagle"), the [Tasmanian masked owl (_Tyto novaehollandiae castanops_)](/wiki/Tasmanian_masked_owl "Tasmanian masked owl"), the [Tasmanian giant freshwater crayfish (_Astacopsis gouldi_)](/wiki/Tasmanian_giant_freshwater_crayfish "Tasmanian giant freshwater crayfish"), the [yellow wattlebird (_Anthochaera paradoxa_)](/wiki/Yellow_wattlebird "Yellow wattlebird"), the [green rosella (_Platycercus caledonicus_)](/wiki/Green_rosella "Green rosella") and others. Tasmania is also home to the world's only three migratory parrots, the critically endangered [Orange-bellied parrot (_Neophema chrysogaster_)](/wiki/Orange-bellied_parrot "Orange-bellied parrot"), the [Blue-winged parrot (_Neophema chrysostoma_)](/wiki/Blue-winged_parrot "Blue-winged parrot"), and the fastest parrot in the world, the [swift parrot (_Lathamus discolor_)](/wiki/Swift_parrot "Swift parrot").[113] Tasmania has 12 [endemic](/wiki/Endemic "Endemic") species of [bird](/wiki/Bird "Bird") in total.[114]

#### Mycology

Tasmania is a hotspot for [fungal](/wiki/Fungi "Fungi") diversity. The importance of fungi in Tasmania's ecology is often overlooked; nonetheless, they play a vital role in the natural vegetation cycle.[115] Tasmania's southwestern wilderness hosts a rich diversity of alpine [lichens](/wiki/Lichen "Lichen"), with at least 162 documented species. The region's alpine lichen flora is particularly notable for its high level of endemism, with approximately 16% of species found nowhere else in the world. The area's cool [maritime climate](/wiki/Maritime_climate "Maritime climate"), Precambrian geology, and extensive [peat](/wiki/Peat "Peat") formations create unique habitats for these organisms. The [lichen communities](/wiki/Lichen_communities "Lichen communities") differ significantly from those found on Tasmania's eastern dolerite mountains, with the southwestern species showing stronger affinities to New Zealand and sub-Antarctic flora rather than to mainland Australia. Major lichen habitats in the alpine southwest include heathlands, alpine lawns, [feldmark](/wiki/Feldmark "Feldmark") (windswept rocky plateaus), and large rock outcrops, each supporting distinct assemblages of species. Some particularly diverse genera in the region include _[Bunodophoron](/wiki/Bunodophoron "Bunodophoron")_ , _[Cladia](/wiki/Cladia "Cladia")_ , _[Cladonia](/wiki/Cladonia "Cladonia")_ , _[Menegazzia](/wiki/Menegazzia "Menegazzia")_ , _[Micarea](/wiki/Micarea "Micarea")_ , _[Pertusaria](/wiki/Pertusaria "Pertusaria")_ , _[Pseudocyphellaria](/wiki/Pseudocyphellaria "Pseudocyphellaria")_ , _[Psoroma](/wiki/Psoroma "Psoroma")_ , _[Siphula](/wiki/Siphula "Siphula")_ , and _[Stereocaulon](/wiki/Stereocaulon "Stereocaulon")_.[116]

#### Conservation

Like the rest of Australia, Tasmania suffers from an [endangered species](/wiki/Endangered_species "Endangered species") problem. In particular, many important Tasmanian subspecies and world-significant species of [animal](/wiki/Animal "Animal") are classified as at risk in some way. A famous example is the [Tasmanian devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_devil "Tasmanian devil"), which is endangered due to [devil facial tumour disease](/wiki/Devil_facial_tumour_disease "Devil facial tumour disease"). Some species have already gone [extinct](/wiki/Extinct "Extinct"), primarily due to [human interference](/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment "Human impact on the environment"), such as in the case of the [thylacine](/wiki/Thylacine "Thylacine") or the [Tasmanian emu](/wiki/Tasmanian_emu "Tasmanian emu").[117][118] In Tasmania, there are about 90 endangered, vulnerable, or threatened vertebrate species classified by the state or Commonwealth governments.[119] Because of a reliance on roads and private vehicle transport, and a high concentration of animal populations divided by this development, Tasmania has the worst (per kilometre) [roadkill](/wiki/Roadkill "Roadkill") rate in the world, with 32 animals killed per hour and at least 300,000 per year.[120]

[Protected areas of Tasmania](/wiki/Protected_areas_of_Tasmania "Protected areas of Tasmania") cover 21% of the island's land area in the form of [national parks](/wiki/National_park "National park").[121] The [Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area](/wiki/Tasmanian_Wilderness_World_Heritage_Area "Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area") (TWWHA) was inscribed by [UNESCO](/wiki/UNESCO "UNESCO") in 1982, where it is globally significant because "most UNESCO World Heritage sites meet only one or two of the ten criteria for that status. The Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area (TWWHA) meets 7 out of 10 criteria. Only one other place on earth—China’s [Mount Taishan](/wiki/Mount_Tai "Mount Tai")—meets that many criteria".[122] Controversy surrounds the decision in 2014 by the [Abbott](/wiki/Abbott_Government "Abbott Government") federal [Liberal government](/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Australia "Liberal Party of Australia") to request the area's delisting and opening for resource exploration (before it was rejected by the UN Committee at Doha),[123] and the current [mining](/wiki/Mining "Mining") and [deforestation](/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation") in the state's [Tarkine](/wiki/Tarkine "Tarkine") region, the largest single [temperate rainforest](/wiki/Temperate_rainforest "Temperate rainforest") in Australia.[124][125]

## Demography

"Tasmanian people" redirects here. For the original inhabitants of Tasmania, see § Aboriginal people, and [Aboriginal Tasmanians](/wiki/Aboriginal_Tasmanians "Aboriginal Tasmanians").

[](/wiki/File:ABS-3101.0-AustralianDemographicStatistics-EstimatedResidentPopulationStatesTerritories-EstimatedResidentPopulation-Persons-Tasmania-A2060848V.svg)Estimated resident population since 1981

The population of Tasmania is the most homogeneous of any Australian state, being mostly of [British](/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland") (primarily [English](/wiki/English_people "English people")) descent.[126]

Until 2012, Tasmania was the only state in Australia with an above-replacement [total fertility rate](/wiki/Total_fertility_rate "Total fertility rate"); Tasmanian women had an average of 2.24 children each.[127] By 2012 the birth rate had slipped to 2.1 children per woman, bringing the state to the replacement threshold, but it continues to have the second-highest birth rate of any state or territory (behind the Northern Territory).[128]

Major population centres include [Hobart](/wiki/Hobart "Hobart"), [Launceston](/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania "Launceston, Tasmania"), [Devonport](/wiki/Devonport,_Tasmania "Devonport, Tasmania"), [Burnie](/wiki/Burnie,_Tasmania "Burnie, Tasmania"), and [Ulverstone](/wiki/Ulverstone,_Tasmania "Ulverstone, Tasmania"). [Kingston](/wiki/Kingston,_Tasmania "Kingston, Tasmania") is often defined as a separate city but is generally regarded as part of the Greater Hobart Area.[129]

### Language

At the [2021 census](/wiki/2021_Australian_census "2021 Australian census"), 86.1% of inhabitants spoke only English at home, with the next most common languages being [Mandarin](/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese") (1.5%), [Nepali](/wiki/Nepali_language "Nepali language") (1.3%), [Punjabi](/wiki/Punjabi_language "Punjabi language") (0.5%) and Spanish (0.3%).[136]

## Government

Main article: [Government of Tasmania](/wiki/Government_of_Tasmania "Government of Tasmania")

Further information: [Governors of Tasmania](/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Tasmania "List of Governors of Tasmania") and [Parliament of Tasmania](/wiki/Parliament_of_Tasmania "Parliament of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Hobart-Tasmania-Australia04.JPG)[Parliament House, Hobart](/wiki/Parliament_House,_Hobart "Parliament House, Hobart")

The form of the government of Tasmania is prescribed in [its constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_Tasmania "Constitution of Tasmania"), which dates from 1934. Since 1901, Tasmania has been a state of the Commonwealth of Australia, and the [Australian Constitution](/wiki/Australian_Constitution "Australian Constitution") regulates its relationship with the Commonwealth and prescribes which powers each level of government is allowed. 

### Commonwealth Parliament

Tasmania is represented in the [Senate](/wiki/Australian_Senate "Australian Senate") by 12 senators, on an equal basis with all other states. In the [House of Representatives](/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives "Australian House of Representatives"), Tasmania is entitled to five seats, which is the minimum allocation for a state guaranteed by the Constitution—the number of House of Representatives seats for each state is otherwise decided on the basis of their relative populations, and Tasmania has never qualified for five seats on that basis alone. 

As of 2026, Tasmania is represented in the Senate by 4 members of the Liberal party, 4 from the Labor party, 2 from the Green party and 2 others. In the House of Representatives, there are 4 Labor members and one independent. 

### State Parliament

The Parliament of Tasmania consists of the Tasmanian Legislative Council and the Tasmanian House of Representatives. The [Legislative Council](/wiki/Tasmanian_Legislative_Council "Tasmanian Legislative Council"), the upper house, consists of 15 members who serve staggered six year terms representing single member districts. Tasmania's [House of Assembly](/wiki/Tasmanian_House_of_Assembly "Tasmanian House of Assembly"), the lower house, use a system of multi-seat [proportional representation](/wiki/Proportional_representation "Proportional representation") known as [Hare-Clark](/wiki/Single_Transferable_Vote "Single Transferable Vote"). Members of the House of Assembly serve terms of up to 4 years. 

At the [2025 state election](/wiki/2025_Tasmanian_state_election "2025 Tasmanian state election"), the Liberal Party won 14 of the 35 House seats versus 10 for Labor. As in the [previous election](/wiki/2024_Tasmanian_state_election "2024 Tasmanian state election"), no party won a majority. The ministry of [Jeremy Rockliff](/wiki/Premiership_of_Jeremy_Rockliff "Premiership of Jeremy Rockliff") retained government with the support of the crossbench.[137]

Between 1998 and 2024 the House consisted of 25 seats (five members per division) rather than 35 (seven members). In 2010, the major party leadership had endorsed reinstating the 35 seat number, but Liberal and Labor support was withdrawn the following year, with only the Greens supporting it.[138]. The House of Assembly Select Committee in 2020 recommended in its report that the number should be increased again from 25 to 35, arguing that such a small representation would undermine democracy and limit the capabilities of the government [139].

### Politics

Tasmania has a number of undeveloped regions. Proposals for local economic development have been faced with requirements for environmental sensitivity, or opposition. In particular, proposals for hydroelectric power generation were debated in the late 20th century. In the 1970s, opposition to the construction of the [Lake Pedder](/wiki/Lake_Pedder "Lake Pedder") reservoir impoundment led to the formation of the world's first [Green party](/wiki/Green_party "Green party"), the [United Tasmania Group](/wiki/United_Tasmania_Group "United Tasmania Group").[140]

In the early 1980s the state debated the proposed [Franklin River Dam](/wiki/Franklin_Dam "Franklin Dam"). The anti-dam sentiment was shared by many Australians outside Tasmania and proved a factor in the election of the [Hawke](/wiki/Bob_Hawke "Bob Hawke") [Labor](/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party "Australian Labor Party") government in 1983, which halted construction of the dam.[141] Since the 1980s the environmental focus has shifted to [old growth](/wiki/Old_growth "Old growth") logging and mining in the Tarkine region, which have both proved divisive.[142] The Tasmania Together process recommended an end to clear felling in high conservation old growth forests by January 2003, but was unsuccessful.

### Local government

Main article: [Local government areas of Tasmania](/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Tasmania "Local government areas of Tasmania")

Tasmania has 29 local government areas. Local councils are responsible for functions delegated by the Tasmanian parliament, such as urban planning, road infrastructure and waste management. Council revenue comes mostly from property taxes and government grants. 

As with the [House of Assembly](/wiki/Tasmanian_House_of_Assembly "Tasmanian House of Assembly"), Tasmania's local government elections use a system of multi-seat [proportional representation](/wiki/Proportional_representation "Proportional representation") known as [Hare–Clark](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote"). Local government elections take place every four years and are conducted by the [Tasmanian Electoral Commission](/wiki/Tasmanian_Electoral_Commission "Tasmanian Electoral Commission") by full [postal ballot](/wiki/Postal_ballot "Postal ballot"). The next local government elections will be held during October 2026.[143]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Tasmania](/wiki/Economy_of_Tasmania "Economy of Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Tasmanian_salmon_on_a_pea_and_corn_fritter.jpg)Smoked Tasmanian salmon. Tasmania is a large exporter of seafood, particularly [salmon](/wiki/Salmon "Salmon"). [](/wiki/File:Derwent_Valley_\(22645573102\).jpg)[Hop farms](/wiki/Hops "Hops") in the [Derwent Valley](/wiki/Derwent_Valley_Council "Derwent Valley Council")

Traditionally, Tasmania's main industries have been mining (including copper, [zinc](/wiki/Zinc "Zinc"), [tin](/wiki/Tin "Tin"), and iron), agriculture, forestry, and [tourism](/wiki/Tourism_in_Tasmania "Tourism in Tasmania"). Tasmania is on [Australia's electrical grid](/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_Australia "Electricity sector in Australia") and in the 1940s and 1950s, a hydro-industrialisation initiative was embodied in the state by [Hydro Tasmania](/wiki/Hydro_Tasmania "Hydro Tasmania"). These all have had varying fortunes over the last century and more, involved in ebbs and flows of population moving in and away dependent upon the specific requirements of the dominant industries of the time.[144] The state also has a large number of food exporting sectors, including but not limited to seafood (such as [salmon](/wiki/Salmon "Salmon"), [abalone](/wiki/Abalone "Abalone") and [crayfish](/wiki/Crayfish "Crayfish")). 

In the 1960s and 1970s there was a decline in traditional crops such as apples and pears,[145] with other crops and industries eventually rising in their place. During the 15 years until 2010, new agricultural products such as wine, [saffron](/wiki/Saffron "Saffron"), [pyrethrum](/wiki/Pyrethrum "Pyrethrum") and [cherries](/wiki/Cherry "Cherry") have been fostered by the [Tasmanian Institute of Agricultural Research](/wiki/Tasmanian_Institute_of_Agricultural_Research "Tasmanian Institute of Agricultural Research"). 

Favourable economic conditions throughout Australia, cheaper air fares, and two new _Spirit of Tasmania_ ferries have all contributed to what is now a rising tourism industry. 

About 1.7% of the Tasmanian population are employed by local government.[146] Other major employers include [Nyrstar](/wiki/Nyrstar "Nyrstar"), [Norske Skog](/wiki/Norske_Skog "Norske Skog"), Grange Resources, [Rio Tinto](/wiki/Rio_Tinto_Group "Rio Tinto Group"),[147] the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Hobart](/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Hobart "Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Hobart"), and [Federal Group](/wiki/Federal_Group "Federal Group"). Small business is a large part of the community life, including [Incat](/wiki/Incat "Incat"), [Moorilla Estate](/wiki/Moorilla_Estate "Moorilla Estate") and [Tassal](/wiki/Tassal "Tassal"). In the late 1990s, a number of national companies based their call centres in the state after obtaining cheap access to broad-band fibre optic connections.[148][144]

34% of Tasmanians are reliant on welfare payments as their primary source of income.[149] This number is in part due to the large number of older residents and retirees in Tasmania receiving Age Pensions. Due to its natural environment and clean air, Tasmania is a common retirement selection for Australians.[150]

### Science and technology

The modern [scientific](/wiki/Science "Science") sector in Tasmania benefits from around $500 million in annual investment.[153] Tasmania has a long history of scientific and [technological](/wiki/Technology "Technology") [innovation](/wiki/Innovation "Innovation").[154] The first scientific-style [observations](/wiki/Observation "Observation") were conducted by the [First Nation Tasmanians](/wiki/Aboriginal_Tasmanians "Aboriginal Tasmanians"), primarily through the [watching](/wiki/Amateur_astronomy "Amateur astronomy") and [mythologising](/wiki/Myth "Myth") of the [night sky](/wiki/Night_sky "Night sky"). Their story explaining the [phases](/wiki/Lunar_phase "Lunar phase") of the [moon](/wiki/Moon "Moon") and [sun](/wiki/Sun "Sun") "is one of the rare accounts that explicitly acknowledges that the light of the Moon is a reflection of the Sun's light".[155]

The French [D'Entrecasteaux](/wiki/Antoine_Bruni_d%27Entrecasteaux "Antoine Bruni d'Entrecasteaux") Expedition of 1792–93 had anchored twice during its search of the missing [La Pérouse](/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Galaup,_comte_de_Lap%C3%A9rouse "Jean-François de Galaup, comte de Lapérouse") in the [Baie de la Recherche (Recherche Bay)](/wiki/Recherche_Bay "Recherche Bay") in far-south Tasmania. During their stay, the crew took [botanical](/wiki/Botany "Botany"), [astronomical](/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy"), and [geomagnetic](/wiki/Earth%27s_magnetic_field "Earth's magnetic field") observations which were the first of their kind performed on Australian soil. As well as this, they engaged in amicable relations with the locals and environment, gifting the area a "French [garden](/wiki/Garden "Garden")", in which "the relatively extensive, well-documented (both pictorially and written) encounters [...] between [them] provided a very early opportunity for meetings and mutual observation".[156]

The longest-running branch of the [Royal Society](/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") outside of the United Kingdom is the [Royal Society of Tasmania](/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Tasmania "Royal Society of Tasmania") which was summoned in 1843. The Tasmanian Society of Natural History had been formed previously in 1838 before its merger with the Royal Society in 1849. It had been served by early [botanists](/wiki/Botany "Botany") working in Tasmania such as [Ronald Gunn](/wiki/Ronald_Campbell_Gunn "Ronald Campbell Gunn") and his correspondences.[157][158]

Although [Tamworth](/wiki/Tamworth,_New_South_Wales "Tamworth, New South Wales") in [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales") is often credited[159] as being the first place in Australia with [electric](/wiki/Electricity "Electricity") [street lighting](/wiki/Street_light "Street light") in 1888, [Waratah](/wiki/Waratah,_Tasmania "Waratah, Tasmania") in [North West Tasmania](/wiki/North_West_Tasmania "North West Tasmania") was actually the first place to do so in Australia in 1886, although at a smaller scale.[160]

## Culture

### Literature

Main article: [Tasmanian literature](/wiki/Tasmanian_literature "Tasmanian literature")

[](/wiki/File:RichardFlanagan_300w.jpg)[Man Booker Prize](/wiki/Man_Booker_Prize "Man Booker Prize")-winner [Richard Flanagan](/wiki/Richard_Flanagan "Richard Flanagan") has written several novels set in his home state of Tasmania.

Notable titles by Tasmanian authors include _[The Museum of Modern Love](/wiki/The_Museum_of_Modern_Love "The Museum of Modern Love")_[161][162] by [Heather Rose](/wiki/Heather_Rose "Heather Rose"), _The Narrow Road to the Deep North_ by [Richard Flanagan](/wiki/Richard_Flanagan "Richard Flanagan"), _The Alphabet of Light and Dark_ by [Danielle Wood](/wiki/Danielle_Wood_\(writer\) "Danielle Wood \(writer\)"), _[The Roving Party](/wiki/The_Roving_Party "The Roving Party")_ by Rohan Wilson and _[The Year of Living Dangerously](/wiki/The_Year_of_Living_Dangerously_\(novel\) "The Year of Living Dangerously \(novel\)")_ by [Christopher Koch](/wiki/Christopher_Koch "Christopher Koch"), _The Rain Queen_[163] by [Katherine Scholes](/wiki/Katherine_Scholes "Katherine Scholes"), _Bridget Crack_[164] by Rachel Leary, and _The Blue Day Book_ by [Bradley Trevor Greive](/wiki/Bradley_Trevor_Greive "Bradley Trevor Greive"). A small part of [Helen Garner](/wiki/Helen_Garner "Helen Garner")'s _[Monkey Grip](/wiki/Monkey_Grip_\(novel\) "Monkey Grip \(novel\)")_ is set in Hobart as the main characters take a sojourn there. Children's books include _They Found a Cave_ by [Nan Chauncy](/wiki/Nan_Chauncy "Nan Chauncy"), _The Museum of Thieves_ by [Lian Tanner](/wiki/Lian_Tanner "Lian Tanner"), _Finding Serendipity_ , _A Week Without Tuesday_ and _Blueberry Pancakes Forever_[165] by Angelica Banks, _[Tiger Tale](/wiki/Tiger_Tale "Tiger Tale")_ by Marion and Steve Isham. Tasmania is home to the eminent literary magazine that was formed in 1979, [Island magazine](/wiki/Island_magazine "Island magazine"), and the biennial Tasmanian Writers and Readers Festival, now renamed the Hobart Writers Festival. 

[Tasmanian Gothic](/wiki/Tasmanian_Gothic "Tasmanian Gothic") is a literary genre which expresses the island state's "peculiar 'otherness' in relation to the mainland, as a remote, mysterious and self-enclosed place."[166] [Marcus Clarke](/wiki/Marcus_Clarke "Marcus Clarke")'s novel _[For the Term of his Natural Life](/wiki/For_the_Term_of_his_Natural_Life "For the Term of his Natural Life")_ , written in the 1870s and set in convict era Tasmania, is a seminal example. This distinctive [Gothic](/wiki/Gothic_fiction "Gothic fiction") is not just restricted to literature, but can be represented through all [the arts](/wiki/The_arts "The arts"), such as in [painting](/wiki/Painting "Painting"), [music](/wiki/Music "Music"), or [architecture](/wiki/Architecture "Architecture"). 

### Visual arts

The biennial _Tasmanian Living Artists' Week_ is a ten-day statewide festival for Tasmania's visual artists. The fourth festival in 2007 involved more than 1000 artists. Tasmania is home to two winners of the prestigious [Archibald Prize](/wiki/Archibald_Prize "Archibald Prize")—[Jack Carington Smith](/wiki/Jack_Carington_Smith "Jack Carington Smith") in 1963 for a portrait of [James McAuley](/wiki/James_McAuley "James McAuley"), and [Geoffrey Dyer](/wiki/Geoffrey_Dyer "Geoffrey Dyer") in 2003 for his portrait of [Richard Flanagan](/wiki/Richard_Flanagan "Richard Flanagan"). Photographers [Olegas Truchanas](/wiki/Olegas_Truchanas "Olegas Truchanas") and [Peter Dombrovskis](/wiki/Peter_Dombrovskis "Peter Dombrovskis") are known for works that became iconic in the [Lake Pedder](/wiki/Lake_Pedder "Lake Pedder") and [Franklin Dam](/wiki/Franklin_Dam "Franklin Dam") conservation movements. English-born painter [John Glover](/wiki/John_Glover_\(artist\) "John Glover \(artist\)") (1767–1849) is known for his paintings of Tasmanian landscapes, and is the namesake for the annual [Glover Prize](/wiki/Glover_Prize "Glover Prize"), which is awarded to the best landscape painting of Tasmania. The [Museum of Old and New Art](/wiki/Museum_of_Old_and_New_Art "Museum of Old and New Art") (MONA) opened in January 2011 at the [Moorilla Estate](/wiki/Moorilla_Estate "Moorilla Estate") in [Berriedale](/wiki/Berriedale,_Tasmania "Berriedale, Tasmania"),[167] and is the largest privately owned museum complex in Australia.[168]

[](/wiki/File:James_Turrell%27s_Amarna_at_Mona_2015.jpg)The [Museum of Old and New Art](/wiki/Museum_of_Old_and_New_Art "Museum of Old and New Art") (MONA), the largest privately owned museum in the Southern Hemisphere

### Music and performing arts

[](/wiki/File:Princess_Theatre_at_night,_Launceston.JPG)The [Princess Theatre](/wiki/Princess_Theatre,_Launceston "Princess Theatre, Launceston") and Earl Arts Centre, [Launceston](/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania "Launceston, Tasmania")

Tasmania has a varied musical scene, ranging from the [Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Tasmanian_Symphony_Orchestra "Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra") whose home is the [Federation Concert Hall](/wiki/Hotel_Grand_Chancellor,_Hobart "Hotel Grand Chancellor, Hobart"), to a substantial number of small bands, orchestras, string quintets, saxophone ensembles and individual artists who perform at a variety of venues around the state. Tasmania is also home to a vibrant community of composers including [Constantine Koukias](/wiki/Constantine_Koukias "Constantine Koukias"), [Maria Grenfell](/wiki/Maria_Grenfell "Maria Grenfell") and [Don Kay](/wiki/Don_Kay_\(composer\) "Don Kay \(composer\)"). Tasmania is also home to one of Australia's leading new music institutions, [IHOS Music Theatre and Opera](/wiki/IHOS_Music_Theatre_and_Opera "IHOS Music Theatre and Opera") and gospel choirs, the [Southern Gospel Choir](/wiki/Southern_Gospel_Choir "Southern Gospel Choir"). Prominent Australian metal bands [Psycroptic](/wiki/Psycroptic "Psycroptic") and [Striborg](/wiki/Striborg "Striborg") hail from Tasmania.[169] Noir-rock band [The Paradise Motel](/wiki/The_Paradise_Motel "The Paradise Motel") and 1980s power-pop band [The Innocents](/wiki/The_Innocents_\(Australian_band\) "The Innocents \(Australian band\)")[170] are also citizens. 

The [Tasmanian Aboriginals](/wiki/Tasmanian_Aboriginals "Tasmanian Aboriginals") were known to have sung [oral traditions](/wiki/Oral_traditions "Oral traditions"), as [Fanny Cochrane Smith](/wiki/Fanny_Cochrane_Smith "Fanny Cochrane Smith") (the last fluent speaker of any [Tasmanian language](/wiki/Tasmanian_languages "Tasmanian languages")) had done so in recordings from 1899 to 1903.[171][172] Tasmania has been home to some early and prominent [Australian composers](/wiki/List_of_Australian_composers "List of Australian composers"). In piano, [Kitty Parker](/wiki/Katharine_Parker "Katharine Parker") from [Longford](/wiki/Longford,_Tasmania "Longford, Tasmania") was described by world-famous Australian composer [Percy Grainger](/wiki/Percy_Grainger "Percy Grainger") as his most gifted student.[173] [Peter Sculthorpe](/wiki/Peter_Sculthorpe "Peter Sculthorpe") was originally from [Launceston](/wiki/Launceston,_Tasmania "Launceston, Tasmania") and became well known in Australia for his works which were influenced by his Tasmanian origins, and he is, by coincidence, distantly related to Fanny Cochrane Smith.[174] In 1996, Sculthorpe composed the piece _Port Arthur: In Memoriam_ for chamber [orchestra](/wiki/Orchestra "Orchestra"), which was first performed by the [Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Tasmanian_Symphony_Orchestra "Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra").[175] [Charles Sandys Packer](/wiki/Charles_Sandys_Packer "Charles Sandys Packer") was an early Tasmanian example of the tradition of [Australian classical music](/wiki/Australian_classical_music "Australian classical music"), transported for the crime of embezzlement in 1839, and at a similar time [Francis Hartwell Henslowe](/wiki/Francis_Hartwell_Henslowe "Francis Hartwell Henslowe") had spent time as a [public servant](/wiki/Civil_servant "Civil servant") in Tasmania. [Amy Sherwin](/wiki/Amy_Sherwin "Amy Sherwin"), known as the _Tasmanian Nightingale_ was a successful [soprano](/wiki/Soprano "Soprano"),[176] and [Eileen Joyce](/wiki/Eileen_Joyce "Eileen Joyce"), who came from remote [Zeehan](/wiki/Zeehan "Zeehan"), became a world-renowned pianist at the time of her peak.[177]

Further information: [Tasmanian Bands League](/wiki/Tasmanian_Bands_League "Tasmanian Bands League")

### Cinema

See also: [List of films shot in Tasmania](/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Tasmania "List of films shot in Tasmania") and [Category:Television shows set in Tasmania](/wiki/Category:Television_shows_set_in_Tasmania "Category:Television shows set in Tasmania")

Films set in Tasmania include _[Young Einstein](/wiki/Young_Einstein "Young Einstein")_ , _[The Tale of Ruby Rose](/wiki/The_Tale_of_Ruby_Rose "The Tale of Ruby Rose")_ , _[The Hunter](/wiki/The_Hunter_\(2011_Australian_film\) "The Hunter \(2011 Australian film\)")_ , _[The Last Confession of Alexander Pearce](/wiki/The_Last_Confession_of_Alexander_Pearce "The Last Confession of Alexander Pearce")_ , _Arctic Blast_ , _[Manganinnie](/wiki/Manganinnie "Manganinnie")_ (with music composed by [Peter Sculthorpe](/wiki/Peter_Sculthorpe "Peter Sculthorpe")), _[Van Diemen's Land](/wiki/Van_Diemen%27s_Land_\(film\) "Van Diemen's Land \(film\)")_ , _[Lion](/wiki/Lion_\(2016_film\) "Lion \(2016 film\)")_ , and _[The Nightingale](/wiki/The_Nightingale_\(2018_film\) "The Nightingale \(2018 film\)")_. Common within [Australian cinema](/wiki/Australian_cinema "Australian cinema"), the Tasmanian landscape is a focal point in most of their feature film productions. _The Last Confession of Alexander Pearce_ and _Van Diemen's Land_ are both set during an episode of Tasmania's convict history. Tasmanian film production goes as far back as the [silent era](/wiki/Silent_era "Silent era"), with the epic _[For The Term of His Natural Life](/wiki/For_the_Term_of_His_Natural_Life_\(1927_film\) "For the Term of His Natural Life \(1927 film\)")_ in 1927 being the most expensive feature film made on Australian shores. _[The Kettering Incident](/wiki/The_Kettering_Incident "The Kettering Incident")_ , filmed in and around [Kettering, Tasmania](/wiki/Kettering,_Tasmania "Kettering, Tasmania"), won the 2016 AACTA Award for Best Telefeature or Miniseries. The [documentary series](/wiki/Nature_documentary "Nature documentary") _[Walking with Dinosaurs](/wiki/Walking_with_Dinosaurs "Walking with Dinosaurs")_ was partly filmed in Tasmania due to its terrain. 

The [Tasmanian Film Corporation](/wiki/Tasmanian_Film_Corporation "Tasmanian Film Corporation"), which financed _[Manganinnie](/wiki/Manganinnie "Manganinnie")_ , was the successor to the Tasmanian Government Department of Film Production but disappeared after privatisation. Its role is now filled by the [Australian Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation "Australian Broadcasting Corporation"), Screen Tasmania, and private ventures such as Blue Rocket Productions.

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in Tasmania](/wiki/Sport_in_Tasmania "Sport in Tasmania")

[](/wiki/File:Bellerive_oval_hobart.jpg)[Bellerive Oval](/wiki/Bellerive_Oval "Bellerive Oval") hosts [cricket](/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") and [Australian rules football](/wiki/Australian_rules_football "Australian rules football"), Tasmania's two most popular spectator sports.

Sport is an important pastime in Tasmania, and the state has produced several famous sportsmen and women and also hosted several major sporting events. The [Tasmanian Tigers](/wiki/Tasmanian_Tigers "Tasmanian Tigers") cricket team represents the state successfully (for example the [Sheffield Shield](/wiki/Sheffield_Shield "Sheffield Shield") in 2007, 2011 and 2013) and plays its home games at the [Bellerive Oval](/wiki/Bellerive_Oval "Bellerive Oval") in Hobart, which is also the home ground for the [Hobart Hurricanes](/wiki/Hobart_Hurricanes "Hobart Hurricanes") in the Big Bash League. In addition, Bellerive Oval regularly hosts international cricket matches. Famous Tasmanian cricketers include [David Boon](/wiki/David_Boon "David Boon"), former Australian captains [Ricky Ponting](/wiki/Ricky_Ponting "Ricky Ponting") and [Tim Paine](/wiki/Tim_Paine "Tim Paine"). 

[Australian rules football in Tasmania](/wiki/Australian_rules_football_in_Tasmania "Australian rules football in Tasmania") is the most watched form of football and a [Tasmanian team](/wiki/Tasmanian_AFL_Bid "Tasmanian AFL Bid") was awarded a license to enter the [Australian Football League](/wiki/Australian_Football_League "Australian Football League") (AFL) in 2028 to be based out of a new [Macquarie Point Stadium](/wiki/Macquarie_Point_Stadium "Macquarie Point Stadium"). AFL matches have been played since 2001 at [Aurora Stadium](/wiki/York_Park "York Park") in Launceston and Bellerive Oval in Hobart. Local leagues include the [North West Football League](/wiki/North_West_Football_League "North West Football League") and [Tasmanian State League](/wiki/Tasmanian_State_League "Tasmanian State League"). 

[Soccer in Tasmania](/wiki/Soccer_in_Tasmania "Soccer in Tasmania") is the most participated football code and there is an active [Tasmanian A-League bid](/wiki/Tasmanian_A-League_bid "Tasmanian A-League bid"). The existing statewide league is the [NPL Tasmania](/wiki/National_Premier_Leagues_Tasmania "National Premier Leagues Tasmania"). 

[Rugby Union](/wiki/Rugby_Union "Rugby Union") is also played in Tasmania and is governed by the [Tasmanian Rugby Union](/wiki/Tasmanian_Rugby_Union "Tasmanian Rugby Union"). Ten clubs take part in the statewide Tasmanian Rugby Competition. 

Tasmania hosts the professional [Moorilla International](/wiki/Moorilla_Hobart_International "Moorilla Hobart International") tennis tournament as part of the lead up to the Australian Open and is played at the [Hobart International Tennis Centre](/wiki/Hobart_International_Tennis_Centre "Hobart International Tennis Centre"), Hobart. 

The [Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race](/wiki/Sydney_to_Hobart_Yacht_Race "Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race") is an annual event starting in Sydney, NSW, on Boxing Day and finishing in Hobart, Tasmania. It is widely considered to be one of the most difficult yacht races in the world.[178]

In basketball, Tasmania has previously been represented in the [National Basketball League (NBL)](/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(Australia\) "National Basketball League \(Australia\)") by [Launceston Casino City](/wiki/Launceston_Casino_City "Launceston Casino City") (1980–1982), the [Devonport Warriors](/wiki/Devonport_Warriors "Devonport Warriors") (1983–1984), and the [Hobart Devils](/wiki/Hobart_Devils "Hobart Devils") (1983–1996). Since the [2021–22 NBL season](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NBL_season "2021–22 NBL season"), Tasmania has been represented by the [Tasmania JackJumpers](/wiki/Tasmania_JackJumpers "Tasmania JackJumpers"), a state-wide franchise which plays its home games in both Hobart and Launceston. The JackJumpers secured their maiden [NBL championship](/wiki/List_of_NBL_champions "List of NBL champions") in the [2023–24 season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NBL_season "2023–24 NBL season"), marking Tasmania's first NBL title since Launceston Casino City in 1981.[179]

### Cuisine

See also: [Tasmanian wine](/wiki/Tasmanian_wine "Tasmanian wine")

Tasmanian Aboriginal people had a diverse diet, including native currants, pigface, and native plums, and a wide range of birds and kangaroos. Seafood has always been a significant part of the Tasmanian diet, including its wide range of shellfish, which are still commercially farmed[180] such as [crayfish](/wiki/Crayfish "Crayfish"), [orange roughy](/wiki/Orange_roughy "Orange roughy"), [salmon](/wiki/Salmon_\(food\) "Salmon \(food\)")[180] and [oysters](/wiki/Oyster "Oyster").[180] Seal meat also formed a significant part of the Aboriginal diet.[181]

Tasmania's non-Aboriginal cuisine has a unique history from that of mainland Australia. It has developed through many subsequent waves of immigration. Tasmanian traditional foods include scallop pies – a pie filled with scallops in curry – and curry powder, which was popularised by [Keen's Curry](/wiki/Keen%27s "Keen's") in the 19th century.[182] Tasmania also produces and consumes wasabi, saffron, truffles and leatherwood honey.[183]

[](/wiki/File:Cascade_Brewery_1.jpg)Built in Hobart in 1824, [Cascade Brewery](/wiki/Cascade_Brewery "Cascade Brewery") is Australia's oldest continuously operating brewery.

Tasmania now has a wide range of restaurants, in part due to the arrival of immigrants and changing cultural patterns. Scattered across Tasmania are many vineyards,[180] and Tasmanian beer brands such as [Boags](/wiki/Boags "Boags") and [Cascade](/wiki/Cascade_Brewery "Cascade Brewery") are known and sold in mainland Australia. [King Island](/wiki/King_Island_\(Tasmania\) "King Island \(Tasmania\)") off the northwestern coast of Tasmania has a reputation for boutique cheeses[180] and dairy products. 

The _Central Cookery Book_ was written in 1930 by [A. C. Irvine](/wiki/Alice_Christina_Irvine "Alice Christina Irvine") and is still popular in Australia and even internationally.[184][185] Tasmanian cuisine is often unique and has won many awards. One example is the Hartshorn Distillery, which has won prizes in the World [Vodka](/wiki/Vodka "Vodka") Awards for three years in a row since 2017.[186]

### Events

Main article: [List of events in Tasmania](/wiki/List_of_events_in_Tasmania "List of events in Tasmania")

To foster tourism, the state government encourages or supports several annual events in and around the island. The best known of these is the [Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race](/wiki/Sydney_to_Hobart_Yacht_Race "Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race"), starting on [Boxing Day](/wiki/Boxing_Day "Boxing Day") in Sydney and usually arriving at [Constitution Dock](/wiki/Constitution_Dock "Constitution Dock") in Hobart around three to four days later, during the [Taste of Tasmania](/wiki/Taste_of_Tasmania "Taste of Tasmania"), an annual food and wine festival. Other events include the [Targa Tasmania](/wiki/Targa_Tasmania "Targa Tasmania") [rally](/wiki/Rallying "Rallying") which attracts rally drivers from around the world and is staged all over the state, over five days. Rural or regional events include [Agfest](/wiki/Agfest "Agfest"), a three-day [agricultural show](/wiki/Agricultural_show "Agricultural show") held at [Carrick](/wiki/Carrick,_Tasmania "Carrick, Tasmania") (just west of Launceston) in early May and [NASA](/wiki/NASA "NASA") supported TastroFest – Tasmania's Astronomy Festival, held early August in [Ulverstone](/wiki/Ulverstone,_Tasmania "Ulverstone, Tasmania") (Northwest Tasmania). The [Royal Hobart Show](/wiki/Royal_Hobart_Show "Royal Hobart Show") and [Royal Launceston Show](/wiki/Royal_Launceston_Show "Royal Launceston Show") are both held in October annually. 

Music events held in Tasmania include the [Falls Festival](/wiki/Falls_Festival "Falls Festival") at [Marion Bay](/wiki/Marion_Bay,_Tasmania "Marion Bay, Tasmania") (a [Victorian](/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\) "Victoria \(Australia\)") event now held in both Victoria and Tasmania on New Year's Eve); the Festival of Voices, a national celebration of song held each year in Hobart attracting international and national teachers and choirs in the heart of Winter; and [MS Fest](/wiki/MS_Fest "MS Fest"), a charity music event held in Launceston to raise money for those with [multiple sclerosis](/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis"). The [Cygnet Folk Festival](/wiki/Cygnet_Folk_Festival "Cygnet Folk Festival") is one of Australia's most iconic [folk music](/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") festivals and is held in [Cygnet](/wiki/Cygnet,_Tasmania "Cygnet, Tasmania") in the [Huon Valley](/wiki/Huon_Valley "Huon Valley") every year in January.[187] The Tasmanian Lute Festival is an early music event held in different locations in Tasmania every two years. Recent additions to the state arts events calendar include the [10 Days on the Island](/wiki/10_Days_on_the_Island "10 Days on the Island") arts festival, [MONA FOMA](/wiki/MONA_FOMA "MONA FOMA"), run by [David Walsh](/wiki/David_Walsh_\(art_collector\) "David Walsh \(art collector\)") and curated by [Brian Ritchie](/wiki/Brian_Ritchie "Brian Ritchie") and [Dark Mofo](/wiki/Dark_Mofo "Dark Mofo") also run by [David Walsh](/wiki/David_Walsh_\(art_collector\) "David Walsh \(art collector\)") and curated by Leigh Carmichael. 

[The Unconformity](/wiki/The_Unconformity "The Unconformity") is a three-day festival held every two years in [Queenstown](/wiki/Queenstown,_Tasmania "Queenstown, Tasmania") on the [West Coast](/wiki/West_Coast_of_Tasmania "West Coast of Tasmania").[188][189] Each February in [Evandale](/wiki/Evandale,_Tasmania "Evandale, Tasmania") a [penny-farthing](/wiki/Penny-farthing "Penny-farthing") championships are held.[190]

### Perception within Australia

Tasmania is perceived within Australia and internationally as an island with pristine wildlife, water and air. It is known for its [ecotourism](/wiki/Ecotourism "Ecotourism") for these reasons, and is considered an idyllic location for Australians considering a "tree-" or "sea-change", or are seeking [retirement](/wiki/Retirement "Retirement") because of Tasmania's [temperate environment](/wiki/Temperate_climate "Temperate climate") and friendly locals.[191] In other parts of the world, Tasmania is considered as the opposite side of the planet to most places, and supposedly home to mythically exotic animals, such as the [Tasmanian Devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil_\(Looney_Tunes\) "Tasmanian Devil \(Looney Tunes\)") as popularised by [Warner Brothers](/wiki/Warner_Brothers "Warner Brothers"). 

#### Stereotypes

Tasmania has a reputation within Australia that is often at odds with the reality of the state or may have only been true during colonial times and has only persevered on the Australian mainland as a [myth](/wiki/Myth "Myth"). Because of these [stereotypes](/wiki/Stereotypes "Stereotypes"), Tasmania is often referred to as the primary target (i.e., "butt") of mainland Australian jokes.[192] In more recent times, references to insults against Tasmania are more [sarcastic](/wiki/Sarcasm "Sarcasm") and jovial, but angst against the island still exists. The most commonly cited sarcastic comment is on the supposedly '[two-headed](/wiki/Polycephaly "Polycephaly")' Tasmanians, which originated due to some colonists developing [goitres](/wiki/Goitre "Goitre") from the low amount of [iodine](/wiki/Iodine "Iodine") in the island's soil.[193] But as Tasmania receives higher volumes of inter-state tourists, the perceptions are in the process of changing.[194]

The most prominent example of negative stereotype is of [inbreeding](/wiki/Inbreeding "Inbreeding") due to the relatively small size of Tasmania compared to the rest of Australia (though Tasmania is nearly as large as the Republic of Ireland in area, and more populous than [Iceland](/wiki/Iceland "Iceland")). This is untrue and if it had once been the case, it would have existed in the rest of colonial Australia as well, though Tasmania's penal establishments were some of the harshest in the entire colony and home to infamous bushrangers. This is a part of the also-receding global stereotype that all Australians are or were derived from criminals, even as most [convicts](/wiki/Convicts_in_Australia "Convicts in Australia") were transported for petty crimes. During this period of European settlement, Tasmania was the second centre of power (and a significant port of the [British Empire](/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire")) on the continent after [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales "New South Wales"), before being surpassed in the latter half of the 19th century by [Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\) "Victoria \(Australia\)") and regions sustained by [mining booms](/wiki/Australian_gold_rushes "Australian gold rushes") following the cessation of transportation in 1853.[195] A mentality developed in certain corners of Australia, and led to a general dislike of Tasmania amongst these people, even if the opinion-holder had never properly visited. It can rise to such an extent as to argue for the secession of Tasmania from the rest of Australia, in an effort to 'recover' Australia's reputation from Tasmania.[196]

## Transport

Further information: [Transport in Australia](/wiki/Transport_in_Australia "Transport in Australia")

[](/wiki/File:HobartAirportTerminal.jpg)[Hobart Airport](/wiki/Hobart_Airport "Hobart Airport")

### Air

Tasmania's main air carriers are [Jetstar](/wiki/Jetstar "Jetstar") and [Virgin Australia](/wiki/Virgin_Australia "Virgin Australia"); [Qantas](/wiki/Qantas "Qantas") and [QantasLink](/wiki/QantasLink "QantasLink"). These airlines fly direct routes to [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane_Airport "Brisbane Airport"), [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne_Airport "Melbourne Airport") and [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney_Airport "Sydney Airport"). Major airports include [Hobart Airport](/wiki/Hobart_Airport "Hobart Airport") and [Launceston Airport](/wiki/Launceston_Airport "Launceston Airport"); the smaller airports, [Burnie](/wiki/Burnie_Airport "Burnie Airport") (Wynyard) and [King Island](/wiki/King_Island_Airport "King Island Airport"), serviced by [Rex Airlines](/wiki/Rex_Airlines "Rex Airlines"); and [Devonport](/wiki/Devonport_Airport "Devonport Airport"), serviced by QantasLink; have services to Melbourne. Intra-Tasmanian air services are offered by [Par Avion](/wiki/Par_Avion_\(airline\) "Par Avion \(airline\)"). Until 2001 [Ansett Australia](/wiki/Ansett_Australia "Ansett Australia") operated majorly out of Tasmania to 12 destinations nationwide. Tourism-related air travel is also represented in Tasmania, such as in the [Par Avion](/wiki/Par_Avion_\(airline\) "Par Avion \(airline\)") route between [Cambridge Aerodrome](/wiki/Cambridge_Aerodrome "Cambridge Aerodrome") near Hobart to [Melaleuca](/wiki/Melaleuca,_Tasmania "Melaleuca, Tasmania") in [Southwest National Park](/wiki/Southwest_National_Park "Southwest National Park"). 

### Antarctica base

Tasmania – Hobart in particular – serves as Australia's chief sea link to Antarctica, with the [Australian Antarctic Division](/wiki/Australian_Antarctic_Division "Australian Antarctic Division") located in [Kingston](/wiki/Kingston,_Tasmania "Kingston, Tasmania"). Hobart is also the home port of the French ship _l'Astrolabe_ , which makes regular supply runs to the [French Southern Territories](/wiki/French_Southern_and_Antarctic_Lands "French Southern and Antarctic Lands") near and in Antarctica. 

### Road

[](/wiki/File:Bridgewater_Bridge,_Tasmania.jpg)Former [Bridgewater Bridge](/wiki/Bridgewater_Bridge "Bridgewater Bridge")

Within the state, the primary form of transport is by road. Since the 1980s, many of the [state's highways](/wiki/List_of_highways_in_Tasmania "List of highways in Tasmania") have undergone regular upgrades. These include the Hobart [Southern Outlet](/wiki/Southern_Outlet,_Hobart "Southern Outlet, Hobart"), Launceston Southern Outlet, [Bass Highway](/wiki/Bass_Highway_\(Tasmania\) "Bass Highway \(Tasmania\)") reconstruction, and the [Huon Highway](/wiki/Huon_Highway "Huon Highway"). Public transport is provided by [Metro Tasmania](/wiki/Metro_Tasmania "Metro Tasmania") bus services, regular taxis and Hobart only[197] UBER ride-share services within urban areas, with [Kinetic Tasmania](/wiki/Kinetic_Tasmania "Kinetic Tasmania"), [Tassielink Transit](/wiki/Tassielink_Transit "Tassielink Transit"), Manions Coaches, Area Connect and Calows Coaches providing bus service between population centres. 

[](/wiki/File:West_Coast_Wilderness_Railway_\(4754055363\).jpg)The [West Coast Wilderness Railway](/wiki/West_Coast_Wilderness_Railway "West Coast Wilderness Railway") runs from [Queenstown](/wiki/Queenstown,_Tasmania "Queenstown, Tasmania") to [Strahan](/wiki/Strahan,_Tasmania "Strahan, Tasmania") and is a reminder of the once elaborate network of rail in Tasmania.

### Rail

Main article: [Rail transport in Tasmania](/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Tasmania "Rail transport in Tasmania")

[Rail transport in Tasmania](/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Tasmania "Rail transport in Tasmania") consists of narrow-gauge lines to major population centres and to mining and forestry operations on the west coast and in the northwest. Services are operated by [TasRail](/wiki/TasRail "TasRail"). Regular [passenger train](/wiki/Passenger_train "Passenger train") services in the state ceased in 1978; the only scheduled trains are freight, but there are tourist and heritage trains on several closed alignments, for example the [West Coast Wilderness Railway](/wiki/West_Coast_Wilderness_Railway "West Coast Wilderness Railway") between [Queenstown](/wiki/Queenstown,_Tasmania "Queenstown, Tasmania") and [Strahan](/wiki/Strahan,_Tasmania "Strahan, Tasmania"). There is an [ongoing proposal](/wiki/Riverline_\(Tasmania\) "Riverline \(Tasmania\)") to reinstate commuter trains to Hobart. This idea however lacks political motivation due to ongoing scrutiny about the [Macquarie Point Stadium](/wiki/Macquarie_Point_Stadium "Macquarie Point Stadium"). The centre of TasRail's freight network is the state of the art [Brighton Transport Hub](/wiki/Brighton_Transport_Hub "Brighton Transport Hub") in Hobart's northern suburbs. Other major rail hubs are located at [Burnie](/wiki/Burnie "Burnie") and [East Tamar Junction](/w/index.php?title=East_Tamar_Junction&action=edit&redlink=1 "East Tamar Junction \(page does not exist\)"). Rail maintenance and loading facilities are located here. The busiest rail service in the state is TasRail's [cement](/wiki/Cement "Cement") trains running between [Devonport](/wiki/Devonport,_Tasmania "Devonport, Tasmania") and [Railton](/wiki/Railton,_Tasmania "Railton, Tasmania"). Mainline heritage rail services ceased in 2004 after [Pacific National](/wiki/Pacific_National "Pacific National") revoked their permission to run. 

### Shipping

See also: [Bass Strait ferries](/wiki/Bass_Strait_ferries "Bass Strait ferries")

[](/wiki/File:Spirit_of_Tasmania_Port_Melbourne.jpg)The _[Spirit of Tasmania](/wiki/Spirit_of_Tasmania "Spirit of Tasmania")_ links the island with mainland Australia.

There is a substantial amount of commercial and recreational shipping within Hobart's harbour, and the port hosts approximately 120 cruise ships during the warmer half of the year, and there are occasional visits from military vessels.[198]

Burnie and Devonport on the northwest coast host ports and several other coastal towns host either small fishing ports or substantial marinas. The domestic sea route between Tasmania and the mainland is serviced by [Bass Strait](/wiki/Bass_Strait "Bass Strait") passenger/vehicle ferries operated by the Tasmanian government-owned _[Spirit of Tasmania](/wiki/Spirit_of_Tasmania "Spirit of Tasmania")_. The state is also home to [Incat](/wiki/Incat "Incat"), a manufacturer of very high-speed aluminium catamarans that regularly broke records when they were first launched.
