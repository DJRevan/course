The **Hän** , **Han** or **Hwëch'in / Han Hwech’in** (meaning "People of the River, i.e. Yukon River", in English also **Hankutchin**) are a [First Nations](/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada "First Nations in Canada") people of Canada and an [Alaska Native](/wiki/Alaska_Native "Alaska Native") [Athabaskan](/wiki/Alaskan_Athabaskans "Alaskan Athabaskans") people of the United States; they are part of the [Athabaskan-speaking](/wiki/Athabaskan_languages "Athabaskan languages") ethnolinguistic group. Their traditional lands centered on a heavily [forested](/wiki/Boreal_forest "Boreal forest") area around the [Upper Yukon River (Chu Kon'Dëk)](/wiki/Yukon_River "Yukon River"), [Klondike River (Tr'on'Dëk)](/wiki/Klondike_River "Klondike River"), [Bonanza Creek (Gàh Dëk)](/wiki/Bonanza_Creek "Bonanza Creek") and [Sixtymile River (Khel Dëk)](/wiki/Sixtymile_River "Sixtymile River") and straddling what is now the [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska "Alaska")-[Yukon Territory](/wiki/Yukon_Territory "Yukon Territory") border. In later times, the Han population became centered in [Dawson City, Yukon](/wiki/Dawson_City,_Yukon "Dawson City, Yukon") and [Eagle, Alaska](/wiki/Eagle,_Alaska "Eagle, Alaska")[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]. 

[](/wiki/File:Chief_Isaac_of_the_Han_in_canoe_near_Dawson,_ca_1898_\(CURTIS_2000\).jpeg)Chief Isaac of the Han in a canoe, 1898

## Etymology

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=H%C3%A4n&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology")]

The name _Hän_ or _Han_ is a shortening of their own name as _Hwëch'in / Han Hwech’in_ , and of the [Gwich’in](/wiki/Gwich%E2%80%99in_language "Gwich’in language") word _Hangʷičʼin_ for the Hän, both literally meaning "People of the River, i.e. the Yukon River". This word has been spelled variously as _Hankutchin_ , _Han-Kootchin_ , _Hun-koo-chin_ , _Hong-Kutchin_ , _An Kutchin_ , _Han Kutchin_ , _Han-Kutchín_ , _Hăn-Kŭtchin´_ , _Hän Hwëch'in_ , and _Hungwitchin_. 

The Hän were often mistaken for just another [Gwich'in (Kutchin)](/wiki/Gwich%E2%80%99in "Gwich’in") band, especially as part of the Dagoo Gwich'in / Tukudh Gwich'in and Teetł'it Gwich'in / Teetl'it Zheh Gwich'in. The French traders called the Hän _Gens du fou_ , _Gens de Fou_ , _Gens de Foux_ , _Gens des Foux_ , or _Gens-de-fine_. The name _Gens de Foux_ (and variants) has also been used to refer to the [Northern Tutchone (Dan or Huč’an)](/wiki/Northern_Tutchone "Northern Tutchone"). The Hankutchin were then known as _Gens de Bois_ or _Gens des Bois,_ in association with their forested territory. 

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=H%C3%A4n&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

The Hän were one of the last Northern Athabascan groups to have contact with European peoples. In 1851 Robert Campbell from the [Hudson's Bay Company](/wiki/Hudson%27s_Bay_Company "Hudson's Bay Company") became the first white man known to have entered Han territory, when he traveled from [Fort Selkirk](/wiki/Fort_Selkirk "Fort Selkirk") to [Fort Yukon](/wiki/Fort_Yukon "Fort Yukon"). It was not until 1873 and 1874 (after the [United States](/wiki/United_States "United States") purchase of [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska "Alaska")), that two trading posts were set up. One was established by Moses Mercier, a former employee of the [Hudson's Bay Company](/wiki/Hudson%27s_Bay_Company "Hudson's Bay Company"), in [Belle Isle](/wiki/Eagle,_Alaska "Eagle, Alaska") across the [Eagle River](/wiki/Eagle_River_\(Alaska\) "Eagle River \(Alaska\)"). The other, [Fort Reliance](/wiki/Fort_Reliance "Fort Reliance"), was established on the [Yukon](/wiki/Yukon "Yukon"), just below the mouth of the [Klondike River](/wiki/Klondike_River "Klondike River"), near [Dawson](/wiki/Dawson_City,_Yukon "Dawson City, Yukon"), by two [Alaska Commercial Company](/wiki/Alaska_Commercial_Company "Alaska Commercial Company") traders, Leroy N. McQuesten and Frank Bonifield. Gradually [trading with whites](/wiki/Native_American_trade "Native American trade") resulted in the Han shifting from their traditional fishing-hunting economy to a fur-trapping economy, as they grew increasingly reliant on such European goods as guns, clothing, and canvas from 1887 to 1895. 

[](/wiki/File:Moosehide_village.jpg)Moosehide village near [Dawson City](/wiki/Dawson_City "Dawson City") in 1900

Bishop William Bompas established the first [Anglican Church](/wiki/Anglican_Church "Anglican Church") mission in Hän territory, and gradually the people shifted away from traditional religion. They also combined it with Christianity in a syncretic fashion. The Han suffered high mortality during several epidemics of new infectious diseases, to which they had no immunity.

### Food

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=H%C3%A4n&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Food")]

Historically, fish, especially salmon, comprised the main part of the Hän diet. [King salmon](/wiki/King_salmon "King salmon") was caught along the Yukon River in June and [chum salmon](/wiki/Chum_salmon "Chum salmon") in August. Fishing tools included [weirs](/wiki/Fishing_weir "Fishing weir"), [traps](/wiki/Fishing_trap "Fishing trap"), [gill nets](/wiki/Gill_net "Gill net"), [dip nets](/wiki/Hand_net "Hand net"), [spears](/wiki/Spearfishing "Spearfishing"), and [harpoons](/wiki/Harpoon "Harpoon"). Salmon was dried and stored for winter consumption. 

Between the [salmon runs](/wiki/Salmon_run "Salmon run") from June–September, the river camps were abandoned. The Han men sought other fish, moose, caribou, birds, bears, and small game. Men hunted game (once after the salmon run and later for caribou in February and March) while women fished (for fish other than salmon). The women traditionally cooked by boiling food with water heated by stones that been placed in a fire and then dropped in woven spruce-root baskets. 

### Housing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=H%C3%A4n&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Housing")]

A square half-recessed house was made of wooden poles and moss insulation (called a _moss house_). This served as the main type of housing. 

The people erected temporary domed houses made of skin stretched over tied branches when they were traveling. 

## Language

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=H%C3%A4n&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Language")]

The [Hän language](/wiki/H%C3%A4n_language "Hän language") is most similar to [Gwich’in](/wiki/Gwich%E2%80%99in_language "Gwich’in language") (Kutchin). It is more distantly related to [Upper Tanana](/wiki/Upper_Tanana "Upper Tanana") and [Northern Tutchone](/wiki/Northern_Tutchone "Northern Tutchone"). The language was used as a [lingua franca](/wiki/Lingua_franca "Lingua franca") by [Gwich’in](/wiki/Gwich%E2%80%99in "Gwich’in"), [Tutchone](/wiki/Northern_Tutchone "Northern Tutchone"), [Tagish](/wiki/Tagish "Tagish"), and Upper Tanana peoples toward the end of the 19th century during the [Gold Rush in the Yukon](/wiki/Klondike_Gold_Rush "Klondike Gold Rush"). The language is now the most [endangered language](/wiki/Endangered_language "Endangered language") of Alaska, with only a few speakers (all are over 60 years of age). The language may have ancient, early [Holocene](/wiki/Holocene "Holocene") origins in the region.[2]
