The [opening ceremony](/wiki/Olympic_opening_ceremony "Olympic opening ceremony") of the [2012 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics "2012 Summer Olympics") took place on the evening of Friday 27 July 2012 in the [Olympic Stadium](/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(London\) "Olympic Stadium \(London\)"), [London](/wiki/London "London"), during which the Games were formally opened by Queen [Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II"). As mandated by the [Olympic Charter](/wiki/Olympic_Charter "Olympic Charter"), the proceedings combined the ceremonial opening of this international sporting event (including welcoming speeches, hoisting of the flags and the parade of athletes) with an artistic spectacle to showcase the host nation's culture. The spectacle was entitled _**Isles of Wonder**_[1] and directed by the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award")-winning British film director [Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle"), with [Paulette Randall](/wiki/Paulette_Randall "Paulette Randall") as associate director. 

Prior to London 2012, there had been considerable apprehension about [Britain](/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom")'s ability to stage an opening ceremony that could reach the standard set at the [Beijing](/wiki/Beijing "Beijing") [Summer Games of 2008](/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics "2008 Summer Olympics").[2][3][4] The [2008 ceremony](/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2008 Summer Olympics opening ceremony") had been noted for its scale, extravagance and expense, hailed as the "greatest ever",[5] and had cost £65m. In contrast, London spent an estimated £27m (out of £80m budgeted for its four ceremonies), which was nevertheless about twice the original budget.[6] Nonetheless, the London opening ceremony was immediately seen as a tremendous success, widely praised as a "masterpiece" and "a love letter to Britain".[7][8][9]

The ceremony began at 21:00 [BST](/wiki/British_Summer_Time "British Summer Time") and lasted almost four hours.[10] It was watched by an estimated worldwide television audience of 900 million, falling short of the [IOC](/wiki/International_Olympic_Committee "International Olympic Committee")'s 1.5 billion viewership estimate for the 2008 ceremony,[11][12] but becoming the most viewed in the UK and US.[13][14] The content had largely been kept secret before the performance, despite involving thousands of volunteers and two public rehearsals. The principal sections of the artistic display represented Britain's [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"), [National Health Service](/wiki/National_Health_Service "National Health Service"), literary heritage, popular music and [culture](/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom "Culture of the United Kingdom"), and were noted for their vibrant [storytelling](/wiki/Storytelling "Storytelling") and use of music. Two shorter sections drew particular comment, involving a filmed [cameo appearance](/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") of [the Queen](/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") with [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond "James Bond") as her escort, and a live performance by the [London Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra "London Symphony Orchestra") joined by comedian [Rowan Atkinson](/wiki/Rowan_Atkinson "Rowan Atkinson"). These were widely ascribed to [Britain's sense of humour](/wiki/British_humour "British humour").[15] The ceremony featured children and young people in most of its segments, reflecting the "inspire a generation" aspiration of [London's original bid for the Games](/wiki/London_bid_for_the_2012_Summer_Olympics "London bid for the 2012 Summer Olympics").[16]

The BBC released footage of the entire opening ceremony on 29 October 2012, edited by Danny Boyle and with background extras, along with more than seven hours of sporting highlights and the complete closing ceremony.[17]

## Preparations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Preparations")]

The London Organising Committee of the Olympic and Paralympic Games ([LOCOG](/wiki/LOCOG "LOCOG")) approached [Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle") to be the director of the ceremony in June 2010, and he immediately accepted.[18][19] Boyle explained that there had been four things that made him take the job: he was a big Olympics fan, he lived a mile from the Stadium and so felt invested in the area, his late father's birthday was on the ceremony's date, and he felt his "Oscar clout" would enable him to push through what he wanted to do.[20] He said it "felt weirdly more like a ... civic or national responsibility" to take the job.[21]

[](/wiki/File:Danny_talks_about_tonight%27s_ceremony_\(cropped\).jpg)[Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle"), the director of the opening ceremony

Boyle acknowledged that the extravagance of the 2008 opening ceremony was an impossible act to follow — "you can't get bigger than Beijing" — and that this realisation had in fact liberated his team creatively. He said "..obviously I'm not going to try and build on Beijing, because how could you? We can't, and you wouldn't want to, so we're going back to the beginning. We're going to try and give the impression that we're rethinking and restarting, because they've (_opening ceremonies_) escalated since Los Angeles in 1984. They've tried to top themselves each time and you can't do that after Beijing."[22][23] Beijing's budget had been £65m, whereas London's final budget was £27m, which was twice the original provision.[6][24] The London stadium had the same number of seats as [Beijing's](/wiki/Beijing_National_Stadium "Beijing National Stadium"), but was half the size; this intimacy of scale meant that Boyle felt he could achieve something personal and connecting.[22][25]

The different sections of the ceremony were designed to reflect aspects of British history and culture, with the title _Isles of Wonder_ partly inspired by [Shakespeare](/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare")'s play _[The Tempest](/wiki/The_Tempest "The Tempest")_ (particularly Caliban's 'Be not afeard' speech[26]), and partly by the [G. K. Chesterton](/wiki/G._K._Chesterton "G. K. Chesterton") aphorism: "The world shall perish not for lack of wonders, but for lack of wonder."[25][27]

In July 2010, Boyle started brainstorming ideas with designer [Mark Tildesley](/wiki/Mark_Tildesley_\(production_designer\) "Mark Tildesley \(production designer\)"), writer [Frank Cottrell-Boyce](/wiki/Frank_Cottrell-Boyce "Frank Cottrell-Boyce") and costume designer [Suttirat Anne Larlarb](/wiki/Suttirat_Anne_Larlarb "Suttirat Anne Larlarb"). They considered "what was essentially British", with the non-British Larlarb able to offer a view of what the world thought Britain meant. Cottrell-Boyce had given Boyle a copy of _[Pandaemonium](/wiki/Pandaemonium_\(history_book\) "Pandaemonium \(history book\)")_ , (named after the capital of Hell in _[Paradise Lost](/wiki/Paradise_Lost "Paradise Lost")_) by [Humphrey Jennings](/wiki/Humphrey_Jennings "Humphrey Jennings"), which collated contemporary reports from the [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution").[18][27][28] It had become traditional during the opening ceremony to "produce" the [Olympic rings](/wiki/Olympic_symbols#Rings "Olympic symbols") in a spectacular manner. Cottrell-Boyce commented: "Danny had a very clear idea that in the first 15 minutes you had to have a great, startling image that could go around the world; it had to climax with something that made people go 'Oh my God!'" Boyle decided that "the journey from the pastoral to the industrial, ending with the forging of the Olympic rings" would be that image.[18]

The ten distinct chapters on which the team started work were gradually compressed into three principal movements: the violent transition from "Green and Pleasant Land" to the "Pandemonium" of industrial revolution, a salute to the NHS and children's literature, and a celebration of pop culture, technology and the digital revolution.[18]

> "At some point in their histories, most nations experience a revolution that changes everything about them. The United Kingdom had a revolution that changed the whole of human existence. 
> 
> In 1709 [Abraham Darby](/wiki/Abraham_Darby_I "Abraham Darby I") smelted iron in a blast furnace, using [coke](/wiki/Coke_\(fuel\) "Coke \(fuel\)"). And so began the [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution"). Out of [Abraham's Shropshire furnace](/wiki/Coalbrookdale "Coalbrookdale") flowed molten metal. Out of his genius flowed the mills, looms, engines, weapons, railways, ships, cities, conflicts and prosperity that built the world we live in. 
> 
> In November 1990 another Briton sparked another revolution – equally far-reaching – a revolution we're still experiencing. The digital revolution was sparked by [Tim Berners-Lee](/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee")'s amazing gift to the world – the [World Wide Web](/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"). This, he said, is for everyone. 
> 
> We welcome you to an Olympic Opening Ceremony for everyone. A ceremony that celebrates the creativity, eccentricity, daring and openness of the British genius by harnessing the genius, creativity, eccentricity, daring and openness of modern London. 
> 
> You'll hear the words of our great poets – [Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare"), [Blake](/wiki/William_Blake "William Blake") and [Milton](/wiki/John_Milton "John Milton"). You'll hear the glorious noise of our unrivalled pop culture. You'll see characters from our great children's literature – [Peter Pan](/wiki/Peter_Pan_\(character\) "Peter Pan \(character\)") and [Captain Hook](/wiki/Captain_Hook "Captain Hook"), [Mary Poppins](/wiki/Mary_Poppins_\(character\) "Mary Poppins \(character\)"), [Voldemort](/wiki/Voldemort "Voldemort"), [Cruella de Vil](/wiki/Cruella_de_Vil "Cruella de Vil"). You'll see ordinary families and extraordinary athletes. Dancing nurses, singing children and amazing special effects. 
> 
> But we hope, too, that through all the noise and excitement that you will glimpse a single golden thread of purpose – the idea of [Jerusalem](/wiki/Jerusalem_\(hymn\) "Jerusalem \(hymn\)") – of a better world, the world of real freedom and true equality, a world that can be built through the prosperity of industry, through the caring nation that built the welfare state, through the joyous energy of popular culture, through the dream of universal communication. A belief that we can build Jerusalem. And that it will be for everyone." 

— Danny Boyle, in the ceremony programme.[29]

When Boyle returned to work on the ceremony in the spring of 2011 he asked [Rick Smith](/wiki/Rick_Smith_\(Underworld\) "Rick Smith \(Underworld\)") of [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_\(band\) "Underworld \(band\)"), with whom he had worked on several film projects as well as his [theatrical production](/wiki/Frankenstein_\(2011_play\) "Frankenstein \(2011 play\)") of [Mary Shelley](/wiki/Mary_Shelley "Mary Shelley")'s _[Frankenstein](/wiki/Frankenstein "Frankenstein")_ , to be the musical director.[18][30] At the same time the team moved to the [Three Mills](/wiki/Three_Mills "Three Mills") studio complex in east London, where a 4x4 metre scale model of the stadium was built. For security reasons, a single CGI-assisted version of the ceremony was kept on editor Sascha Dhillon's laptop; anyone needing it had to come to the studio.[18]

The cast included professional performers and 7,500 volunteers.[31] Boyle considered the volunteers to be "the most valuable commodity of all". In November 2011 they auditioned at Three Mills, and rehearsals began in earnest in spring 2012 at an open-air site at [Dagenham](/wiki/Dagenham "Dagenham") (the abandoned [Ford](/wiki/Ford_Dagenham "Ford Dagenham") plant), often in foul weather. Although key contributors had to sign non-disclosure agreements and some elements were codenamed, Boyle placed immense trust in the volunteers by asking them simply to "save the surprise" and not leak any information.[18][21] Further volunteers were recruited to help with security and marshalling, and to support the technical crew.[23][32] Three weeks before the ceremony, [Mark Rylance](/wiki/Mark_Rylance "Mark Rylance"), who was to have taken a leading part, pulled out after a family bereavement and was replaced by [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh "Kenneth Branagh").[33][34]

The [Olympic Bell](/wiki/Olympic_Bell "Olympic Bell"), the largest harmonically tuned bell in the world,[35] weighing 23 tonnes, had been cast in brass under the direction of the [Whitechapel Bell Foundry](/wiki/Whitechapel_Bell_Foundry "Whitechapel Bell Foundry") by [Royal Eijsbouts](/wiki/Royal_Eijsbouts "Royal Eijsbouts") of the Netherlands, and hung in the Stadium.[36] It was inscribed with a line from [Caliban](/wiki/Caliban "Caliban")'s speech in _The Tempest_ : "Be not afeard, the isle is full of noises".[37]

Boyle gave significant emphasis to the London 2012 theme "inspire a generation" and devised a programme relying heavily on children and young people, and built around themes that would relate to the young. Twenty-five schools in the six original East London host boroughs were used to recruit child volunteers for the performance, and 170 [sixth formers](/wiki/Sixth_form "Sixth form") (16–18-year-olds), between them speaking more than 50 languages, were recruited from their colleges. 

On 12 June 2012, at a press conference, Boyle had promised a huge set of rural [Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain"), which was to include a [village cricket](/wiki/Village_cricket "Village cricket") team, farm animals, a model of [Glastonbury Tor](/wiki/Glastonbury_Tor "Glastonbury Tor"), as well as a [maypole](/wiki/Maypole "Maypole") and a rain-producing cloud. His intention was to represent the rural and urban [landscape](/wiki/Landscape "Landscape") of Britain. The design was to include a mosh pit at each end of the set, one with people celebrating a [rock festival](/wiki/Rock_festival "Rock festival") and the other the [Last Night of the Proms](/wiki/The_Proms "The Proms"). 

Boyle promised a ceremony with which everyone would feel involved; he said, "I hope it will reveal how peculiar and contrary we are – and how there's also, I hope, a warmth about us." Some of the set was designed with real grass turf and soil.[38][39] The use of animals (40 sheep, 12 horses, 3 cows, 2 goats, 10 chickens, 10 ducks, 9 geese and 3 sheep dogs, looked after by 34 animal handlers) drew some criticism from [People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals (PETA)](/wiki/People_for_the_Ethical_Treatment_of_Animals "People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals"). Boyle, who was being advised by the [RSPCA](/wiki/RSPCA "RSPCA"), assured PETA that the animals would be well cared for.[40] After the press conference, much commentary in the UK Press was negative and attracted "hundreds of comments online completely supporting...the view that the opening ceremony would be a disaster."[41]

The overwhelming majority of the music used was British. The team worked next door to the office of the musical director for the closing ceremony, [David Arnold](/wiki/David_Arnold "David Arnold"), and so hearing each other's music there was a scramble to claim a particular song first.[42] [A.R. Rahman](/wiki/A.R._Rahman "A.R. Rahman"), who worked with Boyle on _[Slumdog Millionaire](/wiki/Slumdog_Millionaire "Slumdog Millionaire")_ and _[127 Hours](/wiki/127_Hours "127 Hours")_ , composed a [Punjabi](/wiki/Punjabi_language "Punjabi language") song "Nimma Nimma" to showcase [Indian influence in the UK](/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations "India–United Kingdom relations"), according to Boyle's wishes.[43] More Indian music was also scheduled for inclusion in the medley including a [Tamil](/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") song from the 1989 [Bollywood](/wiki/Hindi_cinema "Hindi cinema") film [Ram Lakhan](/wiki/Ram_Lakhan "Ram Lakhan") titled "Naanthaan Ungappanda".[44][45] [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney "Paul McCartney") was to be the ceremony's closing act.[46]

[Sebastian Coe](/wiki/Sebastian_Coe,_Baron_Coe "Sebastian Coe, Baron Coe") was instrumental in asking the Queen to take part, responding positively when Boyle first pitched the _Happy and Glorious_ film sequence featuring the Queen. Boyle suggested that the Queen be played either by a lookalike, or by a world-class actress such as [Helen Mirren](/wiki/Helen_Mirren "Helen Mirren"), in a location to double as Buckingham Palace. Coe asked [Princess Anne](/wiki/Princess_Anne "Princess Anne"), a [British member of the IOC](/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee "List of members of the International Olympic Committee") and [LOCOG](/wiki/LOCOG "LOCOG"), what she thought, and she told him to ask the Queen. Coe presented the idea to the Queen's Deputy Private Secretary.[47] Boyle was surprised to hear that the Queen would be happy to play herself, and wanted a speaking part.[21] Filming took place in late March 2012, and _Happy and Glorious_ was produced by the BBC, as was the opening film sequence _Journey along the Thames_.[48]

Changes were still being made to the programme in the final days before the ceremony: a [BMX](/wiki/BMX "BMX") bike section was dropped due to time constraints, and the "Pandemonium" and "Thanks..Tim" sections were edited down.[18] In 2016 Boyle recounted how he had come under pressure from [Jeremy Hunt](/wiki/Jeremy_Hunt "Jeremy Hunt"), then the [Olympics and Culture Secretary](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Digital,_Culture,_Media_and_Sport "Secretary of State for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport"), to cut back the NHS section, which he had saved only by threatening to resign and take the volunteers with him.[49]

Two full dress and technical rehearsals took place in the Olympic stadium, on 23 and 25 July, in front of an audience of 60,000 comprising volunteers, cast members' families, competition winners, and others connected to the Games. Boyle asked them not to "spoil the surprise" by using the hashtag #savethesurprise on social media, keeping the performance a secret for the hundreds of millions who would watch on the Friday night.[27][50]

### International dignitaries

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: International dignitaries")]

Three multilateral leaders, more than ninety-five heads of state and government and representatives from five organisations and one hundred and twenty countries attended.[51][52]

### Prologue

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Prologue")]

[](/wiki/File:2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony_\(18\).jpg)Scene before the ceremony commenced, representing rural Britain

At exactly 20:12 [BST](/wiki/British_Summer_Time "British Summer Time"), the [Red Arrows](/wiki/Red_Arrows "Red Arrows") performed a flypast over the [Olympic Stadium](/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(London\) "Olympic Stadium \(London\)") and then over the concert in [Hyde Park](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London "Hyde Park, London").[53] This concert featured artists selected to represent the four nations of the United Kingdom: [Duran Duran](/wiki/Duran_Duran "Duran Duran"), [Stereophonics](/wiki/Stereophonics "Stereophonics"), [Snow Patrol](/wiki/Snow_Patrol "Snow Patrol") and [Paolo Nutini](/wiki/Paolo_Nutini "Paolo Nutini").[54]

At the beginning the stadium contained a rural scene including the model of [Glastonbury Tor](/wiki/Glastonbury_Tor "Glastonbury Tor"), a model village and a water wheel, replete with live animals (removed shortly before the ceremony began), and actors portraying working villagers, football and cricket players. 

[Frank Turner](/wiki/Frank_Turner "Frank Turner") performed three songs ("Sailor's Boots", "Wessex Boy" and "[I Still Believe](/wiki/I_Still_Believe_\(Frank_Turner_song\) "I Still Believe \(Frank Turner song\)")") on the model of Glastonbury Tor, joined by [Emily Barker](/wiki/Emily_Barker "Emily Barker"), [Ben Marwood](/wiki/Ben_Marwood "Ben Marwood") and Jim Lockey, as well as his regular backing band the Sleeping Souls.[55] [LSO](/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra "London Symphony Orchestra") On Track (an orchestra of 80 young musicians from 10 East London boroughs together with 20 LSO members) then performed [Edward Elgar](/wiki/Edward_Elgar "Edward Elgar")'s "[Nimrod](/wiki/Enigma_Variations#Variation_IX_\(Adagio\)_"Nimrod" "Enigma Variations")" from the _[Enigma Variations](/wiki/Enigma_Variations "Enigma Variations")_ , accompanied by extracts from the [BBC Radio](/wiki/BBC_Radio "BBC Radio") _[Shipping Forecast](/wiki/Shipping_Forecast "Shipping Forecast")_ , and maritime images on the big screens, while the audience held up blue sheeting to simulate the sight of the ocean.[56] This performance celebrated Britain's maritime heritage and geographical insularity.[57]

### Countdown (21:00–21:04 BST)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Countdown \(21:00–21:04 BST\)")]

The ceremony began at 21:00 after a one-minute "60 to 1" countdown film made up of shots of numbers, such as those on house doors, street nameplates, [London buses](/wiki/London_buses "London buses"), station platforms and market labels. The number 39 is seen at the foot of a flight of steps, a reference to [John Buchan](/wiki/John_Buchan "John Buchan")'s novel _[The Thirty-Nine Steps](/wiki/The_Thirty-Nine_Steps "The Thirty-Nine Steps")_ , while the 10 is the door number from [10 Downing Street](/wiki/10_Downing_Street "10 Downing Street"). 

[](/wiki/File:Bradley_Wiggins_2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony.jpg)The [2012 Tour de France](/wiki/2012_Tour_de_France "2012 Tour de France") winner [Bradley Wiggins](/wiki/Bradley_Wiggins "Bradley Wiggins") opening the ceremony

A two-minute film, _Journey along the Thames_ , directed by Boyle and produced by the [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC"), opened the ceremony.[58] To the sound of "Surf Solar" by [Fuck Buttons](/wiki/Fuck_Buttons "Fuck Buttons"), it followed the [River Thames](/wiki/River_Thames "River Thames") from [its source](/wiki/Thames_Head "Thames Head") to the heart of London, juxtaposing images of contemporary British life with pastoral shots and flashes of scenes from the stadium. The characters Ratty, Mole and Toad from _[The Wind in the Willows](/wiki/The_Wind_in_the_Willows "The Wind in the Willows")_ were briefly seen, as was a "[Monty Python](/wiki/Monty_Python "Monty Python") hand" pointing towards London on umbrellas, an [InterCity 125](/wiki/InterCity_125 "InterCity 125") train passing the Olympic rings as [crop circles](/wiki/Crop_circle "Crop circle") in a field, and a rowing race (to the tune of the "[Eton Boating Song](/wiki/Eton_Boating_Song "Eton Boating Song")"). 

At [Battersea Power Station](/wiki/Battersea_Power_Station "Battersea Power Station") a [Pink Floyd pig](/wiki/Pink_Floyd_pigs "Pink Floyd pigs") was flying between the towers; the clock sound from another Pink Floyd song "[Time](/wiki/Time_\(Pink_Floyd_song\) "Time \(Pink Floyd song\)")" was heard passing [Big Ben](/wiki/Big_Ben#Tower "Big Ben"). The soundtrack included clips from the theme tune of _[The South Bank Show](/wiki/The_South_Bank_Show "The South Bank Show")_ , "[Caprice No. 24](/wiki/Caprice_No._24_\(Paganini\) "Caprice No. 24 \(Paganini\)")" (as composed by [Andrew Lloyd Webber](/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber "Andrew Lloyd Webber") in his _[Variations](/wiki/Variations_\(Andrew_Lloyd_Webber_album\) "Variations \(Andrew Lloyd Webber album\)")_ fusion album), and the [Sex Pistols](/wiki/Sex_Pistols "Sex Pistols")' "[God Save the Queen](/wiki/God_Save_the_Queen_\(Sex_Pistols_song\) "God Save the Queen \(Sex Pistols song\)")" as the film followed the route of the band's cruise down the River Thames during the [Silver Jubilee](/wiki/Silver_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II "Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II").[59][60]

After lifting to an aerial view of [East London](/wiki/East_London "East London") mirroring the title sequence of the BBC soap opera _[EastEnders](/wiki/EastEnders "EastEnders")_ , to the sound of the drum beats from [the closing theme](/wiki/EastEnders_theme_tune "EastEnders theme tune"), the film flashed down through the [Thames Barrier](/wiki/Thames_Barrier "Thames Barrier"). The view progressed into Bow Creek, and then below surface through a [London Underground](/wiki/London_Underground "London Underground") train and station, including historic footage of [Isambard Kingdom Brunel](/wiki/Isambard_Kingdom_Brunel "Isambard Kingdom Brunel")'s [Thames Tunnel](/wiki/Thames_Tunnel "Thames Tunnel"), and through the [Rotherhithe Tunnel](/wiki/Rotherhithe_Tunnel "Rotherhithe Tunnel"), accompanied by clips of an orchestral rendition of Elgar's [Pomp and Circumstance Marches](/wiki/Pomp_and_Circumstance_Marches "Pomp and Circumstance Marches"), "[London Calling](/wiki/London_Calling_\(song\) "London Calling \(song\)")" by [The Clash](/wiki/The_Clash "The Clash"), the [London Underground](/wiki/London_Underground "London Underground")'s [Mind the Gap](/wiki/Mind_the_Gap "Mind the Gap") public address warning, and "[Smile](/wiki/Smile_\(Lily_Allen_song\) "Smile \(Lily Allen song\)")" by [Lily Allen](/wiki/Lily_Allen "Lily Allen"). It then switched to a sequence filmed outside the stadium shortly before the ceremony, superimposed with posters from previous Summer Olympics (all of them except [1900 Paris](/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics "1900 Summer Olympics"), [1936 Berlin](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics "1936 Summer Olympics"), [1984 Los Angeles](/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics "1984 Summer Olympics"), and [1996 Atlanta](/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics "1996 Summer Olympics")), to a recording of "[Map of the Problematique](/wiki/Map_of_the_Problematique "Map of the Problematique")" by [Muse](/wiki/Muse_\(band\) "Muse \(band\)"). This ended with a live shot of three cast members holding the posters for the 2012 competition. 

There was then a ten-second countdown in the stadium, with children holding clusters of balloons that burst simultaneously, with the audience shouting out the numbers. [Bradley Wiggins](/wiki/Bradley_Wiggins "Bradley Wiggins"), who had won the [Tour de France](/wiki/2012_Tour_de_France "2012 Tour de France") five days earlier, opened the ceremony by ringing the [Olympic Bell](/wiki/Olympic_Bell "Olympic Bell") that hung at one end of the stadium. Four [upper-atmosphere balloons](/wiki/Weather_balloon "Weather balloon") were released, each expected to carry a set of Olympic rings and a camera up to the mid-[stratosphere](/wiki/Stratosphere "Stratosphere"). 

### Green and Pleasant Land (21:04–21:09)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Green and Pleasant Land \(21:04–21:09\)")]

"Be not afeard" speech

> Be not afeard: the isle is full of noises,  
>  Sounds, and sweet airs, that give delight, and hurt not.  
>  Sometimes a thousand twangling instruments  
>  Will hum about mine ears; and sometimes voices,  
>  That, if I then had wak'd after long sleep,  
>  Will make me sleep again: and then, in dreaming,  
>  The clouds methought would open and show riches  
>  Ready to drop upon me; that, when I wak'd,  
>  I cried to dream again. 

— [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare "William Shakespeare"), _[The Tempest](/wiki/The_Tempest "The Tempest")_ , [Act 3, Scene II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Tempest#SCENE_II._Another_part_of_the_island_2 "wikisource:The Tempest")

The depiction of rural life already in the arena was billed as "a reminder and a promise of a once and future better life". Youth choirs began [a cappella](/wiki/A_cappella "A cappella") performances of the informal anthems of the four nations of the UK: "[Jerusalem](/wiki/And_did_those_feet_in_ancient_time "And did those feet in ancient time")" (for [England](/wiki/England "England"), sung by a live choir in the stadium), "[Danny Boy](/wiki/Danny_Boy "Danny Boy")" (from the [Giant's Causeway](/wiki/Giant%27s_Causeway "Giant's Causeway") in [Northern Ireland](/wiki/Northern_Ireland "Northern Ireland")), "[Flower of Scotland](/wiki/Flower_of_Scotland "Flower of Scotland")" (from [Edinburgh Castle](/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle "Edinburgh Castle") in [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland "Scotland")), and "[Bread of Heaven](/wiki/Cwm_Rhondda "Cwm Rhondda")" (from [Rhossili Beach](/wiki/Rhossili "Rhossili") in [Wales](/wiki/Wales "Wales")).[61] These were inter-cut with footage of notable [Rugby Union Home Nations](/wiki/Rugby_Union "Rugby Union")' [tries](/wiki/Try_\(rugby\) "Try \(rugby\)"), England's winning [drop goal](/wiki/Drop_goal "Drop goal") from the [2003 Rugby World Cup Final](/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup_Final "2003 Rugby World Cup Final"), and live shots from the stadium. 

As the last choir performance concluded, vintage [London General Omnibus Company](/wiki/London_General_Omnibus_Company "London General Omnibus Company") [stagecoaches](/wiki/Stagecoach "Stagecoach") entered, carrying businessmen and early [industrialists](/wiki/Business_magnate "Business magnate") in [Victorian](/wiki/Victorian_era "Victorian era") [dress](/wiki/Morning_dress "Morning dress") and [top hats](/wiki/Top_hat "Top hat"), led by [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh "Kenneth Branagh") as [Isambard Kingdom Brunel](/wiki/Isambard_Kingdom_Brunel "Isambard Kingdom Brunel"). The 50 men stepped down from the carriages and [surveyed the land](/wiki/Surveying "Surveying") approvingly. After walking onto the Glastonbury Tor, Brunel delivered [Caliban](/wiki/Caliban "Caliban")'s "Be not afeard" speech, reflecting Boyle's introduction to the ceremony in the programme[62] and signifying an aspiration of new industry or a new era in Britain. This anticipated the next section of the ceremony. 

### Pandemonium (21:09–21:25)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Pandemonium \(21:09–21:25\)")]

This section encapsulated British economic and social development from rural economy through the [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution "Industrial Revolution") to the 1960s. 

[](/wiki/File:2012_Olympics_opening_ceremony,_Industrial_Revolution_scene.jpg)Scene representing [Industrial Britain](/wiki/Life_in_Great_Britain_during_the_Industrial_Revolution "Life in Great Britain during the Industrial Revolution"). Rehearsal 23 July 2012 before the ceremony four days later.

Proceedings were suddenly interrupted by a loud shout, recorded by volunteers during the rehearsals, followed by drumming (the pre-recorded drumming amplified by 965 cast members drumming on inverted household buckets and bins),[63] led by [Evelyn Glennie](/wiki/Evelyn_Glennie "Evelyn Glennie").[64] The three-tonne oak tree on top of the Glastonbury Tor lifted, and industrial workers emerged from both the Tor's brightly lit interior and the entrances to the stadium, to swell the cast to a total of 2,500 volunteers.[18] So began what Boyle had called the "biggest scene change in theatre history" and something he had been advised against attempting.[65] [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_\(band\) "Underworld \(band\)")'s "[And I Will Kiss](/wiki/And_I_Will_Kiss "And I Will Kiss")" began to play, as the cast rolled away the grass and other rural props. 

Seven smoking [chimney stacks](/wiki/Chimney_stacks "Chimney stacks") with accompanying [steeplejacks](/wiki/Steeplejack "Steeplejack") rose from the ground, along with other industrial machinery: five [beam engines](/wiki/Beam_engine "Beam engine"), six looms, a crucible and a water wheel (one of the few items left from the rural scene). Boyle said that this section celebrated the "tremendous potential" afforded by the advancements of the Victorian era.[61] It also included a [minute's silence](/wiki/Minute%27s_silence "Minute's silence") in remembrance of the loss of life of both [world wars](/wiki/World_war "World war"), featuring British "[Tommies](/wiki/Tommy_Atkins "Tommy Atkins")" and shots of [poppies](/wiki/Remembrance_poppy "Remembrance poppy"), during which the names of the [Accrington Pals](/wiki/Accrington_Pals "Accrington Pals") were shown on the stadium screens. Unprompted, members of the audience stood in respect during this segment.[66][67]

Volunteers paraded around the stadium representing some of the groups that had changed the face of Britain: the [woman's suffrage](/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_the_United_Kingdom "Women's suffrage in the United Kingdom") movement, the [Jarrow Crusade](/wiki/Jarrow_Crusade "Jarrow Crusade"), the first [Caribbean](/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean") immigrants arriving in 1948 on board the _[Empire Windrush](/wiki/HMT_Empire_Windrush#West_Indian_immigrants "HMT Empire Windrush")_ , a 1970s DJ float, the Nostalgia Steel Band, and [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") as they appeared on the cover of _[Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band](/wiki/Sgt._Pepper%27s_Lonely_Hearts_Club_Band "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band")_. Also included were real-life [Chelsea Pensioners](/wiki/Chelsea_Pensioners "Chelsea Pensioners"), the [Grimethorpe Colliery Band](/wiki/Grimethorpe_Colliery_Band "Grimethorpe Colliery Band"), and a group of [Pearly Kings and Queens](/wiki/Pearly_Kings_and_Queens "Pearly Kings and Queens"). 

[](/wiki/File:KOCIS_Korea_London_Olympics_TeamKorea_09_\(7683501986\).jpg)The Olympic rings

Workers began [casting](/wiki/Casting "Casting") an iron ring. As the noise level and tension built, driven by the relentless rhythm of the music and the drumming, participants mimed repetitive mechanical movements associated with industrial processes such as [weaving](/wiki/Loom "Loom"). Four glowing orange rings gradually began to be carried high above the stadium toward its centre on overhead wires, and then the ring seemingly being cast and forged in the arena began to rise. The five rings converged, still glowing and accompanied by steam and firework effects to give the impression that they were of hot metal. When the five rings formed [the Olympic symbol](/wiki/Olympic_symbols#Rings "Olympic symbols") above the stadium, they ignited and rained fire in silver and gold. The image of the Olympic rings in flame became the iconic image of the ceremony, reproduced in newspapers and web stories around the world.[59][68]

### Happy and Glorious (21:25–21:35)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Happy and Glorious \(21:25–21:35\)")]

A short film directed by Boyle and produced by the BBC, called _Happy and Glorious_ (after a line in [the national anthem](/wiki/God_Save_the_Queen "God Save the Queen")), featured the character [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond_\(character\) "James Bond \(character\)"), played by [then-Bond actor](/wiki/Portrayal_of_James_Bond_in_film "Portrayal of James Bond in film") [Daniel Craig](/wiki/Daniel_Craig "Daniel Craig"), entering the front gate of [Buckingham Palace](/wiki/Buckingham_Palace "Buckingham Palace") in a [London black cab](/wiki/London_black_cab "London black cab"). His entrance (accompanied by an arrangement of [Handel](/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel "George Frideric Handel")'s "[Arrival of the Queen of Sheba](/wiki/The_Arrival_of_the_Queen_of_Sheba "The Arrival of the Queen of Sheba")") is noticed by Brazilian children (a nod to [Rio de Janeiro](/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro "Rio de Janeiro"), which was to be [the next summer Games](/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics "2016 Summer Olympics") host city) in the throne room. Bond meets [Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") (who played herself, acknowledging Bond with the words, "Good evening, Mr Bond") in [her audience room](/wiki/Queen%27s_audience_room "Queen's audience room") at the palace, walks with her out of the building and into a waiting [AgustaWestland AW139](/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW139 "AgustaWestland AW139") helicopter. The film followed the helicopter across London, with shots of a cheering crowd on [The Mall](/wiki/The_Mall,_London "The Mall, London"), [Nelson's Column](/wiki/Nelson%27s_Column "Nelson's Column"), the [Palace of Westminster](/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster "Palace of Westminster") with an animated [Winston Churchill statue](/wiki/Statue_of_Winston_Churchill,_Parliament_Square "Statue of Winston Churchill, Parliament Square") in [Parliament Square](/wiki/Parliament_Square "Parliament Square"), and of the Thames past the [London Eye](/wiki/London_Eye "London Eye"), [St Paul's Cathedral](/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral "St Paul's Cathedral"), the financial district [City of London](/wiki/City_of_London "City of London"). 

The helicopter then passed through [Tower Bridge](/wiki/Tower_Bridge "Tower Bridge"), accompanied by the [Dambusters March](/wiki/Dambusters_March "Dambusters March"). The film finished with Bond and the Queen apparently jumping from a real helicopter live above the stadium, accompanied by the "[James Bond Theme](/wiki/James_Bond_Theme "James Bond Theme")".[59][69] The Queen and the [Duke of Edinburgh](/wiki/Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh "Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh"), along with [Rogge](/wiki/Jacques_Rogge "Jacques Rogge"), were then introduced to the audience. The Queen was wearing the same dress as in the film, as if she had just arrived with Bond. 

The idea of the royal helicopter jump was first pitched by director [Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle") to [Sebastian Coe](/wiki/Sebastian_Coe "Sebastian Coe"), who loved it so much he took it to [Edward Young](/wiki/Edward_Young_\(courtier\) "Edward Young \(courtier\)"), Private Secretary to the Queen, at Buckingham Palace in the summer of 2011. Young "listened sagely, laughed, and promised to ask the boss". Word came back to Coe that the Queen would love to take part. Young, Boyle and Coe agreed to keep the plan secret so as not to spoil the surprise. On 19 September 2022, the morning of the [Queen's funeral](/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II "Death and state funeral of Elizabeth II"), Coe told BBC News he originally took the concept to [Princess Anne](/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal "Anne, Princess Royal") whose only question was "What kind of helicopter?"[70] Back in 2014, during a State visit by Irish President [Michael Higgins](/wiki/Michael_D._Higgins "Michael D. Higgins"), the Queen herself had credited the humour in some degree to Boyle's Irish heritage, saying, “It took someone of Irish descent, Danny Boyle, to get me to jump from a helicopter."[71]

For the scenes with the helicopter, the Queen was doubled by actress [Julia McKenzie](/wiki/Julia_McKenzie "Julia McKenzie"),[72] and for the parachute jump by [BASE jumper](/wiki/BASE_jumping "BASE jumping") and [stuntman](/wiki/Stunt_performer "Stunt performer") [Gary Connery](/wiki/Gary_Connery "Gary Connery") wearing a dress, hat, jewellery and with a handbag.[73][74] Bond was played by [Mark Sutton](/wiki/Mark_Sutton "Mark Sutton").[75] The helicopter had flown to the stadium from [Stapleford Aerodrome](/wiki/Stapleford_Aerodrome "Stapleford Aerodrome") in Essex, piloted by [Marc Wolff](/wiki/Marc_Wolff "Marc Wolff"). 

The Union Flag was then raised by members of the [British Armed Forces](/wiki/British_Armed_Forces "British Armed Forces"), while the first and third verses of [the national anthem](/wiki/God_Save_the_Queen "God Save the Queen") were performed _[a cappella](/wiki/A_cappella "A cappella")_ by the [Kaos Signing Choir for Deaf and Hearing Children](/wiki/Kaos_Signing_Choir_for_Deaf_and_Hearing_Children "Kaos Signing Choir for Deaf and Hearing Children").[61]

### Second to the right, and straight on till morning (21:35–21:47)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Second to the right, and straight on till morning \(21:35–21:47\)")]

The first part of this sequence celebrated the [National Health Service](/wiki/National_Health_Service "National Health Service") ("the institution which more than any other unites our nation", according to the programme), which had been founded in the year of the previous London Games in 1948. Music was by [Mike Oldfield](/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield"). Six hundred dancers, some of whom were NHS staff, along with 1,200 volunteers, some recruited from British hospitals, entered along with children on 320 hospital beds, some of which functioned as [trampolines](/wiki/Trampoline "Trampoline"). They started a short jive routine. Watching from the tor were specially invited hospital staff and nine child patients from [Great Ormond Street Hospital](/wiki/Great_Ormond_Street_Hospital "Great Ormond Street Hospital").[76] The blankets on the beds illuminated and the beds were arranged to depict a child's face with a smile and a tear (the Hospital Children's Charity's logo). The acronym "GOSH" then changed into the initials "NHS", turning into the shape of a crescent moon as the children were hushed to sleep and read books by the nurses. 

The sequence then moved on to celebrate [British children's literature](/wiki/Children%27s_literature#United_Kingdom "Children's literature"). [J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling") began by reading from [J. M. Barrie](/wiki/James_Matthew_Barrie "James Matthew Barrie")'s _[Peter Pan](/wiki/Peter_and_Wendy "Peter and Wendy")_ (whose copyright was given to Great Ormond Street Hospital). The [Child Catcher](/wiki/Child_Catcher "Child Catcher") appeared amongst the children, followed by giant puppet representations of villains from British children's literature: [the Queen of Hearts](/wiki/Queen_of_Hearts_\(Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland\) "Queen of Hearts \(Alice's Adventures in Wonderland\)"), [Captain Hook](/wiki/Captain_Hook "Captain Hook"), [Cruella de Vil](/wiki/Cruella_de_Vil "Cruella de Vil"), and [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort"). Minutes later, 32 women playing [Mary Poppins](/wiki/Mary_Poppins_\(character\) "Mary Poppins \(character\)") descended with their umbrellas, as the villains deflated and the actors resumed dancing.[59] The music for this sequence included partially rearranged sections from _[Tubular Bells](/wiki/Tubular_Bells "Tubular Bells")_ (with a giant set of [tubular bells](/wiki/Tubular_bells "Tubular bells") at the rear of the stage), _[Tubular Bells III](/wiki/Tubular_Bells_III "Tubular Bells III")_ and, after the villains had been driven away by the Mary Poppins characters, _[In Dulci Jubilo](/wiki/In_Dulci_Jubilo_/_On_Horseback "In Dulci Jubilo / On Horseback")_.[77] During this performance the children in pyjamas jumped up and down on their brightly lit beds, creating a memorable image amid the darkness of the stadium. 

The sequence concluded with a pale, gigantic baby's head, with a rippling sheet for its body, in the centre of the arena. This celebrated the Scottish pioneers of [obstetric ultrasound imaging](/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasonography "Obstetric ultrasonography").[78]

### Interlude (21:47–21:52)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Interlude \(21:47–21:52\)")]

[Simon Rattle](/wiki/Simon_Rattle "Simon Rattle") was then introduced to conduct the [London Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra "London Symphony Orchestra") in a performance of [Vangelis](/wiki/Vangelis "Vangelis")'s "[Chariots of Fire](/wiki/Chariots_of_Fire_\(instrumental\) "Chariots of Fire \(instrumental\)")", as a tribute to the British film industry with [Rowan Atkinson](/wiki/Rowan_Atkinson "Rowan Atkinson") reprising his role as [Mr. Bean](/wiki/Mr._Bean_\(character\) "Mr. Bean \(character\)"), comically playing a repeated note on a [synthesiser](/wiki/Synthesiser "Synthesiser"). He then lapsed into a filmed dream sequence in which he joined the runners from the film _[Chariots of Fire](/wiki/Chariots_of_Fire "Chariots of Fire")_ , beating them in their iconic run along West Sands at [St Andrews](/wiki/St_Andrews "St Andrews") by riding in a car, rejoining the race and tripping the front runner. Danny Boyle later said: "It wasn't actually Mr. Bean. Strictly speaking, the name of his character was Derek."[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] In 2021's _Happy Birthday Mr Bean_ documentary, Atkinson also stated that the performance was not actually intended to be the character Mr. Bean[_[clarification needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")_].[79][80]

### Frankie and June say...thanks Tim (21:52–22:09)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Frankie and June say...thanks Tim \(21:52–22:09\)")]

This sequence celebrated British popular music and culture, paying homage to each decade since the 1960s.[81] To the accompaniment of the BBC [newsreel](/wiki/Television_Newsreel "Television Newsreel") theme "Girls in Grey" and the [theme tune](/wiki/Barwick_Green "Barwick Green") from _[The Archers](/wiki/The_Archers "The Archers")_ , a young mother and son arrive in a [Mini Cooper](/wiki/Mini_Hatch "Mini Hatch") at a full-size replica of a modern British house. The [1987 "don't worry about a hurricane"](/wiki/Great_Storm_of_1987 "Great Storm of 1987") weather forecast by [Michael Fish](/wiki/Michael_Fish "Michael Fish") was shown on the big screens as rain suddenly poured on the house, followed by "[Push the Button](/wiki/Push_the_Button_\(Sugababes_song\) "Push the Button \(Sugababes song\)")", by [Sugababes](/wiki/Sugababes "Sugababes"). In the centre of the arena the sides of another house, three times larger, were used as screens to show clips from various TV programmes, music videos and films, including _[A Matter of Life and Death](/wiki/A_Matter_of_Life_and_Death_\(1946_film\) "A Matter of Life and Death \(1946 film\)")_ (June is named for its protagonist), as well as _[Gregory's Girl](/wiki/Gregory%27s_Girl "Gregory's Girl")_ , _[Kes](/wiki/Kes_\(film\) "Kes \(film\)")_ , _[Bedknobs and Broomsticks](/wiki/Bedknobs_and_Broomsticks "Bedknobs and Broomsticks")_ , _[The Snowman](/wiki/The_Snowman "The Snowman")_ , _[The Wicker Man](/wiki/The_Wicker_Man_\(1973_film\) "The Wicker Man \(1973 film\)")_ , [Modern Family](/wiki/Modern_Family "Modern Family"), _[Four Weddings and a Funeral](/wiki/Four_Weddings_and_a_Funeral "Four Weddings and a Funeral")_ , British soap operas _[Coronation Street](/wiki/Coronation_Street "Coronation Street")_ and _[EastEnders](/wiki/EastEnders "EastEnders")_ , Spanish TV show _[Cuéntame cómo pasó](/wiki/Cu%C3%A9ntame_c%C3%B3mo_pas%C3%B3 "Cuéntame cómo pasó")_ , and Boyle's own _[Trainspotting](/wiki/Trainspotting_\(film\) "Trainspotting \(film\)")_ on the top and the inside of the house on the bottom.[82] A large group of dancers, centred around Frankie and June (19-year-old Henrique Costa and 18-year-old Jasmine Breinburg) on a night out, performed to an assortment of British popular songs arranged broadly chronologically, beginning with "[Going Underground](/wiki/Going_Underground "Going Underground")" by [The Jam](/wiki/The_Jam "The Jam"), suggesting a ride on the [London Underground](/wiki/London_Underground "London Underground"). During this track images of the Underground were projected onto the house and former London Mayor [Ken Livingstone](/wiki/Ken_Livingstone "Ken Livingstone") was briefly seen driving the train. Throughout the sequence cast members were [texting](/wiki/Short_message_service "Short message service") each other or placing [social networking](/wiki/Social_networking "Social networking") status updates on the [Internet](/wiki/Internet "Internet"). Frankie and June first notice each other as a snippet from "[Wonderful Tonight](/wiki/Wonderful_Tonight "Wonderful Tonight")" by [Eric Clapton](/wiki/Eric_Clapton "Eric Clapton") plays, but when Frankie saw that June had dropped her phone on the Tube, he set off to return it (communicating using last number redial to her sister's phone). 

[](/wiki/File:This_is_for_Everyone.jpg)[Tim Berners-Lee](/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee")'s tweet, "This is for everyone"

An extended dance sequence followed, with songs including "[My Generation](/wiki/My_Generation "My Generation")" by [The Who](/wiki/The_Who "The Who"), "[(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction](/wiki/\(I_Can%27t_Get_No\)_Satisfaction "\(I Can't Get No\) Satisfaction")" by the [Rolling Stones](/wiki/Rolling_Stones "Rolling Stones"), "[My Boy Lollipop](/wiki/My_Boy_Lollipop "My Boy Lollipop")" sung by [Millie Small](/wiki/Millie_Small "Millie Small"), "[All Day and All of the Night](/wiki/All_Day_and_All_of_the_Night "All Day and All of the Night")" by the [Kinks](/wiki/Kinks "Kinks"), "[She Loves You](/wiki/She_Loves_You "She Loves You")" by the [Beatles](/wiki/Beatles "Beatles") (with footage of the band performing the song), "[Trampled Under Foot](/wiki/Trampled_Under_Foot "Trampled Under Foot")" by [Led Zeppelin](/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin"), "[Starman](/wiki/Starman_\(song\) "Starman \(song\)")" by [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie"), "[Bohemian Rhapsody](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody "Bohemian Rhapsody")" by [Queen](/wiki/Queen_\(band\) "Queen \(band\)") (during which the sound of the [TARDIS](/wiki/TARDIS "TARDIS") from _[Doctor Who](/wiki/Doctor_Who "Doctor Who")_ could be heard), "[Pretty Vacant](/wiki/Pretty_Vacant "Pretty Vacant")" by the Sex Pistols (during which dancers on power jumpers wearing large heads with [Mohawk hairstyles](/wiki/Mohawk_hairstyle "Mohawk hairstyle") performed a [pogo](/wiki/Pogo_\(dance\) "Pogo \(dance\)") dance, and the lyrics to the song were spelt out in LED lights around the stadium), "[Blue Monday](/wiki/Blue_Monday_\(New_Order_song\) "Blue Monday \(New Order song\)")" by [New Order](/wiki/New_Order_\(band\) "New Order \(band\)"), "[Relax](/wiki/Relax_\(Frankie_Goes_to_Hollywood_song\) "Relax \(Frankie Goes to Hollywood song\)")" by [Frankie Goes to Hollywood](/wiki/Frankie_Goes_to_Hollywood "Frankie Goes to Hollywood") (during which Frankie, asked by June for his name, replied by revealing one of the band's "Frankie say..." T-shirts), "[Back to Life (However Do You Want Me)](/wiki/Back_to_Life_\(However_Do_You_Want_Me\) "Back to Life \(However Do You Want Me\)")" by [Soul II Soul](/wiki/Soul_II_Soul "Soul II Soul"), "[Step On](/wiki/He%27s_Gonna_Step_on_You_Again#Happy_Mondays_version "He's Gonna Step on You Again")" by [Happy Mondays](/wiki/Happy_Mondays "Happy Mondays"), "[Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This)](/wiki/Sweet_Dreams_\(Are_Made_of_This\) "Sweet Dreams \(Are Made of This\)")" by [Eurythmics](/wiki/Eurythmics "Eurythmics"), "[Firestarter](/wiki/Firestarter_\(The_Prodigy_song\) "Firestarter \(The Prodigy song\)")" by [The Prodigy](/wiki/The_Prodigy "The Prodigy"), and "[Born Slippy .NUXX](/wiki/Born_Slippy_.NUXX "Born Slippy .NUXX")" by [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_\(band\) "Underworld \(band\)"), ending with the cast singing "[I'm Forever Blowing Bubbles](/wiki/I%27m_Forever_Blowing_Bubbles "I'm Forever Blowing Bubbles")" as Frankie and June walked towards each other. A sequence from the film _[Four Weddings and a Funeral](/wiki/Four_Weddings_and_a_Funeral "Four Weddings and a Funeral")_ was projected behind them; when they kissed, a montage of memorable kisses from film, TV and real life was shown (including one of the first interracial kisses on British television in _[Emergency Ward 10](/wiki/Emergency_Ward_10 "Emergency Ward 10")_ and the first lesbian kiss from _[Brookside](/wiki/Brookside_\(television_programme\) "Brookside \(television programme\)")_ , which in some countries, including Saudi Arabia, then became the first lesbian kiss ever shown on [pre-watershed](/wiki/Watershed_\(television\) "Watershed \(television\)") television),[59] while "[Song 2](/wiki/Song_2 "Song 2")" by [Blur](/wiki/Blur_\(band\) "Blur \(band\)") was played. A live performance of "[Bonkers](/wiki/Bonkers_\(song\) "Bonkers \(song\)")" by [Dizzee Rascal](/wiki/Dizzee_Rascal "Dizzee Rascal") (who grew up in the host borough of [Tower Hamlets](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Tower_Hamlets "London Borough of Tower Hamlets"))[83] followed, along with a further sequence in which all the cast (and _[Britain's Got Talent](/wiki/Britain%27s_Got_Talent "Britain's Got Talent")_ dancing duo [Signature](/wiki/Signature_\(dance_group\) "Signature \(dance group\)")) attend a party at June's house while, [Amy Winehouse](/wiki/Amy_Winehouse "Amy Winehouse")'s "[Valerie](/wiki/Valerie_\(The_Zutons_song\) "Valerie \(The Zutons song\)")", [Muse](/wiki/Muse_\(band\) "Muse \(band\)")'s "[Uprising](/wiki/Uprising_\(song\) "Uprising \(song\)")", and [Tinie Tempah](/wiki/Tinie_Tempah "Tinie Tempah")'s "[Pass Out](/wiki/Pass_Out_\(Tinie_Tempah_song\) "Pass Out \(Tinie Tempah song\)")" played.[77]

At the close, while "[Heaven](/wiki/Heaven_\(Emeli_Sand%C3%A9_song\) "Heaven \(Emeli Sandé song\)")" by [Emeli Sandé](/wiki/Emeli_Sand%C3%A9 "Emeli Sandé") was played, the larger house was raised to reveal [Tim Berners-Lee](/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") working at a [NeXT Computer](/wiki/NeXT_Computer "NeXT Computer"), like the one on which he invented the [World Wide Web](/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"). He [tweeted](/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") "This is for everyone",[84] instantly spelt out in LED lights around the stadium.[59][85] The programme explained "Music connects us with each other and with the most important moments in our lives. One of the things that makes those connections possible is the World Wide Web". Boyle wanted to honour Berners-Lee for having made the World Wide Web free and available to everyone (hence the tweet), rather than seeking a commercial profit from it.[86]

### Abide with Me (22:09–22:20)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Abide with Me \(22:09–22:20\)")]

A filmed sequence showed extracts from the [torch relay](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_torch_relay "2012 Summer Olympics torch relay") around the UK, to the music "I Heard Wonders" by [David Holmes](/wiki/David_Holmes_\(musician\) "David Holmes \(musician\)"). This then cut live to show [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham") driving a dramatically illuminated motor boat down the [River Thames](/wiki/River_Thames "River Thames") and under [Tower Bridge](/wiki/Tower_Bridge "Tower Bridge"), to fireworks, while footballer [Jade Bailey](/wiki/Jade_Bailey_\(footballer\) "Jade Bailey \(footballer\)") held on to the torch in the boat. This section had been rehearsed on 24 July 2012 when the close-up shots were pre-recorded,[87] and was directed by [Stephen Daldry](/wiki/Stephen_Daldry "Stephen Daldry"). 

There was then a tribute to "..friends and family of those in the stadium who cannot be here tonight", including the victims of the "7/7" [2005 London bombings](/wiki/7_July_2005_London_bombings "7 July 2005 London bombings") (on the day after London had been awarded the Games).[88] Photos of people who had died were displayed on screens as a memorial, accompanied by an excerpt from [Brian Eno](/wiki/Brian_Eno "Brian Eno")'s ambient work "[An Ending (Ascent)](/wiki/An_Ending_\(Ascent\) "An Ending \(Ascent\)")". The hymn "[Abide with Me](/wiki/Abide_with_Me "Abide with Me")" was then sung by [Emeli Sandé](/wiki/Emeli_Sand%C3%A9 "Emeli Sandé")[59][77] while a group of dancers choreographed by and including [Akram Khan](/wiki/Akram_Khan_\(dancer\) "Akram Khan \(dancer\)") performed a contemporary dance on the theme of mortality.[89][90]

### Welcome (22:20–00:00)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Welcome \(22:20–00:00\)")]

See also: [2012 Summer Olympics Parade of Nations](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations "2012 Summer Olympics Parade of Nations")

[](/wiki/File:2012_Summer_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations,_Team_GB.jpg)[Team GB](/wiki/Team_GB "Team GB") enters the [2012 Summer Olympics Parade of Nations](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_Parade_of_Nations "2012 Summer Olympics Parade of Nations") last

The Parade of Nations of athletes (drawn from the 10,490 competing) and officials from 204 nations (and also the "[Independent Olympic Athletes](/wiki/Independent_Olympic_Athletes_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics "Independent Olympic Athletes at the 2012 Summer Olympics")") was led, according to custom, by the [Greek](/wiki/Greece "Greece") team, followed by other competing countries in [alphabetical order](/wiki/Alphabetical_order "Alphabetical order"), and finally the host nation [Great Britain](/wiki/Team_GB "Team GB"). Each of the 205 teams entered the stadium led by their flagbearer, accompanied by a child volunteer carrying a copper petal (later revealed to be part of the [cauldron](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_and_Paralympics_cauldron "2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics cauldron")) and a young woman carrying a sign with the country's name in English (and wearing a dress made from fabric printed with photos of people who had applied to be Olympic volunteers).[91]

The parade was accompanied by mainly British dance tracks and popular songs, including "[Galvanize](/wiki/Galvanize_\(song\) "Galvanize \(song\)")" by [Chemical Brothers](/wiki/Chemical_Brothers "Chemical Brothers"), "[West End Girls](/wiki/West_End_Girls "West End Girls")" by [Pet Shop Boys](/wiki/Pet_Shop_Boys "Pet Shop Boys"), "[Rolling in the Deep](/wiki/Rolling_in_the_Deep "Rolling in the Deep")" by [Adele](/wiki/Adele "Adele"), "[Stayin' Alive](/wiki/Stayin%27_Alive "Stayin' Alive")" by the [Bee Gees](/wiki/Bee_Gees "Bee Gees") and both "[Where the Streets Have No Name](/wiki/Where_the_Streets_Have_No_Name "Where the Streets Have No Name")" and "[Beautiful Day](/wiki/Beautiful_Day "Beautiful Day")" by Irish band [U2](/wiki/U2 "U2"), with [Great Britain](/wiki/Team_GB "Team GB") entering to [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie")'s song "[Heroes](/wiki/%22Heroes%22_\(David_Bowie_song\) ""Heroes" \(David Bowie song\)")".[61] Welsh [drum and bass](/wiki/Drum_and_bass "Drum and bass") DJ [High Contrast](/wiki/High_Contrast "High Contrast") mixed and sequenced the music for the athletes' parade.[42]

Music with a fast rhythm of 120 [bpm](/wiki/Tempo "Tempo")[18] was used in an attempt to keep the teams walking quickly around the stadium, and this was reinforced by the drummers in the stadium; nevertheless the parade part of the programme took 1 hour 40 minutes to complete, compared to the 1 hour 29 minutes estimated in the official media guide. Once all of the athletes were inside the stadium, seven billion [small pieces of paper](/wiki/Confetti "Confetti") were dropped from a [Westland helicopter](/wiki/Westland_Helicopters "Westland Helicopters"), each piece representing one person on Earth. Each nation's [flag](/wiki/Flag "Flag") was planted on the Glastonbury Tor. 

### Bike a.m. (00:00–00:07 BST 28 July)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Bike a.m. \(00:00–00:07 BST 28 July\)")]

[](/wiki/File:KOCIS_Korea_London_Olympics_TeamKorea_14_\(7683500060\).jpg)Doves at the opening ceremony

Once the athletes had gathered in the centre of the stadium, [Arctic Monkeys](/wiki/Arctic_Monkeys "Arctic Monkeys") performed "[I Bet You Look Good on the Dancefloor](/wiki/I_Bet_You_Look_Good_on_the_Dancefloor "I Bet You Look Good on the Dancefloor")" and [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles")' "[Come Together](/wiki/Come_Together "Come Together")", the latter while 75 cyclists circled the stadium with wings lit by [LEDs](/wiki/LED "LED") representing [Doves of Peace](/wiki/Peace_symbol#The_dove_and_olive_branch "Peace symbol"). Doves were traditionally released at Olympic opening ceremonies, although real birds have not been used since 1992. A single dove cyclist, his beak painted yellow in honour of [Bradley Wiggins](/wiki/Bradley_Wiggins "Bradley Wiggins"), appeared to fly out of the stadium. 

### Let the Games Begin (00:07–00:24)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Let the Games Begin \(00:07–00:24\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Muhammed_Ali_receives_the_Olympic_Flag_at_the_London_2012_Olympic_Games_Opening_Ceremony.JPG)[Muhammad Ali](/wiki/Muhammad_Ali "Muhammad Ali") receives the Olympic Flag

The formal part of the ceremony was introduced by [Sebastian Coe](/wiki/Sebastian_Coe,_Baron_Coe "Sebastian Coe, Baron Coe"), speaking from the Tor and surrounded by flags of the participating nations. He welcomed the watching world to [London](/wiki/London "London"). He expressed pride in being [British](/wiki/British_people "British people") and part of the Olympic movement, and said that the Olympics "brings together the people of the world...to celebrate what is best about mankind".[59] He continued to speak of the "truth and drama" of sport, and then thanked Britain for "making all this possible". Rogge responded by thanking London, stating that it was the third time that London had held the Games, following [1908](/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics "1908 Summer Olympics"), held at short notice when [Rome](/wiki/Rome "Rome") was unable to do so (after a volcanic eruption), and [1948](/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics "1948 Summer Olympics") three years after the end of [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). Rogge thanked the thousands of volunteers, to huge cheers. He announced that for the first time in Olympic history, every team had female participants. Rogge acknowledged the important role the UK had played as "the birthplace of modern sport", codifying its "fair play" ethos and building sport into the school curriculum. He appealed to athletes to play fairly and be drug-free, according to the values of [Baron de Coubertin](/wiki/Pierre_de_Coubertin "Pierre de Coubertin"), reminding them that they were role models who would "inspire a generation". After expressing these sentiments again briefly in French, he invited the Queen to open the Games.[59]

The Queen declared the competition officially open, immediately followed by a [trumpet](/wiki/Trumpet "Trumpet") fanfare based on a theme from _[Tubular Bells](/wiki/Tubular_Bells "Tubular Bells")_ by [Mike Oldfield](/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield") and then a [fireworks](/wiki/Fireworks "Fireworks") display.[59] The 2012 ceremony was the second time the Queen had opened an Olympic Games, the first being the [1976 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics "1976 Summer Olympics") in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal "Montreal") in her capacity as [Queen of Canada](/wiki/Queen_of_Canada "Queen of Canada"). It was also the first time any individual had opened a Summer Olympics twice (two more Olympiads had been opened on her behalf, with a further two Winter Games opened on her behalf). 

The [Olympic Flag](/wiki/Olympic_Flag "Olympic Flag") was carried by eight people chosen from around the world to embody the Olympic values: [Doreen Lawrence](/wiki/Doreen_Lawrence "Doreen Lawrence") (chosen for her "tireless thirst for justice"), [Haile Gebrselassie](/wiki/Haile_Gebrselassie "Haile Gebrselassie") (for his "fight against poverty"), [Sally Becker](/wiki/Sally_Becker "Sally Becker") (for her "courage"), [Ban Ki-moon](/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon "Ban Ki-moon") (as UN secretary-general), [Leymah Gbowee](/wiki/Leymah_Gbowee "Leymah Gbowee") (as "a great peacemaker"), [Shami Chakrabarti](/wiki/Shami_Chakrabarti "Shami Chakrabarti") (for "her integrity"), [Daniel Barenboim](/wiki/Daniel_Barenboim "Daniel Barenboim") (for bringing "harmony in place of discord"), and [Marina Silva](/wiki/Marina_Silva "Marina Silva") (as UN Champion of the Earth).[92] The flag paused in front of [Muhammad Ali](/wiki/Muhammad_Ali "Muhammad Ali") (invited to represent "respect, confidence, conviction, dedication, generosity and spiritual strength"), who held it for a few moments. The flag was received by a [colour guard](/wiki/Colour_guard "Colour guard") of [Her Majesty's Armed Forces](/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Armed_Forces "Her Majesty's Armed Forces")[93] and hoisted to the [Olympic Anthem](/wiki/Olympic_Hymn "Olympic Hymn"), performed by the [LSO](/wiki/London_Symphony_Orchestra "London Symphony Orchestra") and the [Grimethorpe Colliery Band](/wiki/Grimethorpe_Colliery_Band "Grimethorpe Colliery Band"). A brief reprise of "And I will Kiss" commenced the [Olympic Oaths](/wiki/Olympic_Oath "Olympic Oath"), taken by [taekwondo](/wiki/Taekwondo "Taekwondo") athlete [Sarah Stevenson](/wiki/Sarah_Stevenson "Sarah Stevenson") on behalf of the athletes, by British [AIBA](/wiki/International_Boxing_Association_\(amateur\) "International Boxing Association \(amateur\)") Referee [Mik Basi](/wiki/Mik_Basi "Mik Basi") on behalf of the officials, and by Eric Farrell on behalf of the coaches.[94][95]

### There Is a Light That Never Goes Out (00:24–00:38)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: There Is a Light That Never Goes Out \(00:24–00:38\)")]

See also: [2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics cauldron](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_and_Paralympics_cauldron "2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics cauldron")

[](/wiki/File:Olympic_Cauldron_after_being_lit_at_the_London_2012_Olympic_Games_Opening_Ceremony.jpg)Thomas Heatherwick's Olympic Cauldron after being lit

This section was named after [the song of the same name](/wiki/There_Is_a_Light_That_Never_Goes_Out "There Is a Light That Never Goes Out") by [The Smiths](/wiki/The_Smiths "The Smiths"). The [motorboat](/wiki/Motorboat "Motorboat") driven by [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham "David Beckham") arrived with the [Olympic Flame](/wiki/Olympic_Flame "Olympic Flame") via the [Limehouse Cut](/wiki/Limehouse_Cut "Limehouse Cut") and the [Lee Navigation](/wiki/Lee_Navigation "Lee Navigation"). [Steve Redgrave](/wiki/Steve_Redgrave "Steve Redgrave") lit his torch from that on the boat, and carried it into the stadium through an honour guard of 500 of the construction workers who had built the [Olympic Park](/wiki/Olympic_Park,_London "Olympic Park, London"). He passed the flame on to a team of seven young people, each nominated by a famous British Olympian to convey the Games' aim to "inspire a generation".[96] Six of the team were athletes, and the seventh was a volunteer young ambassador.[97]

The teenagers made a lap of the stadium, each carrying the torch in turn, while [Alex Trimble](/wiki/Alex_Trimble "Alex Trimble"), lead singer of [Two Door Cinema Club](/wiki/Two_Door_Cinema_Club "Two Door Cinema Club"), performed "[Caliban's Dream](/wiki/Caliban%27s_Dream "Caliban's Dream")"[98] with the Dockhead Choir, [Only Men Aloud](/wiki/Only_Men_Aloud "Only Men Aloud"), Elizabeth Roberts, and Esme Smith. This had been written especially for the ceremony by [Rick Smith](/wiki/Rick_Smith_\(Underworld\) "Rick Smith \(Underworld\)") of [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_\(band\) "Underworld \(band\)").[98]

Each young athlete was greeted by their nominating Olympian (watched by 260 British medallists from previous summer and winter Games since London 1948) and presented with their own torch, which was then lit from the flame. They jogged through a corridor between assembled athletes to the centre of the stadium, where the 204[99] copper petals (each inscribed with the name of the team it accompanied during the parade) were now seen in a circular formation attached to long pipes (the petals were to accompany each team home after the competition, as a souvenir).[100] The young athletes lit some of the petals, and when the flame had spread to all of them, the pipes rose slowly from the floor of the stadium and converged to form the cauldron.[101] The cauldron lighters were (_nominator in brackets_):[102]

  * Callum Airlie ([Shirley Robertson](/wiki/Shirley_Robertson "Shirley Robertson"))
  * Jordan Duckitt ([Duncan Goodhew](/wiki/Duncan_Goodhew "Duncan Goodhew"))
  * [Desiree Henry](/wiki/Desiree_Henry "Desiree Henry") ([Daley Thompson](/wiki/Daley_Thompson "Daley Thompson"))
  * Katie Kirk ([Mary Peters](/wiki/Mary_Peters_\(athlete\) "Mary Peters \(athlete\)"))
  * Cameron MacRitchie ([Steve Redgrave](/wiki/Steve_Redgrave "Steve Redgrave"))
  * Aidan Reynolds ([Lynn Davies](/wiki/Lynn_Davies "Lynn Davies"))
  * [Adelle Tracey](/wiki/Adelle_Tracey "Adelle Tracey") ([Kelly Holmes](/wiki/Kelly_Holmes "Kelly Holmes"))



[The cauldron](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_and_Paralympics_cauldron "2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics cauldron") designed by [Thomas Heatherwick](/wiki/Thomas_Heatherwick "Thomas Heatherwick") was described as "one of the best-kept secrets of the opening ceremony": until this point, its design, location, and who would light it had not been revealed.[103][104]

### And in the end (00:38–00:46)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: And in the end \(00:38–00:46\)")]

A flurry of spectacular fireworks accompanied by [Pink Floyd](/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd")'s song "[Eclipse](/wiki/Eclipse_\(Pink_Floyd_song\) "Eclipse \(Pink Floyd song\)")" was supported by images of memorable Olympic victories shown on the big screens, with the stadium pixels showing [Jesse Owens](/wiki/Jesse_Owens "Jesse Owens") running.[66] The climax of this section was a live view of the Olympic rings 34 kilometres (21 miles) above the Earth, transmitted from one of the balloons launched three and a half hours earlier. The sky was then lit by searchlights piercing the smoke (another iconic London image) from the fireworks, the [Orbit](/wiki/ArcelorMittal_Orbit "ArcelorMittal Orbit") tower was illuminated. [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney "Paul McCartney") and his band performed the closing section of "[The End](/wiki/The_End_\(The_Beatles_song\) "The End \(The Beatles song\)")", and then "[Hey Jude](/wiki/Hey_Jude "Hey Jude")", with its chorus sung by the audience to close the ceremony at 00:46 BST.[59]

## Music

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Music")]

See also: [Isles of Wonder (album)](/wiki/Isles_of_Wonder_\(album\) "Isles of Wonder \(album\)")

[](/wiki/File:London_2012_Olympic_Opening_Ceremony_-_British_Music_section.jpg)Segment of the opening ceremony celebrating British music

The eclectic programme of music was chosen to showcase almost exclusively [British music](/wiki/Music_of_the_United_Kingdom "Music of the United Kingdom")[105][106] with pieces representing [the UK's four nations](/wiki/Countries_of_the_United_Kingdom "Countries of the United Kingdom").[107] It included [classical works](/wiki/Classical_music_of_the_United_Kingdom "Classical music of the United Kingdom") by [British composers](/wiki/List_of_British_classical_composers "List of British classical composers") such as [Hubert Parry](/wiki/Charles_Hubert_Parry "Charles Hubert Parry"), and performances by UK choirs and orchestras. The focus was mainly on [music of the 1960s onwards](/wiki/British_popular_music "British popular music"), causing one [Chinese](/wiki/Chinese_people "Chinese people") journalist to ask: "Will this be the most rock and roll opening ceremony ever?".[108]

Rick Smith and Underworld composed pieces for the ceremony, including "[And I Will Kiss](/wiki/And_I_Will_Kiss "And I Will Kiss")"[109] used during the "Pandemonium" section, and "[Caliban's Dream](/wiki/Caliban%27s_Dream "Caliban's Dream")"[110] heard during the lighting of the cauldron. These were favourably reviewed; in _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ , Michael Hann wrote "Underworld ... had a bit of a triumph: the builds and fades they learned in the world of dance music lent the sometimes overwhelming visual spectacle a sense of structure".[111]

Musical motifs were used to bind the ceremony programme together: for example, the "whistling" theme first heard during the minute's silence embedded within "And I Will Kiss" returned frequently – behind the fury as the ring was being forged, emerging triumphant as the five rings came together, and again later as the main theme of "Caliban's Dream" while the flame was paraded around the stadium. 

[](/wiki/File:London_Olympics_Opening_Ceremony_-_Mike_Oldfield.jpg)Mike Oldfield performing "[Tubular Bells](/wiki/Tubular_Bells "Tubular Bells")" at the opening ceremony

Bells were a theme of the opening day of the Olympics, starting at 8:12 am with artist [Martin Creed](/wiki/Martin_Creed "Martin Creed")'s _[Work No. 1197: All the Bells](/wiki/All_the_Bells "All the Bells")_ , when bells were rung across the UK including forty strikes of Big Ben.[112] "The sound of bells is the sound of England", Boyle had told volunteers during rehearsal.[41] Much of the music for the ceremony contained "bell" references, linking to the large bell forged for the ceremony and evoking bells as "the sound of freedom and peace". Modified sequences based on the traditional British [eight-bell peal](/wiki/Ring_of_bells "Ring of bells") underlaid "And I Will Kiss" and carried through into the "[Tubular Bells](/wiki/Tubular_Bells "Tubular Bells")"/NHS section, with [handbells](/wiki/Handbells "Handbells") and a tolling large bell featured on "Caliban's Dream" and at key points in the ceremony. A handbell chime also played after the close, as the stadium emptied. 

Boyle approached many of the artists personally, to see if they would be interested in performing, and he also flew to [Barbados](/wiki/Barbados "Barbados") for an hour-long meeting with [Mike Oldfield](/wiki/Mike_Oldfield "Mike Oldfield"). A few turned him down, including [Elvis Costello](/wiki/Elvis_Costello "Elvis Costello") and [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie").[21] The performing artists were paid a nominal £1 fee to make their contracts legally binding.[42]

The pre-recorded soundtrack _[Isles of Wonder](/wiki/Isles_of_Wonder:_Music_for_the_Opening_Ceremony_of_the_London_2012_Olympic_Games "Isles of Wonder: Music for the Opening Ceremony of the London 2012 Olympic Games")_ was released on iTunes at midnight of 28 July 2012, with a two-disc CD set released on 2 August.[113] Within two days, the download album had topped the iTunes album charts in Britain, France, Belgium and Spain, and reached No. 5 in the United States, as well as being No. 5 in the British album charts.[106] Rick Smith's concluding comment in the CD cover notes was: "The isle is full of noises. The soundtrack writes itself."

## Technical aspects

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Technical aspects")]

The main loading of the stadium started on 10 May and took ten weeks of what was the wettest summer for a hundred years, posing considerable challenges.[121][122] Dismantling the staging took just sixty hours.[121] The infield staging area was 2.5 metres high, and had to accommodate the elements revealed during the ceremony, such as the chimneys and beam engines from "Pandemonium", and the [cauldron](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics_and_Paralympics_cauldron "2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics cauldron"). To ensure that it remained secret, the cauldron was code-named "Betty", and installed and tested at night.[121]

The stadium was rigged with a one million watt sound system and more than 500 speakers.[123] Some 15,000 square metres (3.7 acres) of staging and 12,956 props were used,[124] as well as 7,346 square metres (1.815 acres) of turf including crops.[125] 70,799[123] 25 centimetre (10 inch) pixel panels were placed around the stadium, including between every seat. Each panel connected to a central computer and was fitted with nine full-colour [LED](/wiki/LED "LED") pixels by Tait Technology.[121] These enabled images to be broadcast during the performance, such as of a 1960s [go-go dancer](/wiki/Go-go_dancer "Go-go dancer"), a [London Underground](/wiki/London_Underground "London Underground") train, and a representation of the birth of the [internet](/wiki/Internet "Internet"). The audience was also able to participate by waving the paddles to create a twinkling effect. These animations were designed by 59 Productions and the video animations were produced by Chinese company Crystal CG.[126] The 2D to 3D transformation and mapping of video content onto the panels were done by [Avolites Media](/wiki/Avolites "Avolites") [media server](/wiki/Media_server "Media server") consoles.[127][128]

[](/wiki/File:Flickr_-_Carine06_-_Olympic_Stadium_\(3\).jpg)At the technical rehearsal of the Opening Ceremony

Technical director Piers Shepperd masterminded the complex change from rural to industrial during "Pandemonium".[68] The seven inflatable chimneys were made by Airworks, and varied in height (three were 22-metre (72 ft), two were 23-metre (75 ft) and two were 30-metre (98 ft) high). They were made of soft fabric, with an outer layer of printed brick pattern. Each contained four industrial fans at the base to inflate them, and a smoke machine near the top, and were hoisted into the air from the overhead rigs.[121] Life-size [beam engines](/wiki/Beam_engine "Beam engine") were constructed onstage by teams of stage hands and members of the Volunteer Staging Team.[68] At the climax of "Pandemonium", in the [Olympic ring](/wiki/Olympic_symbols#Rings "Olympic symbols") forging scene, amber lights lit in sequence created the illusion of a 30-metre (98 ft) molten steel river, with pyrotechnic smoke and [dry ice](/wiki/Dry_ice "Dry ice") as steam. The original grass floor surface had been removed to reveal a giant stylised map of London.[129]

Working alongside the professional crew were more than 800 volunteers; some were production arts students from British drama schools. Many had been working on the Olympic and Paralympic ceremonies since early 2012 at the [Three Mills Studios](/wiki/Three_Mills#Three_Mills_Studios "Three Mills") and [Dagenham](/wiki/Dagenham "Dagenham") rehearsal sites, before moving to the Stadium on 16 June. The thousands of cast were cued and co-ordinated by directions received through earphones ("in-ear monitors"), and adjustments were made during the performance: for example, during Pandemonium extra volunteers were sent to make sure all the turf was cleared on time. The earphones also carried a continuous electronic [metronomic](/wiki/Metronome "Metronome") four-beat to keep everyone walking and moving in time with the music.[130]

In July 2013 it was revealed that on the morning of the ceremony, Britain's surveillance headquarters [GCHQ](/wiki/GCHQ "GCHQ") had detected a credible [cyber attack](/wiki/Cyber_attack "Cyber attack") threat that could have killed the lighting system in the stadium. Counter-measures were taken, and in the afternoon contingency plans were discussed with government ministers at a meeting in the [Cabinet Office](/wiki/Cabinet_Office "Cabinet Office") briefing room. However, this attack never materialised.[131]

## TV coverage

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: TV coverage")]

[](/wiki/File:2012_Summer_Olympics_Opening_Ceremony_Fireworks_Tower_Bridge.jpg)Fireworks at [Tower Bridge](/wiki/Tower_Bridge "Tower Bridge")

The [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s coverage started at 19:00 and continued uninterrupted until 00:50.[142] The BBC audience averaged about 24.46 million viewers and peaked at approximately 26.9 million.[143] This was the largest average audience for any broadcast since 1996 and one of the top 20 most-watched UK television broadcasts of all time.[143] David Stringer of [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press") described the coverage as "a success...so far, the BBC's ambitious – and technically tricky – Olympic plan has worked almost without a flaw."[144] Euan Ferguson of _[The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer "The Observer")_ commented that "Coverage of the Olympics so far ... has been near perfect".[145] However, [Clive James](/wiki/Clive_James "Clive James") was critical of the build-up programme, presented by [Gary Lineker](/wiki/Gary_Lineker "Gary Lineker") and [Sue Barker](/wiki/Sue_Barker "Sue Barker").[146] Commentators for the BBC were [Huw Edwards](/wiki/Huw_Edwards_\(journalist\) "Huw Edwards \(journalist\)"), [Hazel Irvine](/wiki/Hazel_Irvine "Hazel Irvine") and [Trevor Nelson](/wiki/Trevor_Nelson "Trevor Nelson"), the latter criticised by Andy Dawson of the _[Daily Mirror](/wiki/Daily_Mirror "Daily Mirror")_ as floundering "like a ventriloquist's dummy pumped full of low-grade ketamine".[7][147][148][149][150][151] Private talks were held between Boyle and BBC commentators in the run-up to the ceremony. Boyle was unhappy with a voiceover being imposed on the ceremony, which he wanted viewers to be able to enjoy without commentary. The BBC offered several options including "no commentary" coverage for both its TV and online transmissions.[152][153] [Audio description](/wiki/Audio_description "Audio description") was also provided with commentary by [Nick Mullins](/wiki/Nick_Mullins "Nick Mullins").[154]

Nearly 41 million US viewers watched [NBC](/wiki/NBC "NBC")'s coverage of the event. Criticism was levelled at its decision to [tape-delay this broadcast](/wiki/Olympics_on_NBC#2012_Opening_Ceremony "Olympics on NBC"), and not make a live version available even to cable and web users. There were frequent interruptions by commercial breaks.[155] Many US viewers looked for other ways to watch (such as the live [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC") feed),[156] despite both NBC and the IOC vowing to crack down on unauthorised streams.[157] More significant criticism was levelled at NBC for cutting to a [Ryan Seacrest](/wiki/Ryan_Seacrest "Ryan Seacrest") interview with [Michael Phelps](/wiki/Michael_Phelps "Michael Phelps") during the "memorial wall" tribute including commemoration of the victims of the [7/7 London bombings](/wiki/7/7_London_bombings "7/7 London bombings"), which was seen as disrespectful and insensitive.[158] An NBC spokesman said the network had left out that segment because its programming was "tailored for the US audience".[159] There was also criticism of commentators [Matt Lauer](/wiki/Matt_Lauer "Matt Lauer") and [Meredith Vieira](/wiki/Meredith_Vieira "Meredith Vieira") for suggesting that [the Queen](/wiki/Elizabeth_II "Elizabeth II") had actually jumped out of a helicopter.[160] Vieira and Lauer admitted to not knowing that [Tim Berners-Lee](/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") was the inventor of the [World Wide Web](/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"),[161] as she commented "If you haven't heard of him, we haven't either", before Lauer told the audience to [Google](/wiki/Google_Search "Google Search") him.[162] These failings were picked up on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") during the broadcast with the [hashtag](/wiki/Hashtag "Hashtag") #nbcfail.[163]

The ceremony was recorded by three separate broadcasters: the BBC, the [Olympic Broadcasting Services](/wiki/Olympic_Broadcasting_Services "Olympic Broadcasting Services") (directed by the Finnish state broadcaster [YLE](/wiki/YLE "YLE") on behalf of the OBS), and by independent production company Done and Dusted, hired by LOCOG and working under Boyle's direction. This was the first time that an independent production company had been used for an Olympic ceremony.[140] This situation led to some tension, as Boyle wanted more artistic control and felt he was getting no co-operation from the OBS.[153][164][165] He criticised the OBS coverage during his commentary for the BBC DVD.[66] In addition, the BBC filmed some of the pre-recorded parts of the ceremony.[153] The filming was directed for television by [Hamish Hamilton](/wiki/Hamish_Hamilton_\(director\) "Hamish Hamilton \(director\)"), who described it as "easily the most difficult job of my life".[166]

The BBC released footage of the entire ceremony on 29 October 2012, edited by Danny Boyle and with background extras, filling more than one disc of a five [DVD](/wiki/DVD "DVD") or [Blu-ray disc](/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc "Blu-ray Disc") set, which also contained more than seven hours of sporting highlights as well as the complete closing ceremony.[17] A "BBC commentary-free" option for the opening ceremony is available on the DVD, as well as a commentary track by Danny Boyle and [Frank Cottrell-Boyce](/wiki/Frank_Cottrell-Boyce "Frank Cottrell-Boyce").[165]

## Reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: Reception")]

_[The Times](/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ described the ceremony as "a masterpiece", with _[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")_ saying it was "brilliant, breathtaking, bonkers and utterly British".[9] The [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s chief sports writer [Tom Fordyce](/wiki/Tom_Fordyce "Tom Fordyce") called it "eccentric" and "tongue-in-cheek", saying "no-one expected ... it would be quite so gloriously daft, so cynicism-squashingly charming and, well, so much pinch-yourself fun."[167] Two weeks after the ceremony [Jonathan Freedland](/wiki/Jonathan_Freedland "Jonathan Freedland") of _The Guardian_ wrote that "Boyle's spectacular, so beautifully executed and ingeniously conceived it lingers in the mind even as the closing draws near, stood apart from its predecessors thanks not only to its humour and eccentricity, but also because it had something to say."[168] Writing in _[The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer "The Observer")_ , [Jackie Kay](/wiki/Jackie_Kay "Jackie Kay") commented that "it seemed that Boyle had invented a new kind of opening ceremony, a concept ceremony, one that embraces big ideas as passionately as it does technical flamboyancy".[169] _The Stage_ said that "Danny Boyle's spectacular and moving Olympics Opening Ceremony was undoubtedly the theatrical highlight of 2012".[170]

> "Maybe you shouldn't have been able to interpret it that much, because it was about wonder. The theme of the show was to take things that we're very familiar with, and make them seem again wonderful to us: the things that you know about the industrial revolution and the internet, and say 'Aren't these things astonishing, that we live in the middle of?' and to kind of re-polish the pattern of life. So maybe it's alright that you were a little bit bewildered." 

— Frank Cottrell-Boyce on the BBC Radio 4 _Today_ programme, 28 July 2012.[171]

Although praise came from across the political spectrum, a few on the British political right were unhappy. [Aidan Burley](/wiki/Aidan_Burley "Aidan Burley"), a [Conservative](/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\) "Conservative Party \(UK\)") [MP](/wiki/Member_of_Parliament "Member of Parliament"), denounced the ceremony on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") as "[leftie](/wiki/Left_wing "Left wing") multicultural crap".[172][173] Burley's comments were dismissed by many fellow Conservatives, including [David Cameron](/wiki/David_Cameron "David Cameron") and [Boris Johnson](/wiki/Boris_Johnson "Boris Johnson").[173][174]

Foreign reaction was overwhelmingly positive. _[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ said the ceremony was "hilariously quirky ... a wild jumble of the celebratory and the fanciful; the conventional and the eccentric; and the frankly off-the-wall".[9][175] _Forbes_ called it Boyle's "love song to Britain",[14] while _[Sports Illustrated](/wiki/Sports_Illustrated "Sports Illustrated")_ noted its political aspects, calling it "a celebration of protest and dissent".[176] _[The Sydney Morning Herald](/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")_ said it was "an unforgettable start ... at once subversive and sublime"[9] and _[The Times of India](/wiki/The_Times_of_India "The Times of India")_ said that "London presented a vibrant picture of Great Britain's rich heritage and culture".[9][177] The Chinese news agency _[Xinhua](/wiki/Xinhua "Xinhua")_ described the ceremony as "dazzling" and an "eccentric and exuberant celebration of British history, art and culture".[178] Chinese artist [Ai Weiwei](/wiki/Ai_Weiwei "Ai Weiwei") praised the ceremony for its "human touch", saying "In London, they really turned the ceremony into a party ... such a density of information about events and stories and literature and music; about folktales and movies."[169]

Russian President [Putin](/wiki/Vladimir_Putin "Vladimir Putin") said the ceremony was "wonderful and unforgettable".[179] [Dmitry Medvedev](/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev "Dmitry Medvedev") said "It was an exceptional spectacle, very well prepared and quite rich ... it succeeded in creating a very British atmosphere ... they managed to find the right language ... to communicate."[180] Panos Samaras of Greece's [NET](/wiki/New_Hellenic_Television "New Hellenic Television") said "it was more like a big musical, a rock opera ... than an Olympics ceremony". French sports newspaper _[L'Équipe](/wiki/L%27%C3%89quipe "L'Équipe")_ wrote that the ceremony "took the classic from such events and had fun with them", while _[Le Parisien](/wiki/Le_Parisien "Le Parisien")_ said it "was magnificent, inventive and offbeat drawing heavily on the roots of British identity". Germany's _[Die Welt](/wiki/Die_Welt "Die Welt")_ hailed it as "spectacular, glitzy but also provoking and moving".[181]

Chinese news [CCTV-4](/wiki/CCTV-4 "CCTV-4") said the ceremony was a "stunning feast for the eyes".[182] South Korea's [Yonhap](/wiki/Yonhap "Yonhap") said it was "by turns dramatic, imaginative, humorous and solemn" and "weaved the story of the country's past, present and future". Singapore's _[Straits Times](/wiki/Straits_Times "Straits Times")_ said it was a "grand show" noteworthy for both "scale" and "authenticity". _The Australian_ praised a "glorious pandemonium devoted to London's thriving, chaotic energy ... deliberately revelling in the chaos of Britain's free society and popular culture". France's _[Le Figaro](/wiki/Le_Figaro "Le Figaro")_ said the ceremony reflected "the best contributions that Britain has given to the world ... its sense of humour, its music, and of course sport". The [Canadian Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation "Canadian Broadcasting Corporation") said it was a "rocking, rollicking, sometimes quiet and brooding ceremony". Qatar's _[The Peninsula](/wiki/The_Peninsula_\(newspaper\) "The Peninsula \(newspaper\)")_ said that London did a "spectacular job" making the ceremony a "memorable event".[9]

In an end of the year review, British magazine _[Q](/wiki/Q_\(magazine\) "Q \(magazine\)")_ said "It could all have been so different. As the London 2012 Summer Olympics approached, the tide of scepticism seemed almost irreversible. There was the heavy-handed sponsorship, the draconian security, the ticketing problems, the ballooning budget, and the lurking fear that the Opening Ceremony might be, in director Danny Boyle's pungent description, 'shite'. It took less than four hours on the night of Friday 27 July to turn the whole country around. Not only was the ceremony demonstrably _not_ shite, it was the most surprising, moving, spectacular cultural event this country had ever seen...modern Britain, in all its berserk, multi-faceted glory."[18]

The writer of the ceremony, [Frank Cottrell-Boyce](/wiki/Frank_Cottrell-Boyce "Frank Cottrell-Boyce"), said: "People around us thought it might need defending, so I was told to do press the next morning. I was completely surprised _[by the positive response]_. A lot of people were surprised. But I don't think Danny was surprised. Danny never blinked. At no point did he show any feeling that it was going to be anything but amazing. And he was right."[18]

In December 2012 the culture critic of _The Guardian_ picked the ceremony as "best art event of the year".[183] A British public survey by Samsung voted it the second most inspiring television moment of all time, second only to the [1969 Apollo 11 Moon landing](/wiki/Apollo_11 "Apollo 11").[184] A Digital Spy survey of more than 25,000 overwhelmingly voted the ceremony as the entertainment highlight of 2012.[185] The ceremony was the second most-mentioned entertainment event on the internet in 2012, with just over six million mentions, coming second to the [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards").[186] The BBC reported that it was the most requested item from 2012 on its iPlayer on-demand service, with 3.3 million requests.[187]

Boyle was offered a knighthood in late 2012, but turned it down, saying: "I'm very proud to be an equal citizen and I think that's what the Opening Ceremony was actually about."[188]

## Legacy

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2012_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony&action=edit&section=28 "Edit section: Legacy")]

The ceremony was identified by some commentators as precipitating a new mood in the United Kingdom: it "had barely finished before it had become a byword for a new approach, not only to British culture but to Britishness itself. Politicians would soon be referring to it, using it as shorthand for a new kind of patriotism that does not lament a vanished Britain but loves the country that has changed. Boyle's ceremony was hailed from (almost) all sides...for providing a nation that had grown used to mocking its myriad flaws with a new, unfamiliarly positive view of itself ... It was, perhaps, this lack of cynicism that people responded to ... So used to British irony and detachment, it felt refreshing to witness an unembarrassed, positive case for this country.[21] Boyle himself says this was the most important thing he took away from the Olympic experience: "How important it is to believe in something. You might make a fool of yourself and people will go, 'How can you believe in that, you stupid idiot?' But if you believe in something, you carry people with you." 

Business leaders also took inspiration from the event, admiring its risk-taking[207] and creative freedom, as well as the trust placed in and loyalty inspired from the workers and volunteers.[208] In February 2013, the BBC's Head of Drama Ben Stephenson told an audience of writers, commissioners and producers that he "wanted them to seek inspiration from the opening ceremony of the London Olympics" which, he said, "had scale and brilliance and, above all, had succeeded not in spite of its Britishness but because of its Britishness, delighting viewers here and around the world by rooting itself in the authentic stories and spirit of these islands."[21] [Steve Coogan](/wiki/Steve_Coogan "Steve Coogan") told [Frank Cottrell-Boyce](/wiki/Frank_Cottrell-Boyce "Frank Cottrell-Boyce") that he felt it was "like the emperor's new clothes in reverse ... it made irony and postmodernism feel tired and past its sell-by date", and [Russell T Davies](/wiki/Russell_T_Davies "Russell T Davies") told Boyce: "It changed my idea of the possible."[209]

Reviewing the [2014 Winter Olympics opening ceremony](/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics_opening_ceremony "2014 Winter Olympics opening ceremony") at [Sochi](/wiki/Sochi "Sochi"), Russia, Owen Gibson of _The Guardian_ observed that with his "complex, intimate snapshot of 'who we were, who we are and who we wish to be'", Boyle "rewrote the rule book for opening ceremonies".[210]
