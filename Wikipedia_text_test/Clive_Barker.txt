**Clive Barker** (born 5 October 1952) is a British writer, filmmaker, and visual artist. He came to prominence in the 1980s with a series of short stories collectively named the _[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood "Books of Blood")_ , which established him as a leading horror author. His work has been adapted into films, notably the _[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_\(franchise\) "Hellraiser \(franchise\)")_ series (the first installment of which he also wrote and directed) and the _[Candyman](/wiki/Candyman_\(film_series\) "Candyman \(film series\)")_ series. 

Barker's paintings and illustrations have been shown in galleries in the United States, and have appeared in his books. He has also created characters and series for comic books, and some of his more popular horror stories have been featured in ongoing comics series. 

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Barker was born in [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool "Liverpool") on 5 October 1952.[1] His mother, Joan Ruby (née Revill), was a painter and school welfare officer; his father, Leonard Barker, worked as the personnel director for an industrial relations firm.[2][3] He was educated at [Dovedale Primary School](/wiki/Dovedale_Primary_School "Dovedale Primary School") and [Quarry Bank High School](/wiki/Calderstones_School "Calderstones School") in Liverpool before joining the [University of Liverpool](/wiki/University_of_Liverpool "University of Liverpool"), where he studied English and philosophy.[4] At the age of three, he witnessed the infamous death of French skydiver [Léo Valentin](/wiki/L%C3%A9o_Valentin "Léo Valentin"), who plummeted to the ground during a performance at an air show in Liverpool.[5] He would later allude to Valentin in many of his stories.[6]

## Theatrical work

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Theatrical work")]

Barker's involvement in live theatre began while still in school with productions of _Voodoo_ and _Inferno_ in 1967. He collaborated on six plays with _Theatre of the Imagination_ in 1974 and two more that he was the sole writer of, _A Clowns' Sodom_ and _Day of the Dog,_ for _The Mute Pantomime Theatre_ in 1976 and 1977.[7]

Barker co-founded the avant-garde theatrical troupe The Dog Company in 1978 with former schoolmates and up-and-coming actors, many of whom would go on to become key collaborators in his film work; [Doug Bradley](/wiki/Doug_Bradley "Doug Bradley"), his long-time friend and former classmate at [Quarry Bank High School](/wiki/Calderstones_School "Calderstones School") in Liverpool, took on the now-iconic role of [Pinhead](/wiki/Pinhead_\(Hellraiser\) "Pinhead \(Hellraiser\)") in the _[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_\(franchise\) "Hellraiser \(franchise\)")_ series while Peter Atkins wrote the scripts for the first three _Hellraiser_ sequels.[8] Over the next five years Barker wrote nine plays, often serving as director, including some of his best-known stage productions, _The History of The Devil_ , _Frankenstein in Love_ , and _The Secret Life of Cartoons_.[7]

From 1982 to 1983, he wrote _Crazyface_ , _Subtle Bodies_ , and _Colossus_ for the Cockpit Youth Theatre.[7]

His theatrical work came to a close as he shifted focus to writing the _[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood "Books of Blood")_. 

## Writing career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Writing career")]

Barker is an author of horror and [fantasy](/wiki/Fantasy_fiction "Fantasy fiction"), although he has said that he thinks of his writing less and less as horror.[9] He began writing early in his career, mostly in the form of short stories (collected in _[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood "Books of Blood")_ 1–6) and the [Faustian](/wiki/Faustian "Faustian") novel _[The Damnation Game](/wiki/The_Damnation_Game_\(novel\) "The Damnation Game \(novel\)")_ (1985). Later he moved toward modern-day fantasy and [urban fantasy](/wiki/Urban_fantasy "Urban fantasy") with horror elements in _[Weaveworld](/wiki/Weaveworld "Weaveworld")_ (1987), _[The Great and Secret Show](/wiki/The_Great_and_Secret_Show "The Great and Secret Show")_ (1989), the world-spanning _[Imajica](/wiki/Imajica "Imajica")_ (1991), and _[Sacrament](/wiki/Sacrament_\(novel\) "Sacrament \(novel\)")_ (1996).[10][11]

When _Books of Blood_ was first published in the United States in paperback, [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King") was quoted on the book covers: "I have seen the future of horror and his name is Clive Barker."[12] As influences on his writing, Barker lists [Herman Melville](/wiki/Herman_Melville "Herman Melville"), [Edgar Allan Poe](/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe"), [Ray Bradbury](/wiki/Ray_Bradbury "Ray Bradbury"), [William S. Burroughs](/wiki/William_S._Burroughs "William S. Burroughs"), [William Blake](/wiki/William_Blake "William Blake"), and [Jean Cocteau](/wiki/Jean_Cocteau "Jean Cocteau"), among others.[13]

He is the writer of the best-selling _[Abarat](/wiki/Abarat "Abarat")_ series.[14]

In early 2024, he announced he would stop attending conventions and public events so he could focus more on his writing, as he was working on the manuscripts for 31 different projects, some closer to completion than others.[15]

## Personal life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Barker stated on _[Loveline](/wiki/Loveline "Loveline")_ in 1996 that he had several relationships with older women during his teenage years, but realised he was gay when he was around 18 or 19 years old.[16] He dated John Gregson from 1975 to 1986, and was later in a relationship from 1996 to 2009 with photographer David Armstrong, who was described as his husband in the introduction to _[Coldheart Canyon](/wiki/Coldheart_Canyon "Coldheart Canyon")_. 

During his early years as a writer, Barker occasionally worked as an [escort](/wiki/Escort_agency "Escort agency") when his writing did not provide sufficient income.[17] He has been open about his experiences with [sadomasochism](/wiki/Sadomasochism "Sadomasochism"), calling himself a "six" on its "sliding scale".[18]

In 2003, Barker received the Davidson/Valentini Award at the [15th GLAAD Media Awards](/wiki/15th_GLAAD_Media_Awards "15th GLAAD Media Awards").[19]

Barker is critical of organised religion, but has said that the Bible influences his work and spirituality.[20] In 2017, he clarified on [Facebook](/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") that he did not identify as a Christian.[21]

Barker said in a December 2008 online interview (published in March 2009) that he had throat [polyps](/wiki/Polyp_\(medicine\) "Polyp \(medicine\)") which were so severe that a doctor told him he was taking in only 10% of the air he was supposed to. He has had two surgical procedures to remove them and believes his voice has improved as a result. He said he did not have cancer and has given up cigars.[22]

In 2012, Barker fell into a coma for several days after contracting [toxic shock syndrome](/wiki/Toxic_shock_syndrome "Toxic shock syndrome"), triggered by a visit to a dentist where a spillage of poisonous bacteria entered his bloodstream and almost killed him.[23] Realising he might have just a short time to live, he decided to put his personal concerns about the world and society into the novel _Deep Hill_ , which he thought could be his final book.[24]

As of 2015, Barker is a member of the board of advisers for the Hollywood Horror Museum.[25]

## Film work

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Film work")]

Barker wrote the screenplays for _[Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_\(1985_film\) "Underworld \(1985 film\)")_ (1985) and _[Rawhead Rex](/wiki/Rawhead_Rex_\(film\) "Rawhead Rex \(film\)")_ (1986), both directed by [George Pavlou](/wiki/George_Pavlou "George Pavlou"). Displeased by how his material was handled, he moved to directing with _[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser "Hellraiser")_ (1987), based on his novella _[The Hellbound Heart](/wiki/The_Hellbound_Heart "The Hellbound Heart")_. After his film _[Nightbreed](/wiki/Nightbreed "Nightbreed")_ (1990) flopped, Barker returned to write and direct _[Lord of Illusions](/wiki/Lord_of_Illusions "Lord of Illusions")_ (1995). The short story "[The Forbidden](/wiki/The_Forbidden_\(short_story\) "The Forbidden \(short story\)")", from Barker's _Books of Blood_ , provided the basis for the 1992 film _[Candyman](/wiki/Candyman_\(1992_film\) "Candyman \(1992 film\)")_ and its three sequels. He had been working on a series of film adaptations of his _[The Abarat Quintet](/wiki/The_Books_of_Abarat "The Books of Abarat")_ books under [The Walt Disney Company](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_\(division\) "Walt Disney Studios \(division\)")'s management,[26] but due to creative differences, the project was cancelled.[27]

He served as an executive producer for the 1998 film _[Gods and Monsters](/wiki/Gods_and_Monsters_\(film\) "Gods and Monsters \(film\)")_ ,[28][29] a semi-fictional tale of _[Frankenstein](/wiki/Frankenstein_\(1931_film\) "Frankenstein \(1931 film\)")_ director [James Whale](/wiki/James_Whale "James Whale")'s later years, which won an Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay.[30] Barker said of his interest in the project: "Whale was gay, I'm gay; Whale was English, I'm English…Whale made some horror movies, and I've made some horror movies. It seemed as if I should be helping to tell this story."[31] Barker also provided the foreword on the published shooting script. 

In 2005, Barker and horror film producer [Jorge Saralegui](/wiki/Jorge_Saralegui "Jorge Saralegui") created the film production company Midnight Picture Show with the intent of producing two horror films per year.[32]

In October 2006, Barker announced through his website that he will be writing the script to a forthcoming remake of the original _Hellraiser_ film.[33][34] He was developing a film based on his _[Tortured Souls](/wiki/Tortured_Souls "Tortured Souls")_ line of toys from [McFarlane Toys](/wiki/McFarlane_Toys "McFarlane Toys"). In 2020, Barker regained control of the _Hellraiser_ franchise, and served as executive producer on a [2022 reboot](/wiki/Hellraiser_\(2022_film\) "Hellraiser \(2022 film\)") film for the streaming service [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu "Hulu"). 

## Television work

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Television work")]

In May 2015, [_Variety_](/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\) "Variety \(magazine\)") reported that Clive Barker was developing a television series adaptation of various [creepypastas](/wiki/Creepypasta "Creepypasta") in partnership with [Warner Brothers](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures "Warner Bros. Pictures"), to be called _Clive Barker's Creepypastas_ , a feature arc based on _[Slender Man](/wiki/Slender_Man "Slender Man")_ and _[Ben Drowned](/wiki/Ben_Drowned "Ben Drowned")_.[35] Barker was involved in a streaming service [film adaptation](/wiki/Books_of_Blood_\(film\) "Books of Blood \(film\)") of _The Books of Blood_ in 2020,[36] and is developing a [_Nightbreed_ television series](/wiki/Nightbreed_\(TV_series\) "Nightbreed \(TV series\)") directed by [Michael Dougherty](/wiki/Michael_Dougherty "Michael Dougherty") and written by [Josh Stolberg](/wiki/Josh_Stolberg "Josh Stolberg") for [SyFy](/wiki/SyFy "SyFy").[37][38] In April 2020, [HBO](/wiki/HBO "HBO") was announced to be developing a _Hellraiser_ television series that would serve as "an elevated continuation and expansion" of its mythology with [Mark Verheiden](/wiki/Mark_Verheiden "Mark Verheiden") and [Michael Dougherty](/wiki/Michael_Dougherty "Michael Dougherty") writing the series and [David Gordon Green](/wiki/David_Gordon_Green "David Gordon Green") directing several episodes. Verheiden, Dougherty and Green will also be executive producing the series with [Danny McBride](/wiki/Danny_McBride "Danny McBride"), [Jody Hill](/wiki/Jody_Hill "Jody Hill"), [Brandon James](/wiki/Brandon_James "Brandon James") and [Roy Lee](/wiki/Roy_Lee "Roy Lee") of [Vertigo Entertainment](/wiki/Vertigo_Entertainment "Vertigo Entertainment").[39]

## Visual art

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Visual art")]

Barker is a prolific visual artist, often illustrating his own books. His paintings have been seen first on the covers of his official fan club magazine, _Dread_ , published by [Fantaco](/wiki/Fantaco "Fantaco") in the early 1990s; on the covers of the collections of his plays, _Incarnations_ (1995) and _Forms of Heaven_ (1996); and on the second printing of the original British publications of his _Books of Blood_ series. Barker also provided the artwork for his young adult novel _[The Thief of Always](/wiki/The_Thief_of_Always "The Thief of Always")_ and for the _Abarat_ series. His artwork has been exhibited at Bert Green Fine Art in Los Angeles and Chicago, at the Bess Cutler Gallery in New York and La Luz De Jesus in Los Angeles. Many of his sketches and paintings can be found in the collection _[Clive Barker, Illustrator](/wiki/Clive_Barker,_Illustrator "Clive Barker, Illustrator")_ , published in 1990 by Arcane/Eclipse Books, and in _Visions of Heaven and Hell_ , published in 2005 by Rizzoli Books. 

He worked on the horror video game _[Clive Barker's Undying](/wiki/Clive_Barker%27s_Undying "Clive Barker's Undying")_ , providing the voice for the character Ambrose. _Undying_ was developed by [DreamWorks Interactive](/wiki/DreamWorks_Interactive "DreamWorks Interactive") and released in 2001. He worked on _[Clive Barker's Jericho](/wiki/Clive_Barker%27s_Jericho "Clive Barker's Jericho")_ for [Codemasters](/wiki/Codemasters "Codemasters"), which was released in late 2007. 

Barker created [Halloween](/wiki/Halloween "Halloween") costume designs for Disguise Costumes.[40][41]

Around 150 art works by Barker were used in the set of the Academy of the Unseen Arts for the Netflix TV series [_Chilling Adventures of Sabrina_](/wiki/Chilling_Adventures_of_Sabrina_\(TV_series\) "Chilling Adventures of Sabrina \(TV series\)").[42]

## Comic books

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Clive_Barker&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Comic books")]

Barker published his [Razorline](/wiki/Razorline "Razorline") imprint via [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") in 1993. 

Barker horror adaptations and spin-offs in comics include the Marvel/[Epic Comics](/wiki/Epic_Comics "Epic Comics") series _[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_\(comics\) "Hellraiser \(comics\)")_ , _[Nightbreed](/wiki/Nightbreed_\(comics\) "Nightbreed \(comics\)")_ , _[Pinhead](/wiki/Pinhead_\(comics\) "Pinhead \(comics\)")_ , _The Harrowers_ , _Book of the Damned_ , and _Jihad_ ; [Eclipse Books](/wiki/Eclipse_Comics "Eclipse Comics")' series and [graphic novels](/wiki/Graphic_novels "Graphic novels") _Tapping The Vein_ , _Dread_ , _Son of Celluloid_ , _Revelations_ , _The Life of Death_ , _Rawhead Rex_ and _The Yattering and Jack_ , and [Dark Horse Comics](/wiki/Dark_Horse_Comics "Dark Horse Comics")' _Primal_ , among others. Barker served as a consultant and wrote issues of the _Hellraiser_ anthology comic book. 

In 2005, IDW published a three-issue adaptation of Barker's children's fantasy novel _[The Thief of Always](/wiki/The_Thief_of_Always_\(comics\) "The Thief of Always \(comics\)")_ , written and painted by Kris Oprisko and Gabriel Hernandez. IDW is publishing a 12 issue adaptation of Barker's novel _[The Great and Secret Show](/wiki/The_Great_and_Secret_Show_\(comics\) "The Great and Secret Show \(comics\)")_. 

In December 2007, Chris Ryall and Clive Barker announced an upcoming collaboration of an original comic book series, _Torakator_ , to be published by IDW.[43]

In 2008, Barker authored a foreword for the first volume of the _DEMONICSEX_ comic series by Chuck Conner and Sean Platter.[44][45]

In October 2009, IDW published _Seduth_ , co-written by Barker. The work was released with three variant covers.[46]

In 2011, [Boom! Studios](/wiki/Boom_studios "Boom studios") began publishing an original _Hellraiser_ comic book series. 

In 2013, Boom! Studios announced _Next Testament_ , the first original story by Barker to be published in comic book format.
