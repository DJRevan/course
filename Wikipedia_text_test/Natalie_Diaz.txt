**Natalie Diaz** (born September 4, 1978)[2] is a [Pulitzer Prize](/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize")-winning[3] [Mojave](/wiki/Mohave_people "Mohave people") [American](/wiki/United_States "United States") [poet](/wiki/Poet "Poet"),[4] language activist, former professional basketball player, and educator. She is enrolled in the [Gila River Indian Community](/wiki/Gila_River_Indian_Community "Gila River Indian Community") and identifies as [Akimel O'odham](/wiki/Akimel_O%27odham "Akimel O'odham").[4] She is currently an associate professor at [Arizona State University](/wiki/Arizona_State_University "Arizona State University").[5]

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natalie_Diaz&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Natalie Diaz was born in [Needles](/wiki/Needles,_California "Needles, California"), [California](/wiki/California "California"), on September 4, 1978.[6] She grew up in the [Fort Mojave](/wiki/Fort_Mojave_Indian_Reservation "Fort Mojave Indian Reservation") Indian Village in Needles, California, on the border of California, [Arizona](/wiki/Arizona "Arizona"), and [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada "Nevada"). She attended [Old Dominion University](/wiki/Old_Dominion_University "Old Dominion University"), where she played point guard on the [women's basketball team](/wiki/Old_Dominion_Monarchs_women%27s_basketball "Old Dominion Monarchs women's basketball"), reaching the [NCAA Final Four](/wiki/1997_NCAA_Division_I_women%27s_basketball_tournament "1997 NCAA Division I women's basketball tournament") as a freshman and the bracket of sixteen her other three years. She earned a bachelor's degree.[7] After playing professional basketball in Europe and Asia, she returned to Old Dominion University, and completed an MFA in poetry and fiction,[8] in 2006.[9]

## Career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natalie_Diaz&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

Her work appeared in _Narrative_ ,[10] _[Poetry](/wiki/Poetry_\(magazine\) "Poetry \(magazine\)") magazine_,[11] _Drunken Boat_ ,[12] _[Prairie Schooner](/wiki/Prairie_Schooner "Prairie Schooner")_ , _[Iowa Review](/wiki/Iowa_Review "Iowa Review")_ , and _Crab Orchard Review_. 

Diaz's debut book of poetry, _When My Brother Was an Aztec_ , "portrays experiences rooted in Native American life with personal and mythic power."[13] It was a 2012 Lannan Literary Selection,[14] was shortlisted for the 2013 [PEN/Open Book Award](/wiki/PEN/Open_Book_Award "PEN/Open Book Award"),[15] and was a 2013 [American Book Award](/wiki/American_Book_Awards "American Book Awards") winner.[16] One important focus of the book is a sister struggling with her brother's addiction to crystal meth.[17]

In 2012, she was interviewed about her poetry and language rehabilitation work on the _[PBS NewsHour](/wiki/PBS_NewsHour "PBS NewsHour")_.[18]

In 2018, she was named as the Maxine and Jonathan Marshall Chair in Modern and Contemporary Poetry at Arizona State University.[19]

In 2019, she was faculty at the [CantoMundo](/wiki/CantoMundo "CantoMundo") Retreat.[20]

In 2021, her book _Postcolonial Love Poem_ won the [Pulitzer Prize for Poetry](/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Poetry "Pulitzer Prize for Poetry"). It was called "a collection of tender, heart-wrenching and defiant poems that explore what it means to love and be loved in an America beset by conflict."[21] The book was also a finalist for the 2020 National Book Award, a finalist for the 2020 Los Angeles Times Book Prize, a finalist for the 2020 Forward Prize for Best Collection, and shortlisted for the 2020 T. S. Eliot Prize.[22]

## Personal life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natalie_Diaz&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Diaz currently lives in [Mohave Valley, Arizona](/wiki/Mohave_Valley,_Arizona "Mohave Valley, Arizona"), where she used to work on language revitalization at [Fort Mojave](/wiki/Fort_Mojave_Indian_Reservation "Fort Mojave Indian Reservation"), her home reservation. She worked with the last Elder speakers of the Mojave language. She is enrolled as member of the Gila Indian Community.[6]
