**David Lawrence Schwimmer**[1] (born November 2, 1966)[2][3] is an American actor, director, comedian and producer. He gained worldwide recognition for portraying [Ross Geller](/wiki/Ross_Geller "Ross Geller") in the sitcom _[Friends](/wiki/Friends "Friends")_ (1994–2004), for which he received a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards "Screen Actors Guild Awards") and a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") nomination for [Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series") in 1995. While on _Friends_ , his first leading film role was in _[The Pallbearer](/wiki/The_Pallbearer "The Pallbearer")_ (1996), followed by roles in _[Kissing a Fool](/wiki/Kissing_a_Fool "Kissing a Fool"),_ _[Six Days, Seven Nights](/wiki/Six_Days,_Seven_Nights "Six Days, Seven Nights"),_ _[Apt Pupil](/wiki/Apt_Pupil_\(film\) "Apt Pupil \(film\)")_ (all 1998) and _[Picking Up the Pieces](/wiki/Picking_Up_the_Pieces_\(film\) "Picking Up the Pieces \(film\)")_ (2000). Schwimmer was also in the miniseries _[Band of Brothers](/wiki/Band_of_Brothers_\(miniseries\) "Band of Brothers \(miniseries\)")_ (2001) as [Herbert Sobel](/wiki/Herbert_Sobel "Herbert Sobel"). 

Schwimmer began his acting career performing in school plays at Immanuel College Prep, Bushey England and then Beverly Hills High School. He graduated from [Northwestern University](/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") in 1988 with a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in theater and speech. After graduation, Schwimmer co-founded the [Lookingglass Theatre Company](/wiki/Lookingglass_Theatre_Company "Lookingglass Theatre Company") in Chicago. He later moved back to [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") to pursue his acting career, debuting in the television film _A Deadly Silence_ in 1989 and appeared in a number of television roles in the early 1990s, including _[L.A. Law](/wiki/L.A._Law "L.A. Law")_ , _[The Wonder Years](/wiki/The_Wonder_Years "The Wonder Years")_ , _[NYPD Blue](/wiki/NYPD_Blue "NYPD Blue")_ , and _[Monty](/wiki/Monty_\(TV_series\) "Monty \(TV series\)")_. 

After the series finale of _Friends_ in 2004, Schwimmer branched out into film and stage work. He was cast as the title character in the 2005 drama film _[Duane Hopwood](/wiki/Duane_Hopwood "Duane Hopwood")_ , and voiced [Melman the giraffe](/wiki/List_of_Madagascar_\(franchise\)_characters#Melman "List of Madagascar \(franchise\) characters") in the animated [_Madagascar_ film franchise](/wiki/Madagascar_\(franchise\) "Madagascar \(franchise\)"), acted in the dark comedy _[Big Nothing](/wiki/Big_Nothing "Big Nothing")_ (2006), and the thriller _[Nothing but the Truth](/wiki/Nothing_but_the_Truth_\(2008_American_film\) "Nothing but the Truth \(2008 American film\)")_ (2008). Schwimmer made his [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre "West End theatre") stage debut in the leading role in 2005's _[Some Girl(s)](/wiki/Some_Girl\(s\) "Some Girl\(s\)")_. He made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") debut in _[The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial](/wiki/The_Caine_Mutiny_Court-Martial_\(play\) "The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial \(play\)")_ in 2006. His feature film directorial debut followed in 2007 with the comedy _[Run Fatboy Run](/wiki/Run_Fatboy_Run "Run Fatboy Run")_ , and the following year he made his [Off-Broadway](/wiki/Off-Broadway "Off-Broadway") directorial debut in _Fault Lines_. 

He has also worked as a director, including many episodes of _Friends_ during his time on the series. In 2016, Schwimmer starred as lawyer [Robert Kardashian](/wiki/Robert_Kardashian "Robert Kardashian") in _[The People v. O. J. Simpson](/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story "The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story")_ , for which he received his second Primetime Emmy Award nomination, this time for [Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Limited Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Limited Series or Movie"). In 2025, he starred as botany professor Anthony Brewer in the second season of supernatural horror anthology series _[Goosebumps: The Vanishing](/wiki/Goosebumps:_The_Vanishing "Goosebumps: The Vanishing")_ , earning a [Children's and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Performer](/wiki/Children%27s_and_Family_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Performer "Children's and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Performer") nomination. 

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Schwimmer was born on November 2, 1966 (although some sources say November 12)[4], in [Flushing, Queens](/wiki/Flushing,_Queens "Flushing, Queens"),[5] [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), to attorneys Arthur (1941–2024) and Arlene Coleman-Schwimmer (born 1940).[6][7][8] His family is [Jewish](/wiki/Jews "Jews").[9] He has a sister named Ellie (born 1965).[10] His mother was a high profile divorce attorney who had represented several high profile figures including [Elizabeth Taylor](/wiki/Elizabeth_Taylor "Elizabeth Taylor") and [Rod Stewart](/wiki/Rod_Stewart "Rod Stewart"). His family subsequently moved to [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), where Schwimmer, at 10, had his first acting experience when he was cast as the fairy godmother in a Jewish version of _[Cinderella](/wiki/Cinderella "Cinderella")_.[10] At the age of 12, Schwimmer went to a Shakespeare workshop given by English actor Sir [Ian McKellen](/wiki/Ian_McKellen "Ian McKellen") in Los Angeles.[11] He recalls being riveted by the experience.[11] Schwimmer then entered a contest in the Southern California Shakespeare Festival three years in a row, winning two first prizes.[11][12]

Following his mother's successful career as a divorce lawyer, the family moved to [Beverly Hills](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California "Beverly Hills, California"), where Schwimmer attended [Beverly Hills High School](/wiki/Beverly_Hills_High_School "Beverly Hills High School").[11][12] His classmates included actor [Jonathan Silverman](/wiki/Jonathan_Silverman "Jonathan Silverman"). Schwimmer admitted to feeling like an outsider during his time at the school, recalling, "When I was there I always felt: 'This is not me, I'm surrounded by people with a different value system. And I just wanted to get out of California.'"[11] His best subjects were science and math and he originally wanted to become a doctor.[11] Schwimmer enrolled in a drama class, where he appeared in stage productions. Encouraged by his school drama teacher to further his acting, he flew to Chicago for a summer acting program at [Northwestern University](/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University"). He noted that the experience was both "enlightening and exhilarating".[8] In 1984, Schwimmer graduated from Beverly Hills High and wanted to go straight into acting, but his parents insisted he go to college first so he would have something to fall back on. Schwimmer spent a summer as a [process server](/wiki/Process_server "Process server") for his mother’s law firm.[13][11] Schwimmer enrolled in Northwestern University, where he had attended the summer acting program earlier.[11] He had originally intended on becoming a surgeon, having studied various body systems but eventually decided to pursue acting professionally.[14] At the university, he studied theater and was in an improv group with [Stephen Colbert](/wiki/Stephen_Colbert "Stephen Colbert"), the No-Fun Mud Piranhas.[15] After graduating in 1988, with a Bachelor of Arts degree in theater and speech, Schwimmer co-founded the [Lookingglass Theatre Company](/wiki/Lookingglass_Theatre_Company "Lookingglass Theatre Company").[8] Subsequently, he returned to Los Angeles to pursue an acting career.[10] Schwimmer studied clown under [Philippe Gaulier](/wiki/Philippe_Gaulier "Philippe Gaulier") at [École Philippe Gaulier](/wiki/%C3%89cole_Philippe_Gaulier "École Philippe Gaulier").[16][_[better source needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")_]

## Career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

### 1989–1994: Early work

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 1989–1994: Early work")]

After his supporting role debut in the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") television film _A Deadly Silence_ (1989),[17] Schwimmer followed this with roles on the legal drama _[L.A. Law](/wiki/L.A._Law "L.A. Law")_ in 1992, and the comedy-drama series _[The Wonder Years](/wiki/The_Wonder_Years "The Wonder Years")_.[11] He made his feature film debut in _[Flight of the Intruder](/wiki/Flight_of_the_Intruder "Flight of the Intruder")_ (1991),[10] had a recurring role as a lawyer-turned-vigilante in _[NYPD Blue](/wiki/NYPD_Blue "NYPD Blue")_ before auditioning, unsuccessfully, for a series pilot called _Couples_.[10] He landed his first regular series role as the liberal son of a conservative talk show host ([Henry Winkler](/wiki/Henry_Winkler "Henry Winkler")) in the sitcom _[Monty](/wiki/Monty_\(TV_series\) "Monty \(TV series\)")_.[10]

### 1994–2004: Breakthrough

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 1994–2004: Breakthrough")]

[](/wiki/File:David_Schwimmer_2005_Madagascar.jpg)Schwimmer at the London premiere of _[Madagascar](/wiki/Madagascar_\(2005_film\) "Madagascar \(2005 film\)")_ in 2005

In 1994, Schwimmer was cast as [Ross Geller](/wiki/Ross_Geller "Ross Geller") in [NBC](/wiki/NBC "NBC")'s situation comedy _[Friends](/wiki/Friends "Friends")_ , a series that revolved around a group of friends who live near each other in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"). He played a hopeless-romantic [paleontologist](/wiki/Paleontology "Paleontology") who works at a museum and later becomes a professor at a university. Schwimmer initially turned down the role as Ross, but accepted later.[18] Executive producer [Kevin S. Bright](/wiki/Kevin_S._Bright "Kevin S. Bright") said that he had previously worked with Schwimmer,[19] the character of Ross was written with him in mind, and he was the first actor cast.[18] The show debuted on September 22, 1994, and was watched by almost 22 million American viewers.[20] _Friends_ quickly developed a loyal audience, with the show and Schwimmer receiving strong reviews. Much of the _Friends_ success is attributed to the plotline between his character Ross and his on-again-off-again love interest Rachel, which has been described as one of the greatest TV couples of all time by various media outlets.[21][22] The _[Pittsburgh Post-Gazette](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Post-Gazette "Pittsburgh Post-Gazette")_ was complimentary of Schwimmer, calling him "terrific".[23] _[Variety](/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\) "Variety \(magazine\)")_ 's television reviewer said: "All six of the principals, especially (Courteney) Cox and Schwimmer, appear resourceful and display sharp sitcom skills".[24] For this performance, he earned a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") nomination for [Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series "Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series") in 1995.[25]

Schwimmer starred in his first leading film role in the 1996 romantic comedy film _[The Pallbearer](/wiki/The_Pallbearer "The Pallbearer")_ with [Gwyneth Paltrow](/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow").[26] In the film, Schwimmer plays a man asked to deliver the eulogy for a high school friend he cannot remember, and begins an affair with the friend's mother. Critics dismissed _The Pallbearer_ as a poor imitation of the 1967 film _[The Graduate](/wiki/The_Graduate "The Graduate")_.[27][28] _Variety_ 's film reviewer complimented the actor, writing that he had enjoyed his performance, stating that he displayed "a winning, if rather deadpan, personality along with good comic timing".[29] It also concluded that Schwimmer had a "promising bigscreen future".[29] [Janet Maslin](/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") of _[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ cited that his first film "relegates him to a drab role".[26] When asked why he decided to accept the role, Schwimmer admitted the decision was to "make an effort to find roles that are as far away from the character of Ross as possible."[11] Schwimmer was chosen to play the leading role of [Agent J](/wiki/Agent_J "Agent J") in the 1997 _[Men in Black](/wiki/Men_in_Black_\(1997_film\) "Men in Black \(1997 film\)")_ but he turned it down. He later regretted this decision. Ultimately, [Will Smith](/wiki/Will_Smith "Will Smith") got the role.[30]

His next film roles, in 1998, were _[Kissing a Fool](/wiki/Kissing_a_Fool "Kissing a Fool")_ , _[Six Days, Seven Nights](/wiki/Six_Days,_Seven_Nights "Six Days, Seven Nights")_ , and _[Apt Pupil](/wiki/Apt_Pupil_\(film\) "Apt Pupil \(film\)")_. In _Kissing a Fool_ , a romantic comedy, Schwimmer played Max, a smart-mouthed, but dapper and charming man.[31] [Mick LaSalle](/wiki/Mick_LaSalle "Mick LaSalle") of the _[San Francisco Chronicle](/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle "San Francisco Chronicle")_ wrote, "Fans of the sitcom _Friends_ may be surprised by David Schwimmer in _Kissing a Fool_. [...] Take it from someone who has never seen _Friends_ and comes at Schwimmer with no preconceptions: He does just fine. As a TV sports reporter in _Kissing a Fool_ , he oozes the command and self-satisfaction of a young, successful man."[31] The film was critically and financially unsuccessful.[32] In _Six Days, Seven Nights_ , he played the boyfriend of [Anne Heche](/wiki/Anne_Heche "Anne Heche")'s character. In _Apt Pupil_ , adapted from a [novella of the same name](/wiki/Apt_Pupil "Apt Pupil") by [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King"),[33] he had a supporting role as a school guidance counselor. "I was scared of the part", Schwimmer said, "but I wanted to be part of the movie". At the time, he noted it was a "little frustrating" that people would typecast him due to his role on _Friends_.[34] He subsequently appeared opposite [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen "Woody Allen") and [Sharon Stone](/wiki/Sharon_Stone "Sharon Stone") in [Alfonso Arau](/wiki/Alfonso_Arau "Alfonso Arau")'s straight-to-cable comedy _[Picking Up the Pieces](/wiki/Picking_Up_the_Pieces_\(film\) "Picking Up the Pieces \(film\)")_ (2000).[35]

In 2001, Schwimmer played [Captain Herbert M. Sobel](/wiki/Herbert_Sobel "Herbert Sobel") in [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg "Steven Spielberg") and [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks "Tom Hanks")' [HBO](/wiki/HBO "HBO") [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") [miniseries](/wiki/Miniseries "Miniseries") _[Band of Brothers](/wiki/Band_of_Brothers_\(miniseries\) "Band of Brothers \(miniseries\)")_. The television miniseries is based on the book of the same title written by historian and biographer [Stephen Ambrose](/wiki/Stephen_Ambrose "Stephen Ambrose").[36] Although _Band of Brothers_ was met with largely positive reception,[37] Schwimmer's performance was criticized; the [BBC News](/wiki/BBC_News "BBC News") concluded, "Part of the problem ... may have been the ridiculous fact that _Friends_ favourite David Schwimmer plays the hard and cruel Captain Herbert Sobel. The only thing believable about Schwimmer's acting is when he cowers in the face of true battle. His puppy dog eyes make him appear even more pitiful."[38] Later that year he portrayed [Yitzhak Zuckerman](/wiki/Icchak_Cukierman "Icchak Cukierman") in the war drama _[Uprising](/wiki/Uprising_\(2001_film\) "Uprising \(2001 film\)")_ , based on the true events of the [Warsaw Ghetto Uprising](/wiki/Warsaw_Ghetto_Uprising "Warsaw Ghetto Uprising") in 1943.[39][40]

In March 2004, Schwimmer appeared as himself on HBO's comedy series _[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm "Curb Your Enthusiasm")_.[41] During the lengthy run of _Friends_ , Schwimmer directed ten of the show's episodes.[42][43] The show's tenth and final season ended on May 6, 2004.[44]

### 2004–2010: Directing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 2004–2010: Directing")]

[](/wiki/File:David_Schwimmer.jpg)Schwimmer at the premiere of his directorial debut, _[Run Fatboy Run](/wiki/Run_Fatboy_Run "Run Fatboy Run")_ in 2007

In 2004, Schwimmer was director and executive producer on _Nevermind Nirvana_ , a sitcom about an [Indian American](/wiki/Indian_Americans "Indian Americans") family that was not picked up by NBC.[45][46] After _Friends_ , Schwimmer starred in the 2005 independent drama _[Duane Hopwood](/wiki/Duane_Hopwood "Duane Hopwood")_ , as the title character, who is an alcoholic whose life is spiraling downward rapidly after a divorce and is looking to turn his life around. The film received ambivalent reviews.[47] Despite the reception, Schwimmer's performance was favored by critics; [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") of the _[Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")_ reported that the role was Schwimmer's "career-transforming performance".[48] _Duane Hopwood_ was screened at a special presentation at the 2005 [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival "Sundance Film Festival").[49] Furthermore, in the same year he voiced [Melman](/wiki/Melman_\(Madagascar\) "Melman \(Madagascar\)"), a [hypochondriac](/wiki/Hypochondriac "Hypochondriac") [giraffe](/wiki/Giraffe "Giraffe"), in the animated film _[Madagascar](/wiki/Madagascar_\(2005_film\) "Madagascar \(2005 film\)")_ (2005).[50] _[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ noted that Schwimmer is particularly appealing as Melman.[51] Despite the mixed response from critics,[52] the film was a commercial success, earning US$532 million worldwide,[53] making it one of the biggest hits of 2005.[54]

Schwimmer starred on the London stage in May 2005, with [Catherine Tate](/wiki/Catherine_Tate "Catherine Tate"), [Lesley Manville](/wiki/Lesley_Manville "Lesley Manville"), [Sara Powell](/wiki/Sara_Powell "Sara Powell"), and [Saffron Burrows](/wiki/Saffron_Burrows "Saffron Burrows"), in [Neil LaBute](/wiki/Neil_LaBute "Neil LaBute")'s _[Some Girl(s)](/wiki/Some_Girl\(s\) "Some Girl\(s\)")_ at the [Gielgud Theatre](/wiki/Gielgud_Theatre "Gielgud Theatre").[55] In the production, he plays a teacher who is ready to settle down and marry, but decides to visit four ex-girlfriends first.[56] For his performance, Schwimmer received critical reviews. _[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")_ wrote that Schwimmer "is not called upon to extend his range nearly as far as one might have expected in _Some Girl(s)._ [...] Schwimmer remains bland, competent, and boyish—though not fatally boyish in the manner that appears to have turned these women on."[57] However, [Charles Spencer](/wiki/Charles_Spencer_\(journalist\) "Charles Spencer \(journalist\)") of _[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")_ praised Schwimmer, reporting he "proves inspired casting. He takes to the stage with ... his endearing gaucheness seems designed to ensure our continued sympathy. Schwimmer mercilessly lays bare his character's opportunism, casual cruelties, and chronic self-deception."[57]

In 2006, he made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theater "Broadway theater") debut in [Herman Wouk](/wiki/Herman_Wouk "Herman Wouk")'s two-act play _[The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial](/wiki/The_Caine_Mutiny_Court-Martial_\(play\) "The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial \(play\)")_.[58] Schwimmer played the role of Lieutenant Barney Greenwald in the production, which was directed by [Jerry Zaks](/wiki/Jerry_Zaks "Jerry Zaks").[59] In an interview with _[New York](/wiki/New_York_\(magazine\) "New York \(magazine\)")_ magazine, he revealed that he had wanted to try Broadway, however said "a couple of things came up that just never quite felt right. Either because I liked the play but wasn't hot on the director, or there was another star attached that I wasn't jazzed about working with."[60] He further added that when showed a copy of Wouk's novel "...I was shocked at how good the writing was."[60] His next film role was in the 2006 black comedy _[Big Nothing](/wiki/Big_Nothing "Big Nothing")_ , in which he played a bitter, unemployed scientist.[61]

Schwimmer made his directorial feature debut in the 2007 British comedy film _[Run Fatboy Run](/wiki/Run_Fatboy_Run "Run Fatboy Run")_. The film stars [Simon Pegg](/wiki/Simon_Pegg "Simon Pegg") as an out of shape man who signs up for a marathon to convince his former fiancée and five-year-old son that he has turned his life around.[42] When asked why he decided to direct the film, Schwimmer said: "As a director, I was struck by the challenge that I thought the script presented, which was that it was kind of three films in one. You had some great, big physical comedy, and I thought funny dialogue and characters. And then there was some real emotion to it with the relationship between the father and the son and the romance aspect."[62] _Run Fatboy Run_ garnered mixed reception, with the _[New York Daily News](/wiki/Daily_News_\(New_York\) "Daily News \(New York\)")_ rating it one-and-a-half out of five stars and writing, "Most disappointing is how Schwimmer—who spent 10 seasons on a sitcom filled with hyperverbal characters—manages to bumble 'Fatboy's' tender moments."[63] _[USA Today](/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")_ , however, was favorable towards Schwimmer, reporting he possesses filmmaking finesse "having wisely chosen strong comic material for his debut behind the camera".[64] For his directorial work, he was nominated for a [British Independent Film Award](/wiki/British_Independent_Film_Award "British Independent Film Award") in the category of Best Debut Director.[65]

On November 8, 2007, Schwimmer made a guest appearance in the [second season](/wiki/30_Rock_\(season_2\) "30 Rock \(season 2\)") of the television series _[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock "30 Rock")_ , where he played [Greenzo](/wiki/Greenzo "Greenzo"), an NBC environmental mascot.[66] The following year, he was part of an [ensemble cast](/wiki/Ensemble_cast "Ensemble cast") that included [Kate Beckinsale](/wiki/Kate_Beckinsale "Kate Beckinsale"), [Matt Dillon](/wiki/Matt_Dillon "Matt Dillon"), [Alan Alda](/wiki/Alan_Alda "Alan Alda"), [Angela Bassett](/wiki/Angela_Bassett "Angela Bassett"), and [Noah Wyle](/wiki/Noah_Wyle "Noah Wyle") in the thriller _[Nothing But the Truth](/wiki/Nothing_but_the_Truth_\(2008_American_film\) "Nothing but the Truth \(2008 American film\)")_ (2008).[67] The movie received generally favorable reviews.[68] The success of _Madagascar_ led Schwimmer to return to the role of Melman in the 2008 sequel, _[Madagascar: Escape 2 Africa](/wiki/Madagascar:_Escape_2_Africa "Madagascar: Escape 2 Africa")_. The film earned US$603 million at the international box office.[69] Schwimmer took part in directing in-studio segments for _[Little Britain USA](/wiki/Little_Britain_USA "Little Britain USA")_ , an American spin-off of the British [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC") television series _[Little Britain](/wiki/Little_Britain_\(sketch_show\) "Little Britain \(sketch show\)")_.[70] In regard to this, he commented that he had "a good time directing episodes" for the show.[71]

In October 2008, Schwimmer made his Off-Broadway directorial debut in _Fault Lines_ at the [Cherry Lane Theatre](/wiki/Cherry_Lane_Theatre "Cherry Lane Theatre") in New York.[72] The production won a mixed review from the _[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_ , which wrote: "Based on _Fault Lines_ ... we can't really tell whether Schwimmer has much talent as a director. We're surprised he didn't try something more challenging for his debut. If not much else, Schwimmer has encouraged his actors to intense their energy levels and comic timing at all costs".[72] The _[New York Post](/wiki/New_York_Post "New York Post")_ , however, noted that Schwimmer "knows a thing or two about freewheeling banter ... and for a good while the play crackles with terrific dialogue, expertly delivered".[73] In February 2009, he returned to theater in a Chicago production of [Thornton Wilder](/wiki/Thornton_Wilder "Thornton Wilder")'s three-act play _[Our Town](/wiki/Our_Town "Our Town")_ as George Gibbs at the [Lookingglass Theatre](/wiki/Lookingglass_Theatre_Company "Lookingglass Theatre Company").[74][75] "Schwimmer ... turns in a poignant, richly textured and demonstrably heartfelt performance as George Gibbs. I've seen a fair bit of Schwimmer's post-_Friends_ stage work in London and New York, and I've never seen him better", commented the _[Chicago Tribune](/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_.[74]

On August 2, 2009, Schwimmer played himself in the [sixth season](/wiki/List_of_Entourage_episodes#Season_6_\(2009\) "List of Entourage episodes") of the [HBO](/wiki/HBO "HBO") television series, _[Entourage](/wiki/Entourage_\(U.S._TV_series\) "Entourage \(U.S. TV series\)")_. In the episode, [Ari Gold](/wiki/Ari_Gold_\(Entourage\) "Ari Gold \(Entourage\)")'s ([Jeremy Piven](/wiki/Jeremy_Piven "Jeremy Piven")) agency tries to steer his career back to television.[76] Schwimmer directed his second feature, _[Trust](/wiki/Trust_\(2010_film\) "Trust \(2010 film\)")_ , starring [Clive Owen](/wiki/Clive_Owen "Clive Owen") and [Catherine Keener](/wiki/Catherine_Keener "Catherine Keener"). The film, a drama, is about a family whose teenage daughter becomes victim of an online sexual predator.[77][78] _Trust_ premiered at the [2010 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2010_Toronto_International_Film_Festival "2010 Toronto International Film Festival").[79]

### 2010–present: Return to television

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2010–present: Return to television")]

On January 1, 2011, Schwimmer guest-starred on the British comedy series _[Come Fly with Me](/wiki/Come_Fly_with_Me_\(2010_TV_series\) "Come Fly with Me \(2010 TV series\)")_ starring [Matt Lucas](/wiki/Matt_Lucas "Matt Lucas") and [David Walliams](/wiki/David_Walliams "David Walliams"), whom he directed in _Little Britain USA_.[80] The following year, he returned to voice Melman the Giraffe in _[Madagascar 3: Europe's Most Wanted](/wiki/Madagascar_3:_Europe%27s_Most_Wanted "Madagascar 3: Europe's Most Wanted")_.[5] In 2013, Schwimmer appeared as Josh Rosenthal, a mobster who was brought up by the notorious [Roy DeMeo](/wiki/Roy_DeMeo "Roy DeMeo") and part of the [Gambino Family](/wiki/Gambino_Family "Gambino Family"), alongside [Michael Shannon](/wiki/Michael_Shannon "Michael Shannon") in _[The Iceman](/wiki/The_Iceman_\(film\) "The Iceman \(film\)")_.[35]

In 2014, Schwimmer was cast as the lead in the ABC comedy pilot _Irreversible_ , playing "one half of a somewhat eccentric, self-absorbed couple".[81] In 2016, Schwimmer played [Robert Kardashian](/wiki/Robert_Kardashian "Robert Kardashian") in the first season of the FX anthology series _[American Crime Story](/wiki/American_Crime_Story "American Crime Story")_. He received a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award "Primetime Emmy Award") nomination for his performance.[82] In January 2016, Schwimmer and [Jim Sturgess](/wiki/Jim_Sturgess "Jim Sturgess") were cast to star in the new [AMC](/wiki/AMC_\(TV_channel\) "AMC \(TV channel\)") [crime drama](/wiki/Crime_drama "Crime drama") _[Feed the Beast](/wiki/Feed_the_Beast_\(TV_series\) "Feed the Beast \(TV series\)")_. The series premiered on June 5, 2016, and aired 10 episodes through August before being canceled.[83] In November 2016, it was announced that Schwimmer would star in his first audio series.[84] [Gimlet Media](/wiki/Gimlet_Media "Gimlet Media")'s [podcast](/wiki/Podcast "Podcast") _[Homecoming](/wiki/Gimlet_Media#Homecoming "Gimlet Media")_ began airing on November 16, 2016. 

In April 2017, Schwimmer helped adapt the films of [Sigal Avin](/wiki/Sigal_Avin "Sigal Avin") for an American audience. The six short features depict sexual harassment at work by men on women.[85][86]

In 2020, Schwimmer was cast as a main character in the British sitcom _[Intelligence](/wiki/Intelligence_\(British_TV_series\) "Intelligence \(British TV series\)")_ broadcast on [Sky One](/wiki/Sky_One "Sky One").[87] In the same year, he was a celebrity reader on [CBeebies](/wiki/CBeebies "CBeebies") Bedtime Stories,[88] and was signed as the face of British banking chain [TSB](/wiki/TSB_Bank_\(United_Kingdom\) "TSB Bank \(United Kingdom\)").[89]

Schwimmer featured in [_Jews Don't Count_](/wiki/Jews_Don%27t_Count#Documentary_adaptation "Jews Don't Count"), a 2022 documentary by British Jewish comedian [David Baddiel](/wiki/David_Baddiel "David Baddiel") on the subject of [antisemitism](/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism"). Schwimmer's contribution, in which he said that he has never felt white, was described as "erudite" by Rebecca Nicholson of _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_.[90]

From January 2025, Schwimmer starred in season 2 of the anthology horror series _[Goosebumps](/wiki/Goosebumps_\(2023_TV_series\) "Goosebumps \(2023 TV series\)")_ , based on the [book series of the same name](/wiki/Goosebumps "Goosebumps").[91] He earned a [Children's and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Performer](/wiki/Children%27s_and_Family_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Performer "Children's and Family Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Performer") nomination at the [4th Children's and Family Emmy Awards](/wiki/4th_Children%27s_and_Family_Emmy_Awards "4th Children's and Family Emmy Awards"), competing in the category with former _Friends_ co-star [Lisa Kudrow](/wiki/Lisa_Kudrow "Lisa Kudrow").[92]

## Personal life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=David_Schwimmer&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life")]

Schwimmer dated singer-songwriter [Natalie Imbruglia](/wiki/Natalie_Imbruglia "Natalie Imbruglia") in the late 1990s. He also dated his _Kissing A Fool_ co-star [Mili Avital](/wiki/Mili_Avital "Mili Avital"). Schwimmer and [Jennifer Aniston](/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston "Jennifer Aniston") admitted to having crushes on each other early on while filming _[Friends](/wiki/Friends "Friends")_ during HBO Max's _[Friends: The Reunion](/wiki/Friends:_The_Reunion "Friends: The Reunion")_.[93] Schwimmer began a relationship with British artist [Zoë Buckman](/wiki/Zo%C3%AB_Buckman "Zoë Buckman") in 2007[94] and they married on June 4, 2010. Their daughter Cleo Buckman Schwimmer was born in 2011.[95][96][97][98][99] The couple announced in April 2017 that they were "taking some time apart".[94] They divorced later that year.[100] Schwimmer and his ex-wife are on good terms and continue to [co-parent](/wiki/Coparenting "Coparenting") their child amicably. He tends to keep his personal life away from the media to preserve his daughter's childhood.[95]

Schwimmer primarily lives in [East Village, Manhattan](/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan "East Village, Manhattan"). He gained controversy from neighbors after purchasing the historic property in Manhattan, built in 1852, and subsequently demolishing it.[101] He previously had a [loft](/wiki/Loft "Loft") in [Near West Side, Chicago](/wiki/Near_West_Side,_Chicago "Near West Side, Chicago") as well as a house in [Hancock Park, Los Angeles](/wiki/Hancock_Park,_Los_Angeles "Hancock Park, Los Angeles").[102][103]

In June 2006, Schwimmer won a US$400,000 [defamation](/wiki/Defamation "Defamation") lawsuit against Aaron Tonken, a former charity fundraiser. Tonken claimed Schwimmer had demanded [Rolex](/wiki/Rolex "Rolex") watches in order to appear at his own charity event, a claim that Schwimmer had denied.[104]

Schwimmer is an active director of the Rape Treatment Center in [Santa Monica](/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California"), which specializes in helping victims of [date rape](/wiki/Date_rape "Date rape") and [child rape](/wiki/Child_rape "Child rape").[12] He has also campaigned for legislation to ban drugs such as [Rohypnol](/wiki/Flunitrazepam "Flunitrazepam") and [GHB](/wiki/Gamma-Hydroxybutyric_acid "Gamma-Hydroxybutyric acid").[12] In November 2011, he gave the Scottish charity [Children 1st](/wiki/Children_1st "Children 1st") permission to screen his film _[Trust](/wiki/Trust_\(2010_film\) "Trust \(2010 film\)")_ to commemorate World Day for Prevention of Child Abuse and Violence against Children.[105]

In October 2023, he was one of many Hollywood signatories of a letter calling on [President Joe Biden](/wiki/President_Joe_Biden "President Joe Biden") to work toward the release of all Israeli hostages after the [October 7 attacks](/wiki/October_7_attacks "October 7 attacks").[106][107] In January 2024, he signed an open letter calling on the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") to include Jews in its representation and inclusion standards.[108][109] He has spoken about [antisemitism](/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") at the [Anti-Defamation League](/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League "Anti-Defamation League")'s "Never is Now" conference.[110][111][112]

In 2012, he rebutted two longstanding rumors: one that he appeared as a soldier on a train in _[Biloxi Blues](/wiki/Biloxi_Blues_\(film\) "Biloxi Blues \(film\)")_ (1988), saying, "No. I don't know why that's on [IMDb](/wiki/IMDb "IMDb"), but I never was in that" (the credit has since been removed), and the other that he is related to dancer [Lacey Schwimmer](/wiki/Lacey_Schwimmer "Lacey Schwimmer"), saying, "No, not at all. Please set the record straight. I guess it's a natural assumption because we have the same last name, but no. I've never even met her."[5]
