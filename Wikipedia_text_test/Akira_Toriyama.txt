**Akira Toriyama** ([Japanese](/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 鳥山明, [Hepburn](/wiki/Hepburn_romanization "Hepburn romanization"): _Toriyama Akira_ ; April 5, 1955 – March 1, 2024) was a Japanese [manga artist](/wiki/Manga_artist "Manga artist") and character designer. He is regarded as one of the greatest and most influential authors in the [history of manga](/wiki/History_of_manga "History of manga") and created numerous highly influential and popular series, with his most famous and successful project being the _[Dragon Ball](/wiki/Dragon_Ball "Dragon Ball")_ franchise. 

Toriyama first achieved mainstream recognition for creating the [manga](/wiki/Manga "Manga") series _[Dr. Slump](/wiki/Dr._Slump "Dr. Slump")_ , which was [serialized](/wiki/Serial_\(literature\) "Serial \(literature\)") in [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha")'s _[Weekly Shōnen Jump](/wiki/Weekly_Sh%C5%8Dnen_Jump "Weekly Shōnen Jump")_ magazine. He earned the 1981 [Shogakukan Manga Award](/wiki/Shogakukan_Manga_Award "Shogakukan Manga Award") for best _[shōnen](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnen_manga "Shōnen manga")_ series. Dr. Slump went on to sell over 35 million copies in Japan. It was adapted into an [anime](/wiki/Anime "Anime"), with a second series created in 1997, 13 years after the manga ended. Toriyama wrote and illustrated the [_Dragon Ball_](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(manga\) "Dragon Ball \(manga\)") manga from 1984 to 1995. It became one of the [best-selling manga series](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_manga "List of best-selling manga") of all time, with 260 million copies sold worldwide,[1][a][c] and is considered a key work in increasing manga circulation to its peak in the mid-1980s and mid-1990s. Overseas, _Dragon Ball_ 's anime adaptations have been credited with boosting anime's international popularity. 

Beside his manga works, Toriyama acted as a character designer for numerous video games such as [Square Enix](/wiki/Square_Enix "Square Enix")'s _[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest "Dragon Quest")_ series and _[Chrono Trigger](/wiki/Chrono_Trigger "Chrono Trigger")_ , in addition to _[Blue Dragon](/wiki/Blue_Dragon_\(video_game\) "Blue Dragon \(video game\)")_. 

In 2019, Toriyama was decorated a _Chevalier_ of the French [Ordre des Arts et des Lettres](/wiki/Ordre_des_Arts_et_des_Lettres "Ordre des Arts et des Lettres") for his contributions to the arts. In October 2024, Toriyama was posthumously inducted into the [Harvey Awards](/wiki/Harvey_Awards "Harvey Awards") Hall of Fame.[7]

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

Akira Toriyama was born in the town of [Kiyosu](/wiki/Kiyosu_\(town\) "Kiyosu \(town\)"), [Aichi Prefecture](/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture "Aichi Prefecture"), [Japan](/wiki/Japan "Japan").[8][9] He had a younger sister.[10] Toriyama drew pictures since a young age, mainly of the animals and vehicles that he was fond of. He remembered being blown away after seeing _[One Hundred and One Dalmatians](/wiki/One_Hundred_and_One_Dalmatians "One Hundred and One Dalmatians")_ (1961), and said he was drawn deeper into the world of illustration by hoping to draw pictures that good.[11] He was shocked again in elementary school when he saw the [manga](/wiki/Manga "Manga") collection of a classmate's older brother, and again when he saw a television set for the first time at a neighbor's house.[11] He cited [Osamu Tezuka](/wiki/Osamu_Tezuka "Osamu Tezuka")'s _[Astro Boy](/wiki/Astro_Boy "Astro Boy")_ (1952–1968) as the original source for his interest in manga.[12] Toriyama recalled that when he was in elementary school all of his classmates drew imitating [anime](/wiki/Anime "Anime") and manga, as a result of not having many forms of entertainment.[13] He believed that he began to advance above everyone else when he started drawing pictures of his friends.[13] Despite being engrossed with manga in elementary school, Toriyama said he took a break from it in middle school, probably because he became more interested in films and TV shows.[11] When asked if he had any formative experiences with _[tokusatsu](/wiki/Tokusatsu "Tokusatsu")_ entertainment, Toriyama said he enjoyed the _[Ultraman](/wiki/Ultraman_\(1966_TV_series\) "Ultraman \(1966 TV series\)")_ TV show and _[Gamera](/wiki/Gamera "Gamera")_ series of _[kaiju](/wiki/Kaiju "Kaiju")_ films.[14]

Toriyama said it was a "no-brainer" that he would attend a high school focused on creative design, but admitted he was more interested in having fun with friends.[11] Although he still did not read much manga, he would draw one himself every once in a while. Despite his parents' strong opposition, Toriyama was confident about going into the work force upon graduation instead of continuing his education.[11] He worked at an [advertising agency](/wiki/Advertising_agency "Advertising agency") in [Nagoya](/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya") designing posters for three years.[10] Although Toriyama said he adapted to the job quickly, he admitted that he was often late because he was not a "morning person" and got reprimanded for dressing casually. Resenting the routine, he became sick of the environment and quit.[11]

## Career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]

### Early work and _Dr. Slump_ (1978–1983)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Early work and Dr. Slump \(1978–1983\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Dr_Slump_\(2981906771\).jpg)_[Kanzenban](/wiki/Kanzenban "Kanzenban")_ edition of _[Dr. Slump](/wiki/Dr._Slump "Dr. Slump")_ , published by [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha")

After quitting his job at the age of 23 and asking his mother for money while unemployed, Toriyama entered the manga industry by submitting a work to an amateur contest in [Kodansha](/wiki/Kodansha "Kodansha")'s _[Weekly Shōnen Magazine](/wiki/Weekly_Sh%C5%8Dnen_Magazine "Weekly Shōnen Magazine")_ , which he had randomly picked up in a coffee shop.[11][15] The timing did not line up for that contest, but another manga magazine, _[Weekly Shōnen Jump](/wiki/Weekly_Sh%C5%8Dnen_Jump "Weekly Shōnen Jump")_ , accepted submissions for their Newcomer Award every month. [Kazuhiko Torishima](/wiki/Kazuhiko_Torishima "Kazuhiko Torishima"), who would become his editor, read and enjoyed Toriyama's manga, but it was not eligible to compete because it was a parody of _[Star Wars](/wiki/Star_Wars "Star Wars")_ instead of an original work. Torishima sent the artist [a telegram](/wiki/A_telegram "A telegram") and encouraged him to keep drawing and sending him manga.[15][16] This resulted in _[Wonder Island](/wiki/Wonder_Island "Wonder Island")_ , which became Toriyama's first published work when it appeared in _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ in 1978. It finished last place in the readers survey.[11][15] Toriyama later said that he had planned to quit manga after getting paid, but because _[Wonder Island 2](/wiki/Wonder_Island_2 "Wonder Island 2")_ (1978) was also a "flop", his stubbornness would not let him and he continued to draw failed stories for a year; claiming around 500 pages' worth, including the published _[Today's Highlight Island](/wiki/Today%27s_Highlight_Island "Today's Highlight Island")_ (1979).[11] He said he learned a lot during this year and even had some fun. When Torishima told him to draw a female lead character, Toriyama hesitantly created 1979's _[Tomato the Cutesy Gumshoe](/wiki/Tomato_the_Cutesy_Gumshoe "Tomato the Cutesy Gumshoe")_ , which had some success. Feeling encouraged, he decided to draw another female lead and created _[Dr. Slump](/wiki/Dr._Slump "Dr. Slump")_.[11]

_Dr. Slump_ , which was serialized in _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ from 1980 to 1984, was a huge success and made Toriyama a household name. It follows the adventures of a perverted professor and his small but super-strong robot [Arale](/wiki/Arale_Norimaki "Arale Norimaki").[17] In 1981, _Dr. Slump_ earned Toriyama the [Shogakukan Manga Award](/wiki/Shogakukan_Manga_Award "Shogakukan Manga Award") for best _[shōnen](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnen_manga "Shōnen manga")_ or [_shōjo_ manga](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Djo_manga "Shōjo manga") series of the year.[18] An anime adaptation began airing that same year, during the [prime time](/wiki/Prime_time "Prime time") Wednesday 19:00 slot on [Fuji TV](/wiki/Fuji_TV "Fuji TV"). Adaptations of Toriyama's work would occupy this time slot continuously for 18 years—through _Dr. Slump_ 's original run, _Dragon Ball_ and its two sequels, and finally a rebooted _Dr. Slump_ concluding in 1999. By 2008, the _Dr. Slump_ manga had sold over 35 million copies in Japan.[19]

Although _Dr. Slump_ was popular, Toriyama wanted to end the series within roughly six months of creating it, but publisher [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha") would only allow him to do so if he agreed to start another serial for them shortly after.[20][21] So he worked with Torishima on several [one-shots](/wiki/One-shot_\(comics\) "One-shot \(comics\)") for _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ and the monthly _Fresh Jump_.[22] In 1981, Toriyama was one of ten artists selected to create a 45-page work for _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ 's Reader's Choice contest. His manga _[Pola& Roid](/wiki/Pola_%26_Roid "Pola & Roid")_ took first place.[11] Toriyama was selected to participate in the contest again in 1982 and submitted _[Mad Matic](/wiki/Mad_Matic "Mad Matic")_.[11] His one-shot _[Pink](/wiki/Pink_\(1982_manga\) "Pink \(1982 manga\)")_ was published in the December issue of _Fresh Jump_.[23] Selected to participate in _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ 's Reader's Choice contest for a third time, Toriyama had the bad luck of drawing the first slot and had to work over New Year's on 1983's _[Chobit](/wiki/Akira_Toriyama%27s_Manga_Theater "Akira Toriyama's Manga Theater")_. Angry that it was unpopular, he decided to try again and created _[Chobit 2](/wiki/Chobit_2 "Chobit 2")_ (1983).[11]

An official Toriyama fan club, Akira Toriyama Hozonkai (鳥山明保存会; "Akira Toriyama Preservation Society"), was established in 1982. Its newsletters were called _Bird Land Press_ and were sent to members until the club closed in 1987.[24] Toriyama founded Bird Studio in the early 1980s,[25] which is a play on his name; "tori" (鳥) meaning "bird". He began employing an assistant, mostly to work on backgrounds. 

### _Dragon Ball_ and international success (1983–1997)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Dragon Ball and international success \(1983–1997\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Dragon_Ball_manga_1st_Japanese_edition_logo.svg)_[Dragon Ball](/wiki/Dragon_Ball "Dragon Ball")_ logo

Torishima suggested that, as Toriyama enjoyed [kung fu films](/wiki/Kung_fu_films "Kung fu films"), he should create a kung fu _shōnen_ manga.[26] This led to the two-part _[Dragon Boy](/wiki/Dragon_Boy_\(manga\) "Dragon Boy \(manga\)")_ , published in the August and October 1983 issues of _Fresh Jump_.[23] It follows a boy, adept at martial arts, who escorts a princess on a journey back to her home country. _Dragon Boy_ was well-received and evolved to become the serial _[Dragon Ball](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(manga\) "Dragon Ball \(manga\)")_ in 1984.[20][27] But before that, _[The Adventure of Tongpoo](/wiki/The_Adventure_of_Tongpoo "The Adventure of Tongpoo")_ was published in _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ 's 52nd issue of 1983 and also contained elements that would be included in _Dragon Ball_.[23]

Serialized in _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ from 1984 to 1995 and having sold 159.5 million _[tankōbon](/wiki/Tank%C5%8Dbon "Tankōbon")_ copies in Japan alone,[28] _Dragon Ball_ is one of the [best-selling manga series](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_manga "List of best-selling manga") of all time.[29] It began as an adventure/gag manga but later turned into a martial arts fighting series, considered by many to be the "most influential _shōnen_ manga".[17] _Dragon Ball_ was one of the main reasons for the magazine's circulation hitting a record high of 6.53 million copies (1995).[30][31] At the series' end, Toriyama said that he asked everyone involved to let him end the manga, so he could "take some new steps in life".[32] During that near-11-year period, he produced 519 chapters that were collected into 42 volumes. Moreover, the success of the manga led to five anime adaptations, [several animated films](/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_films "List of Dragon Ball films"), [numerous video games](/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_video_games "List of Dragon Ball video games"), and mega-merchandise. Aside from its popularity in Japan, _Dragon Ball_ was successful internationally as well, including Asia, Europe, and the Americas, with 300–350 million copies of the manga sold worldwide. 

While Toriyama was serializing _Dragon Ball_ weekly, he continued to create the occasional one-shot manga. In 1986, _[Mr. Ho](/wiki/Mr._Ho "Mr. Ho")_ was published in the 49th issue of _Weekly Shōnen Jump_.[23] The following year saw publication of _[Young Master Ken'nosuke](/wiki/Young_Master_Ken%27nosuke "Young Master Ken'nosuke")_ , which had a Japanese _[jidaigeki](/wiki/Jidaigeki "Jidaigeki")_ setting.[23] Toriyama published two _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ one-shots in 1988; _[The Elder](/wiki/The_Elder_\(manga\) "The Elder \(manga\)")_ and _[Little Mamejiro](/wiki/Little_Mamejiro "Little Mamejiro")_.[23] _[Karamaru and the Perfect Day](/wiki/Karamaru_and_the_Perfect_Day "Karamaru and the Perfect Day")_ followed in issue #13 of 1989.[23]

Also during _Dragon Ball_ 's serialization, Torishima recruited him to work as character designer for the 1986 [role-playing](/wiki/Role-playing_video_game "Role-playing video game") video game _[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_\(video_game\) "Dragon Quest \(video game\)")_. The artist admitted he was pulled into it without even knowing what an RPG was and that it made his already busy schedule even more hectic, but he was happy to have been a part after enjoying the finished game.[21] Toriyama continued to work on every installment in the [_Dragon Quest_ series](/wiki/Dragon_Quest "Dragon Quest") until his death. He also served as the character designer for the [Super Famicom](/wiki/Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System "Super Nintendo Entertainment System") RPG _[Chrono Trigger](/wiki/Chrono_Trigger "Chrono Trigger")_ (1995) and for the [fighting](/wiki/Fighting_game "Fighting game") games _[Tobal No. 1](/wiki/Tobal_No._1 "Tobal No. 1")_ (1996) and _[Tobal 2](/wiki/Tobal_2 "Tobal 2")_ (1997) for the [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\) "PlayStation \(console\)").[33]

The September 23, 1988, festival film _Kosuke & Rikimaru: The Dragon of Konpei Island_ marked the first time Toriyama made substantial contributions to an animation. He came up with the original story idea, co-wrote the screenplay with its director [Toyoo Ashida](/wiki/Toyoo_Ashida "Toyoo Ashida"), and designed the characters.[34] It was screened at the Jump Anime Carnival, which was held to commemorate the 20th anniversary of _Weekly Shōnen Jump_.[35]

### Short stories and other projects (1996–2011)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Short stories and other projects \(1996–2011\)")]

A third anime adaptation based on _Dragon Ball_ , entitled _[Dragon Ball GT](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_GT "Dragon Ball GT")_ , began airing in 1996, though this was not based on Toriyama's manga directly. He was involved in some overarching elements, including the name of the series and designs for the main cast.[36] Toriyama continued drawing manga in this period, predominantly one-shots and short (100–200-page) pieces, including _[Cowa!](/wiki/Cowa! "Cowa!")_ (1997–1998), _[Kajika](/wiki/Kajika "Kajika")_ (1998), and _[Sand Land](/wiki/Sand_Land "Sand Land")_ (2000). On December 6, 2002, Toriyama made his only promotional appearance in the United States at the launch of _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ 's North American counterpart, _[Shonen Jump](/wiki/Shonen_Jump_\(magazine\) "Shonen Jump \(magazine\)")_ , in New York City.[37] Toriyama's _Dragon Ball_ and _Sand Land_ were published in the magazine in the first issue, which also included an in-depth interview with him.[38] Toriyama also wrote a short [self-parody](/wiki/Self-parody "Self-parody") of _Dragon Ball_ entitled _[Neko Majin](/wiki/Neko_Majin "Neko Majin")_ , in the form of eight one-shots released sporadically from 1999 to 2005. The eight chapters were collected into a single volume and published in April 2005.[39]

On March 27, 2005, CQ Motors began selling an [electric car](/wiki/Electric_car "Electric car") designed by Toriyama.[40] The one-person QVOLT is part of the company's [Choro-Q](/wiki/Choro-Q "Choro-Q") series of small electric cars, with only 9 being produced. It cost 1,990,000 yen (about $19,000 US), has a top speed of 30 km/h (19 mph) and was available in five colors.[40] Toriyama stated that the car took over a year to design, "but due to my genius mini-model construction skills, I finally arrived at the end of what was a very emotional journey."[40]

He worked on a 2006 one-shot called _[Cross Epoch](/wiki/Cross_Epoch_\(manga\) "Cross Epoch \(manga\)")_ , in cooperation with _[One Piece](/wiki/One_Piece "One Piece")_ creator [Eiichiro Oda](/wiki/Eiichiro_Oda "Eiichiro Oda"). The story is a short [crossover](/wiki/Intercompany_crossover "Intercompany crossover") that presents characters from both _Dragon Ball_ and _One Piece_. Toriyama was the character designer and artist for the 2006 [Mistwalker](/wiki/Mistwalker "Mistwalker") [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360 "Xbox 360") exclusive RPG _[Blue Dragon](/wiki/Blue_Dragon_\(video_game\) "Blue Dragon \(video game\)")_ , working with [Hironobu Sakaguchi](/wiki/Hironobu_Sakaguchi "Hironobu Sakaguchi") and [Nobuo Uematsu](/wiki/Nobuo_Uematsu "Nobuo Uematsu"), both of whom he had previously worked with on _Chrono Trigger_.[41] At the time, Toriyama felt the 2007 [_Blue Dragon_ anime](/wiki/Blue_Dragon_\(TV_series\) "Blue Dragon \(TV series\)") might potentially be his final work in animation.[42]

In 2008, he collaborated with [Masakazu Katsura](/wiki/Masakazu_Katsura "Masakazu Katsura"), his good friend and creator of _[I"s](/wiki/I%22s "I"s")_ and _[Zetman](/wiki/Zetman "Zetman")_ , for the _[Jump SQ](/wiki/Jump_Square "Jump Square")_ one-shot _[Sachie-chan Good!!](/wiki/Sachie-chan_Good!! "Sachie-chan Good!!")_.[43][44] It was later published in North America in the free SJ Alpha Yearbook 2013, which was mailed out to annual subscribers of the digital manga magazine _[Shonen Jump Alpha](/wiki/Shonen_Jump_Alpha "Shonen Jump Alpha")_ in December 2012. The two worked together again in 2009, for the three-chapter one-shot _[Jiya](/wiki/Jiya_\(manga\) "Jiya \(manga\)")_ in _[Weekly Young Jump](/wiki/Weekly_Young_Jump "Weekly Young Jump")_.[45]

Toriyama was engaged by [20th Century Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox") as a creative consultant on _[Dragonball Evolution](/wiki/Dragonball_Evolution "Dragonball Evolution")_ , an American [live-action](/wiki/Live_action "Live action") film adaptation of _Dragon Ball_.[46] The film was released in 2009 and failed both critically and financially. Toriyama later stated in 2013 that he had felt the script did not "capture the world or the characteristics" of his series and was "bland" and not interesting, so he cautioned them and gave suggestions for changes. The film's producers did not heed his advice, "And just as I thought, the result was a movie I cannot call _Dragon Ball_."[47][48] [Avex Trax](/wiki/Avex_Trax "Avex Trax") commissioned Toriyama to draw a portrait of pop singer [Ayumi Hamasaki](/wiki/Ayumi_Hamasaki "Ayumi Hamasaki"), and it was printed on the CD of her 2009 single "[Rule](/wiki/Rule/Sparkle "Rule/Sparkle")", which was used as the theme song to the film.[49]

Toriyama drew a 2009 manga titled _Delicious Island's Mr. U_ for [Anjō](/wiki/Anj%C5%8D,_Aichi "Anjō, Aichi")'s Rural Society Project, a nonprofit [environmental organization](/wiki/Environmental_organization "Environmental organization") that teaches the importance of agriculture and nature to young children.[50] They originally asked him to do the illustrations for a pamphlet, but Toriyama liked the project and decided to expand it into a story. It is included in a booklet about [environmental awareness](/wiki/Environmental_awareness "Environmental awareness") that is distributed by the Anjō city government.[50] As part of _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ 's "Top of the Super Legend" project, a series of six one-shots by famed _Jump_ artists, Toriyama created _Kintoki_ for its November 15, 2010, issue.[51] He collaborated with _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ to create a video to raise awareness and support for those affected by the [2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami "2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami") on March 11, 2011.[52]

### Return to _Dragon Ball_ (2012–2024)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Return to Dragon Ball \(2012–2024\)")]

In 2012, _[Dragon Ball Z: Battle of Gods](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_Battle_of_Gods "Dragon Ball Z: Battle of Gods")_ was announced to be in development, with Toriyama involved in its creation. The film marked the series' first theatrical film in 17 years, and the first time Toriyama had been involved in one as early as the screenwriting stages.[53] The film opened on March 30, 2013. A special "[dual ticket](/wiki/Double_feature "Double feature")" that could be used to see both _Battle of Gods_ and _[One Piece Film: Z](/wiki/One_Piece_Film:_Z "One Piece Film: Z")_ was created with new art by both Toriyama and Eiichiro Oda.[54]

On March 27, 2013, the "Akira Toriyama: The World of _Dragon Ball_ " exhibit opened at the [Takashimaya](/wiki/Takashimaya "Takashimaya") department store in [Nihonbashi](/wiki/Nihonbashi "Nihonbashi"), garnering 72,000 visitors in its first nineteen days. The exhibit was separated into seven areas. The first provided a look at the series' history, the second showed the 400-plus characters from the series, the third displayed Toriyama's manga [manuscripts](/wiki/Manuscript "Manuscript") from memorable scenes, the fourth showed special color illustrations, the fifth displayed rare _Dragon Ball_ -related materials, the sixth included design sketches and animation [cels](/wiki/Cel "Cel") from the anime, and the seventh screened _Dragon Ball_ -related videos.[55] It was there until April 15, when it moved to Osaka from April 17 to 23, and ended in Toriyama's native Nagoya from July 27 to September 1.[55][56]

To celebrate the 45th anniversary of _Weekly Shōnen Jump_ , Toriyama launched a new manga series in its July 13, 2013, issue titled _[Jaco the Galactic Patrolman](/wiki/Jaco_the_Galactic_Patrolman "Jaco the Galactic Patrolman")_.[57] [Viz Media](/wiki/Viz_Media "Viz Media") began serializing it in English in their digital _Weekly Shonen Jump_ magazine, beginning just two days later.[58] The final chapter reveals that the story is set before the events of _Dragon Ball_ and features some of its characters. It would become the final manga that Toriyama wrote and illustrated himself. 

The follow-up film to _Battle of Gods_ , _[Resurrection 'F'](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_Resurrection_%27F%27 "Dragon Ball Z: Resurrection 'F'")_ , released on April 18, 2015, features even more contributions from Toriyama, who personally wrote its original script.[59] Toriyama provided the basic story outline and some character designs for _[Dragon Ball Super](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super "Dragon Ball Super")_ , which began serialization in _[V Jump](/wiki/V_Jump "V Jump")_ in June 2015 with an anime counterpart following in July. Although the anime ended in 2018, he continued to provide story ideas for the manga while [Toyotarou](/wiki/Toyotarou "Toyotarou") illustrated it.[60] _[Dragon Ball Super: Broly](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super:_Broly "Dragon Ball Super: Broly")_ , released in theaters on December 14, 2018, and _[Dragon Ball Super: Super Hero](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super:_Super_Hero "Dragon Ball Super: Super Hero")_ , released on June 11, 2022, continued Toriyama's deep involvement with the films, the latter being his final complete work on the Dragon Ball franchise.[61][62]

In January 2024, a logo Toriyama designed to celebrate the 20th anniversary of his hometown of [Kiyosu](/wiki/Kiyosu "Kiyosu") was unveiled.[63] Toriyama created a new story arc for the 2024 [original net animation](/wiki/Original_net_animation "Original net animation") adaptation of his manga _[Sand Land](/wiki/Sand_Land "Sand Land")_.[64] He also created the story and character designs for the upcoming _[Dragon Ball Daima](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Daima "Dragon Ball Daima")_ anime series.[65] Toriyama's final contribution to _Dragon Ball_ was directing Toyotarou to redraw the end of chapter 103 of _Dragon Ball Super_ , so that a departing [Piccolo](/wiki/Piccolo_\(Dragon_Ball\) "Piccolo \(Dragon Ball\)") appears to wave back at the reader. A short tribute was included at the bottom of the page when it was published on March 28, 2024.[66]

On October 11, 2024, the [Harvey Awards](/wiki/Harvey_Awards "Harvey Awards") announced[67] that Toriyama was one of five comics creators to be inducted into the Harvey Awards Hall of Fame at the 36th annual Harvey Awards ceremony on October 18 at the [New York Comic Con](/wiki/New_York_Comic_Con "New York Comic Con").[7][68] Acknowledging the accolade for Toriyama, Bird Studio Co., Ltd. and Capsule Corporation Tokyo issued a statement saying, "We are very honored to receive the news that Akira Toriyama has been selected for the prestigious Harvey Award Hall of Fame. As a creator, he had always said that his work says it all. We are especially grateful to the American fans for their long-standing enthusiasm and dedicated support. We hope you will continue to support his work for many years to come as we continue to develop and expand upon his masterpieces."[69]

## Personal life and death

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life and death")]

[](/wiki/File:BIRD_STUDIO_2025-11_ac.jpg)Toriyama's house and home studio in [Kiyosu](/wiki/Kiyosu "Kiyosu")

Toriyama married Yoshimi Katō on May 2, 1982.[70][71] She is a former manga artist from Nagoya under the pen name Nachi Mikami,[72] and occasionally helped Toriyama and his assistant on _Dr. Slump_ when they were short on time.[73] They had two children: a son named Sasuke (born 1987)[74] and a daughter named Kikka (born 1990).[75][76] Toriyama lived in his home studio in [Kiyosu](/wiki/Kiyosu "Kiyosu").[25][77] He was a well-known [recluse](/wiki/Recluse "Recluse"), who avoided appearing in public or media.[78][79][80] In an extension to his shyness, Toriyama had used a robot avatar since 1980 to represent himself in manga and interviews.[81]

Toriyama had a love of cars and motorcycles, something he inherited from his father who used to race motorbikes and operated an auto repair business for a brief time, although he did not understand the mechanics himself.[82] He was an animal lover, and had kept birds, dogs, cats, fish, lizards, and bugs as pets since childhood.[82] Some were used as models for characters he created such as [Karin](/wiki/Karin_\(Dragon_Ball\) "Karin \(Dragon Ball\)") and [Beerus](/wiki/Beerus "Beerus"). Toriyama had a lifelong passion for [plastic models](/wiki/Plastic_model "Plastic model"),[82] and designed several for the Fine Molds brand. He also collected autographs of famous manga artists, having over 30 including [Yudetamago](/wiki/Yudetamago "Yudetamago") and [Hisashi Eguchi](/wiki/Hisashi_Eguchi "Hisashi Eguchi"), a hobby he gave to the character [Peasuke Soramame](/wiki/Peasuke_Soramame "Peasuke Soramame").[10][83]

### Death

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Death")]

On March 1, 2024, Toriyama died of an acute [subdural hematoma](/wiki/Subdural_hematoma "Subdural hematoma"), at the age of 68. A funeral was held privately with only his family in attendance.[84][85] His death was announced by his production company Bird Studio one week later on March 8.[86] According to sources close to Toriyama, he had planned to undergo surgery for a [brain tumor](/wiki/Brain_tumor "Brain tumor") in February 2024.[87] The news of his death caused an outpouring of grief among admirers of his works, who took to social media to express their condolences and celebrate his legacy.[88][89] [90] Tributes to the artist were given by _[One Piece](/wiki/One_Piece "One Piece")_ creator [Eiichiro Oda](/wiki/Eiichiro_Oda "Eiichiro Oda"), _[Naruto](/wiki/Naruto "Naruto")_ creator [Masashi Kishimoto](/wiki/Masashi_Kishimoto "Masashi Kishimoto"), _[Bleach](/wiki/Bleach_\(manga\) "Bleach \(manga\)")_ creator [Tite Kubo](/wiki/Tite_Kubo "Tite Kubo"), _[My Hero Academia](/wiki/My_Hero_Academia "My Hero Academia")_ creator [Kōhei Horikoshi](/wiki/K%C5%8Dhei_Horikoshi "Kōhei Horikoshi"), _[Yu Yu Hakusho](/wiki/Yu_Yu_Hakusho "Yu Yu Hakusho") _and _[Hunter × Hunter](/wiki/Hunter_%C3%97_Hunter "Hunter × Hunter")_ creator [Yoshihiro Togashi](/wiki/Yoshihiro_Togashi "Yoshihiro Togashi"), _[Video Girl Ai](/wiki/Video_Girl_Ai "Video Girl Ai")_ creator [Masakazu Katsura](/wiki/Masakazu_Katsura "Masakazu Katsura"), and video game designer [Yuji Horii](/wiki/Yuji_Horii "Yuji Horii"), who worked with Toriyama on _[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest "Dragon Quest")_ and _[Chrono Trigger](/wiki/Chrono_Trigger "Chrono Trigger")_.[91][92] In Tokyo, fans publicly mourned while visiting a life-sized statue of _Dragon Ball_ protagonist [Goku](/wiki/Goku "Goku") located outside the headquarters of toy manufacturer [Bandai](/wiki/Bandai "Bandai").[93][94] Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary [Yoshimasa Hayashi](/wiki/Yoshimasa_Hayashi "Yoshimasa Hayashi") credited Toriyama and his work with playing an "extremely important role in demonstrating Japan's [soft power](/wiki/Soft_power "Soft power")" around the world.[95]

French President [Emmanuel Macron](/wiki/Emmanuel_Macron "Emmanuel Macron") shared a photo of an autographed illustration Toriyama gave him as a gift and paid tribute to him and his fans on social media.[96] French Prime Minister [Gabriel Attal](/wiki/Gabriel_Attal "Gabriel Attal") also paid tribute and lamented that not even "the [Dragon Balls] and [Shenron](/wiki/Shenron "Shenron")" could revive him.[97] The foreign ministries of China and El Salvador issued statements of condolences over Toriyama's death.[98][99] [Justin Chatwin](/wiki/Justin_Chatwin "Justin Chatwin"), who portrayed Goku in the live-action film _[Dragonball Evolution](/wiki/Dragonball_Evolution "Dragonball Evolution")_ , paid respects to Toriyama and apologized for the quality of the film by posting on his [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") story, "sorry we messed up that adaptation so badly".[100] Several Mexican voice actors who dubbed _Dragon Ball_ characters in Spanish for Latin America also lamented Toriyama's death via social media.[101] A large gathering was held at the [Plaza de la Constitución](/wiki/Z%C3%B3calo "Zócalo") in Mexico City, where hundreds of fans did the Genki-dama hand motion (arms up, palm facing the zenith, pooling energy together) to honor the artist.[102] During the 18th [Seiyu Awards](/wiki/Seiyu_Awards "Seiyu Awards") on March 9, a [moment of silence](/wiki/Moment_of_silence "Moment of silence") was held for Toriyama and voice actress [Tarako](/wiki/Tarako "Tarako"), who died on March 4, in recognition of their contributions to the anime industry.[103] On March 10, in Argentina, thousands of fans gathered at the [Obelisco](/wiki/Obelisco_de_Buenos_Aires "Obelisco de Buenos Aires") monument to remember Toriyama.[104] In [Lima](/wiki/Lima "Lima"), Peru, over 40 artists led by "Peko" painted a mural tribute to Toriyama, which showcases characters from _Dragon Ball_ as well as Toriyama himself, spanning six meters high and over 110 meters long.[105][106]

## Style

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Style")]

Toriyama admired [Osamu Tezuka](/wiki/Osamu_Tezuka "Osamu Tezuka")'s _[Astro Boy](/wiki/Astro_Boy "Astro Boy")_ and was impressed by [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney "Walt Disney")'s _[One Hundred and One Dalmatians](/wiki/One_Hundred_and_One_Dalmatians "One Hundred and One Dalmatians")_ , which he remembered for its high-quality animation.[16][107] He was a fan of [Hong Kong martial arts films](/wiki/Hong_Kong_martial_arts_films "Hong Kong martial arts films"), especially [Bruce Lee](/wiki/Bruce_Lee "Bruce Lee") films such as _[Enter the Dragon](/wiki/Enter_the_Dragon "Enter the Dragon")_ (1973) and [Jackie Chan](/wiki/Jackie_Chan "Jackie Chan") films such as _[Drunken Master](/wiki/Drunken_Master "Drunken Master")_ (1978), which went on to have a large influence on his later work.[108][109][110] He also cited the [science fiction](/wiki/Science_fiction "Science fiction") films _[Alien](/wiki/Alien_\(film\) "Alien \(film\)")_ (1979) and _[Galaxy Quest](/wiki/Galaxy_Quest "Galaxy Quest")_ (1999) as influences.[111] Toriyama stated he was influenced by animator [Toyoo Ashida](/wiki/Toyoo_Ashida "Toyoo Ashida") and the anime television series adaptation of his own _Dragon Ball_ , from which he learned that separating colors instead of blending them makes the art cleaner and coloring illustrations easier.[107]

It was Toriyama's sound effects in _Mysterious Rain Jack_ that caught the eye of [Kazuhiko Torishima](/wiki/Kazuhiko_Torishima "Kazuhiko Torishima"), who explained that usually they are written in _[katakana](/wiki/Katakana "Katakana")_ , but Toriyama used the [Roman alphabet](/wiki/Roman_alphabet "Roman alphabet"), which he found refreshing.[112] Torishima has stated that Toriyama aimed to be a gag manga artist because the competitions that he submitted to early on required entries in the gag category to only be 15 pages long, compared to story manga entries which had to be 31.[15] In his opinion, Toriyama excelled in black and white, utilizing black areas as a result of not having had the money to buy [screentone](/wiki/Screentone "Screentone") when he started drawing manga.[112] He also described Toriyama as a master of convenience and "sloppy, but in a good way." For instance, in _Dragon Ball_ , destroying scenery in the environment and giving Super Saiyans blond hair were done in order to have less work in drawing and inking. Torishima claimed that Toriyama drew what he found interesting and was not mindful of what his readers thought,[113] nor did he get much inspiration from other comics, as he chose not to re-read previous works or read manga made by other artists, a practice that Torishima supported.[114] Furthermore, the book _A History of Modern Manga_ (2023) describes Toriyama as "a perfectionist at heart" who "didn't hesitate to redraw entire panels under the worried eye of his editor at _Jump_ ".[115]

_Dr. Slump_ is mainly a comedy series, filled with [puns](/wiki/Pun "Pun"), [toilet humor](/wiki/Off-color_humor "Off-color humor"), and sexual innuendos. But it also contained many science fiction elements: aliens, [anthropomorphic](/wiki/Anthropomorphism "Anthropomorphism") characters, [time travel](/wiki/Time_travel "Time travel"), and parodies of works such as [Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla "Godzilla"), _[Star Wars](/wiki/Star_Wars "Star Wars"),_ and _[Star Trek](/wiki/Star_Trek "Star Trek")_.[17] Toriyama also included many real-life people in the series, such as his assistants, wife, and colleagues (such as [Masakazu Katsura](/wiki/Masakazu_Katsura "Masakazu Katsura")), but most notably his editor Kazuhiko Torishima as the series' main antagonist, [Dr. Mashirito](/wiki/Dr._Mashirito "Dr. Mashirito").[17][116] A running gag in _Dr. Slump_ that utilizes feces has been reported as an inspiration for the [Pile of Poo emoji](/wiki/Pile_of_Poo_emoji "Pile of Poo emoji").[117][118]

When _Dragon Ball_ began, it was loosely based on the classic Chinese novel _[Journey to the West](/wiki/Journey_to_the_West "Journey to the West")_ ,[27][119] with [Goku](/wiki/Goku "Goku") being [Sun Wukong](/wiki/Sun_Wukong "Sun Wukong") and [Bulma](/wiki/Bulma "Bulma") as [Tang Sanzang](/wiki/Tang_Sanzang "Tang Sanzang"). It was also inspired by Hong Kong martial arts films,[120] particularly those of Jackie Chan,[121] and was set in a fictional world based on Asia, taking inspiration from several [Asian cultures](/wiki/Culture_of_Asia "Culture of Asia") including [Japanese](/wiki/Culture_of_Japan "Culture of Japan"), [Chinese](/wiki/Chinese_culture "Chinese culture"), [Indian](/wiki/Culture_of_India "Culture of India"), [Central Asian](/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), [Arabic](/wiki/Arabic_culture "Arabic culture"), and [Indonesian cultures](/wiki/Indonesian_culture "Indonesian culture").[17][122] Toriyama continued to use his characteristic comedic style in the beginning, but over the course of serialization this slowly changed, with him turning the series into a "nearly-pure fighting manga" later on.[17] He did not plan out in advance what would happen in the series, instead choosing to draw as he went. This, coupled with him simply forgetting things he had already drawn, caused him to find himself in situations that he had to write himself out of.[17]

Toriyama was commissioned to illustrate the characters and monsters for the first _[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_\(video_game\) "Dragon Quest \(video game\)")_ video game (1986) in order to separate it from other role-playing games of the time.[123] He worked on every installment in [the series](/wiki/Dragon_Quest "Dragon Quest") until he died. For each game [Yuji Horii](/wiki/Yuji_Horii "Yuji Horii") first sends rough sketches of the characters with their background information to Toriyama, who then re-draws them. Lastly, Horii approves the finished work.[124][125] Toriyama explained in 1995 that for video games, because the [sprites](/wiki/Sprite_\(computer_graphics\) "Sprite \(computer graphics\)") are so small, as long as they have a distinguishing feature so people can tell which character it is, he can make complex designs without concern of having to reproduce it like he usually would in manga.[126] Besides the character and monster designs, Toriyama also does the games' packaging art and, for _[Dragon Quest VIII](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_VIII "Dragon Quest VIII")_ , the boats and ships.[125] In 2016, Toriyama revealed that because of the series' established time period and setting, his artistic options are limited, which makes every iteration harder to design for than the last.[79] The series' [Slime](/wiki/Slime_\(Dragon_Quest\) "Slime \(Dragon Quest\)") character, which has become a mascot for the franchise, is considered to be one of the most recognizable figures in gaming.[127]

Manga critic [Jason Thompson](/wiki/Jason_Thompson_\(writer\) "Jason Thompson \(writer\)") declared Toriyama's art influential, saying that his "extremely personal and recognizable style" was a reason for _Dragon Ball'_ s popularity.[17] He points out that the popular _shōnen_ manga of the late 1980s and early 1990s had "[manly](/wiki/Masculinity "Masculinity")" heroes, such as _[City Hunter](/wiki/City_Hunter "City Hunter")_ and _[Fist of the North Star](/wiki/Fist_of_the_North_Star "Fist of the North Star")_ , whereas _Dragon Ball_ starred the cartoonish and small Goku, thus starting a trend that Thompson says continues to this day.[17] Toriyama himself said he went against the normal convention that the strongest characters should be the largest in terms of physical size, designing many of the series' most powerful characters with small statures.[128] Thompson concluded his analysis by saying that only Akira Toriyama drew like this at the time and that _Dragon Ball_ is "an action manga drawn by a gag manga artist."[17] James S. Yadao, author of _The Rough Guide to Manga_ , points out that an art shift does occur in the series, as the characters gradually "lose the rounded, innocent look that [Toriyama] established in _Dr. Slump_ and gain sharper angles that leap off the page with their energy and intensity."[129]

## Legacy and accolades

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Legacy and accolades")]

See also: [Cultural impact of _Dragon Ball_](/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_Dragon_Ball "Cultural impact of Dragon Ball")

> The role of my manga is to be a work of entertainment through and through. I dare say I don't care even if [my works] have left nothing behind, as long as they have entertained their readers. 

— Akira Toriyama, 2013[130]

Patrick St. Michel of _[The Japan Times](/wiki/The_Japan_Times "The Japan Times")_ compared Toriyama to animator [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney "Walt Disney") and [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") creator [Stan Lee](/wiki/Stan_Lee "Stan Lee"): "All three of these individuals, Toriyama included, had a personal artistic style that has become the shorthand for their respective media."[131] Speaking of _Dragon Ball_ , David Brothers of [ComicsAlliance](/wiki/ComicsAlliance "ComicsAlliance") wrote that: "Like Osamu Tezuka and [Jack Kirby](/wiki/Jack_Kirby "Jack Kirby") before him, Toriyama created a story with his own two hands that seeped deep into the hearts of his readers, creating a love for both the cast and the medium at the same time."[132] Thompson stated in 2011 that "_Dragon Ball_ is by far the most influential shonen manga of the last 30 years, and today, almost every _Shōnen Jump_ artist lists it as one of their favorites and lifts from it in various ways."[17] Patrick W. Galbraith, an associate professor at the School of International Communication at [Senshu University](/wiki/Senshu_University "Senshu University"), similarly said, "One can sense the DNA of Toriyama's work in all subsequent shōnen releases."[131]

In a rare 2013 interview, commenting on _Dragon Ball_ 's global success, Toriyama admitted, "Frankly, I don't quite understand why it happened. While the manga was being serialized, the only thing I wanted as I kept drawing was to make Japanese boys happy."[130] He had previously stated in 2010, "The truth is, I didn't like being a manga artist very much. It wasn't until relatively recently that I realized it's a wonderful job."[111] Many artists have named Toriyama and _Dragon Ball_ as influences, including _[One Piece](/wiki/One_Piece "One Piece")_ author [Eiichiro Oda](/wiki/Eiichiro_Oda "Eiichiro Oda"),[133] _[Naruto](/wiki/Naruto "Naruto")_ creator [Masashi Kishimoto](/wiki/Masashi_Kishimoto "Masashi Kishimoto"),[134] _[Fairy Tail](/wiki/Fairy_Tail "Fairy Tail")_ and _[Rave](/wiki/Rave_Master "Rave Master")_ author [Hiro Mashima](/wiki/Hiro_Mashima "Hiro Mashima"),[135] _[Boruto: Naruto Next Generations](/wiki/Boruto:_Naruto_Next_Generations "Boruto: Naruto Next Generations")_ illustrator [Mikio Ikemoto](/wiki/Mikio_Ikemoto "Mikio Ikemoto"),[136] _[Venus Versus Virus](/wiki/Venus_Versus_Virus "Venus Versus Virus")_ author [Atsushi Suzumi](/wiki/Atsushi_Suzumi "Atsushi Suzumi"),[137] _[Bleach](/wiki/Bleach_\(manga\) "Bleach \(manga\)")_ creator [Tite Kubo](/wiki/Tite_Kubo "Tite Kubo"), _[Black Cat](/wiki/Black_Cat_\(manga\) "Black Cat \(manga\)")_ author [Kentaro Yabuki](/wiki/Kentaro_Yabuki "Kentaro Yabuki"), and _[Mr. Fullswing](/wiki/Mr._Fullswing "Mr. Fullswing")_ author Shinya Suzuki.[138] German comic book artist [Hans Steinbach](/wiki/Hans_Steinbach "Hans Steinbach") was strongly influenced by Toriyama,[139] and Thai cartoonist [Wisut Ponnimit](/wiki/Wisut_Ponnimit "Wisut Ponnimit") cited Toriyama as one of his favorite cartoonists.[140] St. Michel wrote that the impact Toriyama and _Dragon Ball_ had extends beyond inspiring newer artists, "he influenced the style of anime as a whole and revealed new economic potential, as the comic series mutated into an anime, video games and infinite merchandise."[131] [Ian Jones-Quartey](/wiki/Ian_Jones-Quartey "Ian Jones-Quartey"), a producer of the American animated series _[Steven Universe](/wiki/Steven_Universe "Steven Universe")_ , is a fan of both _Dragon Ball_ and _Dr. Slump_ , and uses Toriyama's vehicle designs as reference for his own. He also stated that "We're all big Toriyama fans on [_Steven Universe_], which kind of shows a bit."[141] French director [Pierre Perifel](/wiki/Pierre_Perifel "Pierre Perifel") cited Toriyama and _Dragon Ball_ as influences on his [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation "DreamWorks Animation") film _[The Bad Guys](/wiki/The_Bad_Guys_\(film\) "The Bad Guys \(film\)")_.[142]

In 2008, [Oricon](/wiki/Oricon "Oricon") conducted a poll of people's favorite manga artists, with Toriyama coming in second, behind only _[Nana](/wiki/Nana_\(manga\) "Nana \(manga\)")_ author [Ai Yazawa](/wiki/Ai_Yazawa "Ai Yazawa"). He was number one among male respondents and among those over 30 years of age.[143] They held a poll on the Mangaka that Changed the History of Manga in 2010, _mangaka_ being the Japanese word for a manga artist. Toriyama came in second, after only Osamu Tezuka, due to his works being highly influential and popular worldwide.[144] Toriyama won the Special 40th Anniversary Festival Award at the 2013 [Angoulême International Comics Festival](/wiki/Angoul%C3%AAme_International_Comics_Festival "Angoulême International Comics Festival"), honoring his years in cartooning.[145][146] He actually received the most votes for the festival's [Grand Prix de la ville d'Angoulême](/wiki/Grand_Prix_de_la_ville_d%27Angoul%C3%AAme "Grand Prix de la ville d'Angoulême") award that year, though the selection committee chose [Willem](/wiki/Bernard_Willem_Holtrop "Bernard Willem Holtrop") as the recipient.[147] In a 2014 [NTT Docomo](/wiki/NTT_Docomo "NTT Docomo") poll for the manga artist that best represents Japan, Toriyama came in third place.[148] That same year, entomologist Enio B. Cano named a new species of [beetle](/wiki/Beetle "Beetle"), _Ogyges toriyamai_ , after Toriyama, and another, _Ogyges mutenroshii_ , after the _Dragon Ball_ character [Muten Roshi](/wiki/Muten_Roshi "Muten Roshi").[149] Toriyama was decorated a _Chevalier_ or "Knight" of the [Ordre des Arts et des Lettres](/wiki/Ordre_des_Arts_et_des_Lettres "Ordre des Arts et des Lettres") by the French government on May 30, 2019, for his contributions to the arts.[78][150] He was also a 2019 nominee for entry into the [Will Eisner Hall of Fame](/wiki/Will_Eisner_Hall_of_Fame "Will Eisner Hall of Fame").[151] Toriyama was honored with a Lifetime Achievement Award at the 2024 [Tokyo Anime Awards Festival](/wiki/Tokyo_Anime_Awards_Festival "Tokyo Anime Awards Festival").[152] Due to his video game design work, _[IGN](/wiki/IGN "IGN")_ named Toriyama number 74 on their list of the Top 100 Game Creators of All Time.[33]

## Works

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Akira_Toriyama&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Works")]

Main article: [List of works by Akira Toriyama](/wiki/List_of_works_by_Akira_Toriyama "List of works by Akira Toriyama")

Besides _[Dr. Slump](/wiki/Dr._Slump "Dr. Slump")_ (1980–1984) and _[Dragon Ball](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(manga\) "Dragon Ball \(manga\)")_ (1984–1995), Toriyama predominantly drew one-shot manga and short (100–200-page) pieces, including _[Pink](/wiki/Pink_\(1982_manga\) "Pink \(1982 manga\)")_ (1982), _[Go! Go! Ackman](/wiki/Go!_Go!_Ackman "Go! Go! Ackman")_ (1993–1994), _[Cowa!](/wiki/Cowa! "Cowa!")_ (1997–1998), _[Kajika](/wiki/Kajika "Kajika")_ (1998), _[Sand Land](/wiki/Sand_Land "Sand Land")_ (2000) and _[Jaco the Galactic Patrolman](/wiki/Jaco_the_Galactic_Patrolman "Jaco the Galactic Patrolman")_ (2013). Many of his one-shots were collected in his three-volume anthology series, _[Akira Toriyama's Manga Theater](/wiki/Akira_Toriyama%27s_Manga_Theater "Akira Toriyama's Manga Theater")_ (1983–1997). He also collaborated with other manga artists, such as Katsura and Oda,[153][154] to produce one-shots and crossover shorts. 

Toriyama also created many character designs for various video games such as the _[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest "Dragon Quest")_ series (1986–2023), _[Chrono Trigger](/wiki/Chrono_Trigger "Chrono Trigger")_ (1995), _[Blue Dragon](/wiki/Blue_Dragon_\(video_game\) "Blue Dragon \(video game\)")_ (2006), and some [_Dragon Ball_ video games](/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_video_games "List of Dragon Ball video games"). He also designed several characters and mascots for various [manga magazines](/wiki/Manga_magazines "Manga magazines") property of [Shueisha](/wiki/Shueisha "Shueisha"), his career-long employer and Japan's largest publishing company.[155][156][157]

Besides manga-related works, Toriyama also created various illustrations, album and book covers, model kits, mascots and logos.[158][159][160] For example, he sketched several versions of the _[Dragon Ball Z](/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z "Dragon Ball Z")_ logo, which [Toei Animation](/wiki/Toei_Animation "Toei Animation") then refined into a definitive design.[161]
