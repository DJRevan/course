The **Academy Award for Best Director** (officially known as the **Academy Award of Merit for Directing**) is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") (AMPAS). It is given in honor of a [film director](/wiki/Film_director "Film director") who has exhibited outstanding directing while working in the [film industry](/wiki/Film_industry "Film industry"). 

The [1st Academy Awards](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards "1st Academy Awards") ceremony was held in 1929 with the award being split into "Dramatic" and "Comedy" categories; [Frank Borzage](/wiki/Frank_Borzage "Frank Borzage") and [Lewis Milestone](/wiki/Lewis_Milestone "Lewis Milestone") won for _[7th Heaven](/wiki/7th_Heaven_\(1927_film\) "7th Heaven \(1927 film\)")_ and _[Two Arabian Knights](/wiki/Two_Arabian_Knights "Two Arabian Knights")_ , respectively.[1] However, these categories were merged for all subsequent ceremonies.[2] Nominees are determined by [single transferable vote](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") within the directors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a [plurality vote](/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\) "Plurality \(voting\)") from the entire eligible voting members of the academy.[3][4][5]

For the first eleven years of the Academy Awards, directors were allowed to be nominated for multiple films in the same year. However, after the nomination of [Michael Curtiz](/wiki/Michael_Curtiz "Michael Curtiz") for two films, _[Angels with Dirty Faces](/wiki/Angels_with_Dirty_Faces "Angels with Dirty Faces")_ and _[Four Daughters](/wiki/Four_Daughters_\(1938_film\) "Four Daughters \(1938 film\)")_ , at the [11th Academy Awards](/wiki/11th_Academy_Awards "11th Academy Awards"), the rules were revised so that an individual could only be nominated for one film at each ceremony.[6] That rule has since been amended, although the only director who has received multiple nominations in the same year was [Steven Soderbergh](/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh "Steven Soderbergh") for _[Erin Brockovich](/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_\(film\) "Erin Brockovich \(film\)")_ and _[Traffic](/wiki/Traffic_\(2000_film\) "Traffic \(2000 film\)")_ in [2000](/wiki/73rd_Academy_Awards "73rd Academy Awards"), winning the award for the latter. 

The Academy Awards for Best Director and [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") have been very closely linked throughout their history. Of the 92 films that won Best Picture and were also nominated for Best Director, 71 won the award.[7][8] The award has been criticised in recent years for failing to recognise female directors.[9] Of the 260 individual directors nominated in the history of the award, only 9 have been women,[10] with only 2 women having been nominated more than once (compared to 101 men),[11] and only 3 of the 77 individual winners have been women.[12]

Since its inception, the award has been given to 75 different directors or directing teams. As of the [98th Academy Awards](/wiki/98th_Academy_Awards "98th Academy Awards") ceremony, American filmmaker [Paul Thomas Anderson](/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson "Paul Thomas Anderson") is the most recent winner in this category for his work on _[One Battle After Another](/wiki/One_Battle_After_Another "One Battle After Another")_.

## Records

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Director&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Records")]

  * [John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford "John Ford") has received the most awards in this category, with four. [Frank Capra](/wiki/Frank_Capra "Frank Capra") and [William Wyler](/wiki/William_Wyler "William Wyler") won three each. 
    * Wyler has the most nominations, with 12—including a record four years in a row. [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese "Martin Scorsese") is currently second, with 10 nominations.
    * [Clarence Brown](/wiki/Clarence_Brown "Clarence Brown") has the most nominations without a win (6). [Alfred Hitchcock](/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock"), [King Vidor](/wiki/King_Vidor "King Vidor"), and [Robert Altman](/wiki/Robert_Altman "Robert Altman") each received 5 nominations without a win.
  * Four directors have won twice for films that did not win [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"): [Frank Borzage](/wiki/Frank_Borzage "Frank Borzage"), [George Stevens](/wiki/George_Stevens "George Stevens"), [Ang Lee](/wiki/Ang_Lee "Ang Lee"), and [Alfonso Cuarón](/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n "Alfonso Cuarón"). 
    * Of John Ford's four wins, the only film which also won Best Picture was _[How Green Was My Valley](/wiki/How_Green_Was_My_Valley_\(film\) "How Green Was My Valley \(film\)")_ (1941).
    * Ford (1940–1941), [Joseph L. Mankiewicz](/wiki/Joseph_L._Mankiewicz "Joseph L. Mankiewicz") (1949–1950), and [Alejandro González Iñárritu](/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu "Alejandro González Iñárritu") (2014–2015) are the only directors to have won the award in two consecutive years.
    * [Francis Ford Coppola](/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola "Francis Ford Coppola") is the only director to be nominated for each film of a trilogy, [The Godfather trilogy](/wiki/The_Godfather_\(film_series\) "The Godfather \(film series\)"), winning for the [second film](/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II "The Godfather Part II").
  * Four directing teams have been nominated together (a total of five times, winning on three occasions): [Robert Wise](/wiki/Robert_Wise "Robert Wise") and [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins "Jerome Robbins") for _[West Side Story](/wiki/West_Side_Story_\(1961_film\) "West Side Story \(1961 film\)")_ (1961, winners); [Warren Beatty](/wiki/Warren_Beatty "Warren Beatty") and [Buck Henry](/wiki/Buck_Henry "Buck Henry") for _[Heaven Can Wait](/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_\(1978_film\) "Heaven Can Wait \(1978 film\)")_ (1978); [Joel and Ethan Coen](/wiki/Coen_brothers "Coen brothers") for _[No Country for Old Men](/wiki/No_Country_for_Old_Men "No Country for Old Men")_ (2007, winners) and _[True Grit](/wiki/True_Grit_\(2010_film\) "True Grit \(2010 film\)")_ (2010); and [Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert](/wiki/Daniels_\(directors\) "Daniels \(directors\)") for _[Everything Everywhere All at Once](/wiki/Everything_Everywhere_All_at_Once "Everything Everywhere All at Once")_ (2022, winners). 
    * The Coen Brothers are the only siblings to have won the award.
  * Six directors won the award for their feature film debut: [Delbert Mann](/wiki/Delbert_Mann "Delbert Mann") for _[Marty](/wiki/Marty_\(film\) "Marty \(film\)")_ (1955), [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins "Jerome Robbins") for _[West Side Story](/wiki/West_Side_Story_\(1961_film\) "West Side Story \(1961 film\)")_ (1961), [Robert Redford](/wiki/Robert_Redford "Robert Redford") for _[Ordinary People](/wiki/Ordinary_People "Ordinary People")_ (1980), [James L. Brooks](/wiki/James_L._Brooks "James L. Brooks") for _[Terms of Endearment](/wiki/Terms_of_Endearment "Terms of Endearment")_ (1983), [Kevin Costner](/wiki/Kevin_Costner "Kevin Costner") for _[Dances With Wolves](/wiki/Dances_With_Wolves "Dances With Wolves")_ (1990), and [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes "Sam Mendes") for _[American Beauty](/wiki/American_Beauty_\(1999_film\) "American Beauty \(1999 film\)")_ (1999). 
    * Robbins is the only director to have won for his only career directing credit.
  * [Lina Wertmüller](/wiki/Lina_Wertm%C3%BCller "Lina Wertmüller") was the first woman nominated in the category, for _[Seven Beauties](/wiki/Seven_Beauties "Seven Beauties")_ (1976). 
    * [Kathryn Bigelow](/wiki/Kathryn_Bigelow "Kathryn Bigelow") became the first woman to win the award, for _[The Hurt Locker](/wiki/The_Hurt_Locker "The Hurt Locker")_ (2009).
    * [Chloé Zhao](/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao "Chloé Zhao") is the first woman of color to win the award, for _[Nomadland](/wiki/Nomadland "Nomadland")_ (2020). She became the second woman nominated twice for the award for _[Hamnet](/wiki/Hamnet_\(film\) "Hamnet \(film\)")_ (2025).
    * [Jane Campion](/wiki/Jane_Campion "Jane Campion") is the first woman to be nominated twice for the award: _[The Piano](/wiki/The_Piano "The Piano")_ (1993) and _[The Power of the Dog](/wiki/The_Power_of_the_Dog_\(film\) "The Power of the Dog \(film\)")_ (2021)—winning for the latter.
  * [John Singleton](/wiki/John_Singleton "John Singleton") is the first Black (and youngest) nominee for _[Boyz n the Hood](/wiki/Boyz_n_the_Hood "Boyz n the Hood")_ (1991). 
    * [Steve McQueen](/wiki/Steve_McQueen_\(director\) "Steve McQueen \(director\)") is the first Black nominee to direct a [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture") winner, for _[12 Years a Slave](/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_\(film\) "12 Years a Slave \(film\)")_. [Barry Jenkins](/wiki/Barry_Jenkins "Barry Jenkins") subsequently did the same three years later, with _[Moonlight](/wiki/Moonlight_\(2016_film\) "Moonlight \(2016 film\)")_ (2016).
  * [David Lean](/wiki/David_Lean "David Lean") was the first non-American to win—and twice, for _[The Bridge on the River Kwai](/wiki/The_Bridge_on_the_River_Kwai "The Bridge on the River Kwai")_ (1957) and _[Lawrence of Arabia](/wiki/Lawrence_of_Arabia_\(film\) "Lawrence of Arabia \(film\)")_ (1962). This did not recur for five decades with any other non-American directors, until Ang Lee, Alfonso Cuarón, and Alejandro González Iñárritu each won twice themselves. 
    * Lee was the first Asian director to win the award, for _[Brokeback Mountain](/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain "Brokeback Mountain")_ (2005). He won again for _[Life of Pi](/wiki/Life_of_Pi_\(film\) "Life of Pi \(film\)")_ (2012).
    * Cuarón was the first Mexican (and Latin American) director to win the award, for _[Gravity](/wiki/Gravity_\(2013_film\) "Gravity \(2013 film\)")_ (2013). He won again for _[Roma](/wiki/Roma_\(2018_film\) "Roma \(2018 film\)")_ (2018).
  * No married or ex-married couple have won the award for the same film, though [James Cameron](/wiki/James_Cameron "James Cameron") (1997's _[Titanic](/wiki/Titanic_\(1997_film\) "Titanic \(1997 film\)")_) and [Kathryn Bigelow](/wiki/Kathryn_Bigelow "Kathryn Bigelow") (2008's _[The Hurt Locker](/wiki/The_Hurt_Locker "The Hurt Locker")_) were the first (and so far, only) ex-married couple to both win the award. 
    * It was also the first time in Oscar history that ex-married couple were [nominated against each other in competition for an award in the same year](/wiki/82nd_Academy_Awards "82nd Academy Awards").



## Notes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Director&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Notes")]

  1. **^** _[The Circus](/wiki/The_Circus_\(1928_film\) "The Circus \(1928 film\)")_ originally received a nomination for Best Director (Comedy Picture), as well as nominations for Best Actor and Best Writing (Original Story), all for [Charlie Chaplin](/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin "Charlie Chaplin"). However, the [Academy](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") subsequently decided to remove Chaplin's name from the competitive award categories and instead to confer upon him a [Special Award](/wiki/Academy_Honorary_Award "Academy Honorary Award") "for acting, writing, directing and producing _The Circus_ ".
  2. ^ _**a**_ _**b**_ _**c**_ The 2nd Academy Awards is the only ceremony for which there were no official nominees. Subsequent research by AMPAS has resulted in a list of unofficial or _de facto_ nominees, based on records of which films were evaluated by the judges. While Frank Lloyd won for _The Divine Lady_ , his other two nominated films are considered to be a single combined nomination.
  3. ^ _**a**_ _**b**_ According to the Oscars.org database, Brown's directing nomination counts as one singular, joint, cumulative nomination for two films. This same recognition was applicable to all nominees, such as to [Greta Garbo](/wiki/Greta_Garbo "Greta Garbo")'s acting nominations from the same respective films; as well as acting winners, [Norma Shearer](/wiki/Norma_Shearer "Norma Shearer") and [George Arliss](/wiki/George_Arliss "George Arliss"). No explanation was given for why the latter two were nominated with two films, yet only awarded for one each.
  4. ^ _**a**_ _**b**_ Michael Curtiz was not on the original ballot of nominees. However, after the year prior with [Bette Davis](/wiki/Bette_Davis "Bette Davis")'s omission for _[Of Human Bondage](/wiki/Of_Human_Bondage_\(1934_film\) "Of Human Bondage \(1934 film\)")_ , the resulting furor led to a write-in campaign determined to secure her a nomination. Thus, the Academy relaxed their rules and allowed her performance to be amongst the competition. They permitted this once more, prompting further submissions: Curtiz; [Paul Muni](/wiki/Paul_Muni "Paul Muni") for _[Black Fury](/wiki/Black_Fury_\(film\) "Black Fury \(film\)")_ ; and several other categories, including [Hal Mohr](/wiki/Hal_Mohr "Hal Mohr") for _[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream_\(1935_film\) "A Midsummer Night's Dream \(1935 film\)")_. Ultimately, Mohr became the only person to win an Oscar as a result of this process. The Academy discontinued this option from the next ceremony forward to prevent any recurrence.
  5. **^** The eligibility period for the 93rd ceremony was extended through to February 28, 2021, due to the impact of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").
  6. **^** As a consequence of the eligibility period for the 93rd ceremony being extended through to February 28, 2021, the eligibility period for the 94th ceremony was truncated to ten months, running from March 1 to December 31, 2021.
  7. ^ _**a**_ _**b**_ Wise earned two individual nominations (resulting in one win); and one joint nomination with [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins "Jerome Robbins"), which also resulted in the pair of them winning.
  8. **^** While the [Coen Brothers](/wiki/Coen_brothers "Coen brothers"), as a directing duo, earned two nominations, their work on _[Fargo](/wiki/Fargo_\(1996_film\) "Fargo \(1996 film\)")_ was credited as being split apart: Ethan was given [sole producer credit](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture "Academy Award for Best Picture"), while Joel was listed as the sole director. Joel thus has one additional directing nomination combined with his work as part of their dual efforts.
