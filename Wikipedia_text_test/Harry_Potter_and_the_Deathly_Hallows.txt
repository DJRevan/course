_**Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows**_ is a [fantasy novel](/wiki/Fantasy_novel "Fantasy novel") by British author [J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling"). It is the seventh and final novel in the _[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ series. It was released on 21 July 2007 in the United Kingdom by [Bloomsbury Publishing](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing"), in the United States by [Scholastic](/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation "Scholastic Corporation"), and in Canada by [Raincoast Books](/wiki/Raincoast_Books "Raincoast Books"). The novel chronicles the events directly following _[Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Half-Blood_Prince "Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince")_ (2005) and the final confrontation between the two wizards [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)") and [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort"). 

_Deathly Hallows_ shattered sales records upon release, surpassing marks set by previous titles of the _Harry Potter_ series. It holds the [Guinness World Record](/wiki/Guinness_World_Record "Guinness World Record") for most novels sold within 24 hours of release, with 8.3 million sold in the US and 2.65 million in the UK.[1][2] Reception to the book was generally positive, and the American Library Association named it a "Best Book for Young Adults". A film adaptation of the novel was released in two parts: _[Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_1 "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1")_ in November 2010 and _[Part 2](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_2 "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2")_ in July 2011. 

## Plot

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")]

The young wizard [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)") is about to turn seventeen. While being escorted to [The Burrow](/wiki/Places_in_Harry_Potter#The_Burrow "Places in Harry Potter") by members of the [Order of the Phoenix](/wiki/Order_of_the_Phoenix_\(fictional_organisation\) "Order of the Phoenix \(fictional organisation\)"), the group is attacked by [Death Eaters](/wiki/Death_Eater "Death Eater"), who kill ["Mad-Eye" Moody](/wiki/%22Mad-Eye%22_Moody ""Mad-Eye" Moody") and injure [George Weasley](/wiki/George_Weasley "George Weasley"). [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort") attempts to kill Harry but fails. 

After the Death Eaters take over the Ministry of Magic, Harry and his friends [Ron Weasley](/wiki/Ron_Weasley "Ron Weasley") and [Hermione Granger](/wiki/Hermione_Granger "Hermione Granger") begin searching for Voldemort's four remaining [Horcruxes](/wiki/Horcrux "Horcrux"), which they hope to destroy and thus render him mortal. They learn that the [locket Horcrux](/wiki/Salazar_Slytherin%27s_locket "Salazar Slytherin's locket") is in the possession of [Dolores Umbridge](/wiki/Dolores_Umbridge "Dolores Umbridge") at the [Ministry of Magic](/wiki/Ministry_of_Magic "Ministry of Magic"). Harry and his friends infiltrate the Ministry and steal the locket from Umbridge, but are unable to destroy it. The locket's Dark magic affects Ron, who abandons Harry and Hermione. In [Godric's Hollow](/wiki/Godric%27s_Hollow "Godric's Hollow"), Harry and Hermione are attacked by Voldemort's snake [Nagini](/wiki/Nagini_\(Harry_Potter\) "Nagini \(Harry Potter\)"), but manage to escape. One night, a [Patronus](/wiki/Patronus_Charm "Patronus Charm") guides Harry to a pond containing the [Sword of Gryffindor](/wiki/Sword_of_Godric_Gryffindor "Sword of Godric Gryffindor"). When he tries to recover it, the locket tightens around his neck, nearly drowning him. Ron returns and saves Harry, then destroys the Horcrux with the sword. 

The three friends visit [Xenophilius Lovegood](/wiki/Xenophilius_Lovegood "Xenophilius Lovegood"), who tells them of the mythical objects known as the [Deathly Hallows](/wiki/Magical_objects_in_Harry_Potter#Deathly_Hallows "Magical objects in Harry Potter"): the [Elder Wand](/wiki/Elder_Wand "Elder Wand"), the [Resurrection Stone](/wiki/Magical_objects_in_Harry_Potter#Resurrection_Stone "Magical objects in Harry Potter"), and the [Cloak of Invisibility](/wiki/Invisibility_cloak_\(Harry_Potter\) "Invisibility cloak \(Harry Potter\)"). Xenophilius then alerts the Ministry of Magic to the presence of the trio in his home, and Harry and his friends flee. Soon after, they are captured by bounty hunters known as Snatchers, and are imprisoned in [Malfoy Manor](/wiki/Malfoy_Manor "Malfoy Manor"). Harry and Ron are thrown into the cellar with [Dean Thomas](/wiki/Dean_Thomas_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dean Thomas \(Harry Potter\)"), [Luna Lovegood](/wiki/Luna_Lovegood "Luna Lovegood"), [Mr Ollivander](/wiki/Mr_Ollivander "Mr Ollivander"), and [Griphook](/wiki/Griphook "Griphook") the goblin, while the Death Eater [Bellatrix Lestrange](/wiki/Bellatrix_Lestrange "Bellatrix Lestrange") tortures Hermione. The house-elf [Dobby](/wiki/Dobby_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dobby \(Harry Potter\)") helps the prisoners escape, but is killed in the process. 

Harry, Ron, and Hermione break into [Gringotts Bank](/wiki/Gringotts_Bank "Gringotts Bank") and retrieve a Horcrux. Harry has visions which inform him that another Horcrux is hidden at [Hogwarts](/wiki/Hogwarts "Hogwarts"). After the trio enters the school with the help of [Aberforth Dumbledore](/wiki/Aberforth "Aberforth"), Voldemort prepares to assault the castle. As the Death Eaters enter the school and fight the professors and students, Ron and Hermione destroy the Horcrux from Gringotts. They then accompany Harry to the [Room of Requirement](/wiki/Room_of_Requirement "Room of Requirement"), where they discover the next Horcrux, which is the Diadem of Ravenclaw. [Draco Malfoy](/wiki/Draco_Malfoy "Draco Malfoy") and his friends [Crabbe](/wiki/Vincent_Crabbe "Vincent Crabbe") and [Goyle](/wiki/Gregory_Goyle "Gregory Goyle") ambush them, and Crabbe casts a fire spell which grows out of control, killing him and destroying the Horcrux. 

After stealing the Elder Wand from [Albus Dumbledore](/wiki/Albus_Dumbledore "Albus Dumbledore")'s tomb, Voldemort is unable to make it obey him. Believing that [Severus Snape](/wiki/Severus_Snape "Severus Snape") is the master of the wand, Voldemort uses Nagini to mortally wound him. Before dying, Snape passes his memories to Harry, who views them in the [Pensieve](/wiki/Pensieve "Pensieve"). The memories reveal that Snape loved Harry's mother, and acted as a [double agent](/wiki/Double_agent "Double agent") against Voldemort in an attempt to protect her. The memories also reveal that Dumbledore planned his own death at the hands of Snape so that Snape could gain Voldemort's trust, and that Harry himself became a Horcrux when Voldemort first tried to kill him. Now, Harry realizes he must die to render Voldemort mortal. On his way to surrender himself to Voldemort, Harry instructs [Neville Longbottom](/wiki/Neville_Longbottom "Neville Longbottom") to destroy Nagini. 

Voldemort casts the [Killing Curse](/wiki/Magic_in_Harry_Potter#Unforgivable_curses "Magic in Harry Potter") on Harry, who then awakens in a dreamlike place. He is greeted by Dumbledore, who explains that the curse destroyed the fragment of Voldemort's soul inside Harry, and that Harry can now return to life. Harry subsequently regains consciousness and pretends to be dead. Voldemort orders [Hagrid](/wiki/Hagrid "Hagrid") to carry Harry's body back to Hogwarts, and demands that the professors and students surrender; however, Neville pulls the Sword of Gryffindor from the [Sorting Hat](/wiki/Sorting_Hat "Sorting Hat") and kills Nagini. The battle resumes, during which [Molly Weasley](/wiki/Molly_Weasley "Molly Weasley") kills Bellatrix. Harry reveals himself to be alive and duels with Voldemort, who casts another Killing Curse. However, The Elder Wand refuses to kill Harry because he is its true master. Voldemort's spell rebounds and kills him. 

In the novel's epilogue, set nineteen years later, Harry and his friends see their children off to Hogwarts. Harry and [Ginny](/wiki/Ginny_Weasley "Ginny Weasley"), now married, have three children: [James Sirius](/wiki/List_of_supporting_Harry_Potter_characters#James_Sirius_Potter "List of supporting Harry Potter characters"), [Albus Severus](/wiki/Albus_Potter "Albus Potter"), and [Lily Luna](/wiki/Lily_Luna_Potter "Lily Luna Potter"). Ron and Hermione have a daughter named [Rose](/wiki/Rose_Granger-Weasley "Rose Granger-Weasley") and a son named [Hugo](/wiki/Hugo_Granger-Weasley "Hugo Granger-Weasley").[3]

## Background

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Background")]

### Series

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Series")]

The first novel in the _[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ series, _[Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Philosopher%27s_Stone "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone")_ , was published by [Bloomsbury Publishing](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing") in 1997.[4] It was followed by _[Chamber of Secrets](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets")_ (1998), _[Prisoner of Azkaban](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Prisoner_of_Azkaban "Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban")_ (1999), _[Goblet of Fire](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Goblet_of_Fire "Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire")_ (2000), _[Order of the Phoenix](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Order_of_the_Phoenix "Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix")_ (2003) and _[Half-Blood Prince](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Half-Blood_Prince "Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince")_ (2005).[a]

### Title

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Title")]

The title of the novel refers to three mythical objects featured in the story, which are collectively known as the "[Deathly Hallows](/wiki/Magical_objects_in_Harry_Potter#Deathly_Hallows "Magical objects in Harry Potter")". Rowling announced the title in December 2006 through a [Christmas](/wiki/Christmas "Christmas")-themed [hangman](/wiki/Hangman_\(game\) "Hangman \(game\)") puzzle on her website.[10] Other titles that Rowling considered were _Harry Potter and the Elder Wand_ and _Harry Potter and the Peverell Quest_.[11][12]

### Writing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Writing")]

[](/wiki/File:Wellington_Statue.jpg)Rowling completed the final chapters of _Deathly Hallows_ in Room 552 of the Balmoral Hotel.

[J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling") completed the novel in January 2007 while staying at the [Balmoral Hotel](/wiki/Balmoral_Hotel "Balmoral Hotel") in Edinburgh.[13] In a statement on her website, she said, "I've never felt such a mixture of extreme emotions in my life, never dreamed I could feel simultaneously heartbroken and euphoric." She compared her mixed feelings to those expressed by [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens") in the preface of the 1850 edition of _[David Copperfield](/wiki/David_Copperfield_\(novel\) "David Copperfield \(novel\)")_ , "a two-years' imaginative task". "To which," she added, "I can only sigh, try seventeen years, Charles". She ended her message by saying "_Deathly Hallows_ is my favourite, and that is the most wonderful way to finish the series".[14]

When asked before publication about the forthcoming book, Rowling stated that she could not change the ending even if she wanted. "These books have been plotted for such a long time, and for six books now, that they're all leading a certain direction. So, I really can't".[15] She also commented that the final volume related closely to the previous book in the series, _[Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Half-Blood_Prince "Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince")_ , "almost as though they are two-halves of the same novel".[16] She said the last chapter of _Deathly Hallows_ was written around 1990 as part of her earliest work on the series.[17] She also revealed that the last sentence was originally something akin to "Only those who he loved could see his lightning scar". She changed the sentence to "All was well" because she wanted it to be clear that [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)") had triumphed over [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort") and that he would not have to face him again.[18][19]

### Marketing and promotion

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Marketing and promotion")]

The launch of _Deathly Hallows_ was celebrated by an all-night book signing and reading by Rowling at the [Natural History Museum](/wiki/Natural_History_Museum,_London "Natural History Museum, London") in London. The 1,700 guests in attendance were chosen by ballot.[20] Rowling toured the United States in October 2007, where another event was held at [Carnegie Hall](/wiki/Carnegie_Hall "Carnegie Hall") in New York City with tickets allocated by sweepstake.[21] [Scholastic](/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation "Scholastic Corporation"), the American publisher of the _Harry Potter_ series, launched a multimillion-dollar "There will soon be 7" marketing campaign with a [Knight Bus](/wiki/Knight_Bus "Knight Bus") travelling to 40 libraries across the United States, online fan discussions and competitions, collectible bookmarks, tattoos, and the staged release of seven _Deathly Hallows_ questions most debated by fans.[22][23]

Rowling arranged with her publishers for a poster bearing the face of the missing British child [Madeleine McCann](/wiki/Disappearance_of_Madeleine_McCann "Disappearance of Madeleine McCann") to be made available to book sellers when _Deathly Hallows_ was launched on 21 July 2007, and said that she hoped that the posters would be displayed prominently in shops all over the world.[24] After it was announced that the novel would be released on 21 July 2007, [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") stated that the film adaptation of _[Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Order_of_the_Phoenix_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix \(film\)")_ would be released on 13 July.[25] In response, fans proclaimed July 2007 as the month of _Harry Potter_.[26]

### Spoiler embargo

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Spoiler embargo")]

Bloomsbury invested [£](/wiki/Pound_sterling "Pound sterling")10 million in an attempt to keep the book's contents secure until 21 July, the release date.[27] Arthur Levine, US editor of the _Harry Potter_ series, denied distributing any copies of _Deathly Hallows_ in advance for press review, but two US papers published early reviews anyway.[28] There was speculation that some shops would break the embargo and distribute copies of the book early, as the penalty imposed for previous instalments—that the distributor would not be supplied with any further copies of the series—would no longer be a deterrent.[29]

### Online leaks and early delivery

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Online leaks and early delivery")]

In the week before its release, a number of texts purporting to be genuine leaks appeared in various forms. On 16 July, a set of photographs representing all 759 pages of the US edition was leaked and was fully [transcribed](/wiki/Transcription_\(linguistics\) "Transcription \(linguistics\)") prior to the official release date.[30][31][32][33] The photographs later appeared on websites and [peer-to-peer](/wiki/Peer-to-peer "Peer-to-peer") networks, leading [Scholastic](/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation "Scholastic Corporation") to seek a [subpoena](/wiki/Subpoena "Subpoena") in order to identify one source.[34] This represented the most serious security breach in the Harry Potter series' history.[35] Rowling and her lawyer confirmed that there were genuine online leaks.[19][36] Reviews published in both _[The Baltimore Sun](/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun "The Baltimore Sun")_ and _The New York Times_ on 18 July 2007, corroborated many of the plot elements from this leak, and about one day prior to release, _The New York Times_ confirmed that the main circulating leak was real.[35]

Scholastic announced that approximately one-ten-thousandth (0.0001) of the US supply had been shipped early — interpreted to mean about 1,200 copies. One reader in Maryland received a copy of the book in the mail from DeepDiscount.com four days before it was launched, which evoked incredulous responses from both Scholastic and DeepDiscount. Scholastic initially reported that they were satisfied it had been a "human error" and would not discuss possible penalties;[37] however, the following day Scholastic announced that it would be launching legal action against DeepDiscount.com and its distributor, Levy Home Entertainment.[38] Scholastic filed for damages in Chicago's Circuit Court of Cook County, claiming that DeepDiscount engaged in a "complete and flagrant violation of the agreements that they knew were part of the carefully constructed release of this eagerly awaited book."[39] Some of the early-release books soon appeared on [eBay](/wiki/EBay "EBay"), in one case being sold to _[Publishers Weekly](/wiki/Publishers_Weekly "Publishers Weekly")_ for US$250 from an initial price of US$18.[40]

### Price wars and other controversies

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Price wars and other controversies")]

[Asda](/wiki/Asda "Asda"),[41][42] along with several other UK supermarkets, having already taken pre-orders for the book at a heavily discounted price, sparked a price war two days before the book's launch by announcing they would sell it for just £5 a copy. Other retail chains then also offered the book at discounted prices. At these prices the book became a [loss leader](/wiki/Loss_leader "Loss leader"). This caused uproar from traditional UK booksellers who argued they had no hope of competing in those conditions. Independent shops protested loudest, but even [Waterstone's](/wiki/Waterstone%27s "Waterstone's"), the UK's largest dedicated chain bookstore, could not compete with the supermarket price. Some small bookstores hit back by buying their stock from the supermarkets rather than their wholesalers. Asda attempted to counter this by imposing a limit of two copies per customer to prevent bulk purchases. Philip Wicks, a spokesman for the [UK Booksellers Association](/wiki/UK_Booksellers_Association "UK Booksellers Association"), said, "It is a war we can't even participate in. We think it's a crying shame that the supermarkets have decided to treat it as a loss-leader, like a can of baked beans." Michael Norris, an analyst at Simba Information, said: "You are not only lowering the price of the book. At this point, you are lowering the value of reading."[43]

In [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), a similar price war caused controversy regarding sales of the book.[44] Four of the biggest bookstore chains in Malaysia, [MPH Bookstores](/wiki/MPH_Bookstores "MPH Bookstores"), [Popular Bookstores](/wiki/Popular_Bookstore "Popular Bookstore"), Times and Harris, decided to pull _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ off their shelves as a protest against [Tesco](/wiki/Tesco "Tesco") and [Carrefour](/wiki/Carrefour "Carrefour") [hypermarkets](/wiki/Hypermarket "Hypermarket"). The retail price of the book in Malaysia was [MYR](/wiki/Malaysian_ringgit "Malaysian ringgit") 109.90, while the hypermarkets [Tesco](/wiki/Tesco "Tesco") and [Carrefour](/wiki/Carrefour "Carrefour") sold the book at MYR 69.90. The move by the bookstores was seen as an attempt to pressure the distributor [Penguin Books](/wiki/Penguin_Books "Penguin Books") to remove the books from the hypermarkets. However, as of 24 July 2007, the price war had ended, with the four bookstores involved resuming selling the books in their stores with discount. Penguin Books has also confirmed that Tesco and Carrefour were selling the book at a loss, urging them to practise good business sense and fair trade.[45] The book's early Saturday morning release in Israel was criticised for violating [Shabbat](/wiki/Shabbat "Shabbat"). Trade and Industry Minister [Eli Yishai](/wiki/Eli_Yishai "Eli Yishai") commented "It is forbidden, according to Jewish values and Jewish culture, that a thing like this should take place at 2 am on Saturday. Let them do it on another day."[46] Yishai indicated that he would issue indictments and fines based on the Hours of Work and Rest Law.[47]

### Editions

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Editions")]

_Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ was released in hardcover on 21 July 2007[48] and in paperback in the United Kingdom on 10 July 2008[49] and the United States on 7 July 2009.[50] In [SoHo, New York](/wiki/SoHo "SoHo"), there was a release party for the American paperback edition, with many games and activities.[51] An "Adult Edition" with a different cover illustration was released by Bloomsbury on 21 July 2007.[52] To be released simultaneously with the original US hardcover on 21 July with only 100,000 copies was a Scholastic deluxe edition, highlighting a new cover illustration by Mary GrandPré.[53] In October 2010, Bloomsbury released a "Celebratory" paperback edition, which featured a foiled and starred cover.[54] Lastly, on 1 November 2010, a "Signature" edition of the novel was released in paperback by Bloomsbury.[55]

### Translations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Translations")]

Main article: [Harry Potter in translation](/wiki/Harry_Potter_in_translation "Harry Potter in translation")

As with previous books in the series, _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ has been translated into many languages. The first translation to be released was the [Ukrainian](/wiki/Ukrainian_language "Ukrainian language") translation, on 25 September 2007 (as _Гаррі Поттер і смертельні реліквії_ – _Harry Potter i smertel'ni relikviji_).[56] The Swedish title of the book was revealed by Rowling as _Harry Potter and the Relics of Death_ (_Harry Potter och Dödsrelikerna_), following a pre-release question from the Swedish publisher about the difficulty of translating the two words "_Deathly Hallows_ " without having read the book.[57] This is also the title used for the French translation (_Harry Potter et les reliques de la mort_), the Spanish translation (_Harry Potter y las Reliquias de la Muerte_), the Dutch translation (_Harry Potter en de Relieken van de Dood_), the Serbian translation (_Хари Потер и реликвије смрти_ – _Hari Poter i relikvije smrti_) and the Brazilian Portuguese translation (_Harry Potter e as Relíquias da Morte_).[58] The first Polish translation was released with a new title: _Harry Potter i Insygnia Śmierci_ – _Harry Potter and the Insignia of Death_.[59] The [Hindi](/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") translation _Harry Potter aur Maut ke Tohfe_ (हैरी पॉटर और मौत के तोहफे), which means "Harry Potter and the Gifts of Death", was released by Manjul Publication in India on 27 June 2008.[60] The Romanian version was released on 1 December 2007 using the title (_Harry Potter și Talismanele Morții_). 

## Reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Reception")]

### Critical response

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Critical response")]

_[The Baltimore Sun](/wiki/The_Baltimore_Sun "The Baltimore Sun")_ 's critic, Mary Carole McCauley, noted that the book was more serious than the previous novels in the series and had more straightforward prose.[61] Furthermore, reviewer Alice Fordham from _[The Times](/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ wrote that "Rowling's genius is not just her total realisation of a fantasy world, but the quieter skill of creating characters that bounce off the page, real and flawed and brave and lovable". Fordham concluded, "We have been a long way together, and neither Rowling nor Harry let us down in the end".[62] _[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ writer [Michiko Kakutani](/wiki/Michiko_Kakutani "Michiko Kakutani") agreed, praising Rowling's ability to make Harry both a hero and a character that can be related to.[63]

_Time_ magazine's [Lev Grossman](/wiki/Lev_Grossman "Lev Grossman") named it one of the Top 10 Fiction Books of 2007, ranking it at No. 8, and praised Rowling for proving that books can still be a global mass medium.[64] Novelist [Elizabeth Hand](/wiki/Elizabeth_Hand "Elizabeth Hand") criticised that "... the spectacularly complex interplay of narrative and character often reads as though an entire trilogy's worth of summing-up has been crammed into one volume."[65] In a starred review from _[Kirkus Reviews](/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews "Kirkus Reviews")_ , the reviewer said, "Rowling has shown uncommon skill in playing them with and against each other, and also woven them into a darn good bildungsroman, populated by memorable characters and infused with a saving, irrepressible sense of fun". They also praised the second half of the novel, but criticised the epilogue, calling it "provocatively sketchy".[66] In another review from _The Times_ , reviewer Amanda Craig said that while Rowling was "not an original, high-concept author", she was "right up there with other greats of children's fiction". Craig went on to say that the novel was "beautifully judged, and a triumphant return to form", and that Rowling's imagination changed the perception of an entire generation, which "is more than all but a handful of living authors, in any genre, have achieved in the past half-century".[67]

In contrast, Jenny Sawyer of _[The Christian Science Monitor](/wiki/The_Christian_Science_Monitor "The Christian Science Monitor")_ said: "There is much to love about the Harry Potter series, from its brilliantly realised magical world to its multilayered narrative ... A story is about someone who changes. And, puberty aside, Harry doesn't change much. As envisioned by Rowling, he walks the path of good so unwaveringly that his final victory over Voldemort feels, not just inevitable, but hollow."[68] In _The New York Times_ , [Christopher Hitchens](/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens "Christopher Hitchens") compared the series to World War Two-era English boarding school stories, and while he wrote that "Rowling has won imperishable renown" for the series as a whole, he also stated that he disliked Rowling's use of [deus ex machina](/wiki/Deus_ex_machina "Deus ex machina"), that the mid-book camping chapters are "abysmally long", and Voldemort "becomes more tiresome than an [Ian Fleming](/wiki/Ian_Fleming "Ian Fleming") villain".[69] Catherine Bennett of _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ praised Rowling for putting small details from the previous books and making them large in _Deathly Hallows_ , such as Grindelwald being mentioned on a [Chocolate Frog Card](/wiki/Fictional_universe_of_Harry_Potter#Magical_sweets "Fictional universe of Harry Potter") in the first book. While she points out "as her critics say, Rowling is no Dickens", she says that Rowling "has willed into a fictional being, in every book, legions of new characters, places, spells, rules and scores of unimagined twists and subplots".[70]

[Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King") criticised the reactions of some reviewers to the books, including McCauley, for jumping too quickly to surface conclusions of the work. He felt this was inevitable, because of the extreme secrecy before launch which did not allow reviewers time to read and consider the book, but meant that many early reviews lacked depth. Rather than finding the writing style disappointing, he felt it had matured and improved. He acknowledged that the subject matter of the books had become more adult, and that Rowling had clearly been writing with the adult audience firmly in mind since the middle of the series. He compared the works in this respect to _[Huckleberry Finn](/wiki/Adventures_of_Huckleberry_Finn "Adventures of Huckleberry Finn")_ and _[Alice in Wonderland](/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland "Alice in Wonderland")_ which achieved success and have become established classics, in part by appealing to the adult audience as well as children.[71]

### Sales

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Sales")]

[](/wiki/File:Potter_queue.jpg)Lines at a Californian [Borders](/wiki/Borders_\(retailer\) "Borders \(retailer\)"), five minutes until midnight to buy the book

Sales for _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ were record-setting. The initial US print run for _Deathly Hallows_ was 12 million copies, and more than a million were pre-ordered through Amazon and Barnes & Noble,[72] 500 per cent higher than pre-sales had been for _Half-Blood Prince_.[73] On 12 April 2007, [Barnes & Noble](/wiki/Barnes_%26_Noble "Barnes & Noble") declared that _Deathly Hallows_ had broken its [pre-order](/wiki/Pre-order "Pre-order") record, with more than 500,000 copies pre-ordered through its site.[74] On opening day, a record 8.3 million copies were sold in the United States (over 96 per second),[75][76] and 2.65 million copies in the United Kingdom.[77] It holds the [Guinness World record](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") for fastest selling book of fiction in 24 hours for US sales.[2] At WH Smith, sales reportedly reached a rate of 15 books sold per second.[78] By June 2008, nearly a year after it was published, worldwide sales were reportedly around 44 million.[79]

### Awards and honours

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Awards and honours")]

_Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ has won several awards.[80] In 2007, the book was named one of _The New York Times_ 100 Notable Books,[81] and one of its Notable Children's Books.[82] The novel was named the best book of 2007, by _[Newsweek](/wiki/Newsweek "Newsweek")_ 's critic Malcolm Jones.[83] _[Publishers Weekly](/wiki/Publishers_Weekly "Publishers Weekly")_ also listed _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ among their Best Books of 2007.[84] Also in 2007 the book received the Andre Norton Award for Young Adult Science Fiction and Fantasy at the Nebula Awards. In 2008, the [American Library Association](/wiki/American_Library_Association "American Library Association") named the novel one of its Best Books for Young Adults,[85] and also listed it as a Notable Children's Book.[86] Furthermore, _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ received the 2008 Colorado Blue Spruce Book Award.[80]

## Themes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Themes")]

### Death

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Death")]

In a 2006 interview, Rowling said that the main theme of the series is Harry dealing with death,[87] which was influenced by her mother's death in 1990.[b] [Lev Grossman](/wiki/Lev_Grossman "Lev Grossman") of _Time_ asserted that the main theme of the series is the importance of continuing to love in the face of death.[64]

### Living in a corrupted society

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Living in a corrupted society")]

Academics and journalists have developed many other interpretations of themes in the books, some more complex than others, and some including [political subtexts](/wiki/Politics_of_Harry_Potter "Politics of Harry Potter"). Themes such as [normality](/wiki/Normality_\(behavior\) "Normality \(behavior\)"), oppression, survival, and overcoming imposing odds have all been considered as prevalent throughout the series.[90] Similarly, the theme of making one's way through adolescence and "going over one's most harrowing ordeals—and thus coming to terms with them" has also been considered.[91] Rowling has stated that the books comprise "a prolonged argument for tolerance, a prolonged plea for an end to [bigotry](/wiki/Bigotry "Bigotry")" and that also pass on a message to "question authority and ... not assume that the establishment or the press tells you all of the truth".[92] [Timothy Snyder](/wiki/Timothy_Snyder "Timothy Snyder") in his 2017 book _[On Tyranny](/wiki/On_Tyranny "On Tyranny")_ mentioned the novel as a recent example illustrating resistance to totalitarianism.[93][94]

### Christian allegories

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Christian allegories")]

See also: [Religious debates over the Harry Potter series § Christian allegories in Deathly Hallows](/wiki/Religious_debates_over_the_Harry_Potter_series#Christian_allegories_in_Deathly_Hallows "Religious debates over the Harry Potter series")

The _Harry Potter_ series has been criticised for supposedly supporting [witchcraft](/wiki/Witchcraft "Witchcraft") and the [occult](/wiki/Occult "Occult"). Before publication of _Deathly Hallows_ , Rowling refused to speak out about her religion, stating, "If I talk too freely, every reader, whether 10 or 60, will be able to guess what's coming in the books".[95] However, many have commented on [Christian allegories](/wiki/Christian_mythology "Christian mythology") that appear in _Deathly Hallows_.[95] For example, Harry dies and then comes back to life to save mankind, like Christ. The location where this occurs is King's Cross.[96] Rowling also stated that "my belief and my struggling with religious belief ... I think is quite apparent in this book", which is shown as Harry struggles with his faith in Dumbledore.[97]

[](/wiki/File:Michael_Maier_Atalanta_Fugiens_Emblem_21.jpeg)The [Philosopher's Stone](/wiki/Philosopher%27s_Stone "Philosopher's Stone") as pictured in [Michael Maier](/wiki/Michael_Maier "Michael Maier")'s 1617 alchemical work _Atalanta Fugiens_ , similar to the presentation of the _Deathly Hallows_ and _Resurrection Stone_.

_Deathly Hallows_ begins with a pair of epigraphs, one by [Quaker](/wiki/Religious_Society_of_Friends "Religious Society of Friends") leader [William Penn](/wiki/William_Penn "William Penn") and one from [Aeschylus](/wiki/Aeschylus "Aeschylus")' _[The Libation Bearers](/wiki/Oresteia#The_Libation_Bearers "Oresteia")_. Of this, Rowling said "I really enjoyed choosing those two quotations because one is pagan, of course, and one is from a Christian tradition. I'd known it was going to be those two passages since _[Chamber](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets")_ was published. I always knew [that] if I could use them at the beginning of book seven then I'd cued up the ending perfectly. If they were relevant, then I went where I needed to go. They just say it all to me, they really do".[98]

When Harry visits his parents' grave, the biblical reference "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death" ([1 Corinthians 15:26](/wiki/1_Corinthians_15:26 "1 Corinthians 15:26")) is inscribed on the grave.[99] The Dumbledores' family tomb also holds a biblical quote: "Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also", which is from [Matthew 6:21](/wiki/Matthew_6:21 "Matthew 6:21").[99] Rowling states, "They're very British books, so on a very practical note Harry was going to find biblical quotations on tombstones ... [but] I think those two particular quotations he finds on the tombstones at Godric's Hollow, they sum up – they almost epitomise the whole series".[99]

_Harry Potter_ [pundit](/wiki/Pundit_\(expert\) "Pundit \(expert\)") [John Granger](/wiki/John_Granger "John Granger") additionally noted that one of the reasons the _Harry Potter_ books were so popular is their use of literary alchemy (similar to _[Romeo and Juliet](/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet "Romeo and Juliet")_ , [C. S. Lewis](/wiki/C._S._Lewis "C. S. Lewis")'s _[Perelandra](/wiki/Perelandra "Perelandra")_ and [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens")'s _[A Tale of Two Cities](/wiki/A_Tale_of_Two_Cities "A Tale of Two Cities")_) and vision symbolism.[100] In this model, authors weave allegorical tales along the [alchemical](/wiki/Alchemy "Alchemy") [magnum opus](/wiki/Magnum_opus_\(alchemy\) "Magnum opus \(alchemy\)"). Since the medieval period, alchemical allegory has mirrored the passion, death and resurrection of Christ.[101] While the entire series utilises symbols common in alchemy, the _Deathly Hallows_ completes this cycle, tying themes of death, rebirth, and the Resurrection Stone to the principal motif of alchemical allegory, and topics presented in the first book of the series. 

## Adaptations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Adaptations")]

### Films

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Films")]

Main articles: [_Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1_](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_1 "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1") and [_Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2_](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_2 "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2")

The two-part [film adaptation](/wiki/Film_adaptation "Film adaptation") of _Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ was directed by [David Yates](/wiki/David_Yates "David Yates"), written by [Steve Kloves](/wiki/Steve_Kloves "Steve Kloves") and produced by [David Heyman](/wiki/David_Heyman "David Heyman"), [David Barron](/wiki/David_Barron_\(film_producer\) "David Barron \(film producer\)"), and Rowling. _Part 1_ was released on 19 November 2010, with _Part 2_ following on 15 July 2011.[102][103] Filming began in February 2009 and wrapped on 12 June 2010,[104] with reshoots for _Part 2_ 's epilogue scene taking place in December 2010.[105] _Part 1_ ended at Chapter 24 of the book, in which Voldemort acquires the Elder Wand.[106] There were a few omissions, such as the appearances of [Dean Thomas](/wiki/Dean_Thomas_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dean Thomas \(Harry Potter\)") and [Viktor Krum](/wiki/Viktor_Krum "Viktor Krum"), and [Peter Pettigrew](/wiki/Peter_Pettigrew "Peter Pettigrew")'s death.[107] James Bernadelli of _Reelviews_ said that the script stuck closest to the text since _[Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets \(film\)")_ ,[108] yet this was met with negativity from some audiences as the film inherited "the book's own problems".[109]

### Audiobooks

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=23 "Edit section: Audiobooks")]

_Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows_ was released simultaneously on 21 July 2007, in both the UK and the United States.[110][111] The UK edition features the voice of [Stephen Fry](/wiki/Stephen_Fry "Stephen Fry") and runs about 24 hours[112] while the US edition features the voice of [Jim Dale](/wiki/Jim_Dale "Jim Dale") and runs about 21 hours.[113] Both Fry and Dale recorded 146 different and distinguishable character voices, and was the most recorded by an individual on an audiobook at the time.[114] For his work on _Deathly Hallows_ , Dale won the 2008 [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for the [Best Spoken Word Album for Children](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Spoken_Word_Album_for_Children "Grammy Award for Best Spoken Word Album for Children").[115] He also was awarded an Earphone Award by _[AudioFile](/wiki/AudioFile_\(magazine\) "AudioFile \(magazine\)")_ , who claimed, "Dale has raised the bar on audiobook interpretation so high it's hard to imagine any narrator vaulting over it."[116]

### Video games

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=24 "Edit section: Video games")]

Two action-adventure video games were produced by [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts") (EA) to coincide with the release of the film adaptations, as with each of the previous _Harry Potter_ films. [Part 1](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_1_\(video_game\) "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1 \(video game\)") was released on 16 November 2010, and [Part 2](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows_%E2%80%93_Part_2_\(video_game\) "Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 2 \(video game\)") on 12 July 2011. Both games received a mixed to negative reaction from critics.[117][118]

## Subsequent works

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=25 "Edit section: Subsequent works")]

### _The Tales of Beedle the Bard_

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=26 "Edit section: The Tales of Beedle the Bard")]

On 4 December 2008, Rowling released _[The Tales of Beedle the Bard](/wiki/The_Tales_of_Beedle_the_Bard "The Tales of Beedle the Bard")_ both in the UK and US.[119] _The Tales of Beedle the Bard_ is a spin-off of _Deathly Hallows_ and contains fairy tales that are told to children in the "Wizarding World". The book includes five short stories, including "The Tale of the Three Brothers", which is the story of the [Deathly Hallows](/wiki/Magical_objects_in_Harry_Potter#Deathly_Hallows "Magical objects in Harry Potter"). Amazon released an exclusive collector's edition of the book which is a replica of the book that Amazon purchased at auction in December 2007.[120] Seven copies were auctioned off in London by Sotheby's. Each was illustrated and handwritten by Rowling and is 157 pages. It was bound in brown Moroccan leather and embellished with five hand-chased hallmarked sterling silver ornaments and mounted moonstones.[121]

### _Harry Potter and the Cursed Child_

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Deathly_Hallows&action=edit&section=27 "Edit section: Harry Potter and the Cursed Child")]

In 2016, _[Harry Potter and the Cursed Child](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Cursed_Child "Harry Potter and the Cursed Child")_ was released, a two-part play written by [Jack Thorne](/wiki/Jack_Thorne "Jack Thorne") based on an original story by Rowling, Thorne, and [John Tiffany](/wiki/John_Tiffany "John Tiffany").[122] Billed as the eighth story in the _Harry Potter_ series, it picks up where the epilogue of _Deathly Hallows_ left off, following an adult Harry Potter and his son, [Albus Severus Potter](/wiki/Albus_Severus_Potter "Albus Severus Potter").[123] Previews of the play began at the [Palace Theatre, London](/wiki/Palace_Theatre,_London "Palace Theatre, London"), on 7 June 2016,[124] and it premiered on 30 July 2016. The play opened on Broadway on 21 April 2018 at the [Lyric Theatre](/wiki/Lyric_Theatre_\(New_York_City,_1998\) "Lyric Theatre \(New York City, 1998\)"), with previews starting on 16 March 2018. Both parts of the stage play's script have been released in print and digital formats as _Harry Potter and the Cursed Child Parts I & II_. The first edition, the Special Rehearsal Edition, corresponded to the script used in the preview shows and was published on 31 July 2016, the date of Harry's birthday in the series and Rowling's birthday, as well. Since revisions to the script continued after the book was printed, an edited version was released on 25 July 2017, as the "Definitive Collector's Edition".[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] According to CNN, this was the most preordered book of 2016.[125]
