The **Academy Award for Best Actor** is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") (AMPAS). It has been awarded since the [1st Academy Awards](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards "1st Academy Awards") to an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a [leading role](/wiki/Leading_actor "Leading actor") in a film released that year. The award was traditionally presented by the previous year's [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") winner. However, in recent years, it has shifted towards being presented by previous years' Best Actor winners instead. 

The Best Actor award has been presented 98 times, to 87 actors. The first winner was [German actor](/wiki/List_of_German_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees "List of German Academy Award winners and nominees") [Emil Jannings](/wiki/Emil_Jannings "Emil Jannings") for his roles in _[The Last Command](/wiki/The_Last_Command_\(1928_film\) "The Last Command \(1928 film\)")_ (1928) and _[The Way of All Flesh](/wiki/The_Way_of_All_Flesh_\(1927_film\) "The Way of All Flesh \(1927 film\)")_ (1927).[1] The most recent winner is [Michael B. Jordan](/wiki/Michael_B._Jordan "Michael B. Jordan") for his dual role as twin brothers Elijah and Elias Moore in _[Sinners](/wiki/Sinners_\(2025_film\) "Sinners \(2025 film\)")_ (2025). The record for [most wins](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_two_or_more_Academy_Awards_in_acting_categories "List of actors with two or more Academy Awards in acting categories") is three, held by [Daniel Day-Lewis](/wiki/Daniel_Day-Lewis "Daniel Day-Lewis"), and ten other actors have won twice. The record for [most nominations](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_three_or_more_Academy_Award_nominations_in_acting_categories "List of actors with three or more Academy Award nominations in acting categories") is nine, held jointly by [Spencer Tracy](/wiki/Spencer_Tracy "Spencer Tracy") and [Laurence Olivier](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier "Laurence Olivier"). At the [5th Academy Awards](/wiki/5th_Academy_Awards "5th Academy Awards") in 1932, [Fredric March](/wiki/Fredric_March "Fredric March") finished one vote ahead of [Wallace Beery](/wiki/Wallace_Beery "Wallace Beery"); under the rules of the time this resulted in them sharing the award, the only time this has occurred. 

## Nominations process

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Nominations process")]

Nominees are currently determined by [single transferable vote](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote "Single transferable vote") within the actors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a [plurality vote](/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\) "Plurality \(voting\)") from the entire [eligible voting members](/wiki/Film_industry "Film industry") of the Academy.[2]

In the first three years of the awards, actors and actresses were nominated as the best individuals in their categories. At that time, all of their work during the qualifying period (as many as three films, in some cases) was listed after the award.[3] Despite this, at the [3rd Academy Awards](/wiki/3rd_Academy_Awards "3rd Academy Awards"), held in 1930, only one film was cited in each winner's award regardless of how many they were eligible to be considered for during that span.[4] The current system, in which an actor is nominated for a specific performance in a single film, was introduced for the [4th Academy Awards](/wiki/4th_Academy_Awards "4th Academy Awards").[3] Starting with the [9th Academy Awards](/wiki/9th_Academy_Awards "9th Academy Awards"), held in 1937, the category was limited to a maximum five nominations per year.[3]

## Winners and nominees

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Winners and nominees")]

In the following table, the years are listed as per Academy convention, and generally correspond to the year of film release in [Los Angeles County](/wiki/Los_Angeles_County "Los Angeles County"); the ceremonies are always held the following year.[5] For the first five ceremonies, the eligibility period spanned twelve months, from August 1 to July 31.[6] For the 6th ceremony held in 1934, the eligibility period lasted from August 1, 1932, to December 31, 1933.[6] Since the 7th ceremony held in 1935, the period of eligibility became the full previous calendar year from January 1 to December 31.[6]

## Notes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Notes")]

    **A****^** : According to longstanding Hollywood legend,[109] reported by [Susan Orlean](/wiki/Susan_Orlean "Susan Orlean"),[110] [Rin Tin Tin](/wiki/Rin_Tin_Tin "Rin Tin Tin") actually received the most Best Actor votes, but the Academy (not wishing to give the first award to a dog) refactored the votes to ensure that Jannings won.[111]
    **B****^** :_[The Circus](/wiki/The_Circus_\(1928_film\) "The Circus \(1928 film\)")_ originally received three nominations: Best Director (Comedy Picture), Best Actor, and Best Writing (Original Story) – for [Charles Chaplin](/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin "Charlie Chaplin"). However, the [Academy](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") subsequently decided to remove Chaplin's name from the competitive award categories and instead to confer upon him a [Special Award](/wiki/Academy_Honorary_Award "Academy Honorary Award") "for acting, writing, directing and producing _The Circus_ ".[8]
    **C****1** **2** **3** : Rules at the time of the first three ceremonies allowed for a performer to receive a single nomination which could honor his or her work in more than one film. [George Arliss](/wiki/George_Arliss "George Arliss"), [Maurice Chevalier](/wiki/Maurice_Chevalier "Maurice Chevalier"), and [Ronald Colman](/wiki/Ronald_Colman "Ronald Colman") were all nominated for two different roles in the same category. Current Academy rules forbid this from happening. No official reason was ever given as to why Arliss won the award for only one of the two films he was listed for.[112]
    **D****1** **2** : [Fredric March](/wiki/Fredric_March "Fredric March") received one more vote than [Wallace Beery](/wiki/Wallace_Beery "Wallace Beery"). Academy rules at that time considered such a close margin to be a tie, so both March and Beery received the award. Currently, Academy rules stipulate that a tie must result from exactly the same number of votes.[113]
    **E****^** : As in the previous year when the Academy relaxed the rules to allow write-in votes following the outcry over [Bette Davis's](/wiki/Bette_Davis "Bette Davis") snub for _[Of Human Bondage](/wiki/Of_Human_Bondage_\(1934_film\) "Of Human Bondage \(1934 film\)")_ , the Academy permitted write-in votes for that year as well. Thus, [Paul Muni](/wiki/Paul_Muni "Paul Muni") received a write-in nomination for his performance in _[Black Fury](/wiki/Black_Fury_\(film\) "Black Fury \(film\)")_ and actually finished second in the votes. Although as with Davis the previous year, the Academy did not recognize these two as "official nominees", they are nevertheless listed on the official website amongst their respective years' nominations for posterity's sake.[114]
    **F****^** : Due to category confusion, [Barry Fitzgerald](/wiki/Barry_Fitzgerald "Barry Fitzgerald") received nominations (each for the same performance as Father Fitzgibbon in _[Going My Way](/wiki/Going_My_Way "Going My Way")_) in both the leading and [supporting actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor#1940s "Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor") categories for 1944, winning the Oscar for the latter. As a result of this confusion, the Academy amended its rules so that if any actor or actress received enough votes to qualify as one of the final five nominees for both again, the performer would only receive the nomination for the category in which he or she obtained the larger percentage of the votes.[115]

  1. **^** The 2nd Academy Awards is unique in being the only occasion where there were no official nominees. Subsequent research by AMPAS has resulted in a list of unofficial or de facto nominees, based on records of which films were evaluated by the judges.
