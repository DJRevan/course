_**Adventures in Babysitting**_ (also known as _**A Night on the Town**_ in certain countries) is a 1987 American [teen](/wiki/Teen_film "Teen film") [comedy film](/wiki/Comedy_film "Comedy film") written by [David Simkins](/wiki/David_Simkins "David Simkins") and directed by [Chris Columbus](/wiki/Chris_Columbus_\(filmmaker\) "Chris Columbus \(filmmaker\)") in his [directorial debut](/wiki/List_of_directorial_debuts "List of directorial debuts"). It stars [Elisabeth Shue](/wiki/Elisabeth_Shue "Elisabeth Shue"), [Keith Coogan](/wiki/Keith_Coogan "Keith Coogan"), [Anthony Rapp](/wiki/Anthony_Rapp "Anthony Rapp"), and [Maia Brewton](/wiki/Maia_Brewton "Maia Brewton"), and features cameos by [blues](/wiki/Blues "Blues") singer/guitarist [Albert Collins](/wiki/Albert_Collins "Albert Collins") and singer-songwriter [Southside Johnny Lyon](/wiki/Southside_Johnny_Lyon "Southside Johnny Lyon"). 

## Plot

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")]

After her boyfriend Mike Todwell cancels their anniversary date, 17-year-old Chris Parker invites her friend Brenda over to her [Oak Park, Illinois](/wiki/Oak_Park,_Illinois "Oak Park, Illinois") house to cheer her up, but is convinced by her mother to babysit their neighbors' daughter, eight-year-old Sara Anderson, while the parents attend a party in downtown [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). Later that evening, Chris receives a frantic call from Brenda, who ran away to a downtown bus station; Chris plans to pick her up, but is coerced by Sara, Sara's 15-year-old brother Brad, and Brad's best friend Daryl Coopersmith to take them with her. On the freeway, their station wagon suffers a flat tire and they are picked up by a tow truck driver, "Handsome" John Pruitt, who offers to pay for the tire when Chris realizes that she left her purse at the Andersons'. En route, Pruitt receives a call from his boss Dawson with evidence that his wife is cheating on him. Pruitt rushes to his house to confront the infidelity; the car of Chris's mother is damaged when he accidentally shoots out the windshield while trying to kill his wife's lover. Chris and the kids hide in the adulterer's Cadillac, which car thief Joe Gipp then steals. 

Reaching their hideout in the [South Side](/wiki/South_Side,_Chicago "South Side, Chicago"), the kids realize that they have stumbled upon a multi-state car theft operation. They are detained in an upstairs office but escape. They enter a blues club where the band on stage refuses to let them leave until they perform a blues number. The group recounts the events of their evening while the band plays, causing the audience to sing along and applaud. They leave when Joe, Graydon (the operation's second-in-command), and their boss Bleak arrive in the club, whose owners stall them. 

After separating Daryl from a [prostitute](/wiki/Street_prostitution "Street prostitution") who is a runaway, Chris is reminded of Brenda. They are found and chased again by Graydon and Bleak but escape on the [Chicago "L" train](/w/index.php?title=Chicago_%22L%22_train&action=edit&redlink=1 "Chicago "L" train \(page does not exist\)") and wind up in the middle of a gang fight. Brad is injured when one of the gang leaders throws a [switchblade](/wiki/Switchblade "Switchblade") onto his foot. They take Brad to the university hospital where, to his embarrassment/disappointment, he receives a single stitch. They run into Pruitt, who is now on the run from his earlier attacks; he says that he replaced the windshield, but Dawson wants $50 for the tire. The kids come across a fraternity house party. While there, a gentleman named Dan Lynch learns of Chris's problem and manages to donate $45. He takes them to Dawson's Garage and drops them off. 

When they find Dawson, his blond hair and sledgehammer lead Sara to believe that he is [Thor](/wiki/Thor_\(Marvel_Comics\) "Thor \(Marvel Comics\)"). He denies them their car because of the $5 shortage, but when Sara offers him her toy Thor helmet, he has a change of heart and lets them go. Meanwhile, Joe Gipp has told Bleak about their troubles; the three are waiting to follow them. The kids find the restaurant where Mike was supposed to take Chris and discover that he is there with a girl called Sesame Plexer. Sara slips away to look at a toy store while Chris confronts Mike, who leaves Chris visibly hurt after downplaying their relationship. Brad stands up for Chris, but is reluctant to hit Mike, so Daryl kicks Mike into a table, ruining his dinner and causing a commotion. Bleak spots Sara, and Graydon chases her to an office building where she hides; the others note her disappearance and follow, accidentally coming across the Andersons' party. After Sara climbs out an open window and slides down the building, Chris spots her and they run upstairs to help. 

After the group pulls Sara from outside the window, Bleak intervenes on them. Joe knocks his boss unconscious, before giving him a _[Playboy](/wiki/Playboy "Playboy")_ magazine that Daryl had stolen, which contained important notes that the criminals wanted. The kids retrieve Brenda from the bus station and rush home, avoiding the Andersons on [Interstate 290](/wiki/Interstate_290_\(Illinois\) "Interstate 290 \(Illinois\)"). Once home, Chris cleans up the mess left earlier, settling into place just as the Andersons enter. Just as Chris is leaving, Dan arrives with one of Sara's missing skates. With Sara's encouragement, Chris and Dan reconcile and kiss.

## Production

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Production")]

Writer David Simkins was working for New World Pictures when he read a copy of the script for _[Ferris Bueller's Day Off](/wiki/Ferris_Bueller%27s_Day_Off "Ferris Bueller's Day Off")_. Simkins said he wanted to do a teen film in the vein of that film and _[After Hours](/wiki/After_Hours_\(film\) "After Hours \(film\)")_ , a story which told the series of misadventures over a limited time period. He pitched the idea to New World who did not want to make it. Simkins wrote the script and sold it to Paramount. It was assigned to producers Lyda Obst and Debra Hill and Simkins credits them with being of great assistance on the script.[4]

For his directorial debut, Columbus said he reviewed 100 scripts. He chose _Adventures in Babysitting_ because he felt comfortable with its scale. [Paramount Pictures](/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") had a [right of first refusal](/wiki/Right_of_first_refusal "Right of first refusal") but demanded [Molly Ringwald](/wiki/Molly_Ringwald "Molly Ringwald") be cast in the lead.[1] [Touchstone Pictures](/wiki/Touchstone_Pictures "Touchstone Pictures") agreed to make the film after [The Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") received $300 million in financing from [Silver Screen Partners](/wiki/Silver_Screen_Partners "Silver Screen Partners"). Over 150 actresses auditioned for the lead role in [Dallas](/wiki/Dallas "Dallas"), [Florida](/wiki/Florida "Florida"), [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto "Toronto"), [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), and [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"),[1] including [Valerie Bertinelli](/wiki/Valerie_Bertinelli "Valerie Bertinelli").[5] Columbus cast [Elisabeth Shue](/wiki/Elisabeth_Shue "Elisabeth Shue"), who was a student at [Harvard University](/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") at the time.[1]

Simkins says when the project was at Paramount they wanted Molly Ringwald who turned down the film. He says the studio then proposed Cher and Bette Midler as possible stars. He says when they saw Elisabeth Shue they knew she was the actress they wanted but they still saw a lot of alternatives before offering her the role.[4]

Simkins says the idea of the singing scene at the Blues House came from Chris Columbus.[4]

Principal photography began in [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto "Toronto") on January 5, 1987. Many of the scenes shot there doubled for the film's setting of Chicago. Production designer [Todd Hallowell](/wiki/Todd_Hallowell "Todd Hallowell") simulated Chicago streets by adding trash, and reconstructed two stories of the [Associates Center](/wiki/Crain_Communications_Building "Crain Communications Building") skyscraper in the city for the film's iconic shot of the characters dangling out of it. However, there would also be some location shots in Chicago at landmarks such as the [Chicago "L"](/wiki/Chicago_%22L%22 "Chicago "L""), Fitzgerald's Nightclub, [Lower Wacker Drive](/wiki/Lower_Wacker "Lower Wacker"), the [Chicago Expressway](/wiki/Kennedy_Expressway "Kennedy Expressway"), [Wolf Point](/wiki/Wolf_Point,_Chicago "Wolf Point, Chicago"). Some special effects shots also took place in Los Angeles. [Ric Waite](/wiki/Ric_Waite "Ric Waite") later confirmed that he shot the film using techniques similar to a drama rather than a comedy in order to highlight the film's unpredictable tone.[1]

## Release

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Release")]

The film earned $34.4 million in the United States,[3] which the _Los Angeles Times_ attributed to a new ad campaign.[6]

### Home media

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Home media")]

_Adventures in Babysitting_ was the first Walt Disney movie to have a PG-13 rating. 

The film has been released on VHS and Betamax, LaserDisc, DVD and Blu-ray formats. In the United States, it received a VHS release by [Touchstone Home Video](/wiki/Touchstone_Home_Video "Touchstone Home Video") on July 14, 1988.[7] It was released on DVD for the first time on January 18, 2000, by Touchstone Home Video.[8] A 25th anniversary edition Blu-ray was released on August 7, 2012. 

Although it may still be referred to as _A Night on the Town_ on television airings in the United Kingdom, the film was released on rental VHS in the UK under its original title. The VHS was re-released on October 21, 2002, in the United Kingdom by Cinema Club and it received a 15 certificate by the BBFC[9] for strong language and sexual references. It was previously released in an edited PG certificate for family viewing. It was released on DVD in the United Kingdom on May 31, 2004, again uncut like the 15 certificate VHS. It has since been reduced to a 12 certificate.[10]

The PG version currently streams on [Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B "Disney+"), where a notice advises it has been edited for content, primarily removing strong and offensive language.[11][12]

### Soundtrack album

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Soundtrack album")]

In 2015, [Intrada Records](/wiki/Intrada_Records "Intrada Records") released an album from the film, featuring the score by [Michael Kamen](/wiki/Michael_Kamen "Michael Kamen"), including unused music and several of the songs heard in the film. It features "[Then He Kissed Me](/wiki/Then_He_Kissed_Me "Then He Kissed Me")" by [the Crystals](/wiki/The_Crystals "The Crystals"), "Babysitting Blues" by [Albert Collins](/wiki/Albert_Collins "Albert Collins"), "[Twenty-Five Miles](/wiki/Twenty-Five_Miles "Twenty-Five Miles")" by [Edwin Starr](/wiki/Edwin_Starr "Edwin Starr"), and "Just Can't Stop" by [Percy Sledge](/wiki/Percy_Sledge "Percy Sledge"). 

#### "Babysitting Blues" song

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: "Babysitting Blues" song")]

In a 2021 interview in _[New York Magazine](/wiki/New_York_Magazine "New York Magazine")_ , Elisabeth Shue said performing the "iconic _Babysitting Blues_ song," written by pop songwriter [Mark Mueller](/wiki/Mark_Mueller "Mark Mueller") and [Robert Kraft](/wiki/Robert_Kraft_\(composer\) "Robert Kraft \(composer\)"), was one of her "favorite experiences of all time."[13]

## Reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Reception")]

On [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), the film holds an approval rating of 73% based on 48 reviews. The site's consensus states: "Sweet and spry, _Adventures in Babysitting_ gets by on its amiable tone."[14] On [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), the film has a weighted average score of 45 out of 100, based on 11 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.[15]

[Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert "Roger Ebert") of the _[Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times "Chicago Sun-Times")_ gave _Adventures in Babysitting_ two-and-a-half out of four stars. He cited the blues club sequence as the movie's best scene, but criticized the film for not doing more with its black characters. He said the movie had "good raw material," but too many "unrealized possibilities."[16] [Gene Siskel](/wiki/Gene_Siskel "Gene Siskel") of the _[Chicago Tribune](/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ rated the film three out of four stars, calling it "a genial, warm-hearted romp." He praised the performances of the young cast and called Elisabeth Shue "earnestly appealing," but criticized the movie for a lack of "social awareness."[17] Both critics compared the film to _[Risky Business](/wiki/Risky_Business "Risky Business")_ and _[Ferris Bueller's Day Off](/wiki/Ferris_Bueller%27s_Day_Off "Ferris Bueller's Day Off")_.

The film was adapted into an unsold television pilot of the same name for [CBS](/wiki/CBS "CBS") in 1989.[18][19] The pilot was broadcast on Friday night, July 7 in the 8 p.m. timeslot.[20] It starred [Jennifer Guthrie](/wiki/Jennifer_Guthrie "Jennifer Guthrie") as Chris, [Joey Lawrence](/wiki/Joey_Lawrence "Joey Lawrence") as Brad, [Courtney Peldon](/wiki/Courtney_Peldon "Courtney Peldon") as Sara, [Brian Austin Green](/wiki/Brian_Austin_Green "Brian Austin Green") as Daryl, and Ariana Mohit as Brenda.[18] The pilot garnered CBS a 6.9 [rating](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings "Nielsen ratings") and was watched by 9.7 million viewers.[21][22]

## Remake

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Adventures_in_Babysitting&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Remake")]

Main article: [Adventures in Babysitting (2016 film)](/wiki/Adventures_in_Babysitting_\(2016_film\) "Adventures in Babysitting \(2016 film\)")

In 2016, a loose remake of the film, also titled _[Adventures in Babysitting](/wiki/Adventures_in_Babysitting_\(2016_film\) "Adventures in Babysitting \(2016 film\)"),_ premiered on the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel "Disney Channel") in the United States. The film stars [Sabrina Carpenter](/wiki/Sabrina_Carpenter "Sabrina Carpenter") and [Sofia Carson](/wiki/Sofia_Carson "Sofia Carson") as competing babysitters.
