**Ana Celia de Armas Caso** (Spanish pronunciation: [[ˈana ˈselja ðe ˈaɾmas ˈkaso]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish"); born 30 April 1988)[2] is a Cuban and Spanish actress. She has been nominated for several accolades, including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award"), an [Actor Award](/wiki/Actor_Awards "Actor Awards"), a [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award "BAFTA Award"), and two [Golden Globes](/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes"). 

She began her career in Cuba with a leading role in the romantic drama _[Una rosa de Francia](/wiki/Una_rosa_de_Francia "Una rosa de Francia")_ (2006). At the age of 18, she moved to [Madrid](/wiki/Madrid "Madrid"), Spain, and starred in the popular drama _[El Internado](/wiki/El_Internado "El Internado")_ (2007–2010). After moving to [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), de Armas had English-speaking roles in the psychological thriller _[Knock Knock](/wiki/Knock_Knock_\(2015_film\) "Knock Knock \(2015 film\)")_ (2015) and the comedy-crime film _[War Dogs](/wiki/War_Dogs_\(2016_film\) "War Dogs \(2016 film\)")_ (2016). 

De Armas rose to prominence for her roles as the [holographic](/wiki/Holographic "Holographic") [AI](/wiki/AI "AI") Joi in the science fiction film _[Blade Runner 2049](/wiki/Blade_Runner_2049 "Blade Runner 2049")_ (2017) and nurse Marta Cabrera in the mystery film _[Knives Out](/wiki/Knives_Out "Knives Out")_ (2019), receiving a nomination for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical"). She then played Paloma in the [James Bond](/wiki/Portrayal_of_James_Bond_in_film "Portrayal of James Bond in film") film _[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die "No Time to Die")_ (2021) and actress [Marilyn Monroe](/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe "Marilyn Monroe") in the biographical drama _[Blonde](/wiki/Blonde_\(2022_film\) "Blonde \(2022 film\)")_ (2022), for which she became the first Cuban nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"). She then led the action thriller [_Ballerina_](/wiki/Ballerina_\(2025_film\) "Ballerina \(2025 film\)") (2025), a [spinoff](/wiki/Spinoff_\(media\) "Spinoff \(media\)") installment in the [_John Wick_ franchise](/wiki/John_Wick "John Wick"). 

## Early life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ana_de_Armas&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")]

De Armas was born in [Havana](/wiki/Havana "Havana"), Cuba,[2] and raised in [Santa Cruz del Norte](/wiki/Santa_Cruz_del_Norte "Santa Cruz del Norte").[3] Her maternal grandparents were [Spanish immigrants to Cuba](/wiki/Spanish_immigration_to_Cuba "Spanish immigration to Cuba") from the regions of [Valverde de la Sierra](/w/index.php?title=Valverde_de_la_Sierra&action=edit&redlink=1 "Valverde de la Sierra \(page does not exist\)") [[es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valverde_de_la_Sierra "es:Valverde de la Sierra")], [León](/wiki/Province_of_Le%C3%B3n "Province of León"), and [Guardo](/wiki/Guardo "Guardo"), [Palencia](/wiki/Province_of_Palencia "Province of Palencia"), both in the north of Spain.[4][5][6] Her father Ramón de Armas had various jobs, including bank manager, teacher, school principal and deputy mayor of a town.[7] He previously studied philosophy at a Soviet university.[7][8] Her mother Ana Caso worked in the human resources section of the Ministry of Education.[9][10][11] De Armas has one older brother, Francisco Javier de Armas Caso, an American–based photographer[7][12] who, in 2020, was questioned by Cuban police due to his critical stance on [Decree 349](/wiki/Decree_349 "Decree 349") and his links to artists under government surveillance.[13] While de Armas grew up with [food rationing](/wiki/Rationing_in_Cuba "Rationing in Cuba"), fuel shortages and electricity blackouts during Cuba's [Special Period](/wiki/Special_Period "Special Period"),[7][14] she has described her early life as happy.[9]

During her childhood and adolescence, de Armas had limited knowledge of popular culture beyond Cuba.[15][16] She was allowed to watch "20 minutes of cartoons on Saturday and the Sunday movie matinee."[17] Her family did not own a VCR, and she watched Hollywood movies in her neighbor's apartment.[18] She memorized and practiced monologues in front of a mirror,[19][20] and decided to become an actress when she was 12.[21] In 2002, aged 14, she successfully auditioned to join Havana's [National Theatre of Cuba](/wiki/National_Theatre_of_Cuba "National Theatre of Cuba").[9][22] She sometimes hitchhiked to attend the "rigorous" course.[23][24] While a student, she filmed three movies.[7][10] She left the four-year drama course shortly before presenting her final thesis because Cuban graduates are forbidden from leaving the country without first completing three years of mandatory service to the community.[10][25] At age 18, with Spanish citizenship through her maternal grandparents,[4][14] she moved to [Madrid](/wiki/Madrid "Madrid") to pursue an acting career.[10]

### Career beginnings in Spanish cinema (2006–2013)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ana_de_Armas&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Career beginnings in Spanish cinema \(2006–2013\)")]

In her native Cuba, de Armas had a starring role opposite [Álex González](/wiki/%C3%81lex_Gonz%C3%A1lez_\(actor\) "Álex González \(actor\)") in [Manuel Gutiérrez Aragón](/wiki/Manuel_Guti%C3%A9rrez_Arag%C3%B3n "Manuel Gutiérrez Aragón")'s romantic drama _[Una rosa de Francia](/wiki/Una_rosa_de_Francia "Una rosa de Francia")_ (2006).[14] Cuban actor [Jorge Perugorría](/wiki/Jorge_Perugorr%C3%ADa "Jorge Perugorría") suggested that the director consider de Armas for the role, after meeting her while attending a birthday party with his daughters.[26][27] The director visited de Armas's drama school and interrupted the sixteen-year-old during her audition to inform her that the role was hers.[26][28] She travelled to Spain as part of a promotional tour for the film and was introduced to Juan Lanja, who would later become her Spanish agent.[26] She then starred in the movie _El edén perdido_ (2007) and had a supporting role in [Fernando Pérez](/wiki/Fernando_P%C3%A9rez_\(director\) "Fernando Pérez \(director\)")'s _Madrigal_ (2007), filmed at night without the permission of her drama school tutors.[10]

[](/wiki/File:Personajes_de_El_Internado.jpg)De Armas (standing at the center with number 4) with the cast of _[El internado](/wiki/El_internado "El internado")_ in 2008

At age 18, de Armas moved to Madrid. Within two weeks of arriving, she met with casting director Luis San Narciso, who had seen her in _Una rosa de Francia_.[15] Two months later,[29] he cast her as Carolina in the drama _[El Internado](/wiki/El_Internado "El Internado")_ ,[10] in which she starred for six seasons from 2007 to 2010. The television show, set in a boarding school, became popular with viewers and made de Armas a celebrity figure in Spain.[10] In a break from filming, she starred in the successful coming-of-age comedy _[Mentiras y Gordas](/wiki/Mentiras_y_Gordas "Mentiras y Gordas")_ (2009).[30] Despite the popularity of _El Internado_ , de Armas felt typecast and was mainly offered roles as youngsters.[15] She asked to be written out of the show in its second to last season.[31]

After spending a few months living in New York City to learn English,[25] de Armas was persuaded to return to Spain to star in seventeen episodes of the historical drama _[Hispania](/wiki/Hispania,_la_leyenda "Hispania, la leyenda")_ (2010–2011).[3] She then starred in [Antonio Trashorras](/wiki/Antonio_Trashorras "Antonio Trashorras")'s horror films _El callejón_ (2011) and _Anabel_ (2015),[32] and in the drama _[Por un puñado de besos](/wiki/Por_un_pu%C3%B1ado_de_besos "Por un puñado de besos")_ (2014).[33] During a long period without acting work,[23] de Armas participated in workshops at [Tomaz Pandur](/wiki/Tomaz_Pandur "Tomaz Pandur")'s Madrid theatre company[7] and felt "very anxious" about the lack of momentum in her career.[17]

### Transition to Hollywood and breakthrough (2014–2019)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ana_de_Armas&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Transition to Hollywood and breakthrough \(2014–2019\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Ana_de_Armas_by_Gage_Skidmore.jpg)De Armas at the [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con") in 2017

With encouragement from her newly hired Hollywood agent, she decided to move to Los Angeles.[15] When de Armas first arrived in Los Angeles in 2014,[34] she had to start her career again "from scratch."[18] She spoke very little English and, during early auditions, she often "didn't even know what [she] was saying."[8] She spent four months in full-time education to learn English,[35][31] not wanting to be confined to playing characters written specifically for Latina actresses.[10] She starred opposite [Keanu Reeves](/wiki/Keanu_Reeves "Keanu Reeves") in her first Hollywood release—[Eli Roth](/wiki/Eli_Roth "Eli Roth")'s erotic thriller _[Knock Knock](/wiki/Knock_Knock_\(2015_film\) "Knock Knock \(2015 film\)")_ (2015)—and learned her lines phonetically.[36] Despite giving a positive review of the film, Randy Cordova of the _[Arizona Republic](/wiki/Arizona_Republic "Arizona Republic")_ found de Armas to be "unconvincing" in her role.[37] Reeves then telephoned de Armas to invite her to star in a Spanish-language role in the thriller _Daughter of God_ which he acted in and produced.[38] Producer Mark Downie hoped the film would be a star vehicle for de Armas, but due to executive meddling, _Daughter of God_ was severely edited with de Armas' former starring role reduced. The film was ultimately released as _[Exposed](/wiki/Exposed_\(2016_film\) "Exposed \(2016 film\)")_ in 2016.[39][40] [Frank Scheck](/wiki/Frank_Scheck "Frank Scheck") of _[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ noted that while she was "appealing" in her part, de Armas was unable to demonstrate her "character's intense emotional demands."[41]

De Armas had a supporting role in [Todd Phillips](/wiki/Todd_Phillips "Todd Phillips")'s _[War Dogs](/wiki/War_Dogs_\(2016_film\) "War Dogs \(2016 film\)")_ (2016), acting opposite [Miles Teller](/wiki/Miles_Teller "Miles Teller") as the wife of an arms dealer, and again learned her lines phonetically.[42] [David Ehrlich](/wiki/David_Ehrlich "David Ehrlich") of _[IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire "IndieWire")_ found her to be "memorable in a thankless role".[43] She starred opposite [Édgar Ramírez](/wiki/%C3%89dgar_Ram%C3%ADrez "Édgar Ramírez") in the biopic _[Hands of Stone](/wiki/Hands_of_Stone "Hands of Stone")_ (2016) as the wife of Panamanian boxer [Roberto Durán](/wiki/Roberto_Dur%C3%A1n "Roberto Durán"). Despite its delayed release, _Hands of Stone_ was the first Hollywood film de Armas had filmed. While still living in Madrid, she was contacted by director [Jonathan Jakubowicz](/wiki/Jonathan_Jakubowicz "Jonathan Jakubowicz"), who had watched her in _El Internado_ ,[31] who asked her to travel to Los Angeles to audition for the Spanish-language part.[14] In reviewing the film, Christy Lemire of _[RogerEbert.com](/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")_ described de Armas as "a hugely charismatic presence. But except for a couple of showy moments, she gets little to do besides function as the dutiful wife."[44]

In [Denis Villeneuve](/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve "Denis Villeneuve")'s futuristic thriller _[Blade Runner 2049](/wiki/Blade_Runner_2049 "Blade Runner 2049")_ (2017), de Armas had a supporting role as Joi, the [holographic](/wiki/Holographic "Holographic") [AI](/wiki/AI "AI") girlfriend of [Ryan Gosling](/wiki/Ryan_Gosling "Ryan Gosling")'s character, a blade runner. Mark Kermode of _The Guardian_ said she "brings three-dimensional warmth to a character who is essentially a digital projection."[45] Anthony Lane of _[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ found her to be "wondrous": "Whenever Joi appears, the movie's imaginative heart begins to race."[46] While the performance was initially discussed as a breakthrough role,[47][34] the film underperformed commercially, and de Armas spent much of the following year in her native Cuba, where she purchased a house.[36] For her performance, she earned a nomination for the [Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress"). Also in 2017, she had a supporting role in the action thriller _[Overdrive](/wiki/Overdrive_\(2017_film\) "Overdrive \(2017 film\)")_ as the love interest to [Scott Eastwood](/wiki/Scott_Eastwood "Scott Eastwood")'s character.[48] Stephen Dalton of _[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ wrote that she "radiates more kick-ass charisma than her thankless sidekick role might suggest."[49]

[](/wiki/File:Ana_de_Armas,_March_2018,_GQ_Mexico_04_cropped.jpg)De Armas in 2018

In 2018, de Armas starred opposite [Demián Bichir](/wiki/Demi%C3%A1n_Bichir "Demián Bichir") in [John Hillcoat](/wiki/John_Hillcoat "John Hillcoat")'s medical drama _Corazón_. She played a Dominican woman with [congestive heart failure](/wiki/Congestive_heart_failure "Congestive heart failure") in the short film, funded by [Montefiore Medical Center](/wiki/Montefiore_Medical_Center "Montefiore Medical Center") to raise awareness of organ donation.[50] While de Armas's scenes opposite [Himesh Patel](/wiki/Himesh_Patel "Himesh Patel") in the 2019 romantic comedy _[Yesterday](/wiki/Yesterday_\(2019_film\) "Yesterday \(2019 film\)")_ were included in the film's trailer, they were cut from the final product. The director [Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle "Danny Boyle") said that, while de Armas was "really radiant" in her scenes, the introduction of a love triangle subplot did not test well with audiences.[51]

De Armas' role as an immigrant nurse in the ensemble murder mystery film _[Knives Out](/wiki/Knives_Out "Knives Out")_ (2019), written and directed by [Rian Johnson](/wiki/Rian_Johnson "Rian Johnson"), was widely praised and marked a breakthrough for the actress.[52] When first approached about the project, she was unenthusiastic about the idea of playing a stereotypical "Latina caregiver" but soon realized that her character was "so much more than that."[53] Tom Shone of _[The Times](/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ remarked, "The film's standout performance comes from its least well-known member, the Cuban de Armas, who manages the difficult task of making goodness interesting."[54] Benjamin Lee of _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ said her "striking" performance left a "lasting impression."[55] The film was a major box office success.[56] De Armas was nominated for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Comedy or Musical")[57] with her also winning the [Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress "Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress") and the [National Board of Review Award for Best Cast](/wiki/National_Board_of_Review_Award_for_Best_Cast "National Board of Review Award for Best Cast") with the cast.[58]

### Leading roles and mainstream recognition (2020–present)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ana_de_Armas&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Leading roles and mainstream recognition \(2020–present\)")]

[](/wiki/File:Ana_de_armas_\(52409484768\)_\(cropped\).jpg)De Armas at the [San Sebastián International Film Festival](/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_International_Film_Festival "San Sebastián International Film Festival") in 2022

De Armas starred in four films released in the United States in 2020. She had a supporting role in the crime thriller _[The Informer](/wiki/The_Informer_\(2019_film\) "The Informer \(2019 film\)")_ as the wife of [Joel Kinnaman](/wiki/Joel_Kinnaman "Joel Kinnaman")'s character.[59] Guy Lodge of _[Variety](/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\) "Variety \(magazine\)")_ found "her thin role all the more glaring in the wake of her _Knives Out_ stardom."[60] She appeared as a [femme fatale](/wiki/Femme_fatale "Femme fatale") in the noir crime drama _[The Night Clerk](/wiki/The_Night_Clerk "The Night Clerk")_.[61] Brian Tallerico of _[RogerEbert.com](/wiki/RogerEbert.com "RogerEbert.com")_ said the film had "no idea" what to do with her "blinding charisma"[62] while Katie Rife of _The AV Club_ remarked that it would be remembered, "if at all, as a movie de Armas was way too good for."[63] She starred opposite [Wagner Moura](/wiki/Wagner_Moura "Wagner Moura") in the Netflix biopic _[Sergio](/wiki/Sergio_\(2020_film\) "Sergio \(2020 film\)")_ (2020) as Carolina Larriera, a U.N. official and the partner of diplomat [Sérgio Vieira de Mello](/wiki/S%C3%A9rgio_Vieira_de_Mello "Sérgio Vieira de Mello"). John DeFore of _[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter "The Hollywood Reporter")_ found her "magnetic"[64] while Jessica Kiang of _Variety_ said she imbued the part "with an intelligence and will that makes her more than just de Mello's romantic foil."[65] De Armas reunited with Moura to play the wife of one of the [Cuban Five](/wiki/Cuban_Five "Cuban Five") in [Olivier Assayas](/wiki/Olivier_Assayas "Olivier Assayas")'s Netflix spy thriller _[Wasp Network](/wiki/Wasp_Network_\(film\) "Wasp Network \(film\)")_.[66] The film was shot on location in Cuba; it was de Armas's first work in her home country since leaving as a teenager.[67] Glenn Kenny of _The New York Times_ found her "superb"[68] while Jay Weissberg of _Variety_ described her as "a joyous, bewitching presence whose career seems destined for the big time."[69]

In 2021, de Armas reunited with Daniel Craig to play a [Bond girl](/wiki/Bond_girl "Bond girl") in [Cary Joji Fukunaga](/wiki/Cary_Joji_Fukunaga "Cary Joji Fukunaga")'s _[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die "No Time to Die")_.[70][71] Fukunaga wrote the character of a Cuban CIA agent with de Armas in mind.[72] She described the character as bubbly and "very irresponsible".[70] In her short appearance in _No Time to Die_ , her character, Paloma, claims to have little training, but proves to be highly skilled while fighting.[73] _No Time to Die_ was a commercial success, grossing $774.2 million worldwide, and earned positive reviews.[74][75][76][77] [Peter Bradshaw](/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw "Peter Bradshaw") of _[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_ praised de Armas' "witty and unworldly turn".[78] De Armas starred in [Adrian Lyne](/wiki/Adrian_Lyne "Adrian Lyne")'s erotic thriller _[Deep Water](/wiki/Deep_Water_\(2022_film\) "Deep Water \(2022 film\)")_ , based upon the [novel](/wiki/Deep_Water_\(Highsmith_novel\) "Deep Water \(Highsmith novel\)") by [Patricia Highsmith](/wiki/Patricia_Highsmith "Patricia Highsmith"). She and [Ben Affleck](/wiki/Ben_Affleck "Ben Affleck") play a couple in an open marriage.[79][80] In 2022, de Armas starred in the [Russo brothers](/wiki/Russo_brothers "Russo brothers")' Netflix action thriller _[The Gray Man](/wiki/The_Gray_Man_\(2022_film\) "The Gray Man \(2022 film\)")_.[81] Neither _Deep Water_ nor _The Gray Man_ were particularly successful with critics and audiences.[_[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

De Armas portrayed [Marilyn Monroe](/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe "Marilyn Monroe") (as Norma Jean) in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") biopic _[Blonde](/wiki/Blonde_\(2022_film\) "Blonde \(2022 film\)")_ (2022), based on the [biographical fiction novel of the same name](/wiki/Blonde_\(novel\) "Blonde \(novel\)") by [Joyce Carol Oates](/wiki/Joyce_Carol_Oates "Joyce Carol Oates").[82] Director [Andrew Dominik](/wiki/Andrew_Dominik "Andrew Dominik") noticed de Armas's performance in _Knock Knock_[83] and, while she went through a long casting process, Dominik secured the role for her after the first audition.[84] In preparation, de Armas worked with a [dialect coach](/wiki/Dialect_coach "Dialect coach") for about nine months,[85][8][3][86] read Oates' novel and also said she studied hundreds of photographs, videos, audio recordings, and films to prepare for the role.[87] Despite criticism towards her casting, due to her having a notable Spanish accent, de Armas' performance was praised; Catherine Bray of _[Empire](/wiki/Empire_\(magazine\) "Empire \(magazine\)")_ labeled de Armas' performance as "powerful", while [Richard Lawson](/wiki/Richard_Lawson_\(writer\) "Richard Lawson \(writer\)") of [_Vanity Fair_](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_\(magazine\) "Vanity Fair \(magazine\)") remarked that "De Armas is fiercely, almost scarily committed to the role, maintaining high and focused energy through every torrent of tears and screams and traumas."[88][89][90] She received a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress"), in addition to nominations for the [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role "BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role"), [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama "Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Drama") and [SAG Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role "Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role") in the same category.[91][92] She became the first Cuban to be nominated for the first of these.[93]

[](/wiki/File:Ana_de_Armas_at_the_2024_Toronto_International_Film_Festival_05_\(cropped\).jpg)De Armas at the [Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival "Toronto International Film Festival") in 2024

De Armas next starred with [Chris Evans](/wiki/Chris_Evans_\(actor\) "Chris Evans \(actor\)") in the [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") action comedy film _[Ghosted](/wiki/Ghosted_\(2023_film\) "Ghosted \(2023 film\)")_ (2023).[94] Benjamin Lee of _The Guardian_ panned the film and the lack of chemistry between de Armas and Evans.[95] She appeared alongside an ensemble cast in [Ron Howard](/wiki/Ron_Howard "Ron Howard")'s survival thriller _[Eden](/wiki/Eden_\(2024_film\) "Eden \(2024 film\)")_ (2024).[96] She then starred in the _[John Wick](/wiki/John_Wick "John Wick")_ spin-off action thriller _[Ballerina](/wiki/From_the_World_of_John_Wick:_Ballerina "From the World of John Wick: Ballerina")_ (2025)_,_ in which she portrayed a vengeful assassin.[97]

  


## Personal life

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ana_de_Armas&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Personal life")]

De Armas holds triple Cuban, Spanish, and US citizenship.[98] She moved to Los Angeles at 26, and resides in [Vermont](/wiki/Vermont "Vermont") as of 2023.[99][100]

De Armas began a relationship with Spanish actor [Marc Clotet](/wiki/Marc_Clotet "Marc Clotet") in mid-2010. They married on the [Costa Brava](/wiki/Costa_Brava "Costa Brava") in July 2011. They divorced in early 2013.[101][102] After meeting on the set of _[Deep Water](/wiki/Deep_Water_\(2022_film\) "Deep Water \(2022 film\)")_ in late 2019, de Armas dated American actor [Ben Affleck](/wiki/Ben_Affleck "Ben Affleck") from March 2020 to January 2021.[103][104] De Armas received significant criticism, particularly among members of the Cuban diaspora, for reportedly dating Anido Cuesta, the stepson of [Cuban President](/wiki/Cuban_President "Cuban President") [Miguel Díaz-Canel](/wiki/Miguel_D%C3%ADaz-Canel "Miguel Díaz-Canel") since November 2024.[105] It was reported in July 2025 that she was dating American actor [Tom Cruise](/wiki/Tom_Cruise "Tom Cruise") after she was spotted attending an [Oasis](/wiki/Oasis_\(band\) "Oasis \(band\)") concert with him.[106] It has since been reported that the couple separated in October or November 2025.[107]
