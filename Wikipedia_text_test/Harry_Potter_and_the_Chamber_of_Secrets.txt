_**Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets**_ is a [fantasy novel](/wiki/Fantasy_novel "Fantasy novel") by British author [J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling"). The second novel in the _[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter "Harry Potter")_ series, it chronicles [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)")'s second year at [Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry](/wiki/Hogwarts_School_of_Witchcraft_and_Wizardry "Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry"). After several residents of the school are attacked and magically immobilised, Harry and his friends [Ron](/wiki/Ron_Weasley "Ron Weasley") and [Hermione](/wiki/Hermione_Granger "Hermione Granger") investigate. 

The book was published in the United Kingdom on 2 July 1998 by [Bloomsbury](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing") and later in the United States on 2 June 1999 by [Scholastic Inc](/wiki/Scholastic_Inc "Scholastic Inc"). Although Rowling says she found it difficult to finish the book, it won high praise and awards from critics, young readers, and the book industry, although some critics thought the story was perhaps too frightening for younger children. Much like with other novels in the series, _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ [triggered religious debates](/wiki/Religious_debates_over_the_Harry_Potter_series "Religious debates over the Harry Potter series"); some religious authorities have condemned its use of magical themes, whereas others have praised its emphasis on self-sacrifice and the way one's character is the result of one's choices. 

Several commentators have noted that personal identity is a strong theme in the book and that it addresses issues of racism through the treatment of non-human, non-magical, and non-living people. Some commentators regard the story's diary that writes back as a warning against uncritical acceptance of information from sources whose motives and reliability cannot be checked. Institutional authority is portrayed as self-serving and incompetent. 

The [film adaptation of the novel](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets \(film\)"), released in 2002, became (at the time) the [fifth highest-grossing film ever](/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films "List of highest-grossing films") and received generally favourable reviews. Video games loosely based on _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ were also released for several platforms, and most obtained favourable reviews. 

## Plot

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Plot")]

While spending the summer with the [Dursleys](/wiki/List_of_supporting_Harry_Potter_characters "List of supporting Harry Potter characters"), the twelve-year-old [Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_\(character\) "Harry Potter \(character\)") is visited by [Dobby](/wiki/Dobby_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dobby \(Harry Potter\)"), a [house-elf](/wiki/Magical_creatures_in_Harry_Potter "Magical creatures in Harry Potter"). Dobby says Harry is in danger and must promise not to return to [Hogwarts](/wiki/Hogwarts "Hogwarts"). When Harry refuses, Dobby uses magic to destroy a pudding made by Aunt Petunia. Believing that Harry created the mess, Uncle Vernon locks him in his room. The [Ministry of Magic](/wiki/Ministry_of_Magic "Ministry of Magic") sends a notice accusing Harry of performing underage magic and threatening to expel him from Hogwarts if it happens again. 

The Weasley brothers [Ron](/wiki/Ron_Weasley "Ron Weasley"), [Fred](/wiki/Fred_and_George_Weasley "Fred and George Weasley"), and [George](/wiki/Fred_and_George_Weasley "Fred and George Weasley") arrive in their father's [flying car](/wiki/Arthur_Weasley%27s_flying_Ford_Anglia "Arthur Weasley's flying Ford Anglia") and take Harry to their home. When Harry and the Weasleys go to [Diagon Alley](/wiki/Diagon_Alley "Diagon Alley") for school supplies, they meet [Gilderoy Lockhart](/wiki/Gilderoy_Lockhart "Gilderoy Lockhart"), a celebrity author who is the new [Defence Against the Dark Arts](/wiki/Defence_Against_the_Dark_Arts "Defence Against the Dark Arts") professor. At [King's Cross station](/wiki/London_King%27s_Cross_railway_station "London King's Cross railway station"), Harry and Ron cannot enter [Platform 9¾](/wiki/Platform_9_3/4 "Platform 9 3/4") to board the [Hogwarts Express](/wiki/Hogwarts_Express "Hogwarts Express"), so they fly to Hogwarts in the enchanted car. 

During the school year, Harry hears a strange voice emanating from the castle walls. [Argus Filch](/wiki/Argus_Filch "Argus Filch")'s cat is found magically immobilised, along with a warning scrawled on the wall: "The Chamber of Secrets has been opened. Enemies of the heir, beware". Harry learns that the Chamber supposedly houses a monster that attacks [Muggle-born](/wiki/Muggle-born "Muggle-born") students, and which only the Heir of Slytherin can control. During a [Quidditch](/wiki/Quidditch "Quidditch") match, a rogue [Bludger](/wiki/Bludger_\(Harry_Potter\) "Bludger \(Harry Potter\)") strikes Harry, breaking his arm. Professor Lockhart botches an attempt to mend the injury, which sends Harry to the hospital wing. Dobby visits Harry and reveals that he jinxed the Bludger and sealed the portal at King's Cross. He also tells Harry that house-elves are bound to serve a master, and cannot be freed unless their master gives them clothing. 

After another attack from the monster, students attend a defensive duelling class. During the class, Harry displays the rare ability to speak [Parseltongue](/wiki/Parselmouth "Parselmouth"), the language of snakes. [Moaning Myrtle](/wiki/Moaning_Myrtle "Moaning Myrtle"), a ghost who haunts a bathroom, shows Harry and his friends a [diary](/wiki/Tom_Riddle%27s_diary "Tom Riddle's diary") that was left in her stall. It belonged to [Tom Riddle](/wiki/Tom_Riddle "Tom Riddle"), a student who witnessed another student's death when the Chamber was last opened. During the next attack by the monster, [Hermione Granger](/wiki/Hermione_Granger "Hermione Granger") is immobilised. 

Harry and Ron learn that the monster is a [Basilisk](/wiki/Basilisk_\(Harry_Potter\) "Basilisk \(Harry Potter\)"), a gigantic snake that can kill victims with a direct gaze and immobilise them with an indirect gaze. Harry realizes the Basilisk is producing the voice that he hears in the walls. After Ron's sister [Ginny](/wiki/Ginny_Weasley "Ginny Weasley") is abducted and taken into the Chamber, Harry and Ron discover the Chamber entrance in Myrtle's bathroom. When they force Lockhart to enter with them, he confesses that the stories he told of his heroic adventures are fabrications. He attempts to erase the boys' memories, but his spell backfires and obliterates his own memory. 

Harry finds Ginny unconscious in the Chamber. A manifestation of Tom Riddle appears and reveals that Riddle is [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort") and the Heir of Slytherin. After explaining that he opened the Chamber, Riddle summons the Basilisk to kill Harry. Dumbledore's phoenix [Fawkes](/wiki/Fawkes_\(Harry_Potter\) "Fawkes \(Harry Potter\)") arrives, bringing Harry the [Sorting Hat](/wiki/Sorting_Hat "Sorting Hat"). While Fawkes blinds the Basilisk, Harry pulls the [Sword of Gryffindor](/wiki/Sword_of_Godric_Gryffindor "Sword of Godric Gryffindor") from the Hat. He slays the serpent, then stabs the diary with a Basilisk fang, destroying it and the manifestation of Riddle. Later, Harry liberates Dobby by tricking his master, Lucius Malfoy, into giving him clothing. At the end of the novel, the immobilised students are cured and Gryffindor wins the House Cup. 

## Publication and reception

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Publication and reception")]

### Development

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Development")]

[J. K. Rowling](/wiki/J._K._Rowling "J. K. Rowling") found it difficult to finish _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ because she was afraid it would not live up to the expectations raised by _[Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Philosopher%27s_Stone "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone")_. After delivering the manuscript to [Bloomsbury Publishing](/wiki/Bloomsbury_Publishing "Bloomsbury Publishing") on schedule, she took it back for six weeks of revision.[1]

In early drafts of the book, the ghost [Nearly Headless Nick](/wiki/Nearly_Headless_Nick "Nearly Headless Nick") sang a self-composed song explaining his condition and the circumstances of his unknown death. This was cut because the book's editor did not care for the poem, which has been subsequently published as an extra on J. K. Rowling's official website.[2] The family background of [Dean Thomas](/wiki/Dean_Thomas_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dean Thomas \(Harry Potter\)") was removed because Rowling and her publishers considered it an "unnecessary digression" and she considered [Neville Longbottom](/wiki/Neville_Longbottom "Neville Longbottom")'s own journey of discovery "more important to the central plot".[3]

[Gilderoy Lockhart](/wiki/Gilderoy_Lockhart "Gilderoy Lockhart")'s character was inspired by an acquaintance of Rowling who was, in her words, "even more objectionable than his fictional counterpart" and "used to tell whopping great fibs about his past life, all of them designed to demonstrate what a wonderful, brave and brilliant person he was".[4]

### Publication

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Publication")]

_Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ was published in the UK on 2 July 1998 and in the US on 2 June 1999.[5][6] It immediately took first place in UK bestseller lists, displacing popular authors such as [John Grisham](/wiki/John_Grisham "John Grisham"), [Tom Clancy](/wiki/Tom_Clancy "Tom Clancy")[1] and [Terry Pratchett](/wiki/Terry_Pratchett "Terry Pratchett"),[7] and making Rowling the first author to win the [British Book Awards](/wiki/British_Book_Awards "British Book Awards") Children's Book of the Year for two years in succession.[8] In June 1999, it went straight to the top of three US bestseller lists,[9] including in _The New York Times_.[10]

First edition printings had several errors, which were fixed in subsequent reprints.[11] Initially, [Albus Dumbledore](/wiki/Albus_Dumbledore "Albus Dumbledore") said [Lord Voldemort](/wiki/Lord_Voldemort "Lord Voldemort") was the last remaining [ancestor](/wiki/Ancestor "Ancestor") of [Salazar Slytherin](/wiki/Salazar_Slytherin "Salazar Slytherin") instead of his descendant.[11] Gilderoy Lockhart's book on werewolves is entitled _Weekends with Werewolves_ at one point and _Wanderings with Werewolves_ later in the book.[12]

### Critical response

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Critical response")]

In _[The Times](/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ , Deborah Loudon described it as a children's book that would be "re-read into adulthood" and highlighted its "strong plots, engaging characters, excellent jokes and a moral message which flows naturally from the story".[13] Fantasy author [Charles de Lint](/wiki/Charles_de_Lint "Charles de Lint") agreed, and considered the second Harry Potter book to be just as good as _Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone_ , a rare achievement among series of books.[14] Thomas Wagner regarded the plot as very similar to that of the first book, based on searching for a secret hidden under the school. However, he enjoyed the parody of celebrities and their fans that centres round Gilderoy Lockhart, and approved of the book's handling of racism.[15] Tammy Nezol found the book more disturbing than its predecessor, particularly in the rash behaviour of Harry and his friends after Harry withholds information from Dumbledore, and in the human-like behaviour of the [mandrakes](/wiki/Mandrake_\(mythology\) "Mandrake \(mythology\)") used to make a potion that cures immobilisation. Nevertheless, she considered the second story as enjoyable as the first.[16]

Mary Stuart thought the final conflict with Tom Riddle in the Chamber was almost as scary as in some of [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King")'s works, and perhaps too strong for young or timid children. She commented that "there are enough surprises and imaginative details thrown in as would normally fill five lesser books." Like other reviewers, she thought the book would give pleasure to both children and adult readers.[17] According to [Philip Nel](/wiki/Philip_Nel "Philip Nel"), the early reviews gave unalloyed praise while the later ones included some criticisms, although they still agreed that the book was outstanding.[18]

Writing after all seven books had been published, [Graeme Davis](/wiki/Graeme_Davis_\(mediaevalist\) "Graeme Davis \(mediaevalist\)") regarded _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ as the weakest of the series, and agreed that the plot structure is much the same as in _Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone_. He described Fawkes's appearance to arm Harry and then to heal him as a _[deus ex machina](/wiki/Deus_ex_machina "Deus ex machina")_ : he said that the book does not explain how Fawkes knew where to find Harry; and Fawkes's timing had to be very precise, as arriving earlier would probably have prevented the battle with the basilisk, while arriving later would have been fatal to Harry and Ginny.[19]

### Awards and honours

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Awards and honours")]

Rowling's _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ was the recipient of several awards.[20] The [American Library Association](/wiki/American_Library_Association "American Library Association") listed the novel among its 2000 Notable Children's Books,[21] as well as its Best Books for Young Adults.[22] In 1999, [Booklist](/wiki/Booklist "Booklist") named _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ as one of its Editors' Choices,[23] and as one of its Top Ten Fantasy Novels for Youth.[20] The Cooperative Children's Book Center made the novel a CCBC Choice of 2000 in the "Fiction for Children" category.[24] The novel also won Children's Book of the Year British Book Award,[25] and was shortlisted for the 1998 Guardian Children's Award and the 1998 Carnegie Award.[20]

_Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ won the [Nestlé Smarties Book Prize](/wiki/Nestl%C3%A9_Smarties_Book_Prize "Nestlé Smarties Book Prize") 1998 Gold Medal in the 9–11 years division.[25] Rowling also won two other Nestlé Smarties Book Prizes for _Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone_ and _[Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Prisoner_of_Azkaban "Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban")_. The [Scottish Arts Council](/wiki/Scottish_Arts_Council "Scottish Arts Council") awarded their first ever Children's Book Award to the novel in 1999,[26] and it was also awarded Whitaker's Platinum Book Award in 2001.[20][27] In 2003, the novel was listed at number 23 on the [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC")'s survey [The Big Read](/wiki/The_Big_Read "The Big Read").[28]

## Main themes

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Main themes")]

_Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ continues to examine what makes a person who they are, which began in the first book. As well as maintaining that Harry's identity is shaped by his decisions rather than any aspect of his birth,[16][29] _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ provides contrasting characters who try to conceal their true personalities: as Tammy Nezol puts it, Gilderoy Lockhart "lacks any real identity" because he is nothing more than a charming liar.[16] Riddle also complicates Harry's struggle to understand himself by pointing out the similarities between the two: "both half-bloods, orphans raised by [Muggles](/wiki/Muggle "Muggle"), probably the only two Parselmouths to come to Hogwarts since the great Slytherin."[30]

Opposition to class, death and its impacts, experiencing adolescence, sacrifice, love, friendship, loyalty, prejudice, and racism are constant themes of the series. In _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ Harry's consideration and respect for others extends to the lowly, non-human [Dobby](/wiki/Dobby_\(Harry_Potter\) "Dobby \(Harry Potter\)") and the ghost Nearly Headless Nick.[31] According to Marguerite Krause, achievements in the novel depend more on ingenuity and hard work than on natural talents.[32]

Edward Duffy, associate professor at [Marquette University](/wiki/Marquette_University "Marquette University"), says that one of the central characters of _Chamber of Secrets_ is Tom Riddle's enchanted diary, which takes control of Ginny Weasley – just as Riddle planned. Duffy suggests Rowling intended this as a warning against passively consuming information from sources that have their own agendas.[33] Although Bronwyn Williams and Amy Zenger regard the diary as more like an [instant messaging](/wiki/Instant_messaging "Instant messaging") or [chat room](/wiki/Chat_room "Chat room") system, they agree about the dangers of relying too much on the written word, which can camouflage the author, and they highlight a comical example, Lockhart's self-promoting books.[34]

Antonello Fabio Caterino, an Italian forensic linguist and philologist, stated that the conversations Harry and Voldemort had through the diary could be considered a "modern transposition" of [Petrarca](/wiki/Petrarca "Petrarca")'s _[Secretum](/wiki/Secretum_\(book\) "Secretum \(book\)")_ , a text often compared to a diary, which details an imaginary dialogue between the writer and [Augustine of Hippo](/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo "Augustine of Hippo"). Caterino analysed similarities between themes found in both books, starting from the idea of a fragmented soul: Harry and Voldemort are both seeking Voldemort's Horcruxes, which are fragments of his soul, and Petrarca also focuses on the concept _animae fragmenta_ , meaning soul's fragments.[35]

Immorality and the portrayal of authority as negative are significant themes in the novel. Marguerite Krause states there are few absolute moral rules in Harry Potter's world, for example Harry prefers to tell the truth, but lies whenever he considers it necessary – very like his enemy [Draco Malfoy](/wiki/Draco_Malfoy "Draco Malfoy").[32] At the end of _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ , Dumbledore retracts his promise to punish Harry and Ron if they break any more school rules – after [Professor McGonagall](/wiki/Professor_McGonagall "Professor McGonagall") estimates they have broken over 100 – and lavishly rewards them for ending the threat from the Chamber of Secrets.[36] Krause further states that authority figures and political institutions receive little respect from Rowling.[32] William MacNeil of Griffith University, Queensland, Australia states that the [Minister for Magic](/wiki/Minister_for_Magic "Minister for Magic") is presented as a mediocrity.[37] In his article "Harry Potter and the Secular City", Ken Jacobson suggests the [Ministry](/wiki/Ministry_of_Magic "Ministry of Magic") as a whole is portrayed as a tangle of bureaucratic empires, saying that "Ministry officials busy themselves with minutiae (e.g. standardising cauldron thicknesses) and coin politically correct euphemisms like 'non-magical community' (for Muggles) and 'memory modification' (for magical brainwashing)."[29]

This novel implies it begins in 1992: the cake for Nearly-Headless Nick's 500th deathday party bears the words "Sir Nicholas De Mimsy Porpington died 31 October 1492".[38][39]

### Connection to _Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince_

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Connection to Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince")]

_Chamber of Secrets_ has many links with the sixth book of the series, _[Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Half-Blood_Prince "Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince")_. In fact, _Half-Blood Prince_ was the working title of _Chamber of Secrets_ and Rowling says she originally intended to present some "crucial pieces of information" in the second book, but ultimately felt "this information's proper home was book six".[40] Some objects that play significant roles in _Half-Blood Prince_ first appear in _Chamber of Secrets_ : the [Hand of Glory](/wiki/Hand_of_Glory "Hand of Glory") and the opal necklace that are on sale in [Borgin and Burkes](/wiki/Borgin_and_Burkes "Borgin and Burkes"); a Vanishing Cabinet in Hogwarts that is damaged by [Peeves the Poltergeist](/wiki/Peeves "Peeves"); and [Tom Riddle's diary](/wiki/Tom_Riddle%27s_diary "Tom Riddle's diary"), which is later revealed to be a [Horcrux](/wiki/Horcrux "Horcrux").[41] Additionally, these two novels are the ones with the most focus on Harry's relationship with Ginny Weasley. 

## Adaptations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Adaptations")]

### Film

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Film")]

Main article: [_Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ (film)](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Chamber_of_Secrets_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets \(film\)")

The film version of _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ was released in 2002.[42] [Chris Columbus](/wiki/Chris_Columbus_\(filmmaker\) "Chris Columbus \(filmmaker\)") directed the film,[43] and the screenplay was written by [Steve Kloves](/wiki/Steve_Kloves "Steve Kloves"). It became the third film to exceed $600 million in international box office sales, preceded by _[Titanic](/wiki/Titanic_\(1997_film\) "Titanic \(1997 film\)")_ , released in 1997, and _[Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone](/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Philosopher%27s_Stone_\(film\) "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone \(film\)")_ , released in 2001.[44] The film was nominated for a [Saturn Award](/wiki/Saturn_Award "Saturn Award") for the Best Fantasy Film,[44][45] According to [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), the film version of _Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets_ received "generally favourable reviews" with an average score of 63%,[46] and another [aggregator](/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator"), [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes "Rotten Tomatoes"), gave it a score of 82%.[43]
